## Summary

- **Missing:** conjunctions, interjections, *kang*, standalone possessives (*akoa*), indefinite pronouns, ordinals, and the particles *na / pa* as a system.
- **Touched only:** adverbs as a class, the *kanako*-set, place words after *sa*, and verb roots with irregular forms.
- **Taught:** nouns, verbs (A1 scope), adjectives, markers and the linker, the three main pronoun sets, demonstratives, the core particles, and cardinal numbers.

## Proposed lessons

### 1. `ug-pero-kay`: Joining ideas: ug, o, pero, kay, kon (after `negation`)
- **Teaches:** *ug* (and) vs the marker *og*; *o*; *pero*; *kay* (because); *kon* (if; also "when" for things not yet done); *mao nga* (that's why); *aron* (so that) + the *maka-* form.
- **English-speaker trap:** writing *ug* for the marker, or *og* for "and"; the verb after *kon* keeps the future form (*Kon moulan*, not *Kon niulan*); starting a reason with *Tungod kay* where speech uses *kay*.
- **Examples:** *Kape ug pan, palihug.* · *Gigutom ko, mao nga mokaon ko.* · *Kon moulan, dili ko moadto.*
- **Deck:** Top conjunctions.

### 2. `ay-grabe`: Ay!, Aray!, Grabe!: reacting in Cebuano (after `particles`)
- **Teaches:** about 15 reactions grouped by feeling: pain (*Aray! Agoy!*), surprise (*Ay! Hala! Ha?*), delight (*Wow! Kuyaw! Yehey!*), relief (*Hay salamat!*), teasing (*Ay sus! Hala ka!*); attention (*Uy!* friendly, *Hoy!* sharp); and the *ka-* exclamation from any describing word (*Kalami! Kadako!*).
- **English-speaker trap:** carrying over English *ouch / oops*; using *Hoy!* to a stranger; copying the crude local exclamations heard on the street (the lesson names them only as "don't").
- **Examples:** *Aray! Masakit akong tiil.* · *Grabe! Duha ka oras sa dyip.* · *Kalami sa litson!*
- **Deck:** Top interjections (every card has a dialogue).

### 3. `na-pa`: already, still, not yet: na, pa, and how often (after `mag`)
- **Teaches:** *na* (already; now; anymore with *dili na*), *pa* (still; yet; more: *Wala pa*, *Usa pa*); frequency words *kanunay, pirmi, usahay*; *dayon, sayo, ulahi, dugay*; both particles go in second place with the short pronouns.
- **English-speaker trap:** *Wala pa* = "not yet", not "nothing more"; putting *na / pa* at the end the way English puts "already"; using *ka* with *na* in the wrong order (*Nikaon na ka?*, not *Nikaon ka na?*).
- **Examples:** *Nikaon na ka?* · *Wala pa ko kakaon.* · *Usahay moadto mi sa dagat.*
- **Deck:** Top adverbs and Top markers and particles.

### 4. `kanako-akoa`: to me, for you, mine: kanako, kang, akoa (after `an`)
- **Teaches:** the *kanako*-set as a set (*kanako, kanimo, kaniya …*), *kang* before names, *para kanimo*, the standalone possessives *akoa, imoha, iyaha*, and *bisan kinsa / bisan unsa / tanan*.
- **English-speaker trap:** *ni Ana* (of Ana) vs *kang Ana* (to Ana); *akong* (before a noun) vs *akoa* (on its own); *Ihatag nako* (I'll give it) vs *Ihatag kanako* (give it to me).
- **Examples:** *Ihatag kang Pedro ang libro.* · *Para kanimo kini.* · *Akoa kanang bag.*
- **Deck:** Top pronouns (and *kang* in Top markers and particles).

### Smaller gaps (a section in an existing lesson, not a new lesson)

- **Spanish 13–19 and ordinals**: one section in `numbers` (prices *trese pesos*, ages *disiotso anyos*; *una, ikaduha, ikatulo*). Links to Top words, which has 13–19 but no ordinals.
- **Irregular verb roots** (*kan-on, imnon, dad-on; matulog, maligo; mangutana, mangita*): a table in `on` and `mag`. Links to Top verbs.
