Core-words writer for `de`, 2026-11-23. Read from `lessons-1.js`, `lessons-2.js` and `lessons-3.js`: every lesson's title, blurb and section headings, plus word searches across all three files. No lesson was written or changed.

**Key**
- **taught**: a lesson presents it as grammar, with forms and practice.
- **touched**: it appears inside another lesson's topic, or only part of the class is taught.
- **missing**: no lesson presents it.

## Summary

- **Missing:** interjections.
- **Touched only:** adverbs as a class (placement, *schon / noch / erst*, particles); subordinating conjunctions beyond *wenn / weil / dass* (*als, ob, obwohl*), and the inversion after *trotzdem / deshalb*; indefinite and reflexive pronouns (*man, sich, etwas, niemand*); *dieser / jeder / welcher*; ordinal numbers.
- **Taught:** nouns, verbs (A1 scope), adjectives, personal pronouns in three cases, possessives, prepositions with dative / accusative / two-way, articles and *kein*, cardinal numbers.

## Proposed lessons (outlines only; nothing written)

### 1. `interjections`: Ach so!, Na ja, Mist!: reacting in German (after `connectors`)
- **Teaches:** the 15–20 reactions people use, grouped by feeling: understanding (*Ach so!, Genau!, Stimmt!*), doubt (*Na ja, Echt?*), pain and surprise (*Aua!, Huch!, Oh!*), small mistakes (*Ups!, Hoppla!, Mist!*), disappointment and relief (*Schade!, Gott sei Dank!*), the greeting *Na?*, and set phrases (*Gesundheit!, Mahlzeit!*).
- **English-speaker trap:** carrying over *ouch / oops / wow* unchanged; *Mist* and *Krass* sound harsh to English ears but are mild; *Na?* alone is a full greeting, not a question to answer with "na".
- **Examples:** *Ach so! Dann gehen wir morgen.* · *Mist! Der Bus ist weg.* · *Na? Wie war dein Tag?*
- **Deck:** Top interjections (every card already has a dialogue).

### 2. `adverbs`: schon, noch, erst, mal: small words, big meaning (after `bracket`)
- **Teaches:** *schon / noch / noch nicht / nicht mehr / erst* (already, still, not yet, no longer, not until); frequency words (*immer, oft, manchmal, selten, nie*); the middle-field order time → manner → place (*Ich fahre morgen mit dem Bus nach Köln*); the request softener *mal* (*Komm mal her!*).
- **English-speaker trap:** *erst* read as "first" (*erst um neun* = not until nine); putting the time at the end as in English (*Ich fahre nach Köln morgen*); *noch ein Kaffee* (another coffee), not "still a coffee".
- **Examples:** *Ich bin schon fertig.* · *Der Zug ist noch nicht da.* · *Der Film beginnt erst um neun.*
- **Deck:** Top adverbs.

### 3. `als-ob-obwohl`: als, wenn, wann; ob; obwohl (after `connectors`)
- **Teaches:** the three "when"s: *als* (one time in the past), *wenn* (every time, or if), *wann* (questions); *ob* for yes/no questions inside a sentence (*Ich weiß nicht, ob…*); *obwohl*, *bevor*, *nachdem* with the verb at the end; *sondern* after a negative.
- **English-speaker trap:** *wenn* for a single past event (*Wenn ich klein war* is wrong: *Als ich klein war*); *wenn* for "whether" (use *ob*); *aber* after *nicht* where German needs *sondern*.
- **Examples:** *Als ich klein war, wohnten wir in Bremen.* · *Weißt du, ob der Laden offen ist?* · *Ich trinke nicht Tee, sondern Kaffee.*
- **Deck:** Top conjunctions (obwohl and nachdem need `LEX` entries first; see CORE-SOURCES.md §4).

### 4. `man-sich-etwas`: man, sich, etwas, niemand (after `pronouns`)
- **Teaches:** *man* + er-form for "people / you in general" (*Hier darf man nicht rauchen*); reflexive *sich* and *mich / dich / uns / euch* with *sich freuen, sich setzen, sich erinnern*; *etwas / nichts, jemand / niemand, alles*; question words with case: *wen?* and *wem?*
- **English-speaker trap:** using *du* for the general "you" (*man*); dropping the reflexive (*Ich freue* for *Ich freue mich*); double negatives copied from casual English (*Ich habe nicht nichts*).
- **Examples:** *Hier darf man nicht rauchen.* · *Ich freue mich auf das Wochenende.* · *Ist hier jemand? Nein, niemand.*
- **Deck:** Top pronouns.
