Writer EN-ES-T2, 2026-11-22. Read from `lessons-1.js`, `lessons-2.js` and `lessons-3.js`: every lesson's section headings, the opening of every body, and word searches across all three files. No lesson was written or changed.

**Key**
- **taught**: a lesson presents it as grammar, with forms and practice.
- **touched**: it appears inside another lesson's topic, or only some of the class is taught.
- **missing**: no lesson presents it.

## Summary

- **Missing:** interjections; ordinal numbers.
- **Touched only:** adverbs as a class (no *-mente*, no double negative, no *ya / todavía*); subordinating conjunctions (*que, si, cuando, aunque*); indefinite, negative and possessive pronouns (*algo, nadie, mío*); *le / les* outside *gustar*.
- **Taught:** nouns, verbs (A1 scope), adjectives, articles and determiners, prepositions, personal pronouns, cardinal numbers.

## Proposed lessons

Each outline links to the new core decks. The places suggested are after the lessons each one builds on.

### 1. `interjections`: ¡Ay!, ¡Oye!, ¡Órale!: reacting in Spanish (after `connectors`)
- **Teaches:** the 12–15 exclamations people really use, grouped by feeling: pain or surprise (*¡Ay!, ¡Uf!*), attention (*¡Oye!*, and *¡Oiga!* to usted), warnings (*¡Cuidado!, ¡Ojo!*, Mexican *¡Aguas!*), joy and hope (*¡Bravo!, ¡Ojalá!*), Mexican colour (*¡Órale!, ¡Híjole!, ¡Ándale!*), plus the pattern *¡Qué + adjective!* with agreement (*¡Qué bonita!*).
- **English-speaker trap:** English sounds carried over (*ouch, wow, oops*); *¡Oye!* to a stranger (use *¡Oiga!* or *Disculpe*); the ¡ at the start; *¡Qué bonito!* not agreeing with a feminine noun.
- **Examples:** *¡Ay! Me duele el pie.* · *¡Oiga, señor! Se le cayó esto.* · *¡Híjole! El restaurante está cerrado.*
- **Deck:** Top interjections (every card already has a dialogue).

### 2. `adverbs`: siempre, nunca, ya, todavía, and -mente (after `muy-mucho`)
- **Teaches:** frequency words and where they go (*siempre, casi siempre, a veces, nunca*); *ya / todavía / todavía no*; adverbs from adjectives with *-mente* on the feminine form (*rápida → rápidamente*); the double negative (*No… nunca / nada / nadie*).
- **English-speaker trap:** *rápidomente*, built on the masculine form; dropping *no* in *No veo a nadie* ("I see nobody"); calquing "I already have…" word for word.
- **Examples:** *Nunca como carne. / No como carne nunca.* · *¿Ya comiste? Todavía no.* · *Habla lentamente, por favor.*
- **Deck:** Top adverbs (with *nada, nadie* from Top pronouns).

### 3. `que-si-cuando`: Joining ideas: que, si, cuando, aunque (after `connectors`)
- **Teaches:** *que* after verbs of thinking and saying (*Creo que…, Dice que…*); *si* + present for real conditions (*Si llueve, no voy*); *cuando* with no accent mark vs *¿cuándo?*; *aunque*, *mientras*, *como* (since); *así que* and *por eso* for results. Present tense only; the subjunctive after *cuando* (future) and *para que* is left for later.
- **English-speaker trap:** dropping *que* the way English drops "that" (*Creo es tarde*); writing *sí* for "if" and *¿cuándo?* inside a statement; *porque* at the start of a sentence for "since" (use *como*).
- **Examples:** *Creo que es muy tarde.* · *Si tienes tiempo, te llamo.* · *Cuando llego a casa, ceno.*
- **Deck:** Top conjunctions.

### 4. `algo-nadie`: algo, alguien, nada, nadie; mío, tuyo (after `lo-la`)
- **Teaches:** the indefinite and negative pairs (*algo / nada, alguien / nadie, alguno / ninguno*, with *algún / ningún* before a masculine noun); the possessive pronouns that agree with the thing (*mío, mía, míos, mías*; *el mío*); *le / les* with ordinary verbs (*Le doy el libro a Ana*).
- **English-speaker trap:** *No tengo algo* for "I don't have anything" (Spanish says *nada*); *mío* agreeing with the owner instead of the thing; leaving out the extra *le* in *Le doy el libro a Ana*.
- **Examples:** *¿Quieres algo? No, nada, gracias.* · *¿Hay alguien en casa? No, no hay nadie.* · *Ese carro no es mío, es suyo.*
- **Deck:** Top pronouns.

### Smaller gaps (a section in an existing lesson, not a new lesson)
- **Ordinals** (*primero / primer, segundo, tercero*): one section in `numbers`, or in `directions` (floors and streets). It would link to Top words, which has no ordinal cards yet.
- **Comparisons** (*más… que, mejor, peor*): a section in `adjectives`, or a short lesson after `muy-mucho`. It would link to Top adjectives.
