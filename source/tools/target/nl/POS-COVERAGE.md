# Parts of speech in the en-nl lessons

Writer EN-NL-T, 2026-11-23. Based on `lessons-1.js`, `lessons-2.js` and `lessons-3.js`: every lesson's section headings, the plain text of the bodies of `pronouns`, `numbers`, `family`, `travel`, `omdat`, `time`, `compare`, `er`, `geen-niet`, `deze-die`, `adjectives`, `de-het`, `questions`, `shopping` and `inversion`, and word searches across all 28 lessons. No lesson was written or changed.

**Key**
- **taught**: a lesson presents it as grammar, with forms and practice.
- **touched**: it shows up inside another lesson's topic, or only part of the class is taught.
- **missing**: no lesson presents it.

## Summary

- **Missing:** interjections.
- **Touched only:** adverbs as a class, including the *er* / *daar* family and the pronominal adverbs; prepositions; subordinating conjunctions beyond *omdat* / *dat*; indefinite, reflexive and relative pronouns; quantifiers; ordinals.
- **Taught:** nouns (with *de* / *het*, plurals, diminutives), A1 verbs, adjectives with *-e* and comparison, personal, possessive and demonstrative pronouns, articles, and cardinal numbers.
- **Course full:** the profile caps the course at 28 lessons, and it already has 28. Each proposal below needs either a lesson merged or the cap raised. That is Claude's and Kii's call.

## Proposed lessons

### 1. `prepositions`: in, op, aan, bij: where things are (after `travel`)
- **Teaches:** *in / op / aan / bij / naast / tussen / onder / achter / voor*, with fixed pairs (*op school*, *op het werk*, *aan tafel*, *bij mij thuis*, *in de stad*); *van* for possession; *met* and *zonder*; *voor* and *na* in time.
- **English-speaker trap:** using *in* for every "at" (*in school* → *op school*); *van* for "of" in quantities (*een kop van koffie* → *een kop koffie*); putting *de* / *het* where Dutch drops it (*naar de huis* → *naar huis*).
- **Examples:** *Ik zit op school.* · *We eten aan tafel.* · *Ik eet morgen bij mijn moeder.*
- **Deck:** Top prepositions.

### 2. `als-toen`: als, toen, dus: more joining words (after `omdat`)
- **Teaches:** *als* (if / whenever) and *toen* (when, one time in the past) send the verb to the end, like *omdat*; *dus* (so) keeps a main clause; a fronted clause makes the main verb come first (*Als het regent, neem ik de bus*). Recognition only: *terwijl*, *voordat*, *nadat*, *ook al*.
- **English-speaker trap:** *wanneer* for every "when" (*toen* for one past event); English order after *als* (*als het is koud* → *als het koud is*); no inversion after a fronted clause (*Als het regent, ik neem…*).
- **Examples:** *Als het regent, neem ik de bus.* · *Toen ik jong was, woonde ik in Groningen.* · *Ik ben moe, dus ik ga naar bed.*
- **Deck:** Top conjunctions.

### 3. `adverbs`: altijd, vaak, ook, al, nog, wel: where the little words go (after `geen-niet`)
- **Teaches:** frequency words after the finite verb (*Ik fiets vaak*), or in front with inversion (*Soms werk ik thuis*); *ook*, *al*, *nog* and *nog niet*; *wel* as the positive partner of *niet* / *geen*; *er* vs *daar*, and *er* as "there" (*Ik woon er al een jaar*).
- **English-speaker trap:** English order (*Ik altijd fiets* → *Ik fiets altijd*); *nog* vs *al* ("still" vs "already"); dropping *wel* in a contrast.
- **Examples:** *Ik neem altijd de fiets.* · *Ik ben nog niet klaar.* · *Ik heb geen auto, maar wel een fiets.*
- **Deck:** Top adverbs.

### 4. `iets-niemand`: iets, niets, iemand, niemand, and me / zich (after `pronouns`)
- **Teaches:** the indefinite pairs (*iets / niets / niks*, *iemand / niemand*); reflexive verbs with *me, je, zich, ons* (*Ik voel me goed*, *Hij voelt zich ziek*); stressed and unstressed object forms (*mij / me*, *jou / je*); the relative *die / dat* (*de man die daar woont*), with the verb at the end.
- **English-speaker trap:** a double negative carried over from English slang (*Ik zie niet niets*), or *niet iets* for *niets*; leaving out the reflexive (*Ik voel goed*); *dat* for every "who" (*de man dat* → *de man die*).
- **Examples:** *Is er iemand thuis?* · *Hij voelt zich niet goed.* · *Ik zie niets.*
- **Deck:** Top pronouns.

### 5. `interjections`: Au!, Oeps!, Jammer!: reacting in Dutch (anywhere after `greetings`; could be a section of `traps`)
- **Teaches:** 12–15 reactions grouped by feeling: pain or a small accident (*Au!*, *Oeps!*, *Oei!*), sympathy (*Ach!*, *Jammer!*, *Wat jammer!*), surprise (*Wauw!*, *Jeetje!*, *Tjonge!*), set phrases (*Gezondheid!*, *Succes!*, *Proost!*), and starting a reply (*Nou…*, *Tja*).
- **English-speaker trap:** English *ouch* and *oops* carried over (Dutch says *Au!* and *Oeps!*); *Sorry!* for every bump (*Pardon* to a stranger); *Gezondheid* at a sneeze, not *bless you*.
- **Examples:** *Au! Dat is heet!* · *Ik kan zaterdag niet komen. — Jammer!* · *Hatsjoe! — Gezondheid!*
- **Deck:** Top interjections (every card already has a dialogue).

### Smaller gaps (a section in an existing lesson, not a new lesson)
- **Ordinals** (*eerste, tweede, derde, laatste, volgende*): one section in `numbers` or `time` (dates: *de derde mei*). Links to Top words.
- **Quantifiers** (*alle, elke / elk, veel, weinig, sommige, zo'n / zulke*): one section in `deze-die` or `adjectives`, because *elk* / *elke* follows the same de / het split. Links to Top words.
- **zullen, laten, worden:** one section in `modals` (*Zullen we…?*, *Laat maar*). Links to Top verbs.
