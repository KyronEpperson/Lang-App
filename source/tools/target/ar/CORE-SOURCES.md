# Core words, Flirting and Compliments (en-ar): sources, order and skips

Writer CORE-AR, 2026-11-23. Files: `decks-t.js` + `extra-t.js` (the nine Core words decks), `decks-f.js` + `extra-f.js` (Flirting), `decks-c.js` + `extra-c.js` (Compliments, from Deck 1 of `docs/plans/compliments-ar.md`). All Arabic is MSA, fully vowelled in the course's one canonical way, pausal (bare last letter), as `docs/profiles/en-ar.md` §2 requires. Every pron was generated by `tools/target/ar/respell.js` in a build script and pasted unchanged. Every Arabic string in notes, examples, dialogues and patterns (blanks removed) was also run through `respell.js` (the validator does not check those), and all of them pass.

**Read this first: these are form ranks, not lemma ranks.** A rank here is the line number in the file; line 1 is the Arabic comma «،», so the most frequent word («لا») is rank 2. The list has unvowelled surface forms with clitics attached, so:
- **Lookup rule:** strip the vowel marks from the card, fold أ إ آ to ا, then take the better rank of the bare form and the form with ال (so شَيْء = rank of «شيء», طَعَام = better of «طعام» / «الطعام»). For verbs, the better rank of three forms: past "he" (the card), present "I" and present "he" (from the note), because subtitles use the present and the first person far more than the dictionary form.
- **Homographs** share a rank (أَنْتَ / أَنْتِ = «انت»; لَكَ / لَكِ = «لك»), and folding the hamza seats merges more: أَن / أَنَّ / إِن / إِنَّ all = «ان»; أُمّ (mother) = أَم (or) = «ام» 237; أَخ (brother) = آخ (ouch) = «اخ» 3944; أَيّ = أَيْ = «اي»; جَوّ = «جو», also the name Joe. All of these except أَنْتَ / أَنْتِ, لَكَ / لَكِ, أَن / أَنَّ and أَيّ are placed by judgement (at about the shared rank) rather than claimed as source ranks. Where a homograph clearly inflated a rank, the card was given a judgement key instead: غَيَّرَ (its form «غير» is mostly *ghayr* "other than") and تَعَلَّمَ («تعلم» is mostly *taʿlam* "you know") use their present forms' ranks (يغير 4089, أتعلم 506); عَلَيَّ («علي» is also the name Ali) is placed by judgement.
- Subtitles over-rank some film words and under-rank some everyday ones (عَرَبِيّ ranks 24198 in subtitles; it is kept, at the end of its deck). The choice of words is the writer's; the source decides only the order.

**What is ordered by judgement** (marked `*` in §2; everything else follows the source rank exactly, ties in writing order):
- Multi-word cards, which a word list cannot rank: مَرَّة أُخْرَى; لَا شَيْء, لَا أَحَد; مَعَ أَنَّ, بِمَا أَنَّ, قَبْل أَن, بَعْد أَن; يَا إِلٰهِي, يَا رَبّ, يَا سَلَام, سُبْحَانَ اللّٰه, يَا حَرَام; the numbers 12-19, the three weekdays, بَعْد غَد, أَوَّل أَمْس (these last go at the end of Top words in counting order).
- One-letter clitics, which are almost never separate tokens: بِ, لِ (placed near the top), كَ, فَ.
- The eight possessive-ending cards (بَيْتِي … بَيْتُهُم): their form ranks measure "house", not the ending, so they sit as a block right after the subject pronouns.
- إِن and إِنَّ and أَيْ (homographs of commoner words), أُمّ, أَخ, جَوّ, أَم, آخ (hamza-folding homographs), مُجْتَهِد (not in the top 50,000), and the three homograph fixes above.

Not opened: Buckwalter & Parkinson, *A Frequency Dictionary of Arabic* (Routledge; seen only as a publisher's listing in a web search), a lemma list in book form. Wiktionary's Arabic frequency pages and raw.githubusercontent.com did not answer from this session.

**Suggestion:** a lemmatised MSA list (such as Buckwalter & Parkinson's) would mostly reorder the verbs and settle the homograph cases; the choice of words should hold.

## 3. Grammar fit (rule 2): slots kept, none renamed

- **Top prepositions** keeps its name: Arabic has true prepositions (فِي مِن إِلَى عَن مَعَ, the one-letter بِ لِ كَ) and place words used as prepositions (قَبْل بَعْد تَحْت فَوْق أَمَام). As the launch note asks, the **forms with pronoun endings** (لِي لَكَ لَكِ لَهُ لَهَا مَعِي مَعَكَ مَعَكِ عِنْدِي … عَلَيَّ مِنِّي) are cards in this deck: learners meet them as whole words.
- **Top pronouns** holds the independent pronouns, demonstratives, أَيّ and the indefinites, plus the **attached (possessive) endings**. An ending cannot be a card on its own (the generator needs a whole word, and tatweel is banned in targets), so each ending is shown on بَيْت, exactly as the `possessive` lesson's table does: بَيْتِي, بَيْتُكَ, بَيْتُكِ, بَيْتُهُ, بَيْتُهَا, بَيْتُنَا, بَيْتُكُم, بَيْتُهُم. Their meanings say "(possessive ending)", and the notes give the same ending on a verb (ـنِي, ـكَ).
- **Top adjectives** keeps its name: Arabic adjectives are a real class that agrees in gender and the article.
- **No extra deck.** Arabic has no measure words or counters. The verb particles قَد, لَقَد, سَوْفَ, لَن, لَم and إِنَّ fit none of the eight classes, so they are in **Top words**, with لَا, يَا, أَجَل, بَلَى, غَيْر, إِلَّا, the numbers, ordinals and days.
- **Verbs** are cited the way the course cites them (lesson `past`: "Learn each verb by its 'he' form"): the target is the past "he" form (كَتَبَ), and the note gives the present "I" form first, then "he" (أَكْتُب, يَكْتُب), and flags hollow verbs, verbs that drop و, and irregular ones.
- **Nouns** are given indefinite, with the gender, the plural when it is broken, and the "my" form where the course teaches it.
- **Spelling choices to note.** Place prepositions are written with a bare last letter (قَبْل, أَمَام), as the course's own phrase cards already do (أَمَام الْمَحَطَّة, بِجَانِب الْفُنْدُق); مَعَ keeps its vowel (profile §2.3). عِنْد is written bare as a card; the `have` lesson writes عِنْدَ in its body, and the note says so.

## 6. Pron tool: words left out

`respell.js` romanises what is written; it threw on nothing in the final set. Left out because it would produce a wrong pron, and the course has no word list to fix it (the brief forbids editing the tool):
- **الَّذِي, الَّتِي, الَّذِينَ** (who, which, that): the tool reads الـ as the article plus a sun letter and gives *adh-dhī*; the real form is *alladhī*. A top-50 word in the source; it needs a tool exception (a FORMULA-style entry) before it can be a card.
- **أُولٰئِكَ** (those): the tool gives *ūlāʾika*; the word is said *ulāʾika* (its و is silent).
- Words with ة after ا (حَيَاة life, فَتَاة girl, صَلَاة prayer, نِهَايَة / بِدَايَة end, beginning) are refused by the tool by design.

**Generator warnings** (not errors; the output is right): هٰؤُلَاءِ (*hāʾulāʾi*, the tool's own canonical spelling), مُنْذُ (*mundhu*), and the one-letter cards بِ, لِ: the tool flags any word ending in kasra or damma. `repron.js` reports them as "generator unsure".

## 7. Register, culture and gender

- **"You" forms.** Every card that addresses someone says "(to a man)" or "(to a woman)" and gives the other form in the note. In Flirting and Compliments the key phrases have **both forms as separate cards** (اِبْتِسَامَتُكِ / اِبْتِسَامَتُكَ جَمِيلَة, أَنْتِ تُعْجِبِينَنِي / أَنْتَ تُعْجِبُنِي, مَا شَاءَ اللّٰه عَلَيْكِ / عَلَيْكَ, سَلِمَت يَدَاكِ / يَدَاكَ, أَحْسَنْتِ / أَحْسَنْتَ); the rest give the other form in the note. Replies said about oneself are written for one speaker ("(a woman speaking)") with the other form in the note.
- **Flirting is mostly indirect**, as in real life: in much of the Arab world open flirting with a stranger is frowned on, so the phrases suit people who already know each other. Praise is wrapped in the blessing phrases people really use (مَا شَاءَ اللّٰه عَلَيْكِ, تَبَارَكَ اللّٰه, اللّٰه يَحْفَظُكِ) and answered with بَارَكَ اللّٰه فِيكَ. The card نِيَّتِي جَادَّة ("my intentions are serious") is how interest is made respectable in conservative families. Body comments stop at eyes, smile, hair and voice. Six ways to say no: رُبَّمَا فِي وَقْت آخَر (soft), أُفَضِّل أَن نَبْقَى أَصْدِقَاء, شُكْرًا، لٰكِنِّي مُرْتَبِطَة, عُذْرًا، لَسْتُ مُهْتَمَّة, أَنَا مُتَزَوِّجَة, and a firm مِن فَضْلِكَ، تَوَقَّف. One dialogue ends in a yes (أَنْتِ تُعْجِبِينَنِي → "I like you too"), one in a gracious no (أُفَضِّل أَن نَبْقَى أَصْدِقَاء).
- **MSA honesty.** People flirt and joke in dialect, so these cards sound careful, like a film subtitle. Dialect forms appear in notes only, in romanisation, at most once per card (هَيَّا: "people also say yalla").
- **Example vocabulary.** Examples and dialogues use course words, the new core cards and their forms. A script check against every word in the course found only these outside both: the place names دُبَيّ, الْيَمَن, لَنْدَن, الْيَابَان, and تَعَالَ (come!), ذَاهِب (going, from ذَهَبَ).
- **Deck group.** Compliments and Flirting are in **Watch out**, the group that holds the course's social-courtesy decks (Spoken Arabic, Traps), following the courses that have no Conversation group (bn, da, no, pl, ptpt, sv). Changing it to "First steps" is a one-word edit in `profile.json`.
