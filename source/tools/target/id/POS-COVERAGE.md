# Parts of speech in the en-id lessons

The core-words writer, 2026-11-23. Based on the full lesson bodies in `lessons-1.js`, `lessons-2.js` and `lessons-3.js` (section headings, body text and examples), plus word searches across every body and practice set. No lesson was written or changed.

**Key**
- **taught**: a lesson presents it as grammar, with forms and practice.
- **touched**: it appears inside another lesson's topic, or only part of the class is taught.
- **missing**: no lesson presents it.

## Summary

- **Missing:** conjunctions, interjections, ordinal numbers, counting words (classifiers), indefinite pronouns.
- **Touched only:** adverbs as a class (frequency, *juga / saja / hanya*, *mungkin / pasti*); prepositions beyond *di / ke / dari* (*dengan, untuk, tentang, sejak, selama, tanpa*).
- **Taught:** nouns, verbs (A1 scope, including *ber-, meN-, di-*), adjectives, personal / possessive / demonstrative / question pronouns, *ini / itu* and *-nya* as determiners, cardinal numbers.

## Proposed lessons

Each outline links to a new core deck. A place is suggested after the lessons each one builds on.

### 1. `dan-tapi`: Joining ideas: dan, atau, tapi, karena, kalau (after `negation`)
- **Teaches:** *dan, atau, tapi / tetapi, karena, jadi, kalau / jika, ketika / waktu, sebelum, setelah / sesudah, supaya*, and *walaupun* for recognition; the clause order stays as in English; *kalau* is "if" and also "when (in the future)"; the casual *soalnya* and *makanya* for recognition.
- **English-speaker trap:** *kapan* used for a "when" that does not ask (*Kapan saya kecil…* → *Waktu saya kecil…*); *jika* or *tetapi* in casual talk, where *kalau* and *tapi* are natural; *bahwa* forced in after every "think" or "say", where speech leaves it out.
- **Examples:** *Saya mau teh, tapi tidak pakai gula.* · *Kalau hujan, kami di rumah saja.* · *Cuci tangan sebelum makan.*
- **Deck:** Top conjunctions.

### 2. `wah-aduh`: Reacting: wah, aduh, astaga, ya ampun (after `casual`, or after `greetings` as a light lesson)
- **Teaches:** 12–15 exclamations grouped by feeling: delight (*Wah!, Asyik!, Hore!*), pain or worry (*Aduh!, Waduh!, Duh!*), shock (*Astaga!, Ya ampun!*), disbelief (*Masa?, Hah?*), relief (*Syukurlah*), sympathy (*Kasihan!*), and *Alhamdulillah, Insya Allah, Amin*: what they mean and that non-Muslims hear them daily.
- **English-speaker trap:** English *wow, ouch, oops* carried over; *Hei!* to a stranger (use *Permisi, Pak / Bu*); hearing *Insya Allah* as a firm yes.
- **Examples:** *Wah, bagus sekali!* · *Aduh, kaki saya sakit.* · *Besok bisa datang? Insya Allah.*
- **Deck:** Top interjections (every card already has a dialogue).

### 3. `counting`: Counting words and order: seorang, sebuah, pertama (after `numbers`)
- **Teaches:** the counting word between number and noun (*dua orang guru, tiga ekor kucing, sebuah rumah*); *se-* for "a, one" (*seorang teman*); measure words for food and drink (*segelas air, sepiring nasi, dua porsi sate*); that speech often drops the counting word (*dua kucing*); ordinals *pertama, kedua, ketiga, terakhir* after the noun; *kedua* before a noun = both.
- **English-speaker trap:** *orang dua* or *dua guru orang* (word order); using *sebuah* for people; *pertama hari* for *hari pertama* (the noun comes first).
- **Examples:** *Saya punya seorang adik.* · *Minta dua gelas es teh.* · *Ini hari pertama saya di Bali.*
- **Deck:** Top classifiers (and the ordinals in Top words).

### 4. `dengan-untuk`: dengan, untuk, tentang and friends (after `prepositions`)
- **Teaches:** *dengan* (with; casual *sama*), *untuk* (for), *tentang* (about), *sejak* (since), *selama* (for a time), *sampai* (until), *tanpa* (without), *seperti* (like), *pada / kepada* for recognition; *menurut saya* (in my opinion).
- **English-speaker trap:** *untuk* before a verb after *mau / bisa* (*Saya mau untuk pergi*, already a course trap); *dari* for "since" (*dari tahun lalu* is casual; careful *sejak*); *selama* vs *sejak*.
- **Examples:** *Saya datang dengan teman saya.* · *Ini hadiah untuk Ibu.* · *Saya di Bali selama dua minggu.*
- **Deck:** Top prepositions.

### 5. `juga-saja`: juga, saja, hanya, sering, mungkin (after `time-words`)
- **Teaches:** where the small adverbs go: *juga* after the word it adds to (*Saya juga*), *saja* after (*air putih saja*), *hanya / cuma* before; frequency words (*selalu, sering, kadang-kadang, jarang*) before the verb; *mungkin, pasti, tentu, memang*; *sini / situ / sana* with *di / ke*.
- **English-speaker trap:** *juga* put first as English "also" (*Juga saya suka*); *saja* before the noun; *sana* for "there, where you are" (that is *situ*).
- **Examples:** *Saya juga suka kopi.* · *Kami sering makan di warung itu.* · *Mungkin besok hujan.*
- **Deck:** Top adverbs.

### Smaller gap (a section in an existing lesson, not a new lesson)
- **Indefinite pronouns** (*sesuatu, seseorang, apa-apa, siapa-siapa, semua orang*): one section in `negation` (*tidak … apa-apa* = nothing, *tidak ada siapa-siapa* = nobody) or in `ada`. It links to Top pronouns, where every card has an example.
