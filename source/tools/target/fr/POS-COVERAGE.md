# Parts of speech in the en-fr lessons

Read from `lessons-1.js`, `lessons-2.js` and `lessons-3.js`: every lesson's title and section headings, the body of `negation`, and word searches across all three files (2026-11-23). No lesson was written or changed.

**Key:** **taught** = a lesson presents it as grammar, with forms and practice; **touched** = it appears inside another lesson's topic, or only part of the class is taught; **missing** = no lesson presents it.

## Summary

- **Missing:** interjections.
- **Touched only:** adverbs as a class (no -ment, no placement in the passé composé), subordinating conjunctions (que, si, comme), indefinite, possessive and demonstrative pronouns, the -ir and -re verb groups, prepositions beyond place and time, ordinals.
- **Taught:** nouns, verbs (A1–A2 tenses), adjectives, articles and determiners (except tout / chaque / quelques), personal and object pronouns, prepositions of place and time, cardinal numbers.

## Proposed lessons

Outlines only; Claude and Kii decide. Each links to the new core decks.

### 1. `interjections`: Oh là là, Ouf, Mince: reacting in French (after `spoken`)
- **Teaches:** 15 exclamations by feeling: pain and relief (*Ouille! Ouf!*), surprise (*Oh là là! Ah bon? Ça alors!*), annoyance (*Mince! Bon sang!*), acceptance (*Tant pis! Tant mieux! Dommage!*), wishes (*Bon courage!* vs *Bonne chance!*, *À tes souhaits!*), hesitation (*Euh…, Bah*).
- **English-speaker trap:** carrying over "ouch, um, oops"; *Hein?* or *Hé!* to a stranger (use *Pardon?*, *Excusez-moi*); *Bonne chance* for a hard task where French says *Bon courage*.
- **Examples:** *Ouf! On est à l'heure.* · *Tant pis, je prends un pain au chocolat.* · *J'ai un examen demain. Bon courage!*
- **Deck:** Top interjections (every card already has a dialogue).

### 2. `adverbs`: déjà, toujours, souvent, and -ment (after `passe-compose`)
- **Teaches:** frequency and time words and where they go (after the verb in the present: *Je mange souvent ici*; between helper and participle: *J'ai déjà mangé*); *déjà / pas encore*; *toujours* = always and still; adverbs from the feminine + *-ment* (*lente → lentement*, *vrai → vraiment*); *bien / mal* vs *bon / mauvais*.
- **English-speaker trap:** putting the adverb before the verb as in English (*Je souvent mange*); *lentment* built on the masculine; *bon* where French needs *bien* (*Tu parles bon français*).
- **Examples:** *Je vais souvent au cinéma.* · *Tu as déjà mangé? Pas encore.* · *Parlez lentement, s'il vous plaît.*
- **Deck:** Top adverbs.

### 3. `que-si-comme`: Joining ideas: que, si, comme, pendant que (after `connectors`)
- **Teaches:** *que* after *penser, croire, dire, savoir* (*Je pense que tu as raison*); *si* + present for real conditions and *s'il* elision; *comme* = since at the start of a sentence; *pendant que, même si, dès que* (with the future after *dès que* for future time). The subjunctive after *bien que / pour que* is left for later.
- **English-speaker trap:** dropping *que* the way English drops "that" (*Je pense tu as raison*); *si il* (must be *s'il*); *parce que* at the start of a sentence where French prefers *comme*.
- **Examples:** *Je crois que le musée est fermé.* · *S'il pleut, on reste à la maison.* · *Comme il est tard, je rentre.*
- **Deck:** Top conjunctions.

### 4. `quelque-chose`: quelque chose, quelqu'un, le mien, celui-ci (after `object-pronouns`)
- **Teaches:** the pairs *quelque chose / rien*, *quelqu'un / personne* (with *ne*), *quelque chose de bon*; possessive pronouns that agree with the thing (*le mien, la mienne, les miens*); *celui-ci / celle-là*; *lequel?*
- **English-speaker trap:** *Je ne vois pas quelqu'un* for "I don't see anyone" (French: *personne*); *le mien* agreeing with the owner (*c'est la mienne* for a suitcase whoever speaks); *quelque chose bon* without *de*.
- **Examples:** *Tu veux boire quelque chose? Non, rien, merci.* · *Il y a quelqu'un? Non, il n'y a personne.* · *Ce n'est pas ton portable, c'est le mien.*
- **Deck:** Top pronouns.

### 5. `present-ir-re`: finir, vendre, and the big irregulars (after `present`)
- **Teaches:** regular -ir (*finis, finit, finissons, finissent*) and -re (*vends, vend, vendons*; the d is silent in the singular); the present of *dire, voir, mettre, partir / sortir, lire, écrire*.
- **English-speaker trap:** -er endings on every verb (*je finie, ils vendent* said with the d silent); forgetting the *-iss-* in the plural of -ir verbs.
- **Examples:** *Je finis à dix-huit heures.* · *Ils vendent des fruits au marché.* · *Je ne sais pas, je ne vois rien.*
- **Deck:** Top verbs.
