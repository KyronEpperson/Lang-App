## 1. Frequency source

**Read this first: the order is only partly from a source.** Where a word has a rank below, its deck follows that rank. Every other word is placed by judgement after the ranked ones. The judgement leans toward everyday spoken Indian Punjabi, as the profile asks.

**Caveats on this source. These are why the ranks are approximate.**
- **Download refused.** A direct download was refused by this session's network proxy (HTTP 403). The 500 positions were read through the web-fetch tool in two parts (1–250, 251–500). The page prints no rank numbers, so a position is the row's place in the table. Small transcription slips by the fetch tool are possible.
- **No counts.** The list gives order only.
- **Word forms, not lemmas.** A card's rank below is the best rank of any of its forms, named after "via": ਕਰਨਾ 29 via ਕਰ, ਜਾਣਾ 32 via ਗਿਆ, ਕਹਿਣਾ 60 via ਕਿਹਾ, ਸਾਰਾ 252 via ਸਾਰੀ. A noun's rank may likewise come from its plural or oblique form: ਲੋਕ 93 via ਲੋਕਾਂ. The 500 words were saved to the writer's scratch folder, and the build script checks every "N via X" against that list. Forms a reader might link to a card but that are other words were not counted: ਵੱਧ (more) for ਵਧਣਾ, ਜਾਣ for ਜਾਣਨਾ (it is the oblique of ਜਾਣਾ), ਖੇਡ (noun) for ਖੇਡਣਾ.
- **Spelling variants have their own ranks.** ਵਿਚ is 7 and ਵਿੱਚ is 26. ਨਹੀਂ appears twice, at 19 and at 397. ਇਥੇ and ਉਥੇ are ranked, but this course spells them ਇੱਥੇ and ਉੱਥੇ. Where the course's spelling differs from the list's, the card uses the course spelling, takes the variant's rank, and the "via" note says so.
- **Corpus bias.** The corpus is written text with a heavy Sikh-religious and news slant: ਸਿੰਘ 25, ਗੁਰੂ 77, ਸਾਹਿਬ 102, ਸਰਕਾਰ 129, ਪਾਰਟੀ 210, ਮੰਤਰੀ 202. Words that are formal or institutional at A1 were left out (§3).
- **Ambiguous forms.** ਤੇ (6) means both "and" (in speech) and "on" (short for ਉੱਤੇ); it went to Top conjunctions. ਸਿੱਖ (115) is the noun "Sikh" and the stem of ਸਿੱਖਣਾ; the rank went to the noun only. ਮੁੜ (276) is both the stem of ਮੁੜਨਾ and the adverb "again"; the rank went to the verb only.

## 2. Fitting the decks to Punjabi

- **`Top postpositions` replaces `Top prepositions`.** Punjabi puts these words after the noun, and the noun before them takes the oblique (ਘਰ ਵਿੱਚ, ਮੁੰਡੇ ਨੂੰ). The deck holds the simple ones (ਨੂੰ, ਤੋਂ, ਵਿੱਚ, ਨਾਲ) and the compound ones that take ਦੇ / ਤੋਂ (ਦੇ ਸਾਹਮਣੇ, ਤੋਂ ਬਾਅਦ), with the linking word in the note.
- **ਦਾ, ਦੇ and ਦੀ are three cards** (ranks 5, 1 and 3). Each one is among the five most frequent words, and the meaning names the form of noun each goes before. ਦੀਆਂ is in the note of ਦੀ.
- **ਨੇ** (the past-tense agent marker) is in Top postpositions, because Punjabi grammar treats it as one. Its meaning is a bracketed gloss, since English has no word for it.
- **No articles.** Punjabi has none. The articles-and-determiners slot of Top words holds ਹਰ, ਕਈ, ਹੋਰ, ਸਾਰਾ, ਦੋਵੇਂ, ਬਾਕੀ, ਵਾਲਾ and the ordinals. ਇੱਕ ("one", which also works as "a") is already a card.
- **Particles go in Top words:** ਵੀ, ਹੀ, ਨਾ and ਜਿਹਾ. They are not adverbs in Punjabi grammar, and they fit none of the eight classes. No new class was invented for them.
- **Pronouns include the forms used before a postposition** (ਇਸ, ਉਸ, ਕਿਸੇ, ਜਿਸ, ਕਿਸ, ਉਹਨਾਂ, ਇਹਨਾਂ) and the fused ਨੂੰ forms (ਮੈਨੂੰ, ਤੈਨੂੰ, ਸਾਨੂੰ, ਤੁਹਾਨੂੰ, ਉਹਨੂੰ). A learner meets these as whole words. Possessives (ਮੇਰਾ, ਸਾਡਾ, ਤੇਰਾ, ਉਸ ਦਾ, ਆਪਣਾ) agree with the thing owned, and the note gives their forms.
- **Nouns** give the gender in the note, the way the course already does ("Masculine; plural …"). For people, the note also gives the feminine noun where there is one (ਅਧਿਆਪਕ / ਅਧਿਆਪਕਾ).
- **Verbs** are in the infinitive. Each note gives the habitual ਮੈਂ form for a man and for a woman (ਮੈਂ ਕਰਦਾ / ਕਰਦੀ ਹਾਂ) and flags irregular pasts: ਕੀਤਾ, ਗਿਆ, ਦਿੱਤਾ, ਲਿਆ, ਪੀਤਾ, ਖਾਧਾ, ਸੁੱਤਾ, ਧੋਤਾ, ਪਿਆ, ਕਿਹਾ. Three verbs are used of things, not people, so their notes give an example with a thing instead: ਖੁੱਲ੍ਹਣਾ, ਟੁੱਟਣਾ, ਵਧਣਾ. ਸਕਣਾ is shown after a stem: ਮੈਂ ਜਾ ਸਕਦਾ / ਸਕਦੀ ਹਾਂ.
- **Adjectives** say in the note whether they change (ਮਾੜਾ / ਮਾੜੇ / ਮਾੜੀ) or not ("Does not change").
- **Register:** ਤੁਸੀਂ by default in every example, as the profile says. ਤੂੰ appears only where the card or the English says "to a close friend" (ਤੇਰਾ, ਤੈਨੂੰ, the ਓਏ! and ਚੱਕ ਦੇ ਫੱਟੇ! dialogues) and in one Flirting card for couples.
- **Speaker gender:** every first-person example whose verb shows gender has "(a man speaking)" or "(a woman speaking)" in its English, and both genders are used.

## 6. Ten cards a native speaker should check first

1. **ਵਧੀਆ** *vádhīā* (Top adjectives; also in five Compliments cards): the high tone on the first a is the generator's class S, with no source.
2. **ਤੇ** as "and" (Top conjunctions): it is ranked 6, but in written text ਤੇ is mostly "on". Is "and (everyday)" the right main meaning for a learner?
3. **ਆਪਾਂ** (Top pronouns): "we, you and I". Is it as common in Indian Punjab as in the West?
4. **ਓਏ ਹੋਏ!** (Top interjections): is the gloss "Ooh! / Aww! (delight)" right? Is it safe outside close friends?
5. **ਚੱਕ ਦੇ ਫੱਟੇ!** (Top interjections): is the register right for cheering a friend on?
6. **ਨੈਣ** (Flirting): is "eyes (poetic)" natural to say, or only in songs?
