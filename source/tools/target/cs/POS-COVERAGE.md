## Summary

- **Missing:** conjunctions, interjections, ordinal numbers.
- **Touched only:** adverbs as a class; prepositions beyond `v / na / do / z` (and the case each one takes); object, reflexive, question and indefinite pronouns.
- **Taught:** nouns (three cases), verbs (present, past, future, A1 scope), adjectives, subject and possessive pronouns, demonstratives in place of articles, cardinal numbers.

## Proposed lessons

Each outline links to a new core deck. The suggested place is after the lessons it builds on.

### 1. `conjunctions`: a, ale, protože, že, když (after `negation`)
- **Teaches:** `a, ale, nebo` for joining; `protože` (because) and `proto` (that's why); `že` after `myslím, vím, říká`; `když` (when, if) against the question word `kdy`; `jestli` (whether). There is always a comma before `ale, protože, že, když, jestli`.
- **English-speaker trap:** leaving out `že` the way English drops "that" (`Myslím je zavřeno`); writing `kdy` for the statement "when"; no comma before `že`.
- **Examples:** *Myslím, že je zavřeno.* · *Nejdu, protože jsem nemocný.* · *Když prší, jsem doma.*
- **Deck:** Top conjunctions. Over half of the cards there already have an example.

### 2. `interjections`: aha, páni, jejda: reacting in Czech (after `greetings`)
- **Teaches:** sounds grouped by feeling. Understanding (`aha`), surprise (`páni, ty jo, jé`), small accidents and pain (`jejda, au`), disgust (`fuj`), joy (`hurá, super, bezva`), warnings (`pozor, bacha`), and reactions to news (`škoda, ach jo`). Plus the register line between friends and strangers: `čau / nazdar / hej` against `Dobrý den / Pardon / Promiňte`.
- **English-speaker trap:** English "ouch, wow, oops" carried over; `hej` or `čau` to a stranger; reading `no` as English "no" (it means "well, yeah").
- **Examples:** *Aha, děkuji.* · *Jejda, promiňte!* · *Páni, ten je velký!*
- **Deck:** Top interjections. Every card already has a dialogue.

### 3. `adverbs`: moc, často, už, ještě, doma / domů (after `time`)
- **Teaches:** degree (`moc, velmi, trochu, příliš`); frequency and where it goes (`vždycky, často, někdy, nikdy`), with `nikdy` needing `ne-` on the verb (`Nikdy nemám čas`); `už` against `ještě`; adverbs from adjectives with `-e / -ě` (`rychlý → rychle`, `pěkný → pěkně`); place against direction pairs (`doma / domů`, `nahoře / nahoru`, `dole / dolů`).
- **English-speaker trap:** `Nikdy mám čas` with no `ne-`; using the adjective for the adverb (`Mluvíte rychlý`); `Jsem domů` for "I'm home".
- **Examples:** *Mluvím trochu česky.* · *Nikdy nemám čas.* · *Jsem doma. Jdu domů.*
- **Deck:** Top adverbs.

### 4. `prepositions`: each preposition picks a case (after `locative`)
- **Teaches:** the idea that a preposition decides the form of the noun, taught as chunks, not paradigms. `o` and `po` take the place form (`o Praze, po obědě`). `pro, za, přes` take the object form (`pro vás, za hodinu`). `do, z, od, u, bez` take the genitive, as fixed chunks (`u Evy, bez cukru`). It also covers `v / ve, k / ke, z / ze, s / se`, and why the vowel appears.
- **English-speaker trap:** the subject form after any preposition (`bez cukr`); `do` for "at" (`u`); `v` for "with".
- **Examples:** *To je pro vás.* · *Kávu bez cukru, prosím.* · *Jsem u Evy.*
- **Deck:** Top prepositions.

### 5. `pronouns`: mě, ho, ji, se, si, nikdo (after `accusative`)
- **Teaches:** object pronouns (`mě, tě, ho, ji, nás, vás, je`) and the second-place rule they share with `jsem / se / si`; the reflexive `se / si` as a rule (`Jak se máte`, `Dám si`); `někdo / nikdo, něco / nic, všechno / všichni`, with the Czech double negative (`Nikdo tu není`).
- **English-speaker trap:** the pronoun at the end, the English way (`Znáte ho?` is fine, but `Vidím dnes ho` is not); dropping `ne-` after `nikdo` and `nic`.
- **Examples:** *Znáte ho?* · *Nikdo tu není.* · *Nic nechci, děkuji.*
- **Deck:** Top pronouns.

### Smaller gaps (a section in an existing lesson, not a new lesson)
- **Ordinals** (`první, druhý, třetí`): a section in `numbers` or `time`. Links to Top words.
- **Comparing** (`lepší, nejlepší, větší než`): a section in `adjectives`. Links to Top adjectives and to `než` in Top conjunctions.
- **Aspect, named once with the pairs:** the profile already defers aspect. The Top verbs notes name each partner and say that a perfective present means the future. One note in `future` could point to that deck.
