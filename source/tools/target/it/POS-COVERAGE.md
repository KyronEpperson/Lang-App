# Parts of speech in the en-it lessons

Written 2026-11-23 from `lessons-1.js`, `lessons-2.js` and `lessons-3.js`: every lesson's section headings, the full text of the lessons named below, and word searches across all three files. No lesson was written or changed.

**Key**
- **taught**: a lesson presents it as grammar, with forms and practice.
- **touched**: it appears inside another lesson's topic, or only part of the class is taught.
- **missing**: no lesson presents it.

## Summary

- **Missing:** interjections; manner adverbs in *-mente*; indefinite and negative pronouns (*qualcosa, niente, nessuno*); comparison of adjectives.
- **Touched only:** subordinating conjunctions (*che, mentre, anche se*) and the coordinators beyond *e, o, ma* (*però, quindi, oppure*); *gli / le* outside *piacere*; *ne*; prepositions beyond *a, di, da, in*; ordinals beyond *primo*.
- **Taught:** nouns, verbs (A1 scope), adjective agreement and position, articles, possessives, demonstratives, subject, direct-object and reflexive pronouns, simple and contracted prepositions, adverbs of time, place and quantity, cardinal numbers.

## Proposed lessons

Outlines only. The place suggested for each is after the lessons it builds on.

### 1. `interjections`: Boh, dai, magari: reacting in Italian (after `connectors`)
- **Teaches:** 12–15 exclamations grouped by feeling: understanding and surprise (*Ah!, Oh!, Ma dai!*), doubt (*Boh!, Mah!, Beh*), urging (*Dai!, Forza!*), wishes and relief (*Magari!, Meno male!, Peccato!*), annoyance and pain (*Uffa!, Ahi!, Accidenti!*), plus the *Che + adjective / noun!* pattern with agreement (*Che bella!, Che peccato!*).
- **English-speaker trap:** carrying over *ouch, wow, oops* (it is *ahi*, *mamma mia*, *ops*); using *Ehi!* or *Boh!* with a stranger (use *Scusi*, *Non lo so*); *Che bello!* not agreeing with a feminine noun (*Che bella giornata!*).
- **Examples:** *Ahi! Mi fa male il piede.* · *Andiamo a Capri? Magari!* · *Il treno è in orario. Meno male!*
- **Deck:** Top interjections. Every card already has a dialogue.

### 2. `che-se-mentre`: Joining ideas: che, però, quindi, mentre, anche se (after `connectors`)
- **Teaches:** *che* after verbs of knowing and saying (*So che…, Dice che…*), with present indicative only (no *penso che*, which brings the subjunctive); *però* vs *ma*; results with *quindi / perciò*; time with *mentre*; concession with *anche se*; choice with *oppure*; restating with *cioè*.
- **English-speaker trap:** dropping *che* the way English drops "that" (*So sei stanco* ✗); *che* with the "who / which" meaning confused with the question *che cosa*; putting *anche se* where English says "also if".
- **Examples:** *So che sei stanco.* · *È caro, però è buono.* · *Mentre aspetto, leggo un libro.*
- **Deck:** Top conjunctions.

### 3. `qualcosa-niente`: qualcosa, qualcuno, niente, nessuno; the double negative (after `adverbs`)
- **Teaches:** the indefinite and negative pairs (*qualcosa / niente, qualcuno / nessuno*), the obligatory *non* before the verb when *niente* or *nessuno* follows (*Non vedo niente*), *nessuno* at the start without *non* (*Nessuno lo sa*), and *ogni / qualche / alcuni* with nouns.
- **English-speaker trap:** *Vedo niente* ✗ for "I see nothing" (Italian needs *non*); *qualche* with a plural noun (*qualche giorni* ✗); *niente* doubled up with *nulla*.
- **Examples:** *Vuoi qualcosa da bere? No, niente, grazie.* · *C'è qualcuno? No, non c'è nessuno.* · *Resto qualche giorno.*
- **Deck:** Top pronouns (with *ogni, qualche, alcuni* from Top words).

### 4. `gli-le`: gli, le and ne with ordinary verbs (after `lo-la`)
- **Teaches:** the indirect object pronouns beyond *piacere* (*gli scrivo, le telefono, Le porto il menù*), the *a + person* test that tells *lo* from *gli*, and *ne* for quantities (*Quante ne vuoi? Ne voglio due*).
- **English-speaker trap:** *La telefono* ✗ for "I call her" (*telefonare a*, so *le telefono*); *lo* for "to him"; leaving out *ne* with a number (*Voglio due* ✗ → *Ne voglio due*).
- **Examples:** *Gli scrivo un messaggio.* · *Le do il libro domani.* · *Mele? Ne prendo tre.*
- **Deck:** Top pronouns (*ne*, *mi / ti / ci / vi*); *gli* and *le* are noted on their Top words cards.

### Smaller gaps (a section in an existing lesson, not a new lesson)
- **Adverbs in -mente** (feminine form + *-mente*: *vera → veramente*; *-le / -re* drop the *e*: *facilmente*): one section in `adverbs`. Links to Top adverbs (*veramente, finalmente, sicuramente*).
- **Comparisons** (*più… di, meno… di, il più…, migliore / peggiore*): one section in `adjectives`, or a short lesson after it. Links to Top adjectives (*migliore, peggiore*).
- **Prepositions beyond the big four** (*tra / fra, senza, fino a, prima di, davanti a, vicino a*): one section in `prepositions`. Links to Top prepositions.
- **Ordinals** (*primo, secondo, terzo… decimo*): one section in `numbers`. There are no ordinal cards yet except *primo* in Top adjectives and *secondo* (also "according to") in Top prepositions.
