Adjectives work like verbs

English needs "be" before an adjective: she is tall. In Chinese, an adjective is already a full predicate, like a verb, so there is no 是. Instead, a plain statement usually takes 很 hěn.

很 is often just glue

很 means "very", but in a simple statement it is mostly there to make the sentence sound complete. 我很好 is just "I'm fine". Without 很, a bare adjective sounds like a comparison: 她高，我不高。 When you really mean "very", stress 很 or use a stronger word.

{{ex:zh.s0065}}{{ex:zh.s0066}}

Not: 不 + adjective

To say "not", put 不 right before the adjective, with no 很 and no 是: 不忙 bù máng, 不贵 bú guì. Watch the tone: 不 rises before a fourth tone.

{{ex:zh.s0067}}{{ex:zh.s0068}}

Too: 太 … 了

"Too" is 太 before the adjective and 了 at the end. It also works for delight: 太好了！ means "great!".

{{ex:zh.s0069}}{{ex:zh.s0070}}

More about 很

In English, "very" is optional. In Chinese, a plain statement without 很 sounds unfinished, as if you were about to compare two things. In casual talk people also use 好 in place of 很: 我好累 wǒ hǎo lèi means "I'm so tired".

Here are adjectives you can use right away: 忙 busy, 累 tired, 高 tall, 贵 expensive, 好 good, 大 dà big, 小 xiǎo small, 冷 lěng cold, 热 rè hot. Each one works the same way: subject, then 很, 不 or 太, then the adjective.

Before a noun

An adjective that describes a noun goes before it, as in English: 好朋友 hǎo péngyou, a good friend. With 很 in front, add 的 to link it: 很好的朋友, a very good friend.

Why does 他是好朋友 have 是? Because 好朋友 is a noun with a describer in front: 是 joins him to "good friend", noun to noun. The rule is about adjectives that stand alone after the subject.

{{ex:zh.s0071}}{{ex:zh.s0072}}
Most common mistake: putting 是 before an adjective, because English says "is". 他是高。 → 他很高。 And not both: 他是很高。 → 他很高。
