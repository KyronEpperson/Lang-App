English has one word for "not" and builds the rest with helpers: don't, doesn't, didn't, won't, haven't. Chinese has no helpers. It has two words for "not", and you choose by meaning: 不 bù and 没 méi. Both go right before the verb or adjective, and the verb itself never changes.

不: not now, not usually, not going to

Use 不 for habits, likes, states and plans: what you don't do, aren't, or won't do. English needs don't, isn't or won't; Chinese uses 不 for all three.

Remember the tone change from earlier: 不 is said with the second tone before a fourth tone, as in 不是 bú shì and 不去 bú qù. Before the other tones it keeps its fourth tone: 不喝 bù hē, 不忙 bù máng.

没 with 有: don't have

有 yǒu (to have) is the one verb that never takes 不. Its "not" is always 没: 没有 méiyǒu. In speech the 有 can drop when a noun follows.

{{ex:zh.s0091}}{{ex:zh.s0092}}

没: didn't, haven't

For an action that did not happen, use 没 (or 没有) before the verb. English marks the past with didn't; Chinese marks nothing else. The time word, such as 昨天 zuótiān (yesterday), comes before the verb too.

{{ex:zh.s0093}}{{ex:zh.s0094}}

Later you will meet 了, a word that often marks a finished action. It does not go with 没 + verb: the action did not happen, so there is nothing to finish.

How to choose

EnglishChinese
I don't go (usually) / I won't go我不去。
I didn't go / I haven't gone我没去。
I'm not busy我不忙。
I don't have it我没有。

A quick test: if English says didn't or don't have, reach for 没. If it says don't, isn't or won't, reach for 不. Saying no to 是 is always 不是.

{{ex:zh.s0095}}
Most common mistake: putting 不 in front of 有, because English says "don't have". 我不有钱。 → 我没有钱。 And no 了 after 没: 我没去了。 → 我没去。
