English has one word for brother and one for sister. Chinese always says whether they are older or younger than you, and family members are counted with a measure word of their own.

Parents and siblings

EnglishChinese
dad / mom爸爸 / 妈妈
older brother / younger brother哥哥 / 弟弟
older sister / younger sister姐姐 / 妹妹

Each word is one syllable said twice, and the second syllable has the neutral tone: 妈妈 māma, 哥哥 gēge. There is no plain word for one "brother" or "sister" in everyday speech: pick older or younger. (兄弟姐妹 means brothers and sisters as a group.)

As you saw in the lesson on 的, family words drop 的 after a pronoun.

How many people in your family?

To count the members of a family, Chinese uses 口 kǒu (mouth) as the measure word: think of the mouths at the dinner table. 家 jiā means family or home.

{{ex:zh.s0136}}{{ex:zh.s0137}}

The answer counts you too. 个 is also heard here, but 口 is the usual word for a family.

Asking someone's age

English asks everyone "How old are you?". Chinese picks the question by the person's age. To a child, expect a small number and use 几: 你几岁？ To an adult around your age, use 你多大？ Nǐ duō dà?, literally "you how big?". Older people are asked in a more polite way, which you will meet later.

{{ex:zh.s0138}}{{ex:zh.s0139}}

The answer is the number + 岁 suì (years of age), with no 是.

{{ex:zh.s0140}}
Most common mistake: counting people with the counting two. 我有二个哥哥。 → 我有两个哥哥。
