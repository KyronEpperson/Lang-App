Ordering in Chinese is short and direct. You already have the tools: 要 yào (want), numbers, and measure words.

Getting attention

In a restaurant, call the server 服务员 fúwùyuán. It is neutral and normal, not rude. Avoid 小姐: in the mainland it can now suggest a sex worker.

{{ex:zh.s0125}}

Three ways to order

English softens an order with "could I have" or "I'll have". Chinese has three common frames:

  • 我要 wǒ yào…: "I want…". The normal way to order.
  • 来 lái…: literally "come", meaning "bring me". Casual and very common.
  • 请给我 qǐng gěi wǒ…: "please give me". The softest.

Measure words on the menu

Whatever you order, the number needs its measure word: 碗 wǎn for a bowl, 杯 for a cup or glass, 瓶 píng for a bottle, 个 for things like dumplings or buns. English "two milk teas" has none; Chinese must say "two cups of milk tea".

Spicy or not

Many dishes are spicy by default. 辣 là means spicy; 不要辣的 means "not the spicy kind". If you like a little heat, say 微辣 wēilà (mildly spicy).

{{ex:zh.s0129}}

Tasty, and paying

好吃 hǎochī is "good to eat" and 好喝 hǎohē is "good to drink": English says "delicious" for both, Chinese doesn't. To pay, call the server and say 买单 mǎidān. To take leftovers home, or to order to go, say 打包 dǎbāo.
