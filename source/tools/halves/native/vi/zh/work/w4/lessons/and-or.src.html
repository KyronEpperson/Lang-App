English "and" joins anything: two words, two actions, two whole sentences. Chinese 和 hé is pickier. It joins nouns and pronouns, that is, people and things, and nothing else.

和 joins people and things

{{ex:zh.s0222}}{{ex:zh.s0223}}

Two details: it is normal to start with 我 ("I and Wang Ming"), and 一起 yìqǐ (together) goes before the verb.

In everyday speech, 跟 gēn is used the same way: 我跟他一起去. For a list of three or more, write the list comma 、 between the items and put 和 before the last one.

{{ex:zh.s0224}}{{ex:zh.s0225}}

Between sentences: no 和

To join two actions or two sentences, Chinese uses no word at all, just a pause (a comma in writing). 也 can help when the second part adds something.

{{ex:zh.s0226}}{{ex:zh.s0227}}

"Or" in a question: 还是

When you offer a choice and want the other person to pick, use 还是 háishi. It already makes a question, so there is no 吗.

{{ex:zh.s0228}}{{ex:zh.s0229}}

To answer, just name your choice: 茶, or 我要茶. You can also put 还是 between two verbs or two whole questions: 你今天去还是明天去？ (Are you going today or tomorrow?)

"Or" in a statement: 或者

When you are not asking, but saying that either one is fine or possible, use 或者 huòzhě.

{{ex:zh.s0230}}

或者 is also how you give someone options without asking them to choose right now.

tea or coffee (either is fine)茶或者咖啡
Most common mistake: using 和 between two sentences. 我很累和想睡觉 → 我很累，想睡觉. A second one: 或者 when you want the other person to choose. 你要茶或者咖啡？ is heard in casual speech, but the standard way is → 你要茶还是咖啡？
