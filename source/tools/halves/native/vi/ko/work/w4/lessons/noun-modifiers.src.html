So far describing words came at the end: 가방이 커요 (the bag is big). To say "a big bag", the describing verb moves in front of the noun and changes its ending.

Vowel stem + ㄴ, consonant stem + 은

Drop 다. If the stem ends in a vowel, tuck ㄴ underneath; if it ends in a consonant, add 은.

{{ex:ko.s0231}}{{ex:ko.s0232}}{{ex:ko.s0233}}{{ex:ko.s0234}}

있다 / 없다 words take 는

맛있다, 재미있다 and friends use 는 instead.

{{ex:ko.s0235}}{{ex:ko.s0236}}

ㅂ stems: ㅂ becomes 운

Many weather and taste words end in ㅂ. The ㅂ turns into 우, then ㄴ is added.

{{ex:ko.s0237}}{{ex:ko.s0238}}

Use it in a sentence

The describing word and its noun act as one unit; particles go on the noun.

{{ex:ko.s0240}}{{ex:ko.s0241}}{{ex:ko.s0242}}
Action verbs can describe nouns too, with 는: 제가 좋아하는 음식 = "food that I like". Korean has no "that / which / who"; the whole description just sits in front of the noun.
