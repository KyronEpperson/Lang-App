English softens what it asks with "let's", "please", "why don't you" and "don't". Chinese uses a few small words at the end or the start of the sentence. They are some of the most useful words for sounding friendly.

吧: let's, and a softer request

吧 ba at the end of a sentence makes it a suggestion. With 我们 it means "let's". With 你 it means "why don't you" or "go ahead".

{{ex:zh.s0212}}{{ex:zh.s0213}}

The rest of the sentence keeps its usual order: time and place before the verb, 吧 at the very end.

{{ex:zh.s0214}}

别 and 不要: don't

别 bié + verb means "don't". 不要 bú yào + verb means the same and sounds a little stronger. Plain 不 only says "not", so it cannot tell someone what to do.

{{ex:zh.s0215}}{{ex:zh.s0216}}{{ex:zh.s0217}}

请: please

请 qǐng goes first, before the verb: 请坐 (have a seat), 请进 (come in). It is what hosts, teachers and staff say all the time.

{{ex:zh.s0218}}

麻烦你: could you…?

To ask someone to do something for you, start with 麻烦你 máfan nǐ, literally "I trouble you". It works like "could you…, please?"

{{ex:zh.s0219}}{{ex:zh.s0220}}

Saying yes to a suggestion

To agree with a suggestion, say 好 or 好的 (OK). 好吧 is a softer, slightly reluctant "OK, fine". 行 also means "that works".

{{ex:zh.s0221}}

Sounding friendly

A bare verb, such as 走！, can sound blunt, like an order. Adding 吧, 请 or 麻烦你 turns it into a suggestion or a request. With friends, 吧 is usually enough.

Most common mistake: using 不 to say "don't". 不担心 → 别担心. 不 says "not"; 别 tells someone not to.
