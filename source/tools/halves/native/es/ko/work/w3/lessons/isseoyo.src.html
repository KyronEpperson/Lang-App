English uses "is" for two different jobs: I am a student and I am at home. Korean splits them. 이에요/예요 says what something is; 있어요 says that something exists, where it is, or that you have it. Its opposite is 없어요. Two short verbs, and you can already ask for half the things you need.

There is, there isn't

있어요 = there is / is there?; 없어요 = there isn't. Ask with a rising tone, answer with the same verb.

The subject marker 이/가

The thing that exists takes 이 after a consonant and 가 after a vowel.

Having something

Korean says "as for me, X exists". Topic 은/는 for the owner, 이/가 for the thing.

Where something is: place + 에

Put 에 after the place, then 있어요 or 없어요.

이에요 or 있어요?

If the sentence is "A = B", use 이에요/예요. If it is about being somewhere or having something, use 있어요.

EnglishKoreanWhy
I am a student학생이에요I = student
I am at school학교에 있어요location
This is a school여기는 학교예요here = school
There is a school here여기에 학교가 있어요existence
In speech 이/가 often drops in short questions: 시간 있어요? (are you free?) is how people actually ask. You still need 이/가 to read and to build longer sentences.
Common mistakes
  • 이에요 for a location — 저는 학교예요 ✗ → 저는 학교에 있어요 ✓
  • 이에요 for having — 저는 고양이예요 ✗ (= I am a cat) → 저는 고양이가 있어요 ✓
  • Wrong 이/가 — 시간가 있어요 ✗ → 시간이 있어요 ✓
  • Forgetting 에 — 고양이가 집 있어요 ✗ → 고양이가 집에 있어요 ✓
