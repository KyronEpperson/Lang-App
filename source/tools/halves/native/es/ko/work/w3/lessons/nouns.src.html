A noun names a person, place or thing: 친구 (friend), 집 (house), 커피 (coffee). Korean nouns are easier than English ones — there is no a or the, no gender, and the word itself never changes. Plurals are optional: one word covers one or many, and 들 can mark "more than one", mostly for people.

This, that, that over there

Put 이 (this), 그 (that, near you) or 저 (that, far from both of us) in front of the noun.

{{ex:ko.s0033}}{{ex:ko.s0034}}{{ex:ko.s0035}}

Noun + noun = whose

Just put the owner first. The possessive tag 의 exists but is usually dropped. "My" is 제 (polite) or 내 (casual).

{{ex:ko.s0036}}{{ex:ko.s0037}}

Saying "it is a …"

Glue 이에요 onto a noun ending in a consonant, 예요 onto one ending in a vowel. For "is not", use 이/가 아니에요.

{{ex:ko.s0038}}{{ex:ko.s0039}}{{ex:ko.s0040}}{{ex:ko.s0041}}

은/는: what you're talking about

Put 은 (after a consonant) or 는 (after a vowel) on the noun the sentence is about — the topic, like "as for …". Then say what it is.

Many nouns are built from reusable blocks. 학 means learning: 학교 (school), 학생 (student), 학원 (academy). Spot the block and new words get cheaper.
Common mistakes
  • Adding 들 to everything — 사과들 세 개 ✗ → 사과 세 개 ✓
  • Mixing up 이에요 / 예요 — 선생님예요 ✗ → 선생님이에요 ✓ · 가수이에요 ✗ → 가수예요 ✓
  • 은 after a vowel — 저은 학생이에요 ✗ → 저는 학생이에요 ✓
  • Looking for a word for "the" — there isn't one.
