Korean uses pronouns less than English, but you need a handful, plus the question words. A question word simply sits where the answer will go; the word order does not change.

I and we

저 is the polite "I" (나 with close friends). With 가 it becomes 제가. 우리 means "we" and also "my" for home and family.

You, he, she: use a name or title

Korean avoids "you". Say the person's name + 씨, or their title. For "that person" say 그 사람, or politely 이분.

This, that, here, there

이 (near me), 그 (near you), 저 (far from both) build both sets.

The question words

뭐 what · 누구 who · 어디 where · 언제 when · 왜 why · 얼마 how much · 몇 how many.

{{ex:ko.s0065}}{{ex:ko.s0066}}{{ex:ko.s0067}}{{ex:ko.s0068}}
누구 + 가 shrinks to 누가. And an answer just replaces the question word: 이거 뭐예요? → 이거 책이에요.
Common mistakes
  • Calling a stranger 당신 ✗ → use their name + 씨, or no pronoun at all ✓
  • 누구가 있어요? ✗ → 누가 있어요? ✓
  • Moving the question word to the front — 뭐 이거예요? ✗ → 이거 뭐예요? ✓
  • 저 means both "I" and "that over there": 저는 (I) vs 저 사람 (that person)
