Korean is spelled to show meaning, then smoothed when spoken. That is why 감사합니다 sounds like "gamsahamnida", not "hap-ni-da". Four habits explain almost every surprise. The romanization in this app is written the way words sound.

1 · Linking: a final consonant slides over

When the next block starts with silent ㅇ, the final consonant moves into that empty seat.

{{ex:ko.s0019}}{{ex:ko.s0020}}{{ex:ko.s0021}}

2 · Nasal: ㅂ ㄷ ㄱ soften before ㄴ and ㅁ

Before ㄴ or ㅁ, ㅂ becomes ㅁ, ㄷ becomes ㄴ and ㄱ becomes ㅇ (ng).

{{ex:ko.s0022}}{{ex:ko.s0023}}{{ex:ko.s0024}}

3 · ㅎ: it puffs or disappears

ㅎ next to ㄱ ㄷ ㅂ ㅈ turns them into the airy ㅋ ㅌ ㅍ ㅊ. Between vowels, ㅎ goes silent.

{{ex:ko.s0025}}{{ex:ko.s0026}}{{ex:ko.s0027}}

4 · ㄴ meets ㄹ: both become ㄹ

ㄴ and ㄹ side by side are said as a long l.

{{ex:ko.s0028}}{{ex:ko.s0029}}
One more you will hear: after ㄱ ㄷ ㅂ, the next consonant gets tense, so 학교 sounds like 학꾜. To train your ear, open Read and try the 🔀 Sound changes drill.
Common mistakes
  • Reading block by block — gam-sa-hap-ni-da ✗ → gamsahamnida ✓
  • Pausing before ㅇ — han-guk-eo ✗ → hangugeo ✓
  • Saying the ㅎ in 좋아요 — jo-ha-yo ✗ → joayo ✓
  • Spelling by ear — 조아요 ✗ → 좋아요 ✓ (the spelling never changes)
