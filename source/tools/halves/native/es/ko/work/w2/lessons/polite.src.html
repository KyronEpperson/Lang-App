Korean verbs change by how polite you are being, not by who is doing the action. As a beginner you need two levels:

LevelEndingUse with
요-form (polite)…요Strangers, shops, coworkers, anyone older. Your default.
Casual (반말)drop the 요Close friends, younger people, partners, kids.

There is also a formal level (-습니다 / -ㅂ니다) heard in announcements, news, and set phrases like 감사합니다 and 죄송합니다. Recognize it; you don't need to produce it yet.

Rule of thumb: use 요 until a Korean person tells you to drop it. Using casual speech too early comes off as rude; using polite speech too long just comes off as sweet.
Common mistakes
  • Saying 감사해요 to a shop owner — fine, but 감사합니다 is the safer default with strangers.
  • Dropping 요 with someone older because they're friendly — wait until they say 말 놔도 돼요.
  • Mixing levels in one sentence (저는… 먹어) — pick one level per sentence.
