English puts "can", "want" and "must" before the verb. Thai does that with "want" and "must", but "can" goes at the end. That one difference causes most of the mistakes in this lesson.

Want: อยาก + verb

อยาก yàak goes before a verb: "want to go", "want to eat". The verb is needed: Thai does not say "want the beach". For "don't want", put ไม่ in front: ไม่อยากไป, I don't want to go. To turn down an offer of help, ไม่ต้อง with ครับ or ค่ะ does the job; it comes up below.

{{ex:th.s0242}}

To want a thing, use อยากได้ yàak dâai, literally "want to get".

{{ex:th.s0243}}

Can: verb + ได้

English says "I can speak Thai". Thai says "speak Thai can": ได้ dâai comes after the verb and what follows it. For "can't", put ไม่ before it: ไม่ได้.

{{ex:th.s0244}}{{ex:th.s0245}}

For skills you learned, like driving or swimming, you will also hear เป็น in the same place: ขับรถไม่เป็น, "I don't know how to drive".

Watch the position. ได้ before a verb means "got to": ได้ไป is "got to go, had the chance to go", not "can go".

Asking: ได้ไหม

Add ได้ไหม dâai mǎi at the end to ask "can you …?" or "can I …?". Answer with ได้ (yes) or ไม่ได้ (no), never with ใช่.

{{ex:th.s0246}}{{ex:th.s0247}}

Must: ต้อง + verb

ต้อง dtâwng goes before the verb, like "have to". ไม่ต้อง means "you don't have to", a handy "no need" when someone offers help.

{{ex:th.s0248}}{{ex:th.s0249}}
Most common mistake: putting "can" first, as in English. ได้พูดภาษาไทย for "I can speak Thai" → พูดภาษาไทยได้. ได้ goes after the verb.
