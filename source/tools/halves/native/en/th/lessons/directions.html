Getting around in Thai needs only a handful of words, but two habits from English get in the way: putting "where" first, and adding "to" after "go".

Where is it? Place + อยู่ที่ไหน

English starts with "where". Thai starts with the place, then อยู่ yùu (is located), then ที่ไหน thîi-nǎi (where), at the end, in the slot where the answer goes.

Near and far: listen to the tone

ใกล้ glâi with a falling tone is near; ไกล glai with a mid tone is far. They sound almost the same to English ears, so say the falling tone clearly. ใกล้ๆ means "close by, nearby".

{{ex:th.s0261}}

Left, right, straight

ซ้าย sáai is left and ขวา khwǎa is right. เลี้ยว líiao means turn, and ตรงไป dtrong bpai means go straight. To say where to turn, add ที่ and the place. If you are lost, ไปทางไหน asks "which way?", and pointing helps too.

{{ex:th.s0262}}{{ex:th.s0263}}

ข้างหน้า khâang-nâa is "ahead, in front", and ตรงข้าม dtrong-khâam is "across from". Both go before the place, like English prepositions.

{{ex:th.s0264}}

In a taxi or a tuk-tuk

English says "go to the airport". Thai needs no word for "to": just ไป bpai + the place. In a taxi, ask for the meter; in a tuk-tuk, agree on the price before you get in. When you arrive, จอดตรงนี้ means "stop here".

{{ex:th.s0265}}{{ex:th.s0266}}{{ex:th.s0267}}

Add ครับ or ค่ะ to each of these, and the driver will hear a polite passenger.

Most common mistake: asking in English order. ที่ไหนห้องน้ำ → ห้องน้ำอยู่ที่ไหน. The place comes first, and ที่ไหน stays at the end.
