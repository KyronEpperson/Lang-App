Shopping puts together three things you already know: question words, classifiers and describers after the noun. This lesson adds the words for clothes, colors and paying.

How much?

ราคาเท่าไร raa-khaa thâo-rai asks the price; you will often hear เท่าไหร่. Point at the item with noun + classifier + นี้.

Clothes and their classifiers

Each kind of thing has its classifier. English counts pants as "a pair"; Thai counts them like shirts, with ตัว dtuua. Shoes really come in pairs: คู่ khûu.

ThingThaiClassifier
shirtเสื้อตัว
pantsกางเกงตัว
skirtกระโปรงตัว
shoesรองเท้าคู่
hat, bagหมวก, กระเป๋าใบ

Colors after the noun

A color is a describer, so it comes after the noun, with สี sǐi (color) in front. With a classifier, the order is noun, color, classifier, นี้.

{{ex:th.s0271}}{{ex:th.s0272}}

Trying on: too big, too small

ลองได้ไหม asks "can I try it on?". After a describing word, ไป bpai means "too": ใหญ่ไป is too big, แพงไป too expensive.

{{ex:th.s0273}}{{ex:th.s0274}}

Careful with ฟิต fít: it does not mean "fits well". It means tight.

{{ex:th.s0275}}

Paying

Most shops and many stalls take QR payment from a banking app; you can also pay in cash, เงินสด ngoen-sòt. If you are only browsing, ดูก่อน ("look first") with ครับ or ค่ะ tells the seller you are just looking.

{{ex:th.s0276}}
Most common mistake: the color in front, as in English. สีแดงเสื้อ → เสื้อสีแดง. The noun comes first, then สี and the color.
