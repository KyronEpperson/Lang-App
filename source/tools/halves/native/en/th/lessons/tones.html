This course teaches standard Thai as spoken in Bangkok and understood everywhere in Thailand. The north, the northeast (Isan) and the south have their own dialects; you will hear them, and everyone there also understands standard Thai.

Pitch is part of the word

In English, pitch shows feeling: "Really?" goes up because you are surprised, but the word stays the same. In Thai, every syllable has its own pitch pattern, called a tone, and the tone is part of the word. Change the tone and you say a different word. Thai has five tones, and the same sounds maa with three of them give three words:

The five tones and their marks

Under every Thai word you will see a romanization: Thai sounds spelled in English letters. A small mark over the vowel tells you the tone. The mid tone has no mark.

ToneMarkHow it soundsExample
midnone: alevel, in the middle of your normal voiceไกล
lowàlevel, a little lower than normalใหม่
fallingâstarts high and drops, like a firm "No!"ไม่
higháhigh and tense, rising a little at the endม้า
risingǎdips low, then rises, like a doubtful "Hmm?"หมา
{{ex:th.s0004}}{{ex:th.s0005}}

Mid and low are the pair English ears find hardest, because both are level. Low sits clearly lower, as if you were a little bored.

Pairs to listen for

ใกล้ glâi with a falling tone means near; ไกล glai with a mid tone means far. Ask for the wrong one and the driver goes the wrong way.

{{ex:th.s0006}}{{ex:th.s0007}}

Rice, white and he sound alike to a new ear: ข้าว khâao (falling), ขาว khǎao (rising) and เขา khǎo (rising, with a short vowel).

{{ex:th.s0008}}

Reading the romanization

  • A doubled vowel is a long vowel: aa is held about twice as long as a. Length changes words too (the next lesson).
  • Hyphens join the syllables of one word: sà-wàt-dii. A space separates words, and each word is one tile in practice.
  • The romanization is a crutch. Listen first, copy the voice, and use the letters only to check.

Questions keep their tones

English raises the voice at the end of a question. Thai does not need to: the small word ไหม mǎi at the end does the asking, and every word keeps its own tone.

{{ex:th.s0009}}{{ex:th.s0010}}
Most common mistake: saying every word flat, the English way. glai said for near → glâi, with a falling tone. And don't push your voice up on mǎi as in English: it already has its rising tone.
