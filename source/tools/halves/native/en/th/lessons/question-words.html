English moves the question word to the front: "You are eating rice" becomes "What are you eating?". Thai leaves everything where it was and puts the question word in the slot where the answer will go. Ask the question, and the answer simply replaces the question word.

This makes Thai questions easy to build. Start from the statement you would expect as an answer, then swap the unknown part for a question word. "The bathroom is over there" becomes "The bathroom is where?". You never need a helper like "do" or "does", and the verb never changes. Most questions end with the question word or with a polite particle after it, so listen for the end of the sentence.

What: อะไร

อะไร à-rai sits where the thing would be. The answer uses the same order.

Who: ใคร

ใคร khrai goes where the person would be: at the end for "who is that?", or after the verb for "who are you waiting for?".

{{ex:th.s0177}}{{ex:th.s0178}}

Where: ที่ไหน

ที่ไหน thîi-nǎi follows อยู่ for "where is…". In speech it is often shortened to ไหน, as in the everyday greeting ไปไหน, "where are you off to?".

{{ex:th.s0117}}{{ex:th.s0179}}

How much: เท่าไร

เท่าไร thâo-rai is also spelled and said เท่าไหร่; both are correct.

{{ex:th.s0180}}

How many and which: กี่, ไหน

These two need a classifier, just like a number or นี้. กี่ goes before the classifier; ไหน after it.

{{ex:th.s0181}}{{ex:th.s0182}}{{ex:th.s0183}}{{ex:th.s0184}}

When, why, how

เมื่อไร mûuea-rai (when) usually comes at the end, ทำไม tham-mai (why) usually at the start, and ยังไง yang-ngai (how) after the verb. These three can move more freely, so learn the common patterns.

{{ex:th.s0185}}{{ex:th.s0186}}

No ไหม with a question word

ไหม is only for yes/no questions. A question word already makes the question, so never add ไหม after it. A polite particle at the end is still welcome: คุณชื่ออะไรครับ.

Most common mistake: English order. อะไรคุณกิน → คุณกินอะไร, and ที่ไหนห้องน้ำ → ห้องน้ำอยู่ที่ไหน. Put the question word where the answer goes.
