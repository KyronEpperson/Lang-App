English writes letters in the order you say them. Thai writes the consonant as the core of the syllable and places the vowel around it: after, above, below, before, or on several sides at once.

Find the consonant first

Always read the consonant first, then the vowel, wherever it sits. The vowel signs above and below need a consonant to sit on; when a syllable starts with a vowel sound, the silent อ carries them.

Thai names each vowel on the silent อ, so the tiles show it there. The top row is written after the consonant, the next above it (อือ also adds an อ after), the last below it. A short vowel on อ comes out with a low tone; the end of this lesson shows why. อำ, ใอ, ไอ and เอา are the exception: they end in an m, y or w sound and behave like long vowels, so they keep a mid tone. Written first, said second

Five vowels are written before the consonant: เ แ โ ใ ไ. You still say the consonant first. ใ and ไ sound the same, ai; only about twenty words use ใ.

A short vowel often adds ะ after the consonant: เอะ, แอะ, โอะ. Tap a tile to hear it.

The describer comes after the noun: เรือใหม่ is "boat new".

Putting it together

A syllable with no final consonant is a consonant and a vowel. Read the consonant first, then the vowel, wherever it sits, then work out the tone: a mid-class letter with a long vowel gives a mid tone (ปู bpuu), with a short vowel a low tone (จะ jà); a high-class letter with a long vowel gives a rising tone (หู hǔu).
