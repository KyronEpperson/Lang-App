You already know that Thai describing words work like verbs and need no เป็น bpen. This lesson puts them to work: where they sit, how to make them stronger, and how to compare two things.

The describer comes after the noun

English says "a big house", with the describer first. Thai says "house big": บ้านใหญ่ bâan yài. Colors work the same way, with สี sǐi (color) in front of the color word.

{{ex:th.s0216}}{{ex:th.s0217}}

With a classifier and นี้ níi, the order is noun, describer, classifier, นี้: เสื้อสีแดงตัวนี้, this red shirt.

มาก comes after, too

English puts "very" before the word: "very beautiful". Thai puts มาก mâak after it: สวยมาก, "beautiful very".

{{ex:th.s0218}}

More than: กว่า

English has two ways to compare: "-er" (cheaper) and "more" (more expensive). Thai has one: put กว่า gwàa right after the describing word. There is no word for "more" in front of it. To say what you compare with, add it after กว่า, the way English adds "than".

{{ex:th.s0219}}{{ex:th.s0220}}

Near and far are easy to swap: ใกล้กว่า glâi gwàa (falling tone) is closer, ไกลกว่า glai gwàa (mid tone) is farther.

The most: ที่สุด

For "the cheapest" or "the most beautiful", put ที่สุด thîi-sùt after the describing word. Again, nothing goes in front.

{{ex:th.s0221}}{{ex:th.s0222}}

The same: เท่ากัน and เหมือนกัน

เท่ากัน thâo-gan after a describing word means "equally": the same price, the same size. เหมือนกัน mǔuean-gan means "alike, the same", and in conversation it is also a friendly "me too".

{{ex:th.s0223}}{{ex:th.s0224}}
Most common mistake: copying "more expensive" word for word. มากกว่าแพง → แพงกว่า. The describing word comes first, then กว่า, and Thai needs no "more".
