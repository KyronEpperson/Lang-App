English makes a negative with "do not" and a question by moving words around: "You like it" becomes "Do you like it?". Thai changes nothing in the sentence. It adds one small word: ไม่ in front for "not", ไหม at the end for a yes/no question.

ไม่: not, before the verb

Put ไม่ mâi right before the verb or the describing word. There is no "do": "not like" is simply ไม่ชอบ.

{{ex:th.s0159}}{{ex:th.s0160}}

To say something is not a noun ("not a teacher"), keep using ไม่ใช่ from the lesson on เป็น.

ไหม: a yes/no question at the end

Add ไหม mǎi to the end of a statement and it becomes a yes/no question; a polite particle can follow it. Your voice does not rise as in English: every word keeps its own tone, and ไหม does the asking. In speech you will often hear ไหม said with a high tone.

{{ex:th.s0161}}{{ex:th.s0162}}

Answer with the verb

Thai has no all-purpose "yes". To say yes, repeat the verb or describing word from the question. To say no, put ไม่ in front of it.

QuestionYesNo
ชอบไหมชอบไม่ชอบ
เผ็ดไหมเผ็ดไม่เผ็ด
ไปไหมไปไม่ไป
{{ex:th.s0163}}

ใช่: only for "is it…?"

ใช่ châi means "that's right". Use it to confirm a fact, often after a question that ends in ใช่ไหม ("…, right?"). ไม่ใช่ means "no, that's not it".

{{ex:th.s0164}}{{ex:th.s0165}}

หรือเปล่า: "or not?"

หรือเปล่า rǔue-bplàao at the end also asks a yes/no question, a bit like "…or not?". It is very common in speech, and you answer it the same way, with the verb.

{{ex:th.s0166}}

Four words, four tones

These four look and sound alike to English ears. Only the tone tells them apart.

WordToneMeaning
ไม่fallingnot
ไหมrisingyes/no question
ใหม่lownew
ไม้high, long vowelwood

Most common mistake: answering every question with ใช่. Asked ชอบไหม, saying ใช่ → ชอบ (or ไม่ชอบ). Answer with the verb; keep ใช่ for "is it…?".
