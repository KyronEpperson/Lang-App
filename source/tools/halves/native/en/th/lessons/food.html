Thai food is one of the best reasons to learn Thai, and ordering it takes only a few patterns you already know: the classifier, ไม่ and the polite particles.

Asking for a dish: ขอ and เอา

English says "Can I have…" or "I'll have…". Thai says ขอ khǎaw (may I have) + the dish, or the more casual เอา ao (I'll take) + the dish. Add the number and classifier after it, and ครับ or ค่ะ at the end. Dishes on a plate are counted with จาน jaan.

{{ex:th.s0196}}{{ex:th.s0197}}

Dish names follow the same order as the rest of Thai: the main word first, then what describes it. ข้าวผัดกุ้ง is "rice, fried, shrimp", shrimp fried rice. So when you read a menu, the first word tells you what kind of dish it is (rice, noodles, curry), and the words after it tell you how it is cooked and what is in it. You do not need "a" or "the", and there is no plural: the number and classifier do that job.

Drinks with their classifiers

Drinks are counted by glass (แก้ว) or bottle (ขวด), after the number.

Tastes

The four tastes you will be asked about: เผ็ด phèt (spicy), เปรี้ยว bprîiao (sour), หวาน wǎan (sweet), เค็ม khem (salty). To control the heat, say ไม่เผ็ด (not spicy) or เผ็ดนิดหน่อย (a little spicy). Adding นะ before the particle makes it a friendly request. And if it tastes good, say อร่อยมาก.

Calling the server

In Thai restaurants people often call a younger server น้อง náawng, "younger sibling", with the particle: น้องครับ or น้องคะ (คะ, the high tone of questions, is also used when calling someone). ขอโทษครับ or ขอโทษค่ะ ("excuse me") works with anyone.

{{ex:th.s0203}}

Paying and taking it away

To ask for the bill, say เช็คบิลด้วย; ด้วย makes it softer. For takeaway, ห่อกลับบ้าน means "wrap it to take home".

{{ex:th.s0204}}{{ex:th.s0205}}
Most common mistake: using the word for "hot" for spicy food. ร้อน means hot to the touch, like a hot drink. Ordering ไม่ร้อน for "not spicy" → ไม่เผ็ด.
