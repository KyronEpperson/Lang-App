To say "eat a meal", Hmong says noj mov falling · rising, literally "eat rice". mov rising is cooked rice, and it also means a meal.

Meals

noj tshais falling · low is to eat breakfast, noj su falling · mid to eat lunch and noj hmo falling · mid to eat dinner. Each one is noj plus the name of the meal, so there are only three new words to learn: tshais, su and hmo.

Food words

nqaij npuaporkfawmpho, noodle soup
nqaij nyujbeefkua txobchili pepper

nqaij falling means meat, and the animal comes after it: the general word first, then the kind, much as a describing word follows the noun (lesson 12). Fruit in general is txiv hmab txiv ntoo rising · high · rising · mid.

Pork is also written nqaij npuas. To say what you like, use nyiam creaky "like" before the verb; to say what you do not eat, put tsis before noj, as in lesson 8.

noj for eating, haus for drinking

English can "have" a coffee or a sandwich. Hmong uses two verbs: noj falling for food, and haus low for anything you drink: dej falling water, tshuaj yej falling · falling tea, kav fes rising · low coffee, mis nyuj low · falling milk. Watch the tone letters: dej "water" ends in j, dev "dog" in v.

Counting food and drink

Food takes classifiers too: lub for eggs, and the classifiers khob high "a cup of" and poom creaky "a bottle or can of" for drinks.

At the table

English says "I am hungry" with "am"; Hmong needs no "is" here either (lesson 6). tshaib plab high · high means to be hungry, and qab heev high · rising is "very delicious".
