Four questions come up when Hmong people meet: your name, where you are from, your age, and your clan.

Your name

{{ex:hmn.s0064}}{{ex:hmn.s0065}}

Word for word: "your lub name is called how?". The small word lub high is the classifier, the counting word, for npe mid (name), and it cannot be left out: kuv lub npe, not kuv npe. The question ends in hu li cas (called how?) and the answer uses hu ua (is called).

Where you are from

{{ex:hmn.s0066}}{{ex:hmn.s0067}}

The verb tuaj falling (come) sits on both sides of the place: "you come where come?". Omniglot gives the answer as "Kuv tuaj ... tuaj": put your place in the middle. Careful: tuaj has the falling tone, and tuag falling-breathy means to die. Do not mix it up with Koj los qhov twg los?, "Where are you coming from?", a different question.

Your age

{{ex:hmn.s0055}}

hnub nyoog means age and pes tsawg means how many. The answer is Kuv hnub nyoog muaj ... xyoo, "I am ... years old", with the number in the gap; the numbers come in the next lesson.

{{ex:hmn.s0068}}

Your clan

Every Hmong family belongs to a clan (xeem creaky), and the clan name works as a surname: Vang, Yang, Xiong, Lee, Thao, Vue, Moua, Her, Lor. Asking someone's clan is a normal, friendly question.
