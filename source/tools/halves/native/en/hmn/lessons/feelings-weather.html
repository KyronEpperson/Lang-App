English says "I am happy" and "It is hot", with "am" and "is" in the middle. Hmong drops them: a describing word works as the verb by itself, as you saw with Kuv zoo siab in the lesson on yog and nyob.

Feelings live in the siab

siab high is the liver, which Hmong treats as the seat of feelings, the way English uses "heart". Many feelings are a word plus siab.

HmongEnglish
zoo siabhappy, glad
tu siabsad
chim siabangry
kho siablonely, homesick

Two more feelings without siab: ntshai mid (afraid) and txaj muag falling · falling-breathy (shy, embarrassed).

Not and questions

Because the feeling word is the verb, tsis goes before it, and so does puas.

Weather

huab cua high · mid is "weather". For rain, Hmong uses a verb: los nag low · falling-breathy, "rain comes". Snow works the same way: los daus. English needs a dummy "it" ("it's raining"); Hmong starts with the time word or with the verb itself.

{{ex:hmn.s0161}}{{ex:hmn.s0162}}

Hot is kub high, warm is sov rising, and cold is no mid. That last one is also the word for "this", so "today it is very cold" has two of them in a row. The word for wind is cua mid.

{{ex:hmn.s0163}}{{ex:hmn.s0164}}

Watch the tone letter: kub with -b (high tone) is "hot", but kuv with -v (rising tone) is "I".

Most common mistake: putting "is" before a feeling. Kuv yog tu siab. → Kuv tu siab.
