English has one word for "brother" and one for "sister". Hmong has several, and the right one depends on who is speaking.

Brothers and sisters: who is speaking?

A man calls his older brother tij laug falling · falling-breathy and his younger brother kwv rising. A woman calls any brother nus low. A man calls his sister muam creaky, and a woman calls hers viv ncaus rising · low. Brothers and sisters together are nus muag low · falling-breathy.

Because the word depends on the speaker, one person can have two names in the same family: a man calls his younger brother kwv, and their sister calls that same brother nus.

Watch the tone letters: muam "sister" ends in m, and muaj "have" in j.

Grandparents

pog falling-breathy is your father's mother, and yawg falling-breathy is grandfather. niam tais creaky · low means grandmother and also mother-in-law, and yawm txiv creaky · rising means grandfather and also father-in-law. The sources do not say which side of the family the last three belong to, so ask your family how they use them.

Uncles, aunts and in-laws

On your father's side, txiv hlob rising · high is his older brother, txiv ntxawm rising · creaky his younger brother and phauj falling his sister. dab laug high · falling-breathy is your mother's brother. A daughter-in-law is nyab high and a son-in-law is vauv rising.

Most common mistake: one word for "brother". A woman saying Kuv muaj ib tug kwv → Kuv muaj ib tug nus. kwv is what a man calls his younger brother.
