English uses "is" for three jobs: "This is the house", "I am in the house", "I am happy". Hmong has a different tool for each.

yog: X is a Y

Use yog falling-breathy to say what or who something is. Nov rising means this (here).

{{ex:hmn.s0040}}{{ex:hmn.s0039}}{{ex:hmn.s0041}}

The small words lub high and tus low in front of a noun are classifiers, counting words: lub tsev (house), tus phooj ywg (friend). The classifiers lesson explains them; for now, copy each one with its noun.

nyob: to be somewhere

nyob high means to be in a place, to live, to stay. Use it for where.

{{ex:hmn.s0042}}{{ex:hmn.s0043}}

It is also the verb in Kuv nyob zoo, "I'm fine": literally, I stay well. In Kuv nyob hauv lub tsev, the word hauv rising means in, inside.

So ask yourself one question: am I saying what something is, or where it is? What it is takes yog; where it is takes nyob. A third kind of "is", hu ua (is called), comes in the lesson on introducing yourself.

Describing words need no "is"

In Hmong a describing word works like a verb by itself. Nothing goes between the person and the description: "I happy", "she kind".

{{ex:hmn.s0044}}{{ex:hmn.s0045}}{{ex:hmn.s0046}}

Notice zoo siab (happy) and siab zoo (kind): the same two words in the other order mean something else. heev (very) comes after the describing word.

Most common mistake: adding yog before a describing word. Kuv yog zoo siab → Kuv zoo siab.
