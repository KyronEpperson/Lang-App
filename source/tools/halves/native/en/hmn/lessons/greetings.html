Nyob zoo high · mid is hello, to anyone, at any hour: morning, noon and night.

No "good morning"

English changes its greeting with the clock. Hmong does not. Omniglot's phrase list marks Nyob zoo sawv ntxov ("hello, morning") as not common, so do not build "good morning" word by word. Just say Nyob zoo.

Goodbye, and the soft os

Sib ntsib dua high · high · mid means "see you again". To the person who is leaving you can say Mus zoo low · mid, "go well". At night: Pw zoo os, "sleep well". The little word os at the end softens a sentence and sounds warm to Hmong ears.

Meeting someone for the first time, say Zoo siab tau ntsib koj mid · high · mid · high · falling, "happy to meet you". Omniglot gives Kuv zoo siab ntsib koj, with the same meaning.

When you arrive at a Hmong home, older relatives may greet you differently, for example Koj tuaj los?, "You've come!". The lesson on talking with elders covers those.
