English joins two actions with "and" or "to": go and buy, come to eat, want to go. Hmong simply puts the verbs side by side, with one subject in front.

Go and do: mus + verb

"I'm going out to buy vegetables" is Kuv mus yuav zaub rising · low · rising · high: I, go, buy, vegetables. There is no word for "and" or "to", and the subject is said only once, at the front. The verbs come in the order the actions happen: first you go, then you buy. The same goes for "go to work" and "go to school": mus ua hauj lwm, mus kawm ntawv.

Come and do: los + verb

los low is to come back home, so calling the family to the table is Los noj mov! "Come and eat!" Here too, English needs "and" between the two verbs, and Hmong does not.

With the stage words

A stage word from lesson 15 that goes before the verb, such as yuav "will", goes before the first verb of the row.

{{ex:hmn.s0141}}

Want to: xav + verb

xav rising "want" takes the next verb directly, again with no "to". To say "not", put tsis before the first verb, as in lesson 8.

{{ex:hmn.s0142}}{{ex:hmn.s0143}}

Give: muab … rau

"Give" is two verbs with the thing in between: muab high + the thing + rau mid + the person. Watch the tone letters: muab ends in b, the high tone, and muaj "have" in j.

Most common mistake: adding thiab (and) between the verbs. Kuv mus thiab yuav zaub → Kuv mus yuav zaub.
