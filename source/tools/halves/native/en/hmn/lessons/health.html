Health is where a few Hmong words matter most, for families and for nurses, interpreters and social workers alike. With the words below you can say what is wrong and follow a simple visit.

mob: sick, hurting

mob high means "to be sick" or "to hurt". Like a feeling, it works as the verb, with no "is".

{{ex:hmn.s0175}}

To say what hurts, put the body part right after mob. English says "I have a stomachache"; Hmong says "I hurt belly".

People and places

The doctor is kws kho mob low · mid · high, and the interpreter is neeg txhais lus falling-breathy · low · low. They are people, so they take the counting word tus: tus kws kho mob, tus neeg txhais lus. The clinic is tsev kho mob rising · mid · high and the pharmacy is khw muab tshuaj mid · high · falling.

{{ex:hmn.s0179}}{{ex:hmn.s0180}}

Clinics in the US can usually arrange an interpreter. If you are the one helping a relative, these are the words the nurse and the interpreter will use, and the words your relative will listen for.

Medicine and appointments

Medicine is tshuaj falling. Hmong "eats" it: noj tshuaj is "take medicine". An appointment is teem sij hawm, "set a time".

{{ex:hmn.s0181}}{{ex:hmn.s0182}}

Watch the tone letter: mob (high tone, sick) and mov (rising tone, rice) differ only in their last letter.

Most common mistake: adding "am". Kuv yog mob. → Kuv mob.
