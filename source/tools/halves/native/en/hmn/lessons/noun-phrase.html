In English, describing words go before the noun: the red car. In Hmong they go after it, and so do "this" and "that".

The describing word comes after

"The red car" is lub tsheb liab high · high · high: classifier, car, red. The classifier stays in front of the noun, and the describing word follows it.

{{ex:hmn.s0090}}{{ex:hmn.s0091}}{{ex:hmn.s0092}}

Some useful describing words: loj falling big, me mid small, tshiab high new, qub high old (for things), liab high red, dub high black, dawb high white. Put any of them after a noun and its classifier: lub tsev me is a small house, and phau ntawv qub an old book.

This and that come last

Hmong has three words where English has two, and they go at the very end:

WordMeaningExample
nothis (near me)lub tsev no
kothat (near you)lub tsev ko
ntawdthat (away from both of us)lub tsev ntawd
{{ex:hmn.s0093}}{{ex:hmn.s0094}}

So the full order is: number, classifier, noun, describing word, then this or that. English reads the other way round, from "this" to the noun, so read Hmong phrases from the noun outward.

"My" comes first

The owner goes first, as in English: kuv lub tsheb rising · high · high is "my car", koj phau ntawv "your book", nws tus dev "his dog". The owner words are simply the pronouns from lesson 5. A describing word still follows the noun: kuv lub tsheb liab, "my red car".

Whatever English puts before the noun, Hmong puts after it, except the owner and the number (and the classifier, which English does not have).

Most common mistake: English order. liab lub tsheb → lub tsheb liab, and no lub tsev → lub tsev no.
