Hmong numbers are regular. There are only three places where the spelling changes, and once you know them you can count to a million.

Read the last letter as a tone, as always: kaum creaky "ten" ends in the creaky tone letter m, while kaus with s means "umbrella". peb high is both "three" and "we". Zero is ntxaiv rising, the only form the sources give.

Eleven to nineteen, and twenty

English has special words like eleven and twelve. Hmong just says "ten one", "ten two": kaum ib creaky · high, kaum ob creaky · high, up to kaum cuaj creaky · falling. Twenty is the one surprise. It is nees nkaum low · creaky, not "two ten". After that, carry on the same way: nees nkaum ib is 21.

The tens: caug or caum

From thirty up, say the number and then the word for "ten". That word has two spellings: caug falling-breathy (tone letter g, the breathy tone) after three, four and five, but caum creaky (tone letter m, the creaky tone) after six, seven, eight and nine.

Hundreds: puas or pua

"Hundred" changes too. It is puas low (tone letter s, the low tone) in ib puas, ob puas, peb puas, plaub puas, tsib puas and cuaj puas, but pua mid (no tone letter, the mid tone) in rau pua, xya pua and yim pua. Copy each form as you see it here.

Thousands and a million

A thousand is ib txhiab high · high and two thousand ob txhiab. Ten thousand is kaum txhiab creaky · high (also ib vam). A million is ib plhom high · creaky. You will also see ib lab, and Omniglot gives ib roob.

Money

Money is nyiaj falling and a dollar is daus las low · low (EnglishConnect writes daulas). To ask "how many", put pes tsawg where the number will go in the answer.
