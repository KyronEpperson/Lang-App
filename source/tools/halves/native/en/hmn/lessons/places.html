English says "there" for any place away from you. Hmong picks a word by height and distance.

Up, down, over there

pemup there (higher than you)
nramdown there (lower than you)
tomover there, at (about the same level)
timover there, across (farther than tom)
{{ex:hmn.s0116}}{{ex:hmn.s0117}}

Because tom also means "at", Kuv mus tom khw is simply "I'm going to the market". And since these words describe a place from where you stand, the same house can be pem for one person and nram for another.

{{ex:hmn.s0118}}{{ex:hmn.s0119}}

In, on, at, outside

hauv rising is in, saum creaky on top of, ntawm creaky at, nraum creaky outside and hauv qab rising · high under. To say where something is, use nyob (lesson 6), then the place word, then the place.

{{ex:hmn.s0120}}{{ex:hmn.s0121}}

Mind the tone letters: hauv "in" ends in v, and haus "drink" ends in s.

Short forms at the end: tod and saud

At the end of a phrase, some place words change their tone letter to d: tom becomes tod rising-final, and saum becomes saud rising-final. ntawd rising-final "there" is another -d word.

{{ex:hmn.s0122}}

Asking where

To ask where something is, put qhov twg rising · falling-breathy "where" in the place where the answer will go, as you learned in lesson 7. The answer then fills that spot.

{{ex:hmn.s0123}}

Left, right, near, far

sab xis high · low is right, sab laug high · falling-breathy left, ze mid near and deb high far. lem creaky means "turn" (when driving).

{{ex:hmn.s0124}}{{ex:hmn.s0125}}

Go, come, come home

mus low is "go". English "come" has two Hmong words: tuaj falling is to come to a place that is not your home, and los low is to come back home.

{{ex:hmn.s0126}}
Most common mistake: using yog for "is" with a place. Phau ntawv yog saum lub rooj → Phau ntawv nyob saum lub rooj.
