English counts a few things with a measuring word: two sheets of paper, a pair of shoes, a cup of tea. Hmong does this for every noun. The word in the middle is called a classifier, and it tells you what kind of thing you are counting.

Number + classifier + noun

The classifier goes between the number and the noun. "Two houses" is ob lub tsev high · high · rising: two, the classifier lub, house. Leaving the classifier out (ob tsev) is the classic beginner mistake.

English uses a measuring word only for things it cannot count one by one, like paper or tea. Hmong uses one even for people, animals and cars: a dog is ib tug dev, and a car is ib lub tsheb.

One noun, two classifiers

ntawv rising means paper, a letter or a book. The classifier picks the meaning: daim ntawv is a sheet of paper, and phau ntawv is a book.

tus and tug

After ib and ob, the books write tus as tug falling-breathy: ib tug dev, ob tug me nyuam. It is the same word. Copy the form you see.

{{ex:hmn.s0087}}{{ex:hmn.s0088}}

cov for a group

For a group, with no number, cov rising takes the place of the classifier: cov me nyuam is "the children". With a number, go back to the normal classifier: ob tug me nyuam.
