A trip to the khw mid (market, store) needs a handful of words and one habit English does not have: the counting word.

Buying and selling

yuav rising is "to buy" (it also means "want" and "will", so context decides), and muag falling-breathy is "to sell". You met mus kav khw, "go shopping", in the lesson on verbs in a row.

{{ex:hmn.s0165}}

How much?

English moves "how much" to the front. Hmong leaves pes tsawg low · falling-breathy where the answer will go, at the end. Point and say Qhov no pes tsawg?, "How much is this?", or name the thing.

nqi mid is "price". The answer is a number from the numbers lesson plus daus las (dollars).

Expensive and cheap

kim creaky is "expensive" and pheej yig falling · falling-breathy is "cheap". Like feelings, they work as the verb, with no "is".

Paying

them nqi creaky · mid is "to pay (the price)".

{{ex:hmn.s0173}}

Counting what you buy

English says "two shoes" or "a book". Hmong never puts a number straight onto a noun: the counting word goes in between, and "this" (no) goes after the noun.

EnglishHmong
two pairs of shoesob nkawm khau
one bookib phau ntawv
this umbrellalub kaus no
{{ex:hmn.s0174}}
Most common mistake: leaving out the counting word. Kuv yuav ob khau. → Kuv yuav ob nkawm khau.
