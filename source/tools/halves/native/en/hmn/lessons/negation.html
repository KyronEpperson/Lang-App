English says "I do not know", with a helper word, "do". Hmong has no helper: put tsis low right before the verb. English can also put "not" in other places ("not me", "I'm not"); in these lessons tsis always stands right before the verb or describing word.

{{ex:hmn.s0056}}{{ex:hmn.s0057}}{{ex:hmn.s0058}}

Both nkag siab and to taub mean to understand; you will meet both. Notice that nothing else changes: the person, the verb and their order stay exactly as in Kuv paub (I know). You already know one more: Tsis ua li cas, "it's nothing", is built with the same tsis.

With describing words too

Describing words act like verbs, so tsis goes before them as well.

{{ex:hmn.s0059}}

"No, it isn't" and "not yet"

The answer no to a yes-or-no question is often tsis yog low · falling-breathy, "it isn't"; Omniglot also lists a bare tsis for no. tsis tau low · mid before a verb means not yet, the natural answer when an elder asks whether you have eaten.

{{ex:hmn.s0060}}{{ex:hmn.s0061}}

Don't!

To tell someone not to do something, put tsis txhob low · high before the verb. English adds "do"; Hmong does not.

{{ex:hmn.s0062}}{{ex:hmn.s0063}}

A bare "don't" is direct. With older relatives, many speakers soften requests with thov and os; the lesson on requests shows how.

Watch the spelling

These short words have near twins. tsis (not) has no puff of air; tshis low is goat. tsib high, with the high tone, is five. txhob has a puff of air; txob high, without it, is pepper.

Most common mistake: putting tsis after the verb, where English puts "not" after "do". Kuv paub tsis → Kuv tsis paub.
