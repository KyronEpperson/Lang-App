English uses "is" plus a small word for location: the bank is next to the supermarket. Chinese uses one verb, 在 zài (to be at), and puts the location word after the place.

Thing + 在 + place

To say where something or someone is, the thing comes first, then 在, then the place. No 是 is needed. To ask, put 哪儿 nǎr (where) in the place slot.

Location words come after the noun

English puts "on", "in" and "next to" before the noun. Chinese puts them after it: "table on", "room in". These are location words.

EnglishChinese
on the table桌子上
in the room房间里
next to the supermarket超市旁边
across from the supermarket超市对面

An object such as a table or a bag needs its location word to become a place: 在桌子 alone is wrong. Cities and countries never take one, and places such as a bank or a supermarket usually don't either.

{{ex:zh.s0154}}

在 + place before the verb

To say where you do something, English adds "at a bank" after the verb. Chinese puts 在 + place before the verb, just as it does with time words. The order is who, then where, then what they do.

在 or 有?

In the lesson on 有 you met the other order. 在 starts with the thing you are talking about and says where it is. 有 starts with the place and says what is there.

Known thing: where is it?Place: what is there?
银行在超市对面。附近有银行。
The bank is across from the supermarket.There's a bank nearby.
Most common mistake: putting the place after the verb, as in English. 我工作在银行。 → 我在银行工作。
