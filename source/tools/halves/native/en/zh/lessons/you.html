English uses two different phrases for "I have a phone" and "there is a bank nearby". Chinese uses one verb for both: 有 yǒu.

Someone 有 something: have

Person + 有 + thing, in the same order as English. To ask, add 吗 at the end. In a shop, ask the staff with 你们 nǐmen (you all, meaning the shop).

{{ex:zh.s0103}}{{ex:zh.s0104}}

A place 有 something: there is

English says "there is a bank nearby", with the place at the end. Chinese starts with the place and then uses 有: literally "nearby has a bank". There is no word for "there".

{{ex:zh.s0105}}{{ex:zh.s0106}}

Two new pieces in that last one: 桌子上 zhuōzi shàng means "on the table" (the "on" word comes after the noun; more in the lesson on 在), and 一本书 yì běn shū is "a book". The 本 in the middle is a measure word for books; numbers and measure words get their own lessons next.

没有: don't have, there isn't

As you saw in the lesson on 不 and 没, 有 never takes 不. "Don't have" and "there isn't" are both 没有 méiyǒu.

{{ex:zh.s0107}}{{ex:zh.s0108}}

有 or 在?

有 says what there is in a place: the thing is new to the listener. When you already know the thing and want to say where it is, Chinese uses 在 zài, with the thing first. You will practice that later; for now, just notice the two orders.

{{ex:zh.s0109}}
EnglishChinese
I have a phone.我有手机。
There's a bank nearby.附近有银行。
There's no bank nearby.附近没有银行。
The book is on the table.书在桌子上。
Most common mistake: keeping the English "there is … on the table" order, with the place at the end. 有一本书在桌子上。 is heard, but it sounds clumsy → 桌子上有一本书。 Place first, then 有.
