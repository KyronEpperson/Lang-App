English shows an action in progress with "am / is / are" and -ing: "He is watching TV." Chinese has no -ing, and it does not use 是 for "is" here. It puts a small word before the verb instead.

在 + verb

You met 在 zài as "at". Right before a verb it means "in the middle of". This is the everyday spoken form.

{{ex:zh.s0181}}{{ex:zh.s0182}}

The question word stays where the answer goes, as always: 你在做什么？ "I'm watching TV" answers it with the same shape: 我在看电视.

正在, and 呢 at the end

正在 zhèngzài stresses "right at this moment". 呢 ne at the end of the sentence adds the same "right now" feeling and is very common in speech. You can use one, two or all of them: 在…, 正在…, 在…呢, 正在…呢.

{{ex:zh.s0183}}{{ex:zh.s0184}}

Saying where

To say where the action is happening, put 在 + place before the verb, as you learned. That sentence can already mean "is doing", and 呢 at the end makes it clear. You do not need a second 在.

{{ex:zh.s0185}}

Not for plans

English also uses -ing for the future: "I'm going to Beijing tomorrow." That is a plan, not an action in progress, so Chinese uses no 在: just a time word before the verb.

{{ex:zh.s0147}}

And not with 是

Because English says "I am eating", learners reach for 是. But 是 only joins two nouns ("I am a student"). An action needs 在, never 是.

EnglishChinese
I'm a teacher.我是老师。
I'm working.我在工作。
Most common mistake: copying "am + -ing" with 是. 我是吃饭 → 我在吃饭.
