You already ask yes/no questions by adding 吗. Chinese has a second, very common way: say the verb, then 不, then the verb again. "Go not go?" means "Are you going?" Grammar books call it an A-not-A question.

Verb + 不 + verb

{{ex:zh.s0194}}{{ex:zh.s0195}}

The 不 in the middle is said quickly and lightly, with the neutral tone: qù bu qù, shì bu shì.

Adjectives work the same way

Adjectives act like verbs, so they make the same question. Leave out 很: it does not fit inside.

{{ex:zh.s0196}}

有 takes 没

有 is always negated with 没, so its question is 有没有.

{{ex:zh.s0197}}

Two-character verbs

With a verb like 喜欢 xǐhuan, you can say all of it twice (喜欢不喜欢) or shorten the first one to its first character (喜不喜欢). Both are natural.

{{ex:zh.s0198}}

The rest of the sentence

Everything else stays in its usual place. Time and place still go before the verb, and the object comes after the whole verb-not-verb.

{{ex:zh.s0199}}{{ex:zh.s0200}}

Never add 吗

The verb-not-verb already makes the question, so 吗 at the end is one question too many.

Which one to use?

Both forms are everyday Chinese. A question with 吗 is neutral. A verb-not-verb question sounds a little more direct, as if you want a clear yes or no, so it is common when you invite or check: "Do you have time or not?"

{{ex:zh.s0201}}

How to answer

Just as with 吗, answer with the verb itself: 去 or 不去, 有 or 没有, 是 or 不是.

Most common mistake: using both question forms at once. 你有没有中文书吗？ → 你有没有中文书？
