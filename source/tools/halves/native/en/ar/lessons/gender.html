In Arabic every noun is masculine or feminine: not only people, but a car, a school, a door. English does this only with "he" and "she". Arabic does it with "it", with "this", and with every describing word.

The sign: ة

Most feminine nouns end in ة, the taa marbuta: سَيَّارَة sayyāra "car", مَدْرَسَة madrasa "school". Nouns without it are usually masculine: بَيْت, كِتَاب, بَاب. When you meet a new noun, check its last letter first: it tells you which "this", which "it" and which form of every describing word to use. Write ة with its two dots; without them, ه is a different letter, a plain h.

People

Arabic always has to choose a gender for a person, so the course cues it in English: "student (a woman)". Many words for people add ة for a woman: طَالِب ṭālib is a man student, طَالِبَة ṭāliba a woman student; مُدَرِّس mudarris and مُدَرِّسَة mudarrisa work the same way.

Feminine without ة

A few words are feminine because of what they mean, with no ة: أُمّ umm "mother", بِنْت bint "girl", أُخْت ukht "sister". Their partners are masculine: أَب ab "father", وَلَد walad "boy", أَخ akh "brother". For "man" and "woman" Arabic uses two different words: رَجُل rajul and اِمْرَأَة imraʾa.

"It" is he or she

Arabic has no separate "it". A masculine thing is هُوَ huwa, a feminine thing is هِيَ hiya. Talking about a book, "it" is هُوَ; talking about a car, "It's new" is literally "she is new":

Describing words agree

A describing word comes after its noun and agrees with it: it adds ة after a feminine noun. Lesson 17 covers this in detail.

Most common mistake: using the masculine for everything, as English has no gender. هٰذَا سَيَّارَة → هٰذِهِ سَيَّارَة: a feminine noun needs the feminine "this".
