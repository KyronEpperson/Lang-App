Arabic letters write the consonants and the long vowels. The short vowels are small signs, the vowel marks, above or below a letter. Newspapers, signs and books for adults usually leave them out, because readers know the words. Books for beginners put them on everything, and so does this course.

Three short vowels

MarkNameSound
بَfatha: a small slash abovea, between "cat" and "father"
بِkasra: a small slash belowi, as in "sit"
بُdamma: a little و aboveu, as in "put"

Three long vowels

A long vowel is a short mark plus a letter: fatha + ا is ā, kasra + ي is ī, damma + و is ū. Hold a long vowel about twice as long as a short one. The romanisation shows length with a line on top: ā ī ū. A vowel without the line is short. So ي and و are not always the consonants y and w: in كَبِير kabīr "big", the ي is the long ī.

How words end

Arabic words in this course end the way people say them at the end of a sentence. Grammar books add short endings to most words, called case endings; you won't need them, so the last letter usually has no mark: كِتَاب kitāb. A final vowel stays only when it is part of the word, as in هُوَ huwa "he" and هِيَ hiya "she".

In مُدَرِّس mudarris "teacher" the small w-shaped sign on ر doubles the letter; lesson 5 explains it. A woman teacher is مُدَرِّسَة, with ة.

Most common mistake: saying long and short vowels alike. جَمَل "camel" when you mean "beauty" → جَمَال: hold the long ā.
