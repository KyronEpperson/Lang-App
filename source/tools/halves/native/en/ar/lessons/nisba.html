English makes nationality words in many ways: American, Lebanese, Moroccan, French. Arabic has one way. Take the country and add -ī for a man, -iyya for a woman, as in أَمْرِيكِيّ and أَمْرِيكِيَّة. This ending is called the nisba, and it works for countries, cities and many other words.

Country → nisba

The masculine form ends in a doubled ي, said -ī. The feminine form adds ة after it, said -iyya. A final ا of the country name drops: أَمْرِيكَا amrīkā (America) gives أَمْرِيكِيّ amrīkī. A few countries change a little more, so learn each nisba together with its country as you meet it; the table has the ones you will need most.

It agrees, like any adjective

A nisba is an adjective, so it takes the gender of the person: هُوَ مِصْرِيّ huwa miṣrī, هِيَ مِصْرِيَّة hiya miṣriyya. And as in lesson 11, there is no word for "is". Like any adjective it can also follow a noun: طَالِب مِصْرِيّ ṭālib miṣrī is "an Egyptian student". Lesson 17 does more with that.

{{ex:ar.s0093}}

The country's ال drops

Some countries carry the article: الْمَغْرِب al-maghrib (Morocco), الْأُرْدُنّ (Jordan). The nisba drops it. "She's Moroccan" is هِيَ مَغْرِبِيَّة hiya maghribiyya, with no ال, because it is a predicate.

{{ex:ar.s0094}}

From a city: مِن

For a city or a state, say "I'm from…" with مِن min. The place keeps its own name, with no ending and no ال. Arabic writes foreign names the way they sound, so New York is نِيُويُورْك niyūyūrk and Texas is تِكْسَاس tiksās. To ask someone where they are from, use the question from your About me deck.

Languages

A language is the feminine form with the article: الْعَرَبِيَّة al-ʿarabiyya (Arabic), الْفَرَنْسِيَّة al-faransiyya (French). So "I'm studying Arabic" needs the article, exactly as in lesson 12. Without ال, عَرَبِيَّة just means "an Arab woman" or "Arabic" describing something, as in قَهْوَة عَرَبِيَّة qahwa ʿarabiyya (Arabic coffee).

{{ex:ar.s0097}}
Most common mistake: forgetting the woman's ending. A woman from Egypt: أَنَا مِصْرِيّ → أَنَا مِصْرِيَّة. And no ال on the predicate: هُوَ الْمَغْرِبِيّ → هُوَ مَغْرِبِيّ.
