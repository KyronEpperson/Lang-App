English has one "not" and puts it after a helper verb: "I do not know", "she is not busy". Arabic has two words for "not", and which one you use depends on what comes next: a verb, or a sentence with no verb at all. Ask yourself one question: is there a verb to put it in front of?

لَا before a present verb

Put لَا lā straight in front of a present-tense verb. Nothing else changes: there is no "do" to add and no new verb form, just لَا plus the verb you already know. The same word also means "no" as an answer, and نَعَم naʿam means "yes".

لَيْسَ in a sentence with no verb

A sentence with no "is", like هُوَ هُنَا "he is here", has no verb for لَا to stand in front of. Use لَيْسَ laysa, "is not", instead. It agrees with its subject: لَيْسَت laysat for "she" or a feminine noun, لَيْسَ for "he" or a masculine noun.

{{ex:ar.s0178}}{{ex:ar.s0179}}

You already know one more use from the lesson on عِنْدَ: لَيْسَ عِنْدِي, "I don't have".

{{ex:ar.s0145}}

What لَيْسَ does to the word after it

After لَيْسَ, a masculine noun or adjective takes the extra -an ending, written with the doubled mark and an extra ا: أَنَا لَسْتُ طَالِبًا anā lastu ṭāliban "I'm not a student" (a man speaking). لَسْتُ is the "I" form of لَيْسَ. Just recognize this for now, as in the example below. With places, with عِنْدَ, and with feminine words ending in ة, nothing changes, so those are the ones to use yourself.

{{ex:ar.s0180}}

A word you'll hear but not write

In Egypt and the Levant people often say مِش mish for "not" in everyday speech. In the Arabic of this course, use لَا with verbs and لَيْسَ without them.

Most common mistake: putting لَا in front of a noun or adjective, as if it were English "not". أَنَا لَا طَالِب → أَنَا لَسْتُ طَالِبًا.
