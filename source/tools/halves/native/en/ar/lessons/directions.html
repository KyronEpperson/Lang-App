Asking the way puts together almost everything you know: "excuse me" with the right "you", a question word, places with the article, and adjectives that agree. You don't need long sentences. A polite opener, the question word and the place are enough, and most answers are a few short words plus a pointing hand.

Stopping someone

English "excuse me" fits everyone. Arabic changes with the person you speak to: لَوْ سَمَحْتَ law samaḥta to a man, لَوْ سَمَحْتِ law samaḥti to a woman. Only the last vowel differs, so look at the person and choose before you speak. The phrase also works as "please".

Where is it?

Start with أَيْنَ ayna "where", then the place, with no "is". A place you mean, like "the bank" or "the station", takes the article. Many places are an iḍāfa, two nouns with no article on the first: مَحَطَّةْ الْقِطَار maḥaṭṭat al-qiṭār "the train station", literally "station of the train". As in every iḍāfa, the ة of the first word is said -at.

{{ex:ar.s0130}}

The answer

ArabicEnglish
عَلَى الْيَمِينon the right
عَلَى الْيَسَارon the left
عَلَى طُولstraight ahead
هُنَاhere
هُنَاكَover there
قَرِيب مِن هُنَاnear here
بَعِيد عَن هُنَاfar from here

Right and left take the article: عَلَى الْيَمِين ʿalā al-yamīn. Near and far are adjectives, so they agree with the place: a masculine place is قَرِيب, a feminine place ending in ة is قَرِيبَة qarība.

When you don't follow

Ask whether the person speaks English, with the right "you", or say that you don't understand. You may also hear people ask you to repeat with مِن فَضْلِكَ أَعِد, "please repeat"; you only need to recognize it. Asking people to speak slowly or to repeat is perfectly polite, and a smile and شُكْرًا at the end go a long way.

Most common mistake: putting the article on both nouns of a place name, as English "the station" suggests. أَيْنَ الْمَحَطَّة الْقِطَار؟ → أَيْنَ مَحَطَّةْ الْقِطَار؟.
