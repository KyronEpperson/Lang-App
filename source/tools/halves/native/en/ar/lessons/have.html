English has a verb "to have". Arabic has none. To say what someone has, Arabic uses the small word عِنْدَ ʿinda, which means "with" or "at the place of", plus the possessive endings from the last lesson. عِنْدِي ʿindī is literally "with me", so "with me a car" is how Arabic says "I have a car".

One word for each person

The ending says who has the thing, exactly as it does on a noun. Learn these six forms as whole words.

ArabicEnglish
عِنْدِيI have
عِنْدَكَyou have (to a man)
عِنْدَكِyou have (to a woman)
عِنْدَهُhe has
عِنْدَهَاshe has
عِنْدَنَاwe have

What you have is "a", not "the"

The thing you have usually has no article: سَيَّارَة sayyāra alone means "a car". If you add an adjective, it comes after the noun, it agrees with it in gender, and it stays without ال too, because the noun has none: in Arabic the noun and its adjective always match in the article.

{{ex:ar.s0142}}

Asking "Do you have…?"

Put هَل hal in front, as with any yes or no question. Choose the right "you": عِنْدَكَ to a man, عِنْدَكِ to a woman. Only the small mark on the last letter changes, so listen for -ka or -ki.

{{ex:ar.s0143}}{{ex:ar.s0144}}

Saying "I don't have"

English says "I don't have", with a verb to make negative. عِنْدِي is not a verb, so the usual "no" word لَا lā cannot go in front of it. Use لَيْسَ laysa, "is not", instead. Learn the chunk as it is: لَيْسَ عِنْدِي, لَيْسَ عِنْدَهُ, لَيْسَ عِنْدَنَا.

{{ex:ar.s0145}}

"There is", for recognition

English says "there is a room". Arabic often starts with هُنَاكَ hunāka, literally "there", and then the thing, with no verb at all. You only need to recognize it for now.

{{ex:ar.s0146}}
Most common mistake: treating عِنْدِي like a verb and putting لَا in front of it. لَا عِنْدِي سَيَّارَة → لَيْسَ عِنْدِي سَيَّارَة.
