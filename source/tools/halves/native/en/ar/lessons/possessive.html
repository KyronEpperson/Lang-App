This lesson is short on new words and long on one idea. English puts a word in front of the noun: "my house", "her car". Arabic adds an ending to the noun itself: بَيْت bayt (house) becomes بَيْتِي baytī (my house). This is a possessive ending.

The endings

EnglishArabicSaid
my houseبَيْتِيbaytī
your house (to a man)بَيْتُكَbaytuka
your house (to a woman)بَيْتُكِbaytuki
his houseبَيْتُهُbaytuhu
her houseبَيْتُهَاbaytuhā
our houseبَيْتُنَاbaytunā

"Your" to a man and "your" to a woman differ only in the last vowel mark, as in مِن فَضْلِكَ and مِن فَضْلِكِ. "His" and "her" are easier to see: "her" has an extra ا. Look at who you are talking to: a man hears بَيْتُكَ, a woman بَيْتُكِ. It is the same split as أَنْتَ and أَنْتِ, and people notice it. On a phone keyboard you type both the same way, without the marks; the difference is in what you say.

ة turns into ت

Before an ending, a final ة opens into an ordinary ت: مَدِينَة madīna (city) becomes مَدِينَتِي madīnatī (my city), صَدِيقَة ṣadīqa (a woman friend) becomes صَدِيقَتِي ṣadīqatī. The noun stays feminine, so its adjective still takes ة.

{{ex:ar.s0135}}{{ex:ar.s0136}}

Already definite

The ending means "the" as well, so never add the article: بَيْتِي, not الْبَيْتِي. When the adjective is the predicate it has no ال either, so بَيْتِي كَبِير baytī kabīr is "My house is big."

Family words

Your Family and people deck uses "my" all the time: أُمِّي ummī, أُخْتِي ukhtī, زَوْجِي zawjī. Two words are irregular: أَب (father) and أَخ (brother) give أَبِي abī and أَخِي akhī, and they change more with the other endings, so use them with "my" for now.

You already know some

These endings have been in your phrases since the greetings lesson. مِن فَضْلِكَ min faḍlika is literally "from your kindness", كَيْفَ حَالُكِ؟ kayfa ḥāluki? is "how is your state?" (to a woman), and اِسْمِي ismī is simply "my name".

Most common mistake: adding the article as well, or a separate word for "my". الْبَيْتِي → بَيْتِي, and بَيْت أَنَا → بَيْتِي.
