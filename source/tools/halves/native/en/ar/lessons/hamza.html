A few last signs, and you can read every word in this course.

Hamza: the catch in the throat

Hamza ء is the little catch in "uh-oh". It usually sits on a seat. At the start of a word the seat is ا, with hamza above (أ) or below (إ): أَنَا anā "I". Inside a word it can sit on ا, و or a dotless ي: ؤ, ئ. At the start of a word the romanisation writes nothing; inside a word it writes ʾ, as in سُؤَال suʾāl "question". آ, alif with a wavy line, is hamza plus a long ā: آسِف āsif "sorry".

SignWhere you see it
ءalone, on the line
أon alif: at the start of a word it is a or u; it also appears inside a word
إunder alif, at the start of a word: i
ؤon و, inside a word
ئon a dotless ي, inside a word
آhamza plus a long ā

ى: alif maqsura

At the end of some words you'll see ى, which looks like ي without its dots. It is said ā, not ī.

LetterSound
ىā, only at the end of a word: no dots
يī or y: two dots below

One more way to write ā: in هٰذَا hādhā "this", the small upright alif above ه stands for the long ā. You'll see it in only a few common words, such as هٰذَا and هٰذِهِ.

{{ex:ar.s0042}}{{ex:ar.s0043}}

ة: taa marbuta as a letter

ة looks like ه with two dots on top. At the end of a word it is said -a: no t, no h (a later lesson shows when the t comes back). Without the dots, ه is a plain h, a different letter.

LetterSound
ة-a, at the end of a word: two dots on top
هh: no dots
{{ex:ar.s0044}}

Tanwin: the -an ending

Tanwin is a doubled fatha, two small slashes, followed by an extra ا. Together they are said -an.

SpellingSound
رًا-ran: two slashes on the letter, then ا

You meet it in very common words such as شُكْرًا shukran "thanks", جِدًّا jiddan "very" and أَهْلًا ahlan "hi, welcome". Grammar books also show -un and -in endings; like the other case endings, this course leaves them out, so you will only see -an, and only where the spelling has its extra ا.

{{ex:ar.s0045}}{{ex:ar.s0046}}

Notice where جِدًّا goes: after the describing word, the opposite of English "very big".

Most common mistake: reading ى like ي. عَلَى "on" said with a long ī at the end → say a long ā: ى without dots sounds like the ا in بَاب.
