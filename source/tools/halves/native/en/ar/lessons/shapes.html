Inside a word, Arabic letters are joined, the way English handwriting is. A letter changes shape a little depending on whether it starts a word, sits in the middle or ends it. In the tables, the line ـ shows where a letter joins its neighbour.

Six letters that never join forward

ا د ذ ر ز و join to the letter before them, never to the letter after. Each one leaves a small gap, so a word can look like several pieces. سَيَّارَة sayyāra breaks after ا and after ر, but it is still one word.

Look at هٰذِهِ hādhihi "this": the first ه and the last ه hardly look like the same letter. ك at the start is a tall stroke with a flat foot; alone or at the end it has a small mark inside.

لا: lām plus alif

When ل is followed by ا, the two melt into one shape, لا. You'll see it in لَا lā, "no".

Read joined words, letter by letter

Now put it together. Take one word at a time, start on the right, and name each letter and its form: in قَلَم qalam "pen", ق is in its start form, ل in its middle form and م in its end form. Where a word breaks after ا د ذ ر ز و, the next letter starts again. The cards below are real words that use only the letters and vowels you know. Read each one aloud before you flip it; the back shows how it breaks into letters.

The app has no handwriting practice, but copying the letters by hand on paper helps many learners remember them.

Most common mistake: taking a gap for a space. Reading سَيَّارَة as two words because of the gap after ا → it is one word: ا never joins the next letter, so a small gap opens inside the word.
