Arabic has no word for "a": بَيْت bayt is "a house". For "the", Arabic puts ال in front of the word and writes it as part of it: الْبَيْت al-bayt "the house". The romanisation joins it with a hyphen.

Moon letters: say the l

Before about half the letters you hear the l, and it carries sukun: الْقَمَر al-qamar "the moon". These are called moon letters, after this word.

Sun letters: swallow the l

Before the other 14 letters, the l is not said; the next letter is doubled instead: الشَّمْس ash-shams "the sun". These are the sun letters. You can see it in the writing: the ل has no mark, and the sun letter carries a shadda.

Sun letters (l not said)ت ث د ذ ر ز س ش ص ض ط ظ ل ن
Moon letters (l said)ا ب ج ح خ ع غ ف ق ك م ه و ي

A handy test: sun letters are made with the tip of the tongue near the teeth, the same place as l, so the l melts into them.

Little words in front

وَ wa "and" and بِ bi "by, with" are written joined to the next word. Before ال their vowel takes the place of the a: وَالْبَيْت wal-bayt "and the house", بِالسَّيَّارَة bis-sayyāra "by car".

{{ex:ar.s0052}}

The article changes the meaning

English says "the" once, for the whole phrase. Arabic puts the article on the noun and on the describing word after it: الْبَيْت الْكَبِير "the big house". Leave ال off the describing word and you have a full sentence, "The house is big." Arabic needs no "is" there: the two words side by side say it.

From now on, read the Arabic first and check the romanisation after.

Most common mistake: putting ال on one word only. الْبَيْت كَبِير for "the big house" → الْبَيْت الْكَبِير: both words take the article.
