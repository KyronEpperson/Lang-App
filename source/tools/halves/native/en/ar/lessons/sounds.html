Arabic has sounds English doesn't, and they change meaning. You don't need a perfect accent, but you do need to keep them apart. Listen to the audio, then say each pair aloud.

Three sounds from the throat

ح ḥāʾ is a strong, breathy h made low in the throat, as if you were fogging up a glass. Plain English h is a different letter, ه. ع ʿayn is a squeeze deep in the throat with the voice on; English has nothing like it, and the romanisation writes it ʿ. ء hamza is the catch in the middle of "uh-oh", written ʾ. Don't skip ʿ: it is a full letter.

kh and gh

خ khāʾ is the scrape at the end of German "Bach" or Scottish "loch". غ ghayn is made in the same place with the voice on, close to gargling or a French r. Keep خ apart from the smoother ح.

{{ex:ar.s0011}}{{ex:ar.s0012}}

q is not k

ق qāf is a k made much further back, where you swallow. If you say it as English k, قَلْب "heart" turns into كَلْب "dog". The word قَلَم qalam "pen" starts with the same deep q.

{{ex:ar.s0013}}{{ex:ar.s0014}}

The heavy letters

ص ض ط ظ are the heavy versions of s, d, t and dh: the back of the tongue pulls down and back while you say them. The easiest way to hear them is in the vowel next to them, which sounds darker and deeper, a bit like the a in "father" instead of the a in "cat". The romanisation marks them with a dot underneath: ṣ ḍ ṭ ẓ. You already know two heavy words: طَالِب ṭālib "student" and صَغِير ṣaghīr "small", which also has gh.

Long and doubled sounds

Two more differences change a word. A held vowel: جَمَل jamal is "camel", جَمَال jamāl is "beauty". A held consonant: دَرَسَ darasa is "he studied", دَرَّسَ darrasa is "he taught". Lesson 3 shows how long vowels are written and lesson 5 the doubling mark; for now, listen for the length.

The Sound pairs deck drills all of these. Say both words of a pair until you can feel the difference in your throat and tongue.

Most common mistake: swapping in the nearest English sound. Saying قَلْب "heart" with an English k, which gives كَلْب "dog" → start the q further back, where you swallow, and let the vowel after it sound darker.
