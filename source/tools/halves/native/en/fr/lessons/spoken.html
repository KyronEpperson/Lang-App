The French you have learned so far is correct everywhere, and it is what you should write. But French people talking to friends drop and squeeze sounds, just as English speakers say "gonna" and "dunno". This lesson is about recognizing those sentences when you hear them. You don't have to speak this way.

ne disappears

In relaxed everyday speech, the ne of ne … pas is very often dropped. The pas stays, so the sentence is still negative. In writing, keep the ne. This is one of the most common differences between textbook French and the French you hear among friends, so expect it in many of the negative sentences you hear; in careful or formal speech the ne comes back.

on means "we"

In conversation, on replaces nous most of the time. It takes the il form of the verb: on va, on est, never on allons. An adjective after it still agrees with the real people: a group of friends says On est fatigués.

Squeezed words

Some short words lose a sound. tu as often becomes t'as, and il y a often shrinks to y a. You will see them written this way in text messages too. Recognize them, but write the full forms in your own typed answers.

{{ex:fr.s0200}}{{ex:fr.s0201}}

Questions without a question word

English needs "do" or "are" to ask a question. Spoken French usually just raises the voice at the end of a normal sentence. Est-ce que and inversion are correct, but less common between friends.

{{ex:fr.s0202}}

Everyday words

ouais is a relaxed "yeah", ça marche is "sounds good", and d'accord is "okay". Use ouais with friends, not in a job interview.

{{ex:fr.s0203}}

In this course, typed answers always accept the standard written form. The spoken form is accepted only when the prompt says "(spoken)".

Most common mistake: putting the nous ending on on. On allons au cinéma? → On va au cinéma?
