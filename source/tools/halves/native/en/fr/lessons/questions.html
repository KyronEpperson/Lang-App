English builds most questions with "do": Do you speak English? Where do you live? French has no such helper. Leave "do" out completely.

Yes/no questions: three ways

1. Raise your voice at the end. The sentence stays the same; only the tone climbs. This is the most common way in conversation. The answer is oui (yes) or non (no).

{{ex:fr.s0070}}

2. Put est-ce que in front. It means nothing by itself; it just announces "a question is coming". It works with any subject and in any situation. Before a vowel it becomes est-ce qu'.

{{ex:fr.s0071}}{{ex:fr.s0072}}

3. Swap the subject and the verb, joined by a hyphen. This is called inversion. It sounds more formal; learn to recognize it in fixed questions.

{{ex:fr.s0073}}

The pattern is: question word, then est-ce que, then the normal sentence. Among friends you will also hear the question word at the end, with no est-ce que: Tu habites où? Both are correct; this course practices the est-ce que pattern because it works everywhere.

{{ex:fr.s0074}}{{ex:fr.s0075}}

"What" at the start is one block, qu'est-ce que: Qu'est-ce que tu étudies? The short word quoi is used alone (Quoi?) or at the end (Tu étudies quoi?).

Où (where) has an accent. Without it, ou means "or". Type the accent: it changes the word.

‹quel›: which, what

"Which" or "what" before a noun is quel, and it agrees with the noun like an adjective. All four forms sound the same (kehl), so only spelling shows the agreement.
