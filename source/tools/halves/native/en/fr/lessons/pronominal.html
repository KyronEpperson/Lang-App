In English you simply "get up", "shower" and "go to bed". French describes many of these actions as things you do to yourself: "I raise myself", "I put myself to bed". These are reflexive verbs (textbooks also call them pronominal verbs).

The reflexive pronoun

A reflexive verb comes with se in the dictionary: se lever (to get up), se coucher (to go to bed). When you conjugate it, se changes to match the subject, and it goes right before the verb.

SubjectPronounse lever
jemeje me lève
tutetu te lèves
il, elle, onseil se lève
nousnousnous nous levons
vousvousvous vous levez
ils, ellesseils se lèvent

Yes, nous nous and vous vous: the first is the subject, the second the reflexive pronoun.

me, te, se before a vowel

Before a vowel or a silent h, me, te, se become m', t', s'. That's why the verb you already know, s'appeler (to be called), looks the way it does.

{{ex:fr.s0166}}{{ex:fr.s0167}}{{ex:fr.s0168}}

With ne … pas

The pronoun sticks to its verb, so ne … pas wraps around both.

{{ex:fr.s0169}}

After another verb

With aller, vouloir, pouvoir or devoir, the reflexive verb stays in the infinitive, but its pronoun still matches the subject and goes right before it.

{{ex:fr.s0170}}{{ex:fr.s0171}}
Most common mistake: leaving out the pronoun, as in English. Je lève à sept heures. → Je me lève à sept heures. Without me, the sentence means "I lift" something.
