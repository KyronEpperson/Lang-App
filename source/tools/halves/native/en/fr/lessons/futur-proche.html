English talks about plans with "I'm going to": "I'm going to visit Paris." French does exactly the same with aller. This is the futur proche (the "near future"), and it is the everyday way to talk about what's coming.

aller + infinitive

Conjugate aller for the person, then add the second verb in the infinitive. Unlike English, there's no word for "to" in between.

The infinitive can even be aller itself: Je vais aller à Lyon (I'm going to go to Lyon) is perfectly normal.

When

Time words usually go at the start or at the end of the sentence, just as in English. They tell you when; the verbs themselves don't change.

  • demain: tomorrow
  • ce soir: tonight
  • ce week-end: this weekend
  • la semaine prochaine: next week

prochain agrees with its noun, and it comes after it: la semaine prochaine, le mois prochain (next month).

Saying no

ne … pas wraps around the conjugated part, aller, and the infinitive stays outside.

{{ex:fr.s0159}}

Asking about plans

To ask a friend about plans, use Qu'est-ce que tu vas faire?, or just raise your voice at the end of a statement.

Most common mistake: translating the English "to". Je vais à visiter Paris. → Je vais visiter Paris. The infinitive follows aller directly; à is only for places (Je vais à Paris).
