English gives an order by dropping "you": "Turn left." French does the same thing. Take the present tense form for tu, nous or vous, and leave out the subject pronoun.

Three forms

The tu form is for one friend, the vous form for a stranger or for several people, and the nous form means "let's". The form you choose already shows who you are talking to. With two friends, use the vous form too, since vous is the only plural "you".

Directions from a stranger, and signs, use the vous form, so that is the one you will hear most in the street. People also give directions with a plain statement, Vous tournez à gauche, "you turn left": that is fine in speech, but it is not a command form, and signs never use it.

No -s on -er verbs

With a friend, tu regardes loses its -s in a command: Regarde! The same goes for every -er verb, and for aller: tu vas becomes Va! Other verbs keep the -s: Prends, Fais, Viens.

"Let's"

English needs an extra word, "let's". French just uses the nous form with no nous.

{{ex:fr.s0176}}{{ex:fr.s0177}}

Don't

To say "don't", put ne … pas around the verb, as usual.

{{ex:fr.s0178}}

il faut: you have to

Il faut + infinitive says that something is necessary, without naming who. Depending on the situation it means "you have to", "we need to" or "it's necessary to". The il here is not "he": it is the same empty "it" as in il pleut. With ne … pas, it means "you must not".

A bare command can sound abrupt to a stranger. Add s'il vous plaît, or ask with Vous pourriez…?

Most common mistake: keeping the -s of the tu form on an -er verb. Regardes! → Regarde!, and Vas à la gare! → Va à la gare!
