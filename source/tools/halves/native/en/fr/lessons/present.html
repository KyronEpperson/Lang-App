English verbs barely change: I work, she works. French verbs change for every subject. The good news: most French verbs end in -er (parler, to speak), and almost all of them follow the same pattern.

Stem ‹plus› ending

Take off the -er to get the stem (parl-), then add the ending that matches the subject.

SubjectEnding‹parler›
je‹-e›je parle
tu‹-es›tu parles
il, elle, on‹-e›il parle
nous‹-ons›nous parlons
vous‹-ez›vous parlez
ils, elles‹-ent›ils parlent

Three endings you can't hear

-e, -es and -ent are all silent: parle, parles and parlent sound exactly the same. Only -ons and -ez are heard. So in writing, the ending is the test: ils parlent, never ils parle.

{{ex:fr.s0057}}

Verbs that start with a vowel

With aimer (to like, to love), habiter (to live) or étudier (to study), je becomes j', and you hear a z linking nous, vous, ils and elles to the verb.

{{ex:fr.s0058}}{{ex:fr.s0059}}

Two spelling changes

To keep the soft sound before -ons, manger keeps an e and commencer takes a ç: nous mangeons, nous commençons.

{{ex:fr.s0060}}

One tense, three Englishes

French has no "-ing" tense. Je travaille means "I work", "I'm working" and "I do work". Don't add être.

With depuis (since, for), the present also covers "I've been doing", for something that is still going on.

{{ex:fr.s0061}}
Most common mistake: copying "I am working" word for word. Je suis travaille → Je travaille.
