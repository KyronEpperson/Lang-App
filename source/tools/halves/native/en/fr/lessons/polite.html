In a French shop or café, how you ask matters as much as what you ask for. Learn these as fixed phrases; you don't need to know how the verbs are built.

Start with ‹bonjour›

English speakers often go straight to the request: "A baguette, please." In France, skipping bonjour sounds rude, so say it first, every time, and add madame or monsieur to sound warmer. Use vous with all staff.

{{ex:fr.s0117}}

‹je voudrais›: I'd like

Je voudrais is the polite "I'd like", and it is what everyone says at a counter. Je veux means "I want": correct French, but it sounds blunt to a server, like a child asking. In the same way, Donnez-moi… (Give me…) without s'il vous plaît sounds like an order. The article after it still agrees with the noun: un croissant, une baguette, and deux croissants with a plural -s you can't hear.

Can I…? Could you…?

To ask if you may do something, say Est-ce que je pourrais + a verb in the infinitive, or simply Je pourrais…? with a rising voice. To ask someone to do something for you, say Vous pourriez…? The endings follow the person: je pourrais, but vous pourriez.

Ordering a meal

At a table, the server may ask what you'd like. Besides je voudrais, the usual answer is Je vais prendre… (I'll have…). Keep the second verb in the infinitive: prendre, with no à in between.

{{ex:fr.s0124}}

Finishing up

The server may ask Et avec ceci? (Anything else?). Answer C'est tout, merci (That's all, thanks). To pay, ask for l'addition, and add s'il vous plaît to any request: it turns an order into a polite one.

{{ex:fr.s0125}}
Most common mistake: translating "I want" straight from English. Je veux un café. → Je voudrais un café, s'il vous plaît. Both are French, but only the second sounds polite to a server.
