English gets by with "here" and "there". Cebuano splits "there" in two, exactly as it splits "that" into kana and kadto.

Asking where: Asa ang…?

The question word comes first, then ang and the place.

Saying where: naa sa…

naa, which you know as "there is", also means "is at". Put sa before the place. One small word, sa, covers English "at", "in" and "to", so you never have to choose between them. When everyone knows what you are talking about, naa and the place are enough.

Here, there, over there

CebuanoMeansLike
diri, dinhihere, near mekini
dihathere, near youkana
didtoover there, far from us bothkadto

diri and dinhi mean the same; you will hear both. Choose between diha and didto the way you choose between kana and kadto: is the place near the person you are talking to, or away from you both?

{{ex:ceb.s0192}}{{ex:ceb.s0193}}{{ex:ceb.s0194}}

The short pronoun still takes second place: Naa ko diri, "there-is I here".

Which one? Hain

Hain asks "which one?" or "where?" when you expect someone to point. Both asa and hain can ask "where"; only hain also means "which one".

{{ex:ceb.s0195}}
Most common mistake: leaving out sa, because English sometimes has no word there ("I'm going home"). Moadto ko simbahan → Moadto ko sa simbahan.
