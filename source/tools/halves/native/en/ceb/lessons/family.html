Cebuano family words often say less than English ones: one word covers brother and sister, another covers son and daughter. You add a word only when it matters.

Parents and children

amahan and inahan are father and mother; at home most people say papa and mama. anak is your own child, son or daughter, of any age. bata is any child, any young person.

Brothers and sisters

igsoon is a brother or a sister. To say which, add nga lalaki (male) or nga babaye (female) with the linker. Age often matters more than sex: magulang is an older brother or sister, manghod a younger one.

Owners: akong, nako, ni

Everything from the lesson on owners works here: akong igsoon or igsoon nako, and ni before a name, as in asawa ni Pedro. When you tell a guest about "our family", use among (ours, not yours), never atong, which would include the guest.

{{ex:ceb.s0125}}

Family titles as "you"

Cebuanos rarely call older relatives by their first names. They use the title instead, even where English says "you": Kumusta, Lola? The same titles are used for older family friends.

{{ex:ceb.s0126}}

To ask how many brothers and sisters someone has, learn this whole phrase: Pila mo kabuok? Here mo is short for kamo (you all): "how many of you are there?"

{{ex:ceb.s0127}}
Most common mistake: the wrong "our". Talking to a guest, atong pamilya → among pamilya. The guest is not part of your family, so use the "we, not you" word.
