At a market or a sari-sari store, many things have no price tag, so you ask. English asks "How much is it?"; Cebuano has two question words for it.

Pila and tagpila

Pila? is "how much?" or "how many?". Tagpila? adds tag-, "each, per": it asks the price of one piece or one kilo. ni is the short kini (this) from the lesson on this and that.

The answer: the price first

English says "a kilo is forty pesos". Cebuano puts the price first, in Spanish numbers, and then the thing with ang: "forty pesos the kilo".

{{ex:ceb.s0113}}

To ask about the vendor's goods, put imong (your) before the noun, and add a title: Nang for an older woman, Nong for an older man.

{{ex:ceb.s0114}}

Expensive, cheap, a discount

mahal is expensive and barato is cheap. At a market, not in a mall or a supermarket, you can ask for a small discount: Pwede pahangyo? Ask with a smile; a "no" is normal too.

{{ex:ceb.s0115}}

When the price is good, say Barato ra ni, "this is only cheap". ra is the small word for "only, just" from the lesson on small words.

{{ex:ceb.s0116}}{{ex:ceb.s0117}}

Paying

The money you get back is your sukli (change). In a jeepney, the word you say as you pass your fare forward is Bayad. Small prices may also come in centavos, sentimos, again with Spanish numbers.

Most common mistake: Cebuano numbers for prices. tulo pesos → tres pesos. People will understand you, but prices are said in Spanish numbers.
