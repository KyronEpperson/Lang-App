You now know two kinds of verbs: doer-focus (mopalit) and thing-focus (paliton). The third kind puts the focus on the person or place the action is done to or for: the plate you wash, the room you clean, the friend you help. This is a place-focus verb.

The forms

The root takes -an for the future form, gi- and -an together for the past form, and -i for a command.

RootFuturePastCommand
hugas (wash)hugasangihugasanhugasi
tabang (help)tabangangitabangantabangi
sulat (write)sulatangisulatansulati

Who gets ang

The person or place is the focus: ang before a noun, si before a name. The doer, as with thing-focus verbs, takes the nako-set.

If the sentence also mentions a thing, like something you give, it takes og.

{{ex:ceb.s0216}}

Asking with -i

The -i form, with palihug, is how you ask someone to do something to a person or place. You already know one: Tagai ko og tubig is this form of hatag (give), shortened. In Tabangi ko, ko means "me": I am the one being helped.

{{ex:ceb.s0217}}{{ex:ceb.s0218}}

-on or -an?

Very roughly: if the thing itself is taken, eaten or bought, use -on. If something is done to a surface, a place or a person (washing, cleaning, helping, writing to), use -an. Each root has its usual ending, so learn the ending together with the root, as Cebuanos do.

Most common mistake: using the thing-focus ending for a person. Tabangon nako si Ana → Tabangan nako si Ana (I'll help Ana).
