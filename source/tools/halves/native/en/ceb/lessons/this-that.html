English has two pointing words, "this" and "that". Cebuano has three, and the choice depends on where the thing is: near you, near the person you are talking to, or away from you both. Think of a market stall: the mango in your hand, the one the vendor is holding, and the basket at the back.

What's this?

The question word comes first: Unsa kini?, "What is this?". At a market stall that is all you need: point and ask. The answer will usually start with the name of the thing.

{{ex:ceb.s0031}}{{ex:ceb.s0032}}

Short forms

After another word, people often shorten the three to ni, na and to. You will hear Unsa ni? at least as often as Unsa kini?. Learn the full forms first; just recognize the short ones.

{{ex:ceb.s0033}}

This is…: the main word first

English says "This is a book". Cebuano has no word for "is", and it puts the main word first: Libro kini, literally "book this".

{{ex:ceb.s0034}}{{ex:ceb.s0035}}

Just this one

Add lang from the last lesson: Kini lang, "Just this one". It is what you say once you have chosen at a stall.

{{ex:ceb.s0036}}

This is my…

To show something of yours, put kini first and ang before the thing: Kini ang akong balay, "This is my house". Here kini is the main word, and ang akong balay is what the sentence is about. akong stays before the noun.

{{ex:ceb.s0037}}

this house

Right before a noun, the pointing word takes -ng, just like akong: kining balay, "this house". For now you mostly need the words on their own.

{{ex:ceb.s0038}}
Most common mistake: one word for every "that". For a thing in the vendor's hand, Unsa kadto? → Unsa kana? Keep kadto for things far from you both.
