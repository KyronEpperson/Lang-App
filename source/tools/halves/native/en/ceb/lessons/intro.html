Meeting people in Cebu, you will be asked the same few things: your name, where you are from, and often your age. This lesson gives you all of them, in the order people usually ask. Questions about age are common and not meant to be rude, so it helps to have the answer ready.

My name is…

English says "I'm Emma". Cebuano says Ako si Emma: the full form ako, then the name with its marker si. The short ko cannot start a sentence, so here it has to be ako.

{{ex:ceb.s0045}}

To ask a name: Unsa ang imong ngalan?, "What is your name?" In speech, unsa ang shrinks to unsay. imong means "your"; like akong, it goes before the noun and ends in -ng.

{{ex:ceb.s0046}}

Where are you from?

Taga- means "from", and it joins the place with a hyphen: Taga-Amerika ko, "I'm from the US". To ask, join it to asa (where). The place comes first and the short pronoun second.

{{ex:ceb.s0047}}{{ex:ceb.s0048}}

Do not translate "Where are you from?" word for word: Asa ka gikan? means "Where have you just been?"

Where I live

Learn this sentence whole for now; its verb comes in a later lesson: Nagpuyo ko sa Cebu, "I live in Cebu". Swap in your own town after sa.

{{ex:ceb.s0049}}

Age

English "is" thirty; some languages "have" thirty years. Cebuano gives the age in Spanish numbers with anyos (years), main word first: Traynta anyos ko. The numbers come later; for now, learn this one pattern.

{{ex:ceb.s0050}}

Nice to meet you

Also learned whole: Nalipay ko nga nakaila nimo, "I'm glad to have met you". A smile and Salamat work too.

{{ex:ceb.s0051}}
Most common mistake: copying English "Where are you from?". Asa ka gikan? → Taga-asa ka?
