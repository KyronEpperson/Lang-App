English "we" hides a question: does it include the person you are talking to? Cebuano always answers it, with two different words.

we (not you)kamimi

kami or kita

Telling a Cebuano "We're from the US" about you and your family? That is kami: the listener is left out. kita includes the listener: you and me, or all of us here. The choice matters most when you make plans. Talking to friends about a trip you will all take, say kita; telling them about a trip your family took without them, say kami.

Short forms in second place

mi, ta and mo are the short forms of kami, kita and kamo. Like ko and ka, they never start a sentence; they go right after the main word or question word.

{{ex:ceb.s0063}}

You all, and they

English uses "you" for one person or for many. Cebuano uses ikaw / ka for one person and kamo / mo for two or more. sila is "they", for people. Asking a whole family at the table with ka sounds as if you are talking to one person only, so switch to kamo or mo.

{{ex:ceb.s0064}}

More than one: mga

Cebuano nouns never add -s. To show more than one, put mga (said "manga") in front: ang mga bata, "the children".

{{ex:ceb.s0065}}{{ex:ceb.s0066}}

When a number or the context already shows more than one, mga is often not needed, so do not be surprised to hear Amerikano sila with no mga.

Most common mistake: kita for "my family and I" when you are talking to someone outside the family. Taga-Amerika kita. → Taga-Amerika mi.
