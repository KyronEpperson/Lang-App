English says "I am hungry": the person first, then "am", then the feeling. Cebuano has no word for "am", and it turns the order around: the feeling comes first, then the person. Gigutom ko is literally "hungry I".

The feeling first, then ko

The short pronoun ko (I) goes in second place, right after the feeling word. For "you", use ka; for "he" or "she", siya; for a name, si and the name. To ask, keep the same order and let your voice rise.

CebuanoEnglish

Nalipay ko.I'm happy, I'm glad.

At a family meal you will be offered food again and again; Busog na ko, with a smile and a salamat, is the polite way out.

Very and really: kaayo, gyud

kaayo means "very". English puts "very" before the feeling; Cebuano puts kaayo after it. You will also hear gyud (really) in the same spot.

{{ex:ceb.s0096}}

The weather: no person at all

For the weather, English needs "it is". Cebuano needs nothing: just the word, usually with kaayo. tugnaw is the cold you feel; init is hot.

What hurts: masakit

For pain, Cebuano says "hurts my head": masakit first, then the body part with its owner word. As you learned with akong balay, akong goes before the noun.

{{ex:ceb.s0099}}

A cold is something you have, so it uses naa from the last lesson: Naa koy sip-on.
