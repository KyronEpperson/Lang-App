English moves words around to make a question: "You are…" becomes "Are you…?", and "do" appears from nowhere. Cebuano keeps it simple. Put the question word first and any short word, like ka, right after it. You will meet all eight words below again and again; learn unsa, kinsa and asa first.

The question word comes first

Asa ka? is literally "Where you?". People say it in passing as a greeting, the way English says "Where are you off to?", and a vague answer is fine.

Short answers

You do not need a full sentence to answer. Asked Kinsa siya?, you can say just the name with its marker: Si Pedro. English does the same with "Pedro", but Cebuano keeps the si.

{{ex:ceb.s0056}}

unsa ang and unsay

Before ang, unsa often joins it as unsay. Both are correct; the joined form is more casual.

{{ex:ceb.s0057}}

Yes-or-no questions: ba

English turns "You are American" into "Are you American?". Cebuano keeps the sentence and adds the small word ba. Answer Oo (yes) or Dili (no). Dili is Cebuano; hindi is Tagalog.

{{ex:ceb.s0058}}{{ex:ceb.s0059}}

When, and how much

Kanus-a asks "when"; the hyphen marks a catch. Pila asks "how many" or "how much", and you will use it constantly when shopping. On its own, Kanus-a? is a natural reply when someone mentions a party, a trip or a visit and you want to know the day.

{{ex:ceb.s0060}}
Most common mistake: English order, with the short pronoun first. Ka asa? → Asa ka?
