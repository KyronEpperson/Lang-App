English has one way to say "my house". Cebuano has two, and both are everyday: an owner word before the noun, or a different owner word after it. You will hear both in the same conversation, often from the same person, so it pays to know both sets from the start. Each owner word matches one of the pronouns you already know.

Before the noun: akong, imong…

You already know akong (my) and imong (your). The whole set ends in -ng and goes before the noun.

After the noun: nako, nimo…

The second set goes after the noun, with no -ng.

The meaning is the same; only the position changes. What you cannot do is mix the two sets: ako balay and nako balay are both wrong. The our words split like kami and kita: among leaves the listener out, atong includes them. Which set should you use? Both are heard all day, so learn to recognize both; when you speak, use whichever comes to you first. Nobody will mind.

Owners with names: ni

English adds 's: "Pedro's house". Cebuano turns it round: the thing first, then ni and the name, a little like "the house of Pedro". The thing usually keeps its ang.

{{ex:ceb.s0070}}

Owners that are not names: sa

For an owner that is a thing, a place or a common noun, use sa instead, like English "of the".

{{ex:ceb.s0071}}

So owners split the same way as si and ang: names take ni, other nouns take sa.

{{ex:ceb.s0072}}
Most common mistake: sa before a name. ang balay sa Pedro → ang balay ni Pedro.
