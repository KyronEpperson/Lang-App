So far your verbs have been doer-focus: Mopalit ko og saging, "I'll buy some bananas". The sentence is about you, so you get the ako-set. Cebuano has a second kind of verb for when the sentence is about the thing: a thing-focus verb.

Two new forms

The root takes -on for the future form and gi- for the past form.

RootDoer-focusThing-focus
palit (buy)mopalit, nipalitpaliton, gipalit
kaon (eat)mokaon, nikaonkaonon, gikaon
kuha (take)mokuha, nikuhakuhaon, gikuha

Who gets ang

With a thing-focus verb, the thing is the focus and gets ang. The doer switches to the nako-set: nako (or short ko), nimo (or mo), niya and the rest.

{{ex:ceb.s0205}}{{ex:ceb.s0206}}{{ex:ceb.s0207}}

A doer with a name takes ni, and a doer that is a plain noun takes sa, the same words you use for owners.

{{ex:ceb.s0208}}{{ex:ceb.s0209}}

Put the doer right after the verb, and the ang thing after it.

Which verb do I choose?

Ask yourself whether English says "a / some" or "the / this / my". Any old thing, with og, goes with a doer-focus verb. A particular thing, with ang, usually goes with a thing-focus verb.

Not "was bought"

Textbooks sometimes call these verbs "passive". Don't translate them that way. Gipalit nako ang saging is the normal, everyday way to say "I bought the bananas", not a stiff "the bananas were bought by me". Keep your English active.

Most common mistake: keeping the doer in the ako-set. Gipalit ako ang saging → Gipalit nako ang saging.
