English softens a request with "please", "could you" and "just a second". Cebuano has its own tools: palihug (please), a short command form, and small softening words. A bare command with none of these sounds rough, even to a friend.

The command form: just the root

To ask someone to do something, use the bare root of the verb, with no mo- and no "you". Put palihug in front. Some verbs that act on a person or a thing take an ending instead, as in Palihug tabangi ko, "Please help me"; a later lesson explains it.

Softeners: lang and sa

lang means "just". After a command it makes it gentle, like English "just a moment". sa here is short for usa, "first, for a bit". This is not the sa that means "at" or "to": it comes right after the first word.

{{ex:ceb.s0174}}{{ex:ceb.s0175}}

Like the other small words, these go in second place: Hulat lang, never Lang hulat. You will also hear unta, "hopefully", softening a wish.

Asking for things

For "Can I have…?", the simplest way is Palihug ko og…, literally "please me some…". Another is Tagai ko og…, "give me some…". Here ko means "me".

{{ex:ceb.s0176}}{{ex:ceb.s0177}}

May I…?

Pwede (from Spanish) means "possible, allowed". Pwede ko…? is "May I…?", and Pwede ba? on its own is "Is that OK?"

{{ex:ceb.s0178}}{{ex:ceb.s0179}}

Don't: ayaw

You met ayaw in the last lesson. The most useful phrase with it is a kind one.

{{ex:ceb.s0180}}

No po

Tagalog puts po in polite sentences. Cebuano does not use it at all. Respect comes from palihug, salamat and a title such as Nong or Nang.

Most common mistake: a bare command, or Tagalog politeness. Hulat po. → Hulat lang, Nong. or Palihug hulat.
