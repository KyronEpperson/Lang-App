English changes the describing word itself: big, bigger, biggest. Cebuano never does. It adds a small word in front, and the describing word stays exactly as it is.

More: mas

mas (from Spanish) means "more". Put it right before the describing word. The sentence order does not change: the describing word still comes first, and the thing you are describing comes after it.

{{ex:ceb.s0219}}{{ex:ceb.s0220}}{{ex:ceb.s0221}}

Than: kaysa

For "than", add kaysa. Before a noun it is kaysa sa. For "than this one" and "than that one", learn the special forms kaysa niini and kaysa niana.

{{ex:ceb.s0222}}{{ex:ceb.s0223}}

At the market this is how you compare two piles of mangoes: point, and say Mas barato ni?, "Is this one cheaper?" The seller may answer with the same pattern, or simply point to the better pile and say which one is mas lami, "tastier".

Most: pinaka-

For "the most" or "-est", join pinaka- to the front of the describing word, as one word.

{{ex:ceb.s0224}}{{ex:ceb.s0225}}

In careful speech and writing you will also see labing: labing maayo means "the best". In everyday Cebu talk, pinaka- is what you will hear most.

The same: parehas

When there is no difference, say parehas, often with ra, "just".

{{ex:ceb.s0226}}

Owners still follow their rules

Comparing does not change anything else: akong still goes before the noun, names still take si.

{{ex:ceb.s0227}}
Most common mistake: leaving out mas, or trying to add an ending as English does with "-er". Dako kini kaysa niana → Mas dako kini kaysa niana.
