A carinderia (karinderya) is a small eatery where the day's dishes sit in pots on the counter. You look, point and order. Everything in this lesson uses words you already know: numbers with ka, naa, palihug and the short pronouns.

Ordering: usa ka…

English says "one soup". Cebuano joins the number to the food with ka, just as in duha ka saging. Use the Cebuano numbers here, not the Spanish ones you use for prices.

{{ex:ceb.s0181}}{{ex:ceb.s0182}}

Rice is the center of every meal. kan-on is cooked rice; the hyphen marks a catch. Uncooked rice in the market is bugas. Whatever you eat with your rice (meat, fish, vegetables) is the sud-an. If you don't know a dish's name, point at the pot and say Kini, palihug: "This one, please".

Asking what they have

Two useful questions. Unsa inyong naa? is "What do you have?"; learn it as a whole. For one dish, use naa with ba and moy (you all + og), as you did with water.

It's good!

Lami means "tasty". Like every describing word, it comes first. Add kaayo after it for "really".

{{ex:ceb.s0185}}{{ex:ceb.s0186}}

When your host offers more and you cannot eat another bite, say you are full. na means "already".

Paying

Ask for the total with Pila tanan? The answer comes in Spanish numbers, as all prices do: singkuwenta pesos, siyen. Then say Salamat on your way out.

{{ex:ceb.s0112}}
Most common mistake: ordering without ka. Usa sabaw → Usa ka sabaw.
