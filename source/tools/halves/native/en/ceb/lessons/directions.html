Getting around Cebu means jeepneys (dyip), taxis, and for short or rough roads the habal-habal, a motorbike taxi. A handful of words covers almost every ride.

Left, right, straight

liko is "turn". Add sa and the side, just as you add sa before a place.

Yes, "left" is spelled wala, like the wala that means "none". They are two different words, and dictionaries mark them differently, so they may not sound alike. The pronunciation line under every wala in this course shows the "none" word, so it may not fit "left": ask a Cebuano friend to say both for you.

On the jeepney

Jeepneys follow a fixed route, painted on the side, but they have no fixed stops. Before you climb in, check the route.

Getting off: speak up

The driver stops only when someone asks. Say Para! clearly, or Lugar lang, "just here". English speakers often wait quietly for a stop that never comes.

Taxis and habal-habal

For a taxi or a habal-habal, name the place with sa and add a title. The short pronoun goes in second place, as always, when you say where you are going.

{{ex:ceb.s0203}}{{ex:ceb.s0204}}

Nong suits most drivers; for a young driver you will hear Kuya and Dong too.

Most common mistake: staying silent and hoping the jeepney stops. Waiting with no word → Para, Nong! And for directions, Liko tuo → Liko sa tuo.
