English puts a describing word straight in front of the noun: "a big house". Cebuano needs a small joining word between them, the linker nga. You have already used it without knowing: it is the -ng at the end of maayong and akong. In this lesson you learn to use it with any describing word.

The linker: nga and -ng

Put nga between the describing word and the noun. When the first word ends in a vowel, nga shrinks to -ng and joins it, just as in maayong buntag and akong balay.

Either order

The describing word can also come after the noun, still with nga: balay nga dako. Both orders mean "a big house". When the first word ends in -n, keep nga as a separate word.

{{ex:ceb.s0075}}{{ex:ceb.s0076}}

A phrase or a sentence?

This is the key difference. With the linker you have a phrase, a name for a thing: ang dakong balay, "the big house". Without the linker, with the describing word first and ang before the noun, you have a full sentence: Dako ang balay, "The house is big". The main word comes first, as in Maestro si Pedro. A quick test: if you want to say something about the thing, you need the sentence pattern; if you only want to name it, use the linker.

{{ex:ceb.s0077}}{{ex:ceb.s0078}}

Very: kaayo

English puts "very" before the describing word. Cebuano puts kaayo after it: lami kaayo, "very tasty".
