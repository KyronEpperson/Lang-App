English tells the time with its own numbers. Cebuano uses Spanish numbers on the clock, with alas in front of the hour.

The hours: alas + a Spanish number

From two to twelve, say alas and the Spanish number. One o'clock is different: ala una. For half past, add y medya (and a half).

In Cebu City you will also hear English times, such as "at three", in offices and malls. This course uses the Spanish hours, so that one answer is right.

To ask the time, learn the whole phrase Unsa nang orasa? The answer often adds na (already, now).

Morning, afternoon, evening

Cebuano has no a.m. and p.m. Add sa buntag (in the morning), sa hapon (in the afternoon) or sa gabii (in the evening): the same words as in the greetings.

{{ex:ceb.s0156}}

At what time

Put the time after the verb and its short pronoun. You will also hear sa before the time: sa alas tres.

{{ex:ceb.s0157}}{{ex:ceb.s0158}}

The days

The days are Spanish too, written with a capital: Lunes, Martes, Miyerkoles, Huwebes, Biyernes, Sabado, Domingo. "On Monday" is sa Lunes. Sunday, Domingo, is the day for church and a long family lunch.
