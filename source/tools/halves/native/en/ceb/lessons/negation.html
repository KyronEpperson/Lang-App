English has one "not". Cebuano has two, and choosing between them is one of the hardest habits for English speakers. The question to ask is: has it happened, or is there any?

UseForExample
diliwon't, don't (habits), is notDili ko moadto.
waladidn't; there is none, don't haveWala ko moadto.

dili: not yet happened, or "is not"

Use dili for something that will not happen and for habits. The verb keeps its mo- or mag- form.

{{ex:ceb.s0145}}{{ex:ceb.s0146}}

dili is also the "not" of sentences with no verb: what someone is, or what something is like.

{{ex:ceb.s0147}}{{ex:ceb.s0148}}

wala: did not happen, or none

Use wala for something that did not happen. Surprise: after wala, this course puts the verb back in the mo- form, because wala already says "past". You may also hear the ni- form after wala in quick speech; the mo- form is the one to learn.

{{ex:ceb.s0149}}

And, as you learned with naa, wala means "there is no" and "don't have".

{{ex:ceb.s0087}}

The short pronoun moves up

Both words pull the short pronoun forward. It goes in second place, right after dili or wala, before the verb or describing word. ganahan means "like".

{{ex:ceb.s0150}}

Saying no

The same choice works as a one-word answer. To "Will you go?" or "Is it…?", answer Dili. To "Did you go?" or "Is there…?", answer Wala.

In fast speech you will hear di for dili and wa for wala, often joined to the pronoun: Di ko, Wa ko. They are fine to say, but this course writes the full forms in answers, so type dili and wala.

Most common mistake: dili for the past. Dili ko moadto gahapon. → Wala ko moadto gahapon. If it did not happen, the word is wala.
