English says "I am a teacher": the person, then "am", then the main word. Cebuano has no word for "am, is, are", and it turns the order round: Maestro ko, literally "teacher I".

siya means both "he" and "she": Cebuano does not show which. Context tells you, so a sentence about your sister and one about your brother look exactly the same.

The main word comes first

Start with what you are saying about the person (a job, a nationality, a describing word), then say who.

{{ex:ceb.s0039}}{{ex:ceb.s0040}}{{ex:ceb.s0041}}

Words for jobs and nationalities from Spanish change for a woman: maestro for a man, maestra for a woman; Amerikano, Amerikana.

Short or full?

After the main word you can use either form: Maestro ko and Maestro ako both mean "I'm a teacher"; the short one is more common. But ko and ka can never start a sentence. To start with "I" or "you", use the full form, as you will see next lesson in Ako si Emma.

si before names, ang before other nouns

The person or thing the sentence is about needs a small marker in front. Names take si. Other nouns take ang, which is close to "the". English has nothing like si, so it is easy to forget.

{{ex:ceb.s0042}}{{ex:ceb.s0043}}{{ex:ceb.s0044}}

The word with si or ang is the focus of the sentence, and it comes after the main word, just like ko, ka and siya.

Most common mistake: English order with a name and no marker. Ana maestra. → Maestra si Ana.
