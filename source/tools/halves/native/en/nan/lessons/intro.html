This lesson gives you the sentences for meeting someone: your name, your surname, what you do and where you live. They are built from words you know from lesson 9: 我, 你 and 是. Learn them as whole sentences first; the grammar notes explain why each word sits where it does, so that you can swap in your own name and city.

Your name

English asks "What's your name?" with the question word first. Taigi leaves the question word where the answer will go: 你號做啥物名？ Lí hō-tsò siánn-mih miâ? is literally "you are-called what name?". 號做 hō-tsò means "to be called" and 啥物 siánn-mih means "what". To answer, put your name where 啥物名 was.

Your surname

The polite way to ask for a surname is 貴姓？ Kuì-sènn?, literally "precious surname?". Answer with 姓 sènn, "to have the surname", and the surname itself. 姓 is the verb here, so there is no 是. Surnames have their own readings: 林 Lîm, 陳 Tân, 王 Ông, 李 Lí.

What you are

As in lesson 9, 是 joins two nouns: 學生 ha̍k-sing student, 老師 lāu-su teacher. English needs "a" ("I'm a student"); Taigi needs nothing between 是 and the noun.

Where you live, and where you study

蹛 tuà means "to live (somewhere)". The place follows it, with 佇 tī "at, in": 蹛佇臺北.

{{ex:nan.s0077}}

With action verbs such as 讀冊 tha̍k-tsheh "study" and 食飯 tsia̍h-pn̄g "eat", it is the other way round: 佇 and the place come before the verb. English says "I study in Taipei", with the place at the end; Taigi says "I in Taipei study".

{{ex:nan.s0078}}

Nice to meet you

Finish with the set phrase 請你多多指教。 Tshiánn lí to-to tsí-kàu., literally "please teach me a lot". It is what people say when they are introduced.

{{ex:nan.s0043}}
Most common mistake: writing the Mandarin word for "at". 我在臺北讀冊 → 我佇臺北讀冊. And put 佇 + place before an action verb, the order of the MOE's examples (我欲佇臺北車頭落車); verbs of staying such as 蹛 can take it after.
