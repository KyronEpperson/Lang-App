English has one "not" and puts it anywhere. Taigi has four everyday negatives, plus a fifth for "no need", and each one carries its own meaning. You have met them one by one; here they are together.

WordMeaningExample
毋isn't; won't (refuse)毋是
無doesn't have; didn't無食
袂can't; won't happen袂曉
莫don't! (telling someone)莫買
免no need免來

毋: isn't, won't

毋 m̄ negates 是 and also says you refuse: 毋是, isn't. You also know it from 毋知, I don't know. Because it can sound like "I won't", it is the wrong choice for things that simply did not happen.

{{ex:nan.s0178}}

無: doesn't have, didn't

無 bô is the negative of 有, so it covers "don't have" and "didn't do". English "I didn't eat" uses "not"; Taigi uses 無.

{{ex:nan.s0109}}

For "don't want", Taigi says 無愛 bô ài. 毋 there would sound like a flat refusal.

{{ex:nan.s0179}}

袂: can't, won't happen

袂 bē is the negative of the 會 words from the last lesson: 袂 (won't happen), 袂曉 (don't know how), 袂使 (not allowed), 袂當 (not able). Whenever the positive has 會, the negative has 袂.

{{ex:nan.s0180}}

莫: don't!

莫 mài tells someone not to do something. It is the word for warnings and advice, and it often comes with 啦 at the end to soften or urge, as in 莫買啦.

{{ex:nan.s0181}}

免: no need

免 bián means it isn't necessary, and 毋免 m̄-bián says the same thing. On its own, 免啦！ is the MOE's example for "no need!" (不用啦).

{{ex:nan.s0182}}{{ex:nan.s0183}}

Where the negative goes

The negative sits right before the verb, after any time word: 我明仔載無愛去, I don't want to go tomorrow. Classifiers and 兩 stay as they are: 我無買兩本冊.

A quick test before you speak: is it about being something (毋), having or doing (無), being able (袂), telling someone (莫) or necessity (免)? Answer that, and the word picks itself.

Most common mistake: using 毋 for everything, like English "not". 我毋食早起 → 我無食早起 for "I didn't eat breakfast".
