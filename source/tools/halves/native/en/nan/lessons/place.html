English says where things are with "is" and a preposition: "The cat is under the chair." Taigi uses one verb, 佇 tī, "be at", and puts the place word after the noun it belongs to.

Thing + 佇 + place

佇 alone means "is at, is in". No 是 is needed, and 佇 does not change for the person: 我佇, 你佇, 伊佇. To ask where someone or something is, put the question word 佗位 after 佇, where the place would be.

Here and there

遮 tsia is "here" and 遐 hia is "there". They can follow 佇 like any place, and they can also stand before the verb as the place of an action. Before an adjective the same characters mean "so", as in 遮貴 from lesson 16, with a different reading.

{{ex:nan.s0131}}

Place words come after the noun

English puts "under", "on" and "in front of" before the noun. Taigi puts the place word after it: "chair under". A place word can also follow 佇 on its own: 伊佇頭前 is "he's in front".

Where you do something: 佇 + place before the verb

For actions such as eating and studying, English puts the place at the end: "He's sleeping at home now." Taigi puts 佇 + place before the verb, after the time word.

{{ex:nan.s0049}}{{ex:nan.s0135}}

A few verbs of staying, such as 蹛 "live" and 睏 "sleep", can also take 佇 + place after them, as in 蹛佇臺北 and 貓睏佇椅仔下跤. For eating, studying, buying and most other actions, the MOE's example sentences put the place first (佇菜市仔賣物件, 佇臺北車頭落車), and so does this course.

Putting things somewhere

囥 khǹg means "put, keep". The place word follows it directly, as in this sentence about shelves: 下跤格 is the bottom shelf, 頂懸格 the top shelf.

{{ex:nan.s0136}}
Most common mistake: the Mandarin 在 for "at". 我在厝裡食飯 → 我佇厝裡食飯, with 佇 + place before the action, as in the MOE's 伊這馬佇厝裡咧睏.
