A few phrases keep a conversation going when you are lost. Learn them early and use them often.

Teacher and student

A teacher is 老師 lāu-su, and a student is 學生 ha̍k-sing. English says "Ms. Lin" or "Teacher Lin" with the title first. Taigi puts the surname first: 林老師. Surnames have their own readings: 林 Lîm, 王 Ông, 陳 Tân.

{{ex:nan.s0055}}

Once more, please

請閣講一擺: 請 asks politely, 閣 is "again", 講 is "say", and 一擺 is "one time". 擺 is the classifier for times, so "twice" is 兩擺, with 兩 as always before a classifier. The shorter 閣講一擺 means the same without the polite 請.

{{ex:nan.s0056}}{{ex:nan.s0057}}

請 is not an all-purpose "please". It invites, treats and asks politely, and it comes at the start of the request. You already met it in 請你多多指教. Do not tack it on at the end the way English adds "please".

Slowly

English puts "slowly" after the verb: "speak slowly". Taigi puts it before the verb: 慢慢仔講, "slowly speak".

{{ex:nan.s0058}}{{ex:nan.s0059}}

I don't know

我毋知 and 我毋知影 both mean "I don't know". The not is 毋, the one you met in lesson 7.

{{ex:nan.s0045}}{{ex:nan.s0060}}

Where you study

As with any place, 佇 + place goes before the verb, the same order as 我佇厝裡食飯 in lesson 7.

{{ex:nan.s0061}}

Putting it together

When the teacher asks you something and you are lost, you now have three honest answers. If you missed it, ask for it again with 請閣講一擺. If it was too fast, ask for 慢慢仔講. If you heard it but have no idea, say 我毋知. Each one keeps the conversation in Taigi instead of switching to English, which is the whole point of a class.

Most common mistake: adding 請 at the end like English "please". 慢慢仔講請 → 請慢慢仔講, with 請 first.
