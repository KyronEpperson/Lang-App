This lesson gives you your first full sentences: "I'm a student", "she isn't a teacher", and the pronouns to say them about anyone.

是 joins two nouns

是 sī works like English "am, is, are" between two nouns. Taigi verbs never change: 是 is the same for I, you, she and they, past or present. No word for "a" is needed either: 我是學生 is literally "I am student".

毋是: is not

The negative is 毋是 m̄ sī, written as two words in Tâi-lô. To ask "isn't it?", put 是毋是 in place of 是.

{{ex:nan.s0064}}{{ex:nan.s0065}}

No 是 before an adjective

English says "she is pretty". Taigi drops 是 before an adjective and uses a word like 真 (very) instead.

The pronouns

EnglishTaigi
I我 guá
you (one person)你 lí
he, she伊 i
we, including you咱 lán
we, not including you阮 guán
you all恁 lín
they𪜶 in

伊 is both "he" and "she", so the English meaning depends on who you are talking about. There is no polite "you": 你 works for everyone, from a child to a grandparent.

𪜶 does double duty: besides "they", it also means "their", and it can mean "his" or "her", as in 𪜶某 (his wife) above.

Two words for we

English has one "we". Taigi asks: is the listener included? 咱 means you and me; 阮 means my group, not you. Inviting someone along takes 咱. Talking about your own family or class to an outsider takes 阮, because the listener is not part of the group.

Classifiers with people

Counting or pointing at people takes 个 before the noun: 兩个 (the two of them), 這个人 (this person), 彼个人 (that person).

Most common mistake: putting 是 before an adjective. 伊是媠 → 伊真媠.
