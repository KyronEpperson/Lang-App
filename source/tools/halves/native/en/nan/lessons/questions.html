English moves the question word to the front and adds "do": "What do you want to eat?" Taigi does neither. Say the sentence as if you knew the answer, then put the question word in the answer's place. The answer then has exactly the same shape as the question, which makes answering easy: swap the question word back out.

The question words

TaigiTâi-lôEnglish
啥物siánn-mihwhat
啥人siánn-lângwho

按怎án-tsuánnhow
偌濟guā-tsēhow much, how many

What and who

"I want to eat fried rice" is 我欲食炒飯 (欲 beh "want to" is lesson 17). Swap the food for 啥物 siánn-mih and you have the question. For "who", put 啥人 siánn-lâng where the person would be.

Where and when

A place or a time goes before the verb in Taigi, so the question words for them do too. 佗位 often follows 佇 "at": "at where". 當時 goes after the subject and before the verb.

How, and how much

按怎 án-tsuánn goes before the verb it asks about. 偌濟 guā-tsē asks for an amount; with 錢 tsînn "money" it is the price question you will use in lesson 16.

And you?

To send a question back, add 咧 after the person: 你咧？ Lí--leh? "and you?". It is said light, in the neutral tone.

{{ex:nan.s0086}}

No rising voice needed

In English, your voice often goes up at the end of a question. Taigi has tones on every syllable, so a rising voice can change the words instead of asking. Here the question word does the asking, and the sentence ends in each syllable's own tone. In lesson 14 you will meet yes/no questions, which end in a particle such as 無.

Most common mistake: moving the question word to the front, as in English. 啥物你欲食？ → 你欲食啥物？
