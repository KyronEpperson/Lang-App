English has "want", "would like", "like", "need to" and "be going to". Taigi covers them with three words, and each one goes right before the verb. None of them changes for the person or for time: 我欲, 你欲 and 伊欲 are all the same word. The table sums them up; the sections below show each one at work.

TaigiTâi-lôMain English meanings
欲behwant to; going to; about to
想欲siūnn-behwould like to
愛àilike to, love to; must
無愛bô àidon't want; don't like

欲: want to, going to

欲 beh + verb is "want to" or "going to". It is the word for ordering and for plans. With the weather, it means "about to": 矣 at the end shows the change is coming.

想欲: would like

想欲 siūnn-beh is softer, like "I'd like to". Use it for polite wishes.

{{ex:nan.s0125}}

愛: like, love, and must

愛 ài + verb means "like to" or "love to". In advice it means "must, need to": 你行路愛小心 is "you must be careful when you walk".

{{ex:nan.s0126}}{{ex:nan.s0127}}

無愛: don't want

English uses "don't" for every "not want". In Taigi, the negative of 愛 is 無愛 bô ài, "don't want, don't like". 毋 means refusing (won't), which sounds much stronger.

{{ex:nan.s0128}}

Yes/no questions

Add 無 at the end, as in lesson 14: 你欲去無？ Lí beh khì--bô? "Do you want to go?". The 無 is said light, in the neutral tone. The same works for 愛: ask with 無 at the end, and answer 愛 or 無愛.

Ordering at the night market

To order, say 欲 + verb + number + classifier + food. The classifier is the container: a bowl is 碗, a cup is 杯.

{{ex:nan.s0129}}{{ex:nan.s0130}}

If you know some Mandarin, you will reach for 要. In Taigi, write and say 欲. The same goes for the verbs: eat is 食 and drink is 啉.

Most common mistake: using the Mandarin word. 我要食炒飯 → 我欲食炒飯.
