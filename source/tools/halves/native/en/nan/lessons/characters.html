Taigi is written in traditional characters, like Taiwan Mandarin, but many everyday words have their own characters. This course uses the Ministry of Education's recommended characters. If you read Mandarin, this lesson is about unlearning a few habits. Taigi and Mandarin are different languages with different words, so a Mandarin character is not just another spelling: 不 is a Mandarin word, while 毋 is the Taigi word m̄, and they are not used the same way.

Others to know: 予 hōo (give, let), 囝 kiánn (child, son), 睏 khùn (sleep), 蹛 tuà (live), 遮 tsia (here) and 遐 hia (there), 冊 tsheh (book), 囥 khǹg (put, place) and 佮 kah (and, with). Never write the Mandarin character for a Taigi word, even if a keyboard offers it first.

Watch your reading too. A Mandarin reader's eye gives each character its Mandarin sound, but 毋 is read m̄ and 佇 is read tī. Always read the Tâi-lô line under a Taigi word, not the sound you already know.

Place before the verb

English puts "at home" after the verb. Taigi puts 佇 and the place before the verb: "I at home eat".

{{ex:nan.s0050}}

个 and 本: counting things

English says "one person, two books". Taigi needs a classifier between the number and the noun: 个 ê is the general one, 本 pún is for books. For two things, use 兩 nn̄g, never 二. "This" is 這 plus a classifier.

Putting characters together

Many Taigi words are two or three characters, and you read them one character, one syllable, at a time: 食 tsia̍h (eat) + 飯 pn̄g (rice) is 食飯 tsia̍h-pn̄g, eat a meal. Three rare characters

Three common words use characters some phones cannot show: 𪜶 in (they), 𠢕早 gâu-tsá (good morning) and 𨑨迌 tshit-thô (have fun). If you see an empty box, read the Tâi-lô.

Most common mistake: writing the Mandarin character. 我不是 → 我毋是, and 伊在厝裡 → 伊佇厝裡.
