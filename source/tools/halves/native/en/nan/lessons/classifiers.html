English counts some things directly ("three books") and others with a unit word ("three bowls of rice", "two cups of tea"). Taigi always uses a unit word, called a classifier, between the number and the noun. You cannot leave it out, even for "a" or "one": "a dog" is "one (animal-unit) dog".

Number + classifier + noun

Each noun has its usual classifier. 个 ê is the general one, used for people and many things. Food and drink are counted by their container: a bowl, a cup. Learn each new noun together with its classifier, the way the cards in the Classifiers deck show it.

Notice that English "a" becomes 一 plus the classifier: 一隻狗 is "a dog", 一杯茶 is "a cup of tea". 件 counts pieces of clothing, as in 一件衫, and also matters, as in 這件代誌, "this matter".

Remember from lesson 12: before a classifier, two is 兩, never 二. Every number works the same way: 三碗, 十二本, 一百个.

擺 pái counts times, not things: 一擺 is "once", as in 請閣講一擺 from lesson 8.

This and that: 這 and 彼

English says "this book". Taigi puts the classifier in here too: "this (classifier) book". 這 is "this" and 彼 is "that". Before a classifier, 這 and 彼 have the readings you see in the Tâi-lô of these examples.

The noun can drop out when it is clear: 彼塊 means "that one" (that table). The classifier stays.

This on its own

When nothing follows, or a verb such as 是 follows, 這 means "this (thing)" and has a different reading, as the Tâi-lô of this example shows. 彼 changes in the same way.

Most common mistake: leaving out the classifier, as English does. 這冊 → 這本冊, and 一人 → 一个人.
