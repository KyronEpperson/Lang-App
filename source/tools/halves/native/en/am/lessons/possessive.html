English puts a separate word in front: my house, your house, her house. Amharic puts the person on the end of the noun. The house is ቤት. The ending says whose it is. You already did this with names: ስሜ, ስምህ, ስምሽ.

WhoseHouse
Iቤቴ
you, a manቤትህ
you, a womanቤትሽ
you, politeቤትዎ
heቤቱ
sheቤቷ

ቤትህ is your house when you speak to a man. ቤትሽ is the woman form. ቤትዎ is the polite form, for a stranger or an older person. ቤቷ is her house. For እኛ the ending is ቤታችን, for you all it is ቤታችሁ, and for them it is ቤታቸው. English your hides that choice, so make it on purpose before you speak. A man friend, a woman friend, and someone older are three different endings. Then finish with ነው, because the verb is about the house. Say the line once to a friend and once to a stranger, so the three endings stay apart.

The verb looks at the house

ቤቱ is two things you already need. From the definite lesson it is the house. Here it is also his house. The sentence around it tells you which. It is not her house: that is ቤቷ. The house is masculine in this course, so the verb ending on all of these is ነው, not ነኝ and not ናት. The possessive ending shows the owner. The verb ending shows the house. Your name works the same way: ስምሽ ማን ነው stays ነው, because the subject is the name.
