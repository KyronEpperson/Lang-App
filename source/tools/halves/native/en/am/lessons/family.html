Four words cover the family you need first. አባት is father. እናት is mother. ወንድም is brother. እህት is sister. The possessive ending you already know on ቤት goes on these words too, and it matches the person who has the relative.

My, your, his, her

The manual's table for father is the model. አባቴ is my father. አባትህ is the man form, your father when you speak to a man. አባትሽ is the woman form. አባትዎ is the polite form, for እርስዎ. አባቱ is his father in that table. አባቷ is her father. Mother, brother, and sister take the same my, man, and woman endings: እናቴ, ወንድሜ, እህቴ, and እናትሽ, ወንድምህ, እህትሽ. The polite -ዎ is printed on father. Mother, brother, and sister take that same ending.

Keep two matches apart. The ending on the noun matches the person you are speaking to. The verb at the end matches the relative, not the listener. Mother and sister are feminine, so they take ናት. Father and brother take ነው. My mother is in Addis Ababa is እናቴ በአዲስ አበባ ናት, even when you say it to a man.

Have, and where they are

"I have a father" uses አለኝ, the having word you know. "I have no brother" is the negative. The person comes first, the verb last. A sister is feminine, so she takes ናት. A brother takes ነው. አቶ before a man's name is the title the manual uses.

The manual also lists ባል (husband) and ሚስት (wife), and it says those two can sound blunt. The safer word it gives is ባለቤት. You can leave all three until you need them.

The man form said to a woman is the mistake: አባትህ → አባትሽ.
