What time is it? is ስንት ሰዓት ነው. The hour is the subject, so the verb ending stays ነው even when you ask a woman. Do not switch it to ነሽ. To a stranger, say ይቅርታ first.

Two clocks, from the manual's pairs

Ethiopia also uses a clock counted from morning. Do not learn this as one slogan. The Peace Corps manual gives pairs. Use those. ጠዋት አስራ ሁለት ሰዓት is 6 a.m. ከጠዋቱ 1 ሰዓት is 7 a.m., and ከጠዋቱ 2 ሰዓት is 8 a.m. Noon is ከቀኑ 6 ሰዓት. ከቀኑ 8 ሰዓት is 2 p.m. ከምሽቱ 3 ሰዓት is 9 p.m. ከለሊቱ 10 ሰዓት is 4 a.m. The same page writes 1 as a digit. In words, 7 a.m. is ከጠዋቱ አንድ ሰዓት.

{{ex:am.s0181}}{{ex:am.s0182}}{{ex:am.s0183}}

So ከጠዋቱ ሶስት ሰዓት is not 3 a.m. Count from the pair you have: 1 in the morning is 7 a.m., so 3 in the morning is 9 a.m. When you answer, keep the verb last: it is three o'clock is ሶስት ሰዓት ነው. Name the part of the day when you need it, with the words in those pairs. When you make a plan, ask whether the hour is ሀበሻ ሰዓት (Ethiopian time) or ፈረንጅ ሰዓት (European time). The same number can be either clock. Do not invent a private formula. Use the pairs, then ask. You may also see the hour spelled ሰአት. This course writes ሰዓት.

Days

The manual's week is ሰኞ Monday, ማክሰኞ Tuesday, ሮብ Wednesday, ሀሙስ Thursday, አርብ Friday, ቅዳሜ Saturday, እሁድ Sunday. You will also see ረቡዕ, ሐሙስ, and እሑድ. Those are other spellings, not mistakes. This course writes the manual's forms. ዛሬ is today. ነገ is tomorrow.

{{ex:am.s0184}}{{ex:am.s0185}}

Person shows up when the subject is a person, not the clock. The clock never takes the man form or the woman form. A day does not either. ዛሬ ሰኞ ነው stays ነው whoever you are talking to. In the manual's dialog, "you go" to a man is ትሄዳለህ and to a woman is ትሄጃለሽ. Save those for a person who is going.
