English puts have in the middle: I have a book. Amharic does not. It says the thing, then a form of አለ that means "there is" pointed at a person. መጽሐፍ አለኝ is a book exists-for-me: I have a book. The thing comes first. The verb comes last.

Same letters, two words

Said with a long l, አለ means there is. Said with a short l, the same letters mean he said. አለኝ is built on the long one. The fidel does not show the long consonant. Listen. ቤት አለ is there is a house. ቤት አለኝ is I have a house. ነህ is you are. አለህ is you have. They are different verbs.

The woman form is አለሽ, not the man's አለህ. The form for she has is አላት, not አለው. A stranger or an older person gets the polite form አለዎት. For እኛ, the forms are አለን and የለንም. With no owner, "there is not" is የለም. The ም at the end means not. Other verbs get a wider pattern in the negative lesson. Do not say አላለኝም. If you name the owner, put that word first: እኔ መጽሐፍ አለኝ. You can leave it off, because አለኝ already says I. Do not borrow ነው here. Have is አለ, not the copula. A pen, a book and a house are enough to practice every person in the set. Those are one thing, so the verb is አለ. Two or more switch to the they-form. The Peace Corps manual says ሁለት ድመቶች አሉኝ, I have two cats.
