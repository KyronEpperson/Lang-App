You have the rows. Reading is the part the rows cannot do alone: letters that share a sound, a shape with a w built in, and a long consonant that never appears on the page.

Same sound, different letter

In Addis Ababa these sets are each one sound. You still spell the word the way it is spelled. Swapping a homophone letter is a wrong spelling, not a shortcut.

Country is a useful pair. ሀገር and ሃገር are both country. ሀ is the first order, ሃ is the fourth, and for this letter those two orders are the same vowel. You will see both spellings. Neither one is a different word.

The -wa forms

Beside the seven orders, some consonants have a -wa form: a w sound built into the shape. Learn these four first. ቧ is on b. ሏ is on l. ኳ is on plain k, the ከ row, not on the ejective ቀ. ጓ is on g. Other consonants have one too. Learn the shape with the word. Tap a shape to hear it. ቋ is the -wa form on the ejective ቀ, as in ቋንቋ, language.

ሏ is the same kind of shape on l. Read it as l with a w. You do not need a separate word for the letter itself.

Many other rows have a -wa form. You do not need cards for these. Read each one as its consonant plus wa when you meet it. እሷ, she, is isswa.

One spelling, two lengths

Do not give a spelling one pronunciation and keep it forever. ገና with a short n is still. ገና with a long n is Christmas. አለ with a short l is he said. አለ with a long l is there is. Same letters each time. Context and the audio tell you which one you heard. You cannot mark the long consonant in writing, and you should not try.

A rolled r, and a real place

ረ is a trill when it is held, and a tap when it is short. English speakers often use only the tap. In ሀገር, follow the audio. The book መጽሐፍ puts three lessons in one word: the ejective ጸ, the h letter ሐ, and a sixth-order ፈ at the end. You will also see መጽሀፍ, the Peace Corps spelling. Same word. The city you are learning for is two words.

The practice still asks for the man form and the woman form. ነህ and ነሽ did not go away when the letters got easier.

Most common mistake: one pronunciation for one spelling. ገና read only as still → ገና as still or Christmas, with the long n left to your ear.
