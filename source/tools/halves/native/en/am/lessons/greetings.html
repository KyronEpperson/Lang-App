English has one you. Amharic does not. From this lesson on, a friend or a peer who is a man is አንተ. A friend or a peer who is a woman is አንቺ. A stranger, a shopkeeper, or an older person is እርስዎ. When you are not sure, use እርስዎ.

Any hour

ሰላም is hello. It works with anyone, at any hour. It is also the word for peace. ጤና ይስጥልኝ is the formal hello for any person and any time of day. Use it with a stranger or an older person, and any time you want to be formal. You can answer with the same phrase. ጤና is health, which you can already read.

Morning, afternoon, evening, night

To sound warmer, add the time of day. The verb ending matches the person. Morning asks how someone passed the night. Afternoon asks about the day. Evening asks about the evening. You can use the phrase without that picture. Night is a goodbye, not a hello.

Morning and afternoon put ክ and ሽ on a consonant: አደርክ, ዋልክ. Evening does not. The man form is አመሸህ and the woman form is አመሸሽ. አመሽክ is not the evening greeting. In the morning, አደሩ is the polite form, for እርስዎ. A group of friends uses እናንተ, and the morning greeting for them is አደራችሁ. The pronoun lesson lines እናንተ up. At night, ደህና እደሩ is the polite goodbye, and it is also what you say to more than one person. Do not use one morning line for every person and every hour.

The reply, and thank you

A simple reply is ደህና ነኝ, I am fine. ነኝ is the verb ending for I. It stays ነኝ whether the speaker is a man or a woman. The ending that changes is the one in the greeting you said first, and it matches the person you are talking to. Then say አመሰግናለሁ, thank you. That word already means I thank. Do not change its ending to match the listener.
