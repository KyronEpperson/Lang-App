English puts "want" in the middle: "I want coffee", "I want to go". Amharic puts the thing first and the verb last. Coffee I-want is the shape, not I-want coffee. The sentences in this lesson leave off እኔ, አንተ, and the other subject pronouns. The ending already shows who wants it, so type the short form.

{{ex:am.s0164}}

Coffee is not the subject. The person who wants it is. Said to a man, "you want coffee" is the man form. Said to a woman, it is the woman form. Said to a shopkeeper you do not know, or to anyone older, it is the polite form. The cup does not choose the ending.

The to-form, then want

English "to go" is an infinitive, the to-form. Amharic builds it with መ-. To go is መሄድ. "I want to go" uses that to-form, then want. It does not use a second finished verb. You already know እሄዳለሁ (I go) and ሄድኩ (I went). Neither of those sits after want.

The ending still matches the person

Want changes for the person. እፈልጋለሁ is I. ትፈልጋለህ is the man form. ትፈልጊያለሽ is the woman form. The g stays. It does not become j the way go does. ይፈልጋሉ is the polite form, for እርስዎ, a stranger or an older person. He is ይፈልጋል, the same pattern as ይሄዳል. She is ትፈልጋለች.

I don't want

The negative you already know wraps the verb. "I don't want" is አልፈልግም. The thing still comes first. "I don't want coffee" is not a separate English "not" in the middle.

{{ex:am.s0170}}{{ex:am.s0171}}

Listen to the audio for long consonants inside the verb. The fidel does not write them. A specific coffee, one you both already mean, takes the object ending you learned: ቡናውን እፈልጋለሁ, I want the coffee. Plain ቡና is just "coffee".

The usual slip is English order, እፈልጋለሁ ቡና → ቡና እፈልጋለሁ. The other is a second finished verb: እፈልጋለሁ እሄዳለሁ → መሄድ እፈልጋለሁ.
