English has one "come", one "sit", and one "please". Amharic changes each of them for the person you are talking to. A friend who is a man, a friend who is a woman, and a stranger do not hear the same word.

Come, sit, and go

Come is irregular. ና is the man form. ነይ is the woman form. ኑ is for more than one person. For እርስዎ, a stranger or an older person, come is ይምጡ.

Sit and go are regular, and the woman form changes the last sound. Sit is ተቀመጥ to a man and ተቀመጪ to a woman. Go is ሂድ to a man and ሂጂ to a woman. Listen for that change. This lesson does not list every verb. For more than one person, sit is ተቀመጡ and go is ሂዱ. The polite form is different again: ይቀመጡ and ይሂዱ.

Please matches the same person

እባክህ is please to a man. እባክሽ is please to a woman. እባክዎ is please to እርስዎ. Put please first, then the command. A shopkeeper, a driver, or a host you have just met gets the polite form, not the man form.

Use the man form with a friend who is a man, and the woman form with a friend who is a woman. ኑ, ተቀመጡ, and ሂዱ are for more than one person. They are not the polite form. One older person gets ይምጡ, ይቀመጡ, and ይሂዱ. If you are not sure who you are talking to, the polite form is the safe choice.

One command for everyone is the mistake: ተቀመጥ said to a woman → ተቀመጪ. And እባክህ said to a woman → እባክሽ.
