English to be changes with the person: am, are, is. Amharic does the same, and the gender changes it too. The little word is a copula. It cannot be left off. ደህና by itself is not I am fine. ደህና ነኝ is.

The set

ደህና stays ደህና. Only the verb ending changes. It agrees with the subject. A man and a woman are not the same ending. እሷ does not take the ending that belongs to እሱ.

You will also hear ነች for she: እሷ ደህና ነች. ናት is the form to learn. Both are Addis Ababa. Do not "correct" someone who said ነች.

እሳቸው is one respected person, not a group. The verb ending is ናቸው, the same shape as they. The pronoun is what tells you it is one person. እርስዎ is the you you are talking to, when that person is a stranger or older. እሳቸው is a respected person you are talking about.

Word order, and dropping the pronoun

The pronoun comes first, then ደህና, then the verb ending. The verb comes last. The ending already says who, so the pronoun can drop: ደህና ነኝ is still I am fine. When you build a sentence from tiles, use every word the prompt gives you, in that order.

Names follow the same split. She is Abebech is written እሷ አበበች ናት. I am Adam is written እኔ አዳም ነኝ. My name is, from the last lesson, stays ስሜ plus the name plus ነው, because there the subject is the name. The speaker's gender does not choose that ነው.

Most common mistake: one verb ending for every subject. እሷ ደህና ነው → እሷ ደህና ናት. The verb ending agrees with the subject.
