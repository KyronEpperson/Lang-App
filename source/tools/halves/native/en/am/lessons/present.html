English makes a new verb with will: I go, I will go. Amharic uses one form for both. It is the imperfective, which covers present and future. ወደ ቤት እሄዳለሁ is I go home and I will go home. You do not add a second tense. A time word, later, can say which you mean. The past you just learned is a different ending: ሄድኩ is I went, not I go.

The start and the ending change together. I starts with እ. He starts with ይ. She and you start with ት. The polite form, and they, share ይሄዳሉ and ይጠጣሉ. Say እርስዎ or እነሱ when you need to show which. For እኛ, the form is እንሄዳለን. The place or the object still comes before the verb. A daily habit and a later plan use this same form. Both "I drink coffee every day" and "I will drink coffee later" are ቡና እጠጣለሁ. What you cannot skip is the person: ትሄጃለሽ to a woman, ትሄዳለህ to a man, and ይሄዳሉ to a stranger. The woman form changes the last consonant of the root. d becomes j.

Do not hand a woman the man's form

ትሄዳለህ is you go, to a man. ትሄጃለሽ is the woman form: the d has become j. Do not say ትሄዳለሽ. She does not palatalize in this present. She goes is ትሄዳለች, not ይሄዳል. Drink splits the same way. ትጠጣለህ is the man form. ትጠጫለሽ is the woman form: the last ጠ becomes ጨ. Do not say ትጠጣለሽ. The definite coffee still takes the object ending: ቡናውን እጠጣለሁ, I drink the coffee. Some coffee, with no "the," is just ቡና.
