English builds most questions with a helper: Where do you live? Do you have a pen? Amharic has no word that does the job of do. A yes-or-no question is the same sentence you already know, said with your voice up at the end. Nothing moves to the front. The verb stays last, and its ending still says who you mean. Asking a stranger or an older person does not change that shape. It only chooses the polite form of the ending, not the ending you use with a friend.

Six question words

AmharicEnglish
ምንwhat
ማንwho
የትwhere
መቼwhen
ስንትhow many, how much
እንዴትhow

Put the question word in front of the copula. ምንድን ነው is "what is it?" Said quickly, that is one word, ምንድነው. ምን ነው is the same question. Names use ማን, who, which you already saw in ስምሽ ማን ነው. ስም, the word for name, is the subject there, so the verb ending stays ነው even when you ask a woman. ማን ነሽ is different: it asks who you are, and you, a woman, are the subject.

The ending still names the person

የት ነህ is where are you, to a man. የት ነሽ is the woman form. የት ነዎት is the polite form, for a stranger or an older person. እንዴት ነህ, እንዴት ነሽ and እንዴት ነዎት ask how someone is. መቼ ነው asks when. እነሱ ስንት ናቸው asks how many they are. The answer is a number, which is next. እሷ ማን ናት asks who she is. You will also hear ነች in that slot.
