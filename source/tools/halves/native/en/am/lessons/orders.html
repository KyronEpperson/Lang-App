Amharic is written in the fidel, left to right. This course teaches Addis Ababa Amharic, the standard of the city. A fidel is an abugida: one character is a consonant plus a vowel, not an English letter on its own. There are no capital letters. The course goes in this order: the seven orders in this lesson, then the consonant rows over three lessons, then reading whole words.

One consonant, seven orders

Each consonant has seven orders. The consonant stays and the vowel changes. Here is b. The English words are a rough guide for your ear, not a spelling to write.

Keep each vowel short. The o does not slide into a w. A few letters, named later, use the father vowel in the first order too. በ does not. Tap a shape to hear it.

The same orders on l and m

Every row works the same way. Here are l and m. The marks for the vowels change a little from row to row, but the order never does: e, u, i, a, é, i, o in the English letters. Tap a shape to hear it.

Two characters side by side make a word. ል + ብ is ልብ, heart: ል is said li, ብ has no vowel, and the b is long, libb. The spelling does not show the long b.

The sixth order

The sixth order may be a short vowel, and that vowel is not the ee of see, or it may be no vowel. Word endings often drop it. The spelling will not tell you which reading a word uses. Listen, and learn the word.

A long consonant is not written

The fidel writes a consonant once, long or short. The length can change the meaning. ገና with a short n is still. ገና with a long n is Christmas. Same letters. You will not add a dot. Listen.

The English letters under each word

Every card and example also shows the word in English letters, so you can read it before you know the fidel. It is a reading aid, not a second spelling. The Written column above gives the vowels. The first order is written e, but say it short, between the e of bed and the u of but. The fifth order is written é, like the e of café. The letter i stands for two sounds: the third order, the ee of see said short, and the sixth order, a quick weak vowel like the i of bit. When the sixth order is silent, nothing is written.

A doubled letter is a long consonant: hold it, as in the middle of book case. ቡና is bunna, with a long n. The fidel does not show this, but the English letters do. An apostrophe after a consonant marks an ejective, a popped sound: t', k', p', ch', ts'. ቀ is k'e and ከ is ke. An apostrophe after a vowel is a short catch in the throat, as in ሰዓት, se'at.

The other letters sound the way they do in English: sh, ch, j as in jam, y as in yes, ny as in canyon, zh as the s in measure. Some words look like English words. They are not read that way: ነው new is ne plus w, ሰው sew is se plus w, ዋልክ walk is wa plus l plus k, and ግን gin has a hard g, as in get. The English letters come from a checked word list, so a word you meet later may surprise you. Trust the audio when it differs.

What the rows will add

Some consonants are ejectives: a catch in the throat, and a different consonant from the plain one. In Addis Ababa, different letters can also share one sound. Keep each word's own spelling. The row lessons take both of these up.

A verb ending in every practice

English has one you and one are. Amharic changes the verb ending for the person and the gender. ደህና means fine. ደህና ነህ is you are fine, to a man. ደህና ነሽ is you are fine, to a woman. Greetings will say who gets which form, including a stranger. For now the practice names the person in English.
