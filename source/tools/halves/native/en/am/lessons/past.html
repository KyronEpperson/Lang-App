English changes the middle of the sentence for the past: I went, she went. Amharic keeps the verb at the end and changes the verb ending for the person and the gender. This past form is called the perfective. Go and drink are the two to learn now. You may hear a long consonant inside the past form. The fidel does not write it. Do not add a mark. Listen.

Iyou, a manyou, a womanheshepolite
goሄድኩሄድክሄድሽሄደሄደችሄዱ
drinkጠጣሁጠጣህጠጣሽጠጣጠጣችጠጡ

The woman form ends in ሽ on both verbs: ሄድሽ, ጠጣሽ. I and you, a man, are the pair that changes shape. Go uses ሄድኩ and ሄድክ. Drink uses ጠጣሁ and ጠጣህ. Do not trade those. He is the plain form, ሄደ or ጠጣ. She adds ች. A stranger or an older person, and also they, share ሄዱ and ጠጡ. The sentence will show which. For እኛ, the forms are ሄድን and ጠጣን. Put the place before the verb: ወደ ቤት ሄድኩ. If you name the person, that word comes first, and the ending still has to match. A woman you are speaking to is ሄድሽ. She, the one you are talking about, is ሄደች.

The object ending

A definite object takes the object ending ን. ቡና is coffee. ቡናው is the coffee, with the masculine definite ending. ቡናውን is that coffee as the object. ቡናውን ጠጣሁ is I drank the coffee. Bare ቡና, some coffee, does not take ን. The object comes before the verb. English puts the verb in the middle. Amharic does not.

The definite coffee needs the object ending, and the verb stays last. ቡናው ጠጣሁ → ቡናውን ጠጣሁ.
