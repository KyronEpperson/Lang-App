English has one word, this, for a book and for a woman. Amharic looks at the noun. Near you, a masculine noun takes ይህ and a feminine noun takes ይህች. Farther away, a masculine noun takes ያ and a feminine noun takes ያች. One English word cannot cover those four.

The noun, then "to be"

English says "this is a book," with is in the middle. Amharic says the demonstrative, then the noun, then the copula at the end. Copula is the little "to be." A book, a pen and a house are masculine in this course, so the verb ending is ነው. For "this is a book," the noun stays bare: ይህ መጽሐፍ ነው, not ይህ መጽሐፉ. A house you already have in mind can still be ቤቱ. ይህ ቤቱ ነው is a real sentence.

A woman takes ናት

Point at a woman and two pieces change. The demonstrative is the feminine one, and the verb ending is ናት. Around Addis Ababa you will also hear ነች for that feminine is, and ይቺ for feminine this. This course writes ናት and ይህች. Both are ordinary. A man stays on ነው. ሴት means a woman, and ወንድ means a man.

Who you are speaking to

A friend or a peer is አንተ if he is a man and አንቺ if she is a woman. A stranger or an older person is እርስዎ, and the verb ending is the polite form ነዎት. When you talk about an older person, እሳቸው takes ናቸው. The demonstrative still matches the noun in front of you, not how polite you are being.
