English sets in, from and for out as separate words, and it can park them almost anywhere. Amharic joins በ, ከ and ለ to the next word. No space. በ is in or at. ከ is from. ለ is for. ወደ is toward, and it stays its own word: ወደ ቤት, to the house. The going verb comes in the next lessons. Learn the piece now.

From, in, and for

ከአሜሪካ ነኝ is I am from America. ነኝ is I, whether the speaker is a man or a woman. Your own gender does not change it. Do not put the man's ነህ on "I." To a man, from Gondar is ከጎንደር ነህ. To a woman, ከጎንደር ነሽ. To a stranger, the polite form is ነዎት. In Addis Ababa is በአዲስ አበባ, joined, then a space before አበባ.

{{ex:am.s0129}}{{ex:am.s0130}}{{ex:am.s0131}}{{ex:am.s0132}}

At home is በቤት. In the house, with the definite ending, is በቤቱ. For a person, ለ joins too: ለአንቺ to a woman, ለአንተ to a man, ለእርስዎ polite, ለእኔ for me. The book is still the subject, so the verb ending is ነው. A city or a country, when it is the subject, takes the feminine verb ending ናት. Read each join as one word. A gap after ከ, በ or ለ is the English habit, and it is not how these are written. ወደ is the one that keeps its space.
