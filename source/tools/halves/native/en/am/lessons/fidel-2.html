Four letters, one h

You now have ሀ, ሐ, ኀ and ኸ. In Addis Ababa they are one h. English speakers often invent four h sounds because they see four letters. There are not four. There is one sound, and four spellings. The word remembers which letter it uses. You do too.

ኀ and ኸ, like ሀ and ሐ, use the father vowel in the first order. አ and ዐ do the same. A few words keep a different vowel on these letters. ኧረ is one of them. Learn that kind of word whole. Do not build a private rule for it.

The vowel carriers

አ and ዐ do not start with a consonant. They write a vowel by itself. In Addis Ababa they do the same job. A word that begins with a vowel uses one of them, and the dictionary spelling is the one you copy. You can read a name now: አበበች. Hello to her is two words.

Plain k, and n

ከ is a plain k. ቀ, from the last lesson, is the ejective. They are different consonants. Saying ከ when the word is spelled with ቀ gives a different word. ነ is n. You can now read the first letter of the endings ነህ and ነሽ. Here is the whole n row.

Putting it together

With these rows you can read many small everyday words. እ, the sixth order of አ, is the vowel i with no consonant: እኔ is እ + ኔ, iné, I. Long consonants are still not written: አባት is abbat, father, with a long b. Read each card one character at a time before you flip it.

Most common mistake: four h sounds for four letters. ሀ, ሐ, ኀ and ኸ as four h sounds → one h in Addis Ababa, with the spelling still kept apart.
