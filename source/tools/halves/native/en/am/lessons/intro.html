English can say "Tigist" and let the sentence mean my name is Tigist. Amharic cannot. A name left by itself is not a sentence. You need a verb ending that means is.

My name is

The pattern is ስሜ, then the name, then ነው. ስሜ is my name. The subject of the sentence is ስም, the word for name, not the person speaking. ስም takes ነው. A woman does not switch it to the she form. Abebech says ስሜ አበበች ነው. Adam says ስሜ አዳም ነው. Tigist says ስሜ ትግስት ነው.

There is a second pattern, for I am, not for my name is. እኔ ትግስት ነኝ means I am Tigist. ነኝ is the verb ending for I, the same ending as in ደህና ነኝ. Use ስሜ … ነው when you mean my name is. Use እኔ … ነኝ when you mean I am. Do not mix them.

Asking

The ending on name changes for the person you ask. ስምህ is your name, said to a man, አንተ. ስምሽ is your name, said to a woman, አንቺ. ስምዎ is your name, said to እርስዎ, a stranger or an older person. These four chunks are enough for now. The full set of possessive endings comes later.

ማን means who. It sits where the name will sit in the answer, and ነው stays at the end. Even when you ask a woman, the verb ending is ነው, because the subject is still ስም. Her form is the ሽ on ስምሽ, not a she ending on the verb. The verb comes last. ማን ስምሽ ነው is the English order, and it is wrong.

You may hear አቶ before a man's name, as in አቶ ከበደ. It is a title. Leave it off when you give your own name.

Most common mistake: a name with no is. ስሜ ትግስት → ስሜ ትግስት ነው. The verb ending ነው is what makes it a sentence.
