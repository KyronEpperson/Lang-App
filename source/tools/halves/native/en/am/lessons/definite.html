English puts the before a noun, and a or an when the noun is new. Amharic has no separate word for a. ቤት is house, or a house. What English does with the, Amharic does with a definite ending on the noun. A specific noun, one you and the listener can pick out, needs that ending. Leaving it off is the common miss.

The house

ቤት takes the masculine definite ending. The last letter is sixth-order t. For the, that letter takes the u vowel: ቤቱ, the house. More than one house is ቤቶች. The plural ending is -ኦች. Do not add an English s. ቤትስ is not a word. A specific group is ቤቶቹ, the houses. The plural definite ending does not switch for a group of women. It stays this u ending.

A boy and a girl

ልጅ is a child. The masculine definite ending gives ልጁ, the boy. The feminine form is not that same u. ልጇ is the girl. The last letter has a w in it. The same letters also mean her child. Context tells you which, the way ቤቱ is the house or his house. You will also see ልጅቱ for the girl when someone wants it unambiguous. Towns and countries are often feminine too. This lesson does not spell that ending. Learn ቤቱ and ልጇ first.

More than one child is ልጆች. The specific group is ልጆቹ, the children. Same plural ending, same plural definite ending.

In a sentence

ትልቅ is big. ትንሽ is small. In a sentence the noun comes first, then the adjective, then the copula. The verb comes last. The verb ending still agrees with the subject. A masculine subject takes ነው. A feminine subject takes ናት. The children take ናቸው, the same ending as they.
