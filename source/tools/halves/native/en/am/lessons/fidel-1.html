ሀ and ሐ, ሠ and ሰ

In Addis Ababa, ሀ and ሐ are one h. ሠ and ሰ are one s. They are not two sounds that you choose by ear. The spelling is part of the word, so you learn which letter a word uses. Do not swap them to taste.

ሀ and ሐ are also the vowel exception. Their first order uses the vowel of father, the same vowel as their fourth order. So ሀ and ሃ are the same vowel. A few words break even that pattern. You learn those as whole words.

One row, written out

The ሰ row runs ሰ ሱ ሲ ሳ ሴ ስ ሶ. Same seven orders as በ. ስ, the sixth, is s with a vowel or with none. You can already read hello: ሰ, then ላ (l with the father vowel), then ም (sixth-order m). The word is spelled with ሰ, not with ሠ.

ቀ is an ejective

ቀ is an ejective, a popped consonant, with a catch in the throat. It is not a plain k. The plain k letter is ከ, and it comes in the next lesson. If you say an ordinary k, you have said a different consonant. Do not call ቀ a k. Listen for that catch. The English letters write it k'e: the apostrophe is the pop.

The man form and the woman form stay in the practice. You can now see the last letter of each ending. ህ is the sixth order of ሀ. ሽ is the sixth order of ሸ. The ነ row comes next. Until then, treat ነህ and ነሽ as whole shapes: man form, woman form.

Putting it together

You can now read real words with only these rows and በ. Read a word one character at a time. The spelling leaves two things to you: whether a sixth-order character keeps its vowel, and whether a consonant is long. ሶስት is ሶ + ስ + ት, sost, three: both sixth orders are silent. ቢራ is ቢ + ራ, birra, beer: the r is long. Read each card aloud before you flip it.

Most common mistake: calling ቀ a plain k. ቀ said as an ordinary k → ቀ as its own ejective. The plain k letter is still ahead of you.
