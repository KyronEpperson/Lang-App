A verb says what happens. In the dictionary every Korean verb ends in 다. Take 다 off and you have the stem; endings go on the stem.

DictionaryStemPolite (요)Meaning
가다가가요go
먹다먹먹어요eat
하다하해요do

One form for everyone

The verb does not change for I, you, he or they. Who is doing it comes from context.

{{ex:ko.s0069}}

Four kinds worth knowing

1 · Action verbs — things you do.

{{ex:ko.s0070}}{{ex:ko.s0071}}

2 · Describing verbs — English adjectives. In Korean they are verbs, so there is no separate "is".

{{ex:ko.s0072}}{{ex:ko.s0073}}

3 · 하다 verbs — a noun + 하다 (to do). Hundreds of verbs are made this way.

{{ex:ko.s0074}}{{ex:ko.s0075}}

4 · 있다 / 없다 — to exist or have / to not exist or not have.

{{ex:ko.s0076}}{{ex:ko.s0077}}
The verb is the one part a sentence can't drop. 가요 on its own is a complete sentence.
Common mistakes
  • Saying the dictionary form out loud — 먹다 is for dictionaries; say 먹어요.
  • Adding "is" to a describing verb — 좋아요 already means "is good".
  • Using 이에요 for "there is" — a cat exists = 고양이가 있어요.
