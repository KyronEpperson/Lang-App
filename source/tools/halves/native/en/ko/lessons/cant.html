You already say "don't" with 안. Korean has a separate word for "can't": 못. For "can" there is a pattern, -(으)ㄹ 수 있어요, and its negative -(으)ㄹ 수 없어요. Add the two ways to say "I don't know" and you can turn down anything politely.

안 = won't, 못 = can't

Both go right before the verb. Pick by meaning: a choice (안) or something stopping you (못).

하다 verbs split, like 안

못 goes between the noun and 해요. Not 못 공부해요.

{{ex:ko.s0296}}{{ex:ko.s0257}}

Can: -(으)ㄹ 수 있어요 / 없어요

Stem + ㄹ 수 있어요 after a vowel, 을 수 있어요 after a consonant. Swap in 없어요 for "cannot".

{{ex:ko.s0258}}{{ex:ko.s0259}}{{ex:ko.s0260}}{{ex:ko.s0261}}

몰라요 vs 모르겠어요

몰라요 = I don't know (a fact). 모르겠어요 = I'm not sure / I don't get it — softer and more common when you're asked something. The positive is 알아요.

{{ex:ko.s0262}}{{ex:ko.s0263}}{{ex:ko.s0264}}
Being asked 매운 거 먹을 수 있어요? (can you eat spicy food?) is a rite of passage. 네, 잘 먹어요 earns respect; 조금요 is the honest answer. And 한국어 잘 못해요 (I'm not good at Korean) is written as one word when it means "be bad at".
Common mistakes
  • 못 before the noun of a 하다 verb — 못 공부해요 ✗ → 공부 못 해요 ✓
  • 안 for inability — 매운 거 안 먹어요 when it hurts ✗ → 매운 거 못 먹어요 ✓
  • Missing 을 after a consonant — 먹 수 있어요 ✗ → 먹을 수 있어요 ✓
  • Blunt 몰라요 when you didn't understand — 몰라요 ✗ → 잘 모르겠어요 ✓
