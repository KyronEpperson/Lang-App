English uses one verb, like, for activities, things and wishes. German splits it three ways: gern, mögen and möchte.

Verb + gern: liking an activity

To say you like doing something, use the normal verb and add gern ("gladly"). Ich spiele gern Tennis is literally "I play gladly tennis". The verb is still the second element, and gern comes right after it, or after the subject when the subject follows the verb. For "don't like doing", put nicht before gern. You'll also hear gerne: it's the same word. It works with any verb, so you never need a second verb for "like".

To ask about someone's hobbies, use machen + gern.

lieber and am liebsten

lieber means "rather": Ich trinke lieber Tee (I'd rather have tea, I prefer tea). am liebsten means "most of all". You can put it first; the verb still comes second.

mögen + a noun: liking things and people

For a food, a thing, a person or an animal, use mögen. Its singular forms are irregular:

Subjectmögen
ichmag
dumagst
er, sie, esmag
wirmögen
ihrmögt
sie, Siemögen

The thing you like is a direct object, so a masculine noun takes den or einen. Keep mögen for nouns; for activities, gern is the everyday choice.

möchte: what you want right now

Ich möchte means "I would like": a wish for now, often when you order. Ich mag Kaffee is about your taste in general; Ich möchte einen Kaffee is an order.

Most common mistake: copying English "like to". Ich mag spielen Tennis → Ich spiele gern Tennis. And keep the verb second: Ich gern spiele → Ich spiele gern.
