German and English are related, so many words look alike. Most of them mean the same thing: das Haus, der Hund, gut. A few are false friends (false cognates): they look English but mean something else. These are the ones that cause real misunderstandings.

will is want

Ich will means "I want", not "I will". It is the modal verb wollen. For the future, German usually just uses the present tense with a time word. To order politely, say Ich möchte.

{{ex:de.s0217}}

bekommen is get, not become

bekommen means "to get" or "to receive". "To become" is werden: Er wird Arzt, "He's becoming a doctor". So in a restaurant, Ich bekomme ein Steak is a normal way to order.

Small words

also means "so" or "therefore"; "also" is auch. fast means "almost"; "fast" is schnell. aktuell means "current", and eventuell means "maybe".

{{ex:de.s0222}}{{ex:de.s0223}}

Three more that trip people up: bald means "soon", not "bald". spenden means "to donate"; to spend money is ausgeben. And sensibel means "sensitive"; a sensible person is vernünftig.

When a German word looks English, check it before you use it. Most look-alikes are safe, but the ones above come up every day in shops, offices and at work.

Word-for-word copies

Some English sentences cannot be copied word for word (a calque). German says "I have hunger" and "to me it is hot": Ich habe Hunger, Mir ist heiß. Ich bin heiß means something else, so avoid it. German also says "I have right": Ich habe recht. Age is the good news: Ich bin dreißig, just like English.

Most common mistake: using will for the future. "I will come tomorrow": Ich will morgen kommen (I want to come tomorrow) → Ich komme morgen.
