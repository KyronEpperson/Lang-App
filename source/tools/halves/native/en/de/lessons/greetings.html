Greetings change with the time of day, and German has two ways to say "you". Start with the polite one: it is what you need with strangers.

Hello and goodbye

Guten Morgen works until about ten or eleven, Guten Tag through the day, and Guten Abend from about six. Gute Nacht is only for leaving late or going to bed. Hallo is casual and fine almost anywhere. To leave, say Auf Wiedersehen (polite) or Tschüss (casual).

Two ways to say "you"

English has one "you". German uses Sie for people you would call Frau or Herr plus their last name: staff, officials, older strangers, customers. It uses du for friends, family, children and people your age at a party. When in doubt, use Sie: too polite is quickly fixed, too casual can sound rude.

To a stranger (Sie)To a friend (du)
What's your name?Wie heißen Sie?Wie heißt du?
Where are you from?Woher kommen Sie?Woher kommst du?
How are you?Wie geht es Ihnen?Wie geht's? Wie geht es dir?

The verb changes too

The form of a verb you find in a word list is the infinitive: heißen (to be called), kommen (to come). With Sie, the verb looks exactly like the infinitive: Sie heißen, Sie kommen. With du, it ends in -st: kommst. heißen already ends in an s sound, so it adds only -t: du heißt. When you talk about yourself, with ich, the verb ends in -e.

Sie or sie?

Sie with a capital S means "you". sie with a small s means "she" or "they". In speech they sound the same; in writing the capital letter matters.

Small polite words

danke is "thank you". bitte means "please" and is also the answer to danke: "you're welcome". Entschuldigung gets attention or says sorry.
