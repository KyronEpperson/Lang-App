English has one form for orders and requests: "Come in!", "Take a pill." It works for everyone. German has three, one for each "you": Sie, du and ihr.

Sie: the verb first, then Sie

To a stranger, a doctor or anyone you call Sie, put the infinitive first and Sie right after it. Never leave out Sie: without it, the request sounds wrong.

{{ex:de.s0175}}{{ex:de.s0176}}{{ex:de.s0177}}

du: the du form without -st and without du

To a friend, start from the du form and take off -st and the word du: du kommst → Komm! Verbs that change e to i or ie keep the change: du nimmst → Nimm!, du sprichst → Sprich! When the stem ends in s, only the t goes: du liest → Lies! You will sometimes see an extra -e (Bleibe!); that is correct too, just a little more formal.

{{ex:de.s0178}}{{ex:de.s0179}}

One exception: a → ä

Verbs that add an umlaut in the du form (du fährst, du schläfst) drop it again in the imperative.

ihr: the ihr form without ihr

To a group of friends, use the ihr form and leave out ihr.

{{ex:de.s0181}}

sein

"Be careful!" needs the verb sein, which has its own forms: Sei to a friend, Seid to friends, and Seien Sie to a stranger.

{{ex:de.s0182}}

bitte and separable verbs

bitte makes any request softer; put it after the verb or at the end. A separable prefix goes to the end, as in a statement.

{{ex:de.s0183}}

With staff, a doctor or a stranger, the Sie form plus bitte is the normal polite way to ask for something. Between friends, a du imperative without bitte is common; add bitte when you are asking a favor.

Most common mistake: putting du in front of the verb. Du komm! → Komm!, but to a stranger: Kommen Sie!
