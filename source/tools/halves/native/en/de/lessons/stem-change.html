English has a few verbs that change their vowel (I do, she does). German has a group of common verbs that change the vowel of the stem, but only in two forms: with du and with er, sie, es.

a becomes ä

fahren (to drive, to go by vehicle), schlafen (to sleep) and tragen (to carry, to wear) take an umlaut: du fährst, er fährt.

e becomes i or ie

essen (to eat), nehmen (to take), sprechen (to speak) and geben (to give) change e to i. lesen (to read) and sehen (to see) change e to ie. nehmen also changes its consonants: du nimmst, er nimmt. In du isst, the stem already ends in an s sound, so only -t is added.

Only two forms change

ich, wir, ihr, sie (they) and Sie keep the normal stem. So it is ich fahre, never ich fähre, and ihr fahrt, without the umlaut. A dictionary or a word list marks these verbs; learn the er form with the infinitive: fahren, fährt.

Ordering food

nehmen is the everyday verb for ordering: "I'll have…" is Ich nehme…. A server asks Was nehmen Sie?; a friend asks Was nimmst du?
