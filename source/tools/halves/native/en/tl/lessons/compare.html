English adds -er and -est ("cheaper, cheapest") or uses "more" and "most". Tagalog never changes the describing word itself: it puts mas or pinaka- in front. There are no irregular forms like "better" or "best" to learn: mas mabuti is "better", and every other describing word works the same way.

More: mas + describing word

mas means "more". The thing you are talking about is the topic, with ang, si or an ang pronoun such as ito.

{{ex:tl.s0202}}

Than: kaysa

"Than" is kaysa. After it, a thing takes sa and a person's name takes kay, the same split you know from places and people. Before a word like diyan, no marker is needed.

{{ex:tl.s0203}}{{ex:tl.s0204}}{{ex:tl.s0205}}

The order is the usual Tagalog one: the describing word first, then the topic, then kaysa and the other thing.

Most: pinaka-

pinaka- is joined to the describing word as one word: pinakamura (cheapest), pinakamalapit (nearest), pinakamalaki (biggest). To say "X is the cheapest", use ang: Ito ang pinakamura, "this is the cheapest one".

{{ex:tl.s0206}}

Before a noun, the pinaka- word takes the linker like any describing word: pinakamalaking bahay, pinakamalapit na botika.

{{ex:tl.s0207}}

As … as: kasing-

For "as big as", Tagalog uses kasing- with the root: kasinglaki (as big as). For now, just recognize it when you hear it.
