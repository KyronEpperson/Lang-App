English shows who does what mostly by word order. Tagalog puts a small word, a marker, in front of almost every noun. The marker tells you the noun's job, so learn each noun together with its marker.

Three jobs, two sets

Ordinary nouns (house, teacher) take ang, ng or sa. A person's name takes si, ni or kay instead.

JobOrdinary nounName
the topic: what the sentence is aboutangsi
the owner (later also the doer or the thing)ngni
the place, or the person something goes tosakay

For two or more names there are plural forms: sina, nina, kina (sina Ana at Juan, Ana and Juan).

ang and si: the topic

English says "The house is big". Tagalog puts the description first and marks the topic with ang: "Big the house". A simple sentence has only one topic, so only one ang or si.

ng and ni: the owner

English puts the owner first with 's: "the teacher's house". Tagalog puts the thing first and the owner after it, with ng (said nang) or, for a name, ni.

{{ex:tl.s0052}}{{ex:tl.s0053}}

sa and kay: place and person

sa marks a place: to Makati, in the kitchen. When the goal is a person, use kay with the name. para means "for".

Names are different

English never marks names, so learners put ang or sa before them. Swap to the name set: si Ana, ni Ana, kay Ana. Titles count as part of the name: si Lola, ni Kuya Jun.

Most common mistake: using the ordinary set with a name, or the name set with a place. Ang Ana ay guro. → Guro si Ana. bahay si Ana → bahay ni Ana. kay Maynila → sa Maynila.
