You have met it in every greeting: magandang umaga. Now for the rule behind it. It is the glue of Tagalog, and you will use it in almost every sentence.

English joins nothing; Tagalog joins with a linker

English puts "big" straight before "house". Tagalog never puts a describing word right next to its noun. It joins them with a linker, and the linker's shape depends on how the first word ends.

First word ends inLinkerExample
a vowel-ng, added to the wordmalaking aso
n-g, added to the wordpagkaing masarap
any other consonantna, a separate wordmabait na bata

Either order

English allows only "big house". Tagalog allows both orders, with the linker always between the two words. The linker's shape follows the word in front: magandang bahay and bahay na maganda mean the same thing. The first order is a little more common in speech, but both are correct.

ma- describing words

Many describing words start with ma-: maganda (beautiful), malaki (big), mabait (kind), masarap (delicious), matangkad (tall). maliit (small) looks like one too. Learn each with its ending, because the ending decides the linker.

In a sentence

The linked phrase works like one noun. It can be the description at the front of the sentence, and the topic still takes si or ang. With no noun after it, a describing word needs no linker: Masarap ang pagkain (the food is delicious).

Numbers too

Numbers are joined to their noun the same way. You will learn them later; for now, notice the pattern.

Most common mistake: leaving the linker out, or adding -ng after a consonant. maganda bahay → magandang bahay. mabaitng bata → mabait na bata.
