English turns a sentence into a question by moving words around: "You are tired" becomes "Are you tired?" Tagalog keeps the order and adds a little word, ba, or starts with a question word.

Yes-or-no questions: ba

ba is a question mark you can say. It never starts the sentence. It goes right after the first word, and after ka if ka is there too. Longer pronouns like siya and names with si come after ba. In relaxed speech people often drop ba and just raise their voice, but keep it while you learn.

Question words

A question word comes first, and needs no ba. The rest of the sentence keeps its normal order, and the thing you ask about usually takes ang, as in Alin ang bahay mo? To an older person, add po right after the question word: Kailan po?

Where: saan or nasaan

English has one "where". To ask where a thing or a person is right now, Tagalog uses nasaan, and the thing takes ang. saan asks where something happens or where someone is going, as in the everyday greeting Saan ka pupunta?

What + noun: anong

"What name", "what time": join ano to the noun with the linker, anong, as in Anong pangalan mo? In the same way, ilan becomes ilang before a noun: Ilang taon ka na?

Most common mistake: starting with ba, or with the pronoun, as English does. Ba pagod ka? → Pagod ka ba? Ikaw pagod ba? → Pagod ka ba?
