Family is at the center of Filipino life, and family words are used far beyond the family. Learn the words first, then how Filipinos use them for everyone else.

kapatid: brother or sister

English has two words; Tagalog has one, kapatid, with no sex. To be clear, add lalaki (male) or babae (female) with the linker na. Older siblings are often just kuya and ate.

Family words for everyone

kuya and ate are about age, not only family. Call a young man a bit older than you Kuya (a driver, a guard) and a young woman Ate (a shop assistant). Your parents' friends are Tito and Tita, and an elderly stranger can be Lolo or Lola. Before a name: Kuya Jun, Ate Liza. These titles sound warm and respectful; calling an older person by a bare first name can sound rude, so when in doubt, add a title.

Jobs

To say what someone does, put the job first and the person after it: Guro ako, "I'm a teacher". There is no word for "a", and the job word stays the same for a man or a woman. Common jobs: guro (teacher), nars (nurse), doktor, inhinyero (engineer), estudyante (student). To ask an older person, say Ano po ang trabaho ninyo?

Most common mistake: using "my" the English way, in front of the noun or with the ang form. ako nanay → nanay ko; Guro ang nanay ako → Guro ang nanay ko.
