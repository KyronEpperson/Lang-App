Getting around Manila means jeepneys, tricycles, taxis and Grab, and asking people the way. Here is what you need to say and to understand. Drivers, guards and vendors are usually happy to help, and a polite po at the right moment makes every answer friendlier.

Left, right, straight

kaliwa is left, kanan is right, diretso is straight ahead, and kanto is the corner. To tell someone to turn, Tagalog makes a verb from the word with -um-: kumaliwa (turn left), kumanan (turn right). As in any command, the person takes the ang form: ka to a friend, po kayo to anyone who gets po.

Places take sa, people take kay

English uses "to" and "at" for places and people alike. Tagalog uses the sa marker for a place and kay for a person. "Juan's house" is bahay ni Juan, so "to Juan's house" is sa bahay ni Juan.

{{ex:tl.s0180}}

Riding

The jeep (jeepney) runs fixed routes; a tricycle is a motorcycle with a sidecar for short trips; taxis and Grab cars take you to the door. Ask where to get a ride and how much it costs, with po.

{{ex:tl.s0181}}{{ex:tl.s0182}}

To a driver, start with Kuya (for a man) and the place.

{{ex:tl.s0183}}

Inside the jeepney

Passengers pay by passing coins forward, saying Bayad po. When you want to get off, say Para po! loudly enough for the driver to hear.

Most common mistake: using kay for a place. Pupunta ako kay Makati → Pupunta ako sa Makati. kay is only for people: kay Juan.
