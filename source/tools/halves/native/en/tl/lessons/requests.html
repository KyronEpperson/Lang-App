English softens a request with "please" and "could you". Tagalog has a "please" that goes inside the verb, and the polite po does the rest. These are the words you need when you ask for help, at home, in a shop or at the pharmacy.

Commands: verb + ka or kayo

An English command has no "you": "Eat!" A Tagalog command keeps the doer, in the ang form: ka to a friend, kayo to a group, and po kayo to anyone who gets po. A bare verb to an older person sounds rude. To offer a seat to Lola, say Umupo po kayo (Please sit down).

paki-: "please do this"

Put paki- in front of the root of a verb and you have a polite request: abot (reach, pass) → pakiabot, sara (close) → pakisara, tulungan (help) → pakitulungan. The thing or person you want something done to takes ang. Add po for elders and strangers.

The little word nga makes a request sound friendlier, a bit like "would you": Pakiabot nga.

Asking permission: Puwede po bang…?

To ask "May I…?" or "Is it OK to…?", say Puwede po bang + the verb. The linker sits on ba, the last little word before the verb.

{{ex:tl.s0191}}

"Don't": huwag + linker

English makes "don't" from "not". Tagalog does not use hindi here: "don't" is huwag, and the pronoun after it takes the linker: Huwag kang…, Huwag po kayong….

Most common mistake: making "don't" with hindi. Hindi ka umalis! → Huwag kang umalis!
