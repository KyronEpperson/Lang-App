English verbs show time: past, present, future. Tagalog verbs show something slightly different, called aspect: has the action started, and is it finished? There are three forms, and every -um- and mag- verb has all three.

  • Completed: started and finished (ate).
  • Ongoing: started, not finished, or done regularly (is eating, eats).
  • Future: not started yet (will eat). Grammar books call it "contemplated".

Repeat the first syllable

The key move is repeating the first syllable of the root: kain gives ka-kain, luto gives lu-luto. If the root starts with a vowel, repeat just that vowel: inom gives i-inom, aral gives a-aral.

bilibumilibumibilibibili
inomuminomumiinomiinom
lutonaglutonaglulutomagluluto
aralnag-aralnag-aaralmag-aaral

For -um- verbs: ongoing keeps -um- and repeats the syllable; future repeats the syllable and drops -um-. For mag- verbs: ongoing is nag- plus the repeat; future is mag- plus the repeat.

Time words must agree

kahapon (yesterday) goes with the completed form, ngayon (now) with the ongoing form, bukas (tomorrow) with the future. If you mix up a form at first, the time word usually still makes your meaning clear, but listen for the repeated syllable.

Ongoing for habits and jobs

English uses the simple present for habits: "I work at a hospital". Tagalog uses the ongoing form, because the action keeps going.

{{ex:tl.s0148}}{{ex:tl.s0039}}

Aspect changes only the verb. The markers stay the same: the doer still takes ang, the thing ng, the place sa.

Most common mistake: using the completed form for a future plan, as if it were a plain "base" form. Kumain ako bukas → Kakain ako bukas. And repeat only one syllable: kumaain → kumakain.
