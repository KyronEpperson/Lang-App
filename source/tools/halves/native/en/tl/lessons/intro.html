When you meet Filipinos, the first questions are almost always the same: your name, where you are from, and often your age. Here are the questions and the answers.

Your name

You already know Ako si… with si before the name. To ask a friend's name, say Ano ang pangalan mo? (word for word "What the name your?"). In speech you will usually hear Anong pangalan mo?, with ano joined to the noun by the linker -ng (some people write ano'ng, short for ano ang). To an older person: Ano po ang pangalan ninyo?

Where you are from: taga-

English says "I'm from Manila". Tagalog puts taga- (from) in front of the place, with a hyphen, and the pronoun at the end: Taga-Maynila ako. To ask, put taga- on saan (where). Answer with your city, your state or your country; a Filipino relative may also ask which province your family comes from.

Nationality

For nationality, the word itself comes first: Amerikano ako (I'm American), Pilipino ako (I'm Filipino). Many heritage learners say Pilipino-Amerikano ako.

Learning Tagalog

Learn this sentence as one block for now; the verb will make sense in the verb lessons. People will be delighted to hear it, and many will switch to slow, careful Tagalog for you.

Your age

Filipinos ask age openly; it decides who gets po, kuya or ate. English asks "How old?"; Tagalog asks "How many years already?": ilan (how many) + the linker -g + taon (year). After a word ending in n, the linker is just -g.

The numbers come in a later lesson; until then, you can answer with the English number, which everyone understands.

Most common mistake: translating "How are you?" word for word. Paano ka? → Kumusta ka? And do not drop taga-: Ako ay Amerika. → Taga-Amerika ako.
