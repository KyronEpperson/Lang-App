English uses two different verbs for two ideas: "there is" (There's rice) and "have" (I have a dog). Tagalog uses one small word for both: may. Its opposite is wala, which you met in the last lesson.

may + noun

may goes straight in front of the noun, with no marker in between: no ang, no linker. On its own, may + noun means "there is". Add ba and it becomes a question.

Who has it

To say who has something, put the owner after the noun. A pronoun comes in its ang form (ako, siya, kami); a name takes si.

{{ex:tl.s0099}}{{ex:tl.s0100}}{{ex:tl.s0101}}

mayroon: the longer word

mayroon means the same as may, but it works differently. The pronoun comes right after it, joined by the linker -ng, and then the noun. In everyday speech mayroon is usually said and written meron. Mayroon. or Meron. alone is the answer "Yes, there is."

{{ex:tl.s0102}}{{ex:tl.s0103}}

Both May aso ako and Mayroon akong aso are correct and mean the same. Use whichever comes to you first; at a carinderia or a sari-sari store you will hear both all day long, from the staff and from the customers.

wala: none

wala works like mayroon: a pronoun after it takes the linker. For "there's no…", link wala straight to the noun: walang + noun.

{{ex:tl.s0104}}{{ex:tl.s0105}}

Asked May kanin ba?, answer Mayroon. if there is rice and Wala. if there isn't. Never Hindi.: that is "not", not "none".

Most common mistake: forgetting the linker after mayroon or putting a marker after may. Mayroon ako kotse → Mayroon akong kotse; May ang tubig? → May tubig ba?
