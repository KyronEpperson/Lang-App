You know two forms of every pronoun: the ang form (ako) and the ng form (ko). There is a third, the sa form, used after sa and para sa ("for"). English uses "me" for all of these jobs; Tagalog picks a different word.

The sa pronouns

ang formng formsa formEnglish
akokosa akinto me, for me
ikaw, kamosa iyoto you
siyaniyasa kaniyato him, to her
kami, tayonamin, natinsa amin, sa atinto us
kayoninyosa inyoto you (polite or plural)
silanilasa kanilato them

kaniya is very often written kanya; both are fine. Without sa, the same words mean "mine", "yours" and so on: Akin ito is "This is mine".

People: kay and kina

With a person's name, sa becomes kay, and kina for two or more names. A title used as a name, such as Lola or Kuya Jun, counts as a name. sa stays for places and ordinary nouns.

{{ex:tl.s0118}}

Here and there

English has two words, "here" and "there". Tagalog has three, by distance, just like ito, iyan and iyon: dito (here, near me), diyan (there, near you), doon (over there, far from both of us). Filipinos often point with their lips while saying these words: a quick pout toward the place.

{{ex:tl.s0119}}

Where something is: nasa and nasaan

To say where someone or something is right now, use nasa + the place. It already contains "is at", so nothing else is needed. With a name, the place comes first and si + the name after it. The question is nasaan, "where is…?". Use saan for where something happens, and nasaan for where something is.
