English puts "already", "still", "too" and "just" almost anywhere in a sentence. Tagalog has short words (some books call them particles) for these ideas, and they always sit right after the first word of the sentence, never at the end.

na and pa: already and still

na means "already" or "now". pa means "still", "yet" or "more". With hindi they make a pair that English speakers often swap: Hindi na is "not anymore", Hindi pa is "not yet".

rin / din: also, too

"Too" is rin after a vowel and din after a consonant. You will hear speakers mix them, but that is the rule to aim for.

naman, muna, nga, daw

naman softens an answer or turns the question around: Ikaw naman? (And you?). muna means "first, for now". nga adds emphasis or softens a request. daw (raw after a vowel) means "they say, I heard".

Their order

When several come together, the order is fixed: a one-syllable pronoun (ka, ko, mo) right after the first word, then na or pa, then po, then ba, and a two-syllable pronoun (ako, siya, kayo) last.

If a verb follows the little words, the linker goes on the last one: Gusto ko pang kumain (I still want to eat).

Most common mistake: swapping na and pa. Hindi na ako kumakain for "I haven't eaten yet" → Hindi pa ako kumakain. (Hindi na means you don't eat anymore.)
