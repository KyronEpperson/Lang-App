Walk through a Manila mall and you will hear sentences that are half Tagalog, half English. This is Taglish, and it is how many educated Filipinos talk every day. It is not broken Tagalog: the grammar is still Tagalog, with English words placed inside it.

English nouns, Tagalog grammar

Words for modern things are often simply English: cellphone, aircon, mall, jeep, and CR (comfort room, the everyday word for a restroom). They take the usual markers and pronouns.

{{ex:tl.s0120}}

English verbs with Tagalog affixes

Speakers also put Tagalog affixes on English verbs. You only need to recognize these; this course always asks you for the Tagalog verb.

TaglishEnglish
Nag-shopping kami.We went shopping.
Magda-drive ako.I'll drive.
Paki-on ang aircon.Please turn on the aircon.

Casual short forms

In relaxed talk, words get shorter. 'yung replaces ang before a noun, 'yan is iyan, 'di is hindi, and ayoko is ayaw ko.

Words you hear all day

Sige (okay, go ahead), Tara! (let's go), Ewan ko (I don't know, casual), Grabe! (wow, that's a lot), talaga (really), and 'no or 'di ba at the end for "right?". Between adult friends, Pare (to a man) and Mare (to a woman) mean "buddy".

When to switch

With friends and people your age, Taglish and the short forms sound natural. With Lola, a tita, your boss or a stranger, use the careful form with po: Hindi ko po alam, not Ewan ko.

{{ex:tl.s0215}}
Most common mistake: using casual forms with elders. Ewan ko to Lola → Hindi ko po alam.
