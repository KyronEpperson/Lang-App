English has separate words for "I" and "my", "he" and "his". Tagalog also has a second set of pronouns, the ng pronouns. They do the job of "my, your, his, her, our, their", and a few more jobs you will use every day.

After the noun

English puts "my" before the noun. Tagalog puts ko after it, just as ni Ana comes after the thing: bahay ko (my house), pangalan mo (your name).

Polite your

To an older person, use the polite plural: ninyo, often said niyo, with po. It matches the polite kayo.

{{ex:tl.s0068}}

After gusto, ayaw and alam

Here English speakers slip. English says "I want", "I know", with the same "I" as everywhere. Tagalog uses the ng form after gusto (want, like), ayaw (not want) and alam (know): word for word "wanted by me". So it is Gusto ko, never Gusto ako. The thing you want takes the ng marker: Gusto ko ng kape.

hindi pulls it forward

hindi means "not". When it starts the sentence, the short pronoun moves up to sit right after it: Hindi ko alam (I don't know), not Hindi alam ko. Little words like ko and mo always want to be near the front.

{{ex:tl.s0071}}
Most common mistake: using the ang form after gusto or alam. Gusto ako ng kape. → Gusto ko ng kape. Hindi ako alam. → Hindi ko alam.
