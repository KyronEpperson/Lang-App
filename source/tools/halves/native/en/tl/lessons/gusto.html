English says "I want", "I need", "I can", with the doer first and the same "I" every time. Tagalog puts the wanting word first, and the person who wants takes the ng form: ko, mo, niya, or ni before a name. Think of Gusto ko as "my want is…".

Wanting a thing: gusto + ng form + ng + thing

The thing you want takes the ng marker, just like the thing after a verb.

Wanting to do something: add the linker

To want to do something, put the verb after the pronoun and join them with the linker -ng: ko becomes kong, mo becomes mong, niya becomes niyang. Use the basic form of the verb. For -um- verbs it looks like the completed form (kumain, uminom, umuwi); for mag- verbs it is the mag- form (magluto, magtrabaho). If a little word such as ba comes before the verb, the linker moves onto it: bang.

ayaw and kailangan work the same way

ayaw is "not want", so it needs no hindi. Ayaw ko is usually said as one word, ayoko, and with the linker ayokong. kailangan is "need".

puwede and marunong take the ang form

Two "can" words are different. puwede (also written pwede) means "may, it's allowed, it's possible". marunong means "know how to". With the -um- and mag- verbs you know, both take the ang form: ako, ka, siya, joined to the verb with the linker. So "I can speak Tagalog" is about skill: Marunong akong mag-Tagalog. Puwede akong mag-Tagalog would mean "I'm allowed to speak Tagalog".

Most common mistake: using the ang form after gusto, and leaving out the linker. Gusto ako ng kape → Gusto ko ng kape; Gusto ko kumain → Gusto kong kumain.
