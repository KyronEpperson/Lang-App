English changes the verb for time: go, went, going, will go. Vietnamese verbs never change. đi is "go", "went" and "going". Time comes from small time words before the verb, or from a word like hôm qua (yesterday).

đã: it happened

đã before the verb shows the action is done. All three time words in this lesson sit in the same slot, between the person and the verb: tôi đã đi, tôi đang đi, tôi sẽ đi.

{{ex:vi.s0141}}

đang: it is happening

đang before the verb shows the action is going on now or these days, like English "-ing". It is fine with bây giờ (now), even though the time is already clear.

sẽ: it will happen

A time word can do the job

Useful time words: hôm qua (yesterday), hôm nay (today), bây giờ (now), ngày mai (tomorrow). Once you say one of them, the time is clear, and đã or sẽ becomes optional. Most people leave đã out. The time word usually starts the sentence, but it can also go at the end.

{{ex:vi.s0145}}

Questions work the same way. The verb stays as it is, and the question word stays where the answer goes.

{{ex:vi.s0146}}

không: not

không goes right before the verb, whatever the time. With sẽ, it comes after it: sẽ không (won't).

One time word at a time

English stacks helpers ("I have been going"). Vietnamese picks one: đã, đang or sẽ, never two together. Often you need none at all: Tôi học tiếng Việt is a perfectly good "I study Vietnamese".

Most common mistake: stacking time words to copy English "was doing". Tôi đã đang học. → Tôi đang học. (or Hôm qua tôi học.)
