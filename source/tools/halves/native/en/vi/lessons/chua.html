English asks "Have you eaten yet?" and answers "yes" or "no". Vietnamese has a special question ending for "yet?", and two special answers that replace yes and no.

… chưa? means "… yet?"

Put chưa at the end of the sentence, where không goes in a yes/no question. You may add đã before the verb, but it is optional: with or without it, the question means the same. With a place, it also covers "Have you ever been…?".

The answers: Rồi and Chưa

Here is the part English speakers miss. You don't answer with có or không. "Yes, already" is Rồi. "No, not yet" is Chưa. You can say just the one word, or a whole sentence.

{{ex:vi.s0175}}{{ex:vi.s0176}}

Look at where the words sit. rồi (already) goes after the verb, or after the verb and its object. chưa (not yet) goes before the verb, in the same place as không.

EnglishVietnamese
Have you eaten yet?Anh ăn cơm chưa?
I've already eaten.Tôi ăn rồi.
I haven't eaten yet.Tôi chưa ăn.
I don't eat (it).Tôi không ăn.

The last row shows why the difference matters: không means "not, in general", chưa means "not so far". Mind the mark, too: chưa has the horn on the u. Without it, chua means "sour".

A question that is really a greeting

Ăn cơm chưa? (Have you eaten?) is a friendly hello between people who know each other. It is not an invitation to lunch. Just answer Rồi or Chưa, and ask back. Heritage learners will hear it at almost every family visit, often from a grandparent at the door. If you answer Chưa, be ready: in a Vietnamese home, the next thing you hear may be an invitation to sit down and eat.

{{ex:vi.s0178}}
Most common mistake: answering a chưa question with không. Anh ăn cơm chưa? Không. → Anh ăn cơm chưa? Chưa.
