English has one "this" and one "that", and both can go before a noun ("this book") or stand alone ("This is my house"). Vietnamese uses different words for the two jobs, and the ones that go with a noun come after it.

Starting a sentence: Đây là, Đó là

To introduce someone or name something, start with Đây là ("This is") or Đó là ("That is"). It is how you introduce a friend, show someone your home, or name a dish.

With a noun: này, đó, kia after the noun

For "this table", the pointing word goes after the noun: "table this". này is "this", đó is "that" (near the listener, or already mentioned), and kia is "that over there". Use đó for something the listener has or something you just talked about, and kia for something you can both see at a distance.

A small word in front

Did you notice cái? When you point at one thing, Vietnamese puts a classifier before the noun: a little word that says what kind of thing it is. English does something similar in "a piece of cake". For now, learn three:

ClassifierForExample
cáimost objectscái bàn này (this table)
conanimalscon mèo kia (that cat)
quyểnbooksquyển sách này (this book)

A later lesson covers classifiers fully. In the South, people say cuốn for books.

This one, that one

With no noun, keep the classifier and drop the noun: cái này is "this one", cái kia is "that one over there". It is perfect for shopping and ordering: just point.

{{ex:vi.s0074}}

On their own, đây and đó also mean "here" and "there". You will meet them again in the lesson on places.

One more thing: Đây never goes in front of a noun. "This book" is never đây sách.

Most common mistake: putting "this" first, as in English. này cái bàn → cái bàn này.
