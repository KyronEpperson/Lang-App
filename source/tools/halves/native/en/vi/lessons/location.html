English needs the verb "to be" to say where something is: "I am in Hanoi", "The book is on the table". Vietnamese uses one small word instead: ở.

ở means "be at" and "live at"

ở is a verb of its own. It already means "is at", so it never takes là. With a person, ở can mean both "am in" and "live in". Like every Vietnamese verb, it never changes: the same ở works for I, you, she and they.

{{ex:vi.s0158}}{{ex:vi.s0159}}

In the second sentence ở follows another verb, sống (live), and works like English "in" or "at". It does the same after làm việc (work) and học (study): you work ở a place, you study ở a place.

Where? X ở đâu?

English moves "where" to the front. Vietnamese leaves đâu (where) at the end, in the spot where the answer will go. To answer, keep the same frame and put the place where đâu was.

{{ex:vi.s0160}}{{ex:vi.s0161}}

On, under, in: the place words

These words go before the place, just as "on" goes before "the table" in English. Put them right after ở. Two of them also work for time, just as in English: trước is "in front of" or "before", and sau is "behind" or "after". To ask whether someone is somewhere, wrap the sentence in có … không: Chị Lan có ở nhà không? (Is Lan at home?)

The thing still comes noun first

The thing you are locating keeps its classifier and its describing words, in the usual order: quyển sách này (this book), con mèo của tôi (my cat). Only then come ở and the place.

{{ex:vi.s0165}}
Most common mistake: adding là because English says "am" or "is". Tôi là ở Hà Nội → Tôi ở Hà Nội. ở already does the job of "is".
