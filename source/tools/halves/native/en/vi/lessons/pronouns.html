English has one "you" for everyone, from a child to the president. Vietnamese has no neutral "you". Instead it borrows family words: you call a man a little older than you anh ("older brother"), even if you just met him.

Hello and thank you, with a person

Xin chào is a safe hello with anyone. People more often say Chào plus the word for the listener. Cảm ơn (also spelled cám ơn) is "thank you", and it sounds warmer with the person added too.

Xin lỗi is "sorry" and "excuse me". Tạm biệt is "goodbye", a little formal; friends just say Chào again.

If you are not sure of someone's age, it is safer to treat them as a little older than you. Between friends of the same age, bạn and tôi are fine.

The words go both ways. Talking to chị, you are em; she calls you em and herself chị. tôi ("I") is never wrong, just more distant. For people your parents' or grandparents' age you will learn cô, chú, ông and bà later: never call them bạn.

Calling someone

To get a waiter's or a driver's attention, add ơi: Anh ơi! for a man older than you, Em ơi! for anyone younger.

{{ex:vi.s0043}}

Two small polite words

ạ at the end of a sentence shows respect to someone older. Dạ is a polite "yes?" when an older person calls you. You will use them actively later; for now, notice them.
