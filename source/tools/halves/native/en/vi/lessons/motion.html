English "go" covers almost every trip. Vietnamese splits it by direction: going out, arriving, and going back home.

đi: go, with no "to"

đi is the general "go". The place comes straight after it, with no word for "to".

{{ex:vi.s0179}}{{ex:vi.s0180}}

với means "with", for the people you go with. On its own, đi can also mean "leave": Tôi đi đây is "I'm off now".

về: go back, go home

When you return to where you belong (your home, your hotel, your hometown, your country), Vietnamese uses về, not đi. English says "go home"; Vietnamese says "return home". An American going back to the US next month says Tháng sau tôi về Mỹ: home again, so về.

{{ex:vi.s0181}}

đến (or tới): arrive, come to

đến is about reaching a place. You will also hear tới, with the same meaning. Use it for the moment you get there, or for coming to someone's place.

{{ex:vi.s0182}}

Up, down, in, out

Four more verbs give the direction. They work alone, or with a place after them. With vehicles, lên xe is getting on or in and xuống xe is getting off or out, whether the vehicle is a bus, a car or a motorbike taxi.

By bus, by train: bằng

English "by" becomes bằng, followed by the vehicle. Don't use với here: với is for people.

{{ex:vi.s0183}}{{ex:vi.s0184}}

You can also drop bằng and use the vehicle like an object: đi xe máy (ride a motorbike), đi tàu (take the train). A vehicle you point at keeps the noun first: xe buýt này (this bus).

{{ex:vi.s0185}}
Most common mistake: đi for going home. Tôi đi nhà → Tôi về nhà. Going back to where you live is always về.
