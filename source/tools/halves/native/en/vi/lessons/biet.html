English uses one word, "can", for three different ideas: a skill you learned, something that is possible, and something you are able or allowed to do. Vietnamese has a word for each.

biết + verb: know how to

biết means "know". Put it in front of a verb and it means you learned how to do it: swimming, driving, speaking a language. For "can't", put không in front of biết. To ask, wrap it in có … không, as in the third example, and answer Có, tôi biết or Không, tôi không biết.

{{ex:vi.s0166}}{{ex:vi.s0167}}{{ex:vi.s0168}}

On its own, biết is just "know": Tôi không biết is "I don't know".

có thể + verb: can, it's possible

có thể goes before the verb, like English "can" or "be able to". It is about whether something is possible, not about a skill. For "can't", say không thể. It sounds a little more careful than the other two, so you will often hear it from shop and hotel staff, as in the second example.

{{ex:vi.s0169}}{{ex:vi.s0170}}

verb + được: able to, allowed to

This one has no English twin. được goes after the verb, or after the verb and its object, and says you are able to do it. With không at the end it makes the everyday way to ask "Can I…?" or "Is it OK to…?".

{{ex:vi.s0171}}{{ex:vi.s0172}}

The answer is Được (yes, that's fine) or Không được (no, you can't).

The three overlap, and Vietnamese speakers often combine them, but this table will keep you on safe ground.

Most common mistake: có thể for every English "can". For a skill you learned, biết is the natural choice: Tôi có thể nói tiếng Việt → Tôi biết nói tiếng Việt. có thể puts the weight on "it's possible", not on the skill.
