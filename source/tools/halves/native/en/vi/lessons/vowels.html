English spells about fifteen vowel sounds with five letters, so a in cat, car and cake are three sounds. Vietnamese gives each vowel sound its own letter, and the vowel marks tell you which one. Once you know the twelve vowel letters (i and y share one sound), a word always sounds the way it is spelled.

Three families

LetterLineSound
a / ă / âah / a / uhfather; a short ah; cup
o / ô / ơaw / oh / erlaw; go, with no glide; her, with no r
e / êeh / eybed; hey, with no glide
u / ưoo / uufood; oo said with spread lips, as in a smile

ă and â are short, so they never end a syllable: something always follows them. ơ is the vowel you may have heard in pho ("fuh"): the line writes it er, but there is no r anywhere.

For ư, say oo, then pull your lips into a smile without moving your tongue.

Long and short

Some vowels change length before a glide, and the length changes the word. ai has a long ah, ay a short one; ao is long, au short.

{{ex:vi.s0030}}{{ex:vi.s0031}}{{ex:vi.s0032}}{{ex:vi.s0033}}

âu and ây use the short â. And ă and â are short on their own, as in ăn and chân. Tap each glide ending.

{{tiles:vi.tl007}}

Vowel pairs

Three pairs glide from one vowel into a soft uh, inside one syllable: iê (written ia at the end), uô (ua at the end) and ươ (ưa at the end). The line shows them with a hyphen.

{{ex:vi.s0034}}{{ex:vi.s0035}}

Tap each pair. Both spellings of a pair sound the same; the second is used when nothing follows.

{{tiles:vi.tl008}}

o and u as a glide

Before another vowel, o and u often turn into a quick w: hoa sounds like hwah, thuê like twey. The line writes the glide as a w, so you never read it as an extra syllable.

{{ex:vi.s0036}}

Six glide spellings to know. Tap each one.

{{tiles:vi.tl009}}
Most common mistake: saying o, ô and ơ the same way, or adding an r. cơm said "kohm" or "kurm" → cơm said "kerm", with no r.

Read whole words

Now read words with glides and vowel pairs. However many vowel letters you see, one chunk is one syllable: ch + uô + i is chuối, choo-uhy/, one quick glide. A few cards add three more glide endings built the same way: eo (eh, then oo), ơi (er, then ee) and ui (oo, then ee). Every card below is a common word. Read it aloud before you flip it.
