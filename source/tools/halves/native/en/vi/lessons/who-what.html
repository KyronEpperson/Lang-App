English moves its question words to the front: "Who is he?", "What do you want?" Vietnamese does not. The question word sits exactly where the answer will go, and the rest of the sentence keeps its normal order.

ai: who

Start from an answer: Anh ấy là anh Minh. (He is Minh.) Now put ai in place of the name: Anh ấy là ai? Nothing moves and nothing is added.

{{ex:vi.s0084}}{{ex:vi.s0085}}

When "who" is the person doing something, the answer comes first in the sentence, so ai comes first too.

{{ex:vi.s0086}}

gì: what

gì works the same way. muốn means "want": Tôi muốn phở. (I want pho.) Put gì where the pho was, and you have the question.

cái gì: what thing

To ask what an object is, use cái gì, "what thing". You met cái in cái này (this one). On its own, Cái gì? is a quick "What?"

{{ex:vi.s0089}}

gì can also follow a noun, like any describing word: nghề gì is "what job".

Let the word do the asking

A question with ai or gì needs no không at the end and no rising voice: say it flat and keep each syllable's own tone. If you do add có … không, the meaning changes: Bạn có muốn gì không? means "Do you want anything?"

Most common mistake: moving the question word to the front, as in English. Gì bạn muốn? → Bạn muốn gì? Say the answer in your head, then swap in ai or gì.
