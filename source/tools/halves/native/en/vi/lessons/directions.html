You already know how to ask where something is: Ngân hàng ở đâu? This lesson adds the answers you will hear, how to ask about distance, and what to say to a taxi or motorbike-taxi driver.

Left, right, straight on

rẽ means turn. trái is left and phải is right, so "turn left" is rẽ trái. "Go straight" is đi thẳng. rồi joins the steps, like English "then". In the South you will also hear quẹo for turn. Mind the dipping tone on phải: with the rising tone, phái is a different word.

To ask for directions, use chỉ đường (show the way) and giúp (help), with the right kinship words. People often answer with a landmark instead of a street name: ở ngã tư (at the intersection), bên trái (on the left), bên phải (on the right).

{{ex:vi.s0194}}

Near, far, and how far

gần is near and xa is far. They work like any adjective: no là, and có … không makes the question.

{{ex:vi.s0195}}

To ask the distance, English starts with "How far". Vietnamese starts with the place, then cách đây (away from here), and puts bao xa (how far) at the end, where the answer will go. The answer keeps the same frame: Cách đây năm trăm mét (five hundred meters from here).

In a taxi or on a xe ôm

To a driver, Cho tôi đến… means "Take me to…", and Cho tôi xuống đây means "Let me off here". cho here means "let" or "give", so the frame is literally "let me arrive at…". It works with a motorbike taxi, a taxi, or a ride-hailing driver who calls to check where you are going. A place you point at still puts the noun first: khách sạn này (this hotel).

{{ex:vi.s0198}}{{ex:vi.s0199}}
Most common mistake: putting "how far" first, as in English. Bao xa Hồ Gươm cách đây? → Hồ Gươm cách đây bao xa? The question word waits at the end.
