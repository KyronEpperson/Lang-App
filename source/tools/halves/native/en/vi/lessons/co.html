English uses "have" for what you own and "there is / there are" for what exists somewhere. Vietnamese uses one word for both: có.

có: have

Person, then có, then the thing. Counted things keep their classifier.

{{ex:vi.s0110}}{{ex:vi.s0111}}

có: there is, there are

Say the place first, then có, then what is there. ở đây means "here".

{{ex:vi.s0112}}{{ex:vi.s0113}}

When the place is clear from the situation, leave it out and start with có: Có wifi không? is a quick "Is there wifi?" at a café.

English "there is" never becomes là. là joins two nouns ("A is B"); it does not say that something exists.

không có: don't have, there isn't

Put không before có, as before any verb.

{{ex:vi.s0114}}

Asking: có … không?

You already ask yes/no questions with có … không. With "have", the có is the verb itself, so the question is simply có + thing + không. This is how you ask in a shop or at a stall.

{{ex:vi.s0115}}{{ex:vi.s0116}}

You can ask about one particular thing too. Keep its classifier and này: Chị có quyển sách này không? (Do you have this book?)

Answer yes with Có. Answer no with Không or Không có. A seller may soften it: Dạ, không có ạ.

{{ex:vi.s0117}}

Not for age

Don't stretch có to ages. "I'm twenty" is Tôi hai mươi tuổi, with no có and no là.

Most common mistake: using là for "there is". Ở đây là nhà vệ sinh không? → Ở đây có nhà vệ sinh không?
