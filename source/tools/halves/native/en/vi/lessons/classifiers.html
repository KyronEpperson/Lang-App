English counts a few things with a helper word: "two cups of coffee", "a pair of shoes". Vietnamese does this with almost every noun. The helper word is called a classifier (or counter word), and it goes between the number and the noun.

If you are unsure, cái works for most objects. It never works for animals or people.

Pointing: classifier + noun + này

You met cái bàn này (this table). The classifier goes in front, này or kia at the end, and a number before everything.

{{ex:vi.s0105}}{{ex:vi.s0106}}

Describing words still come after the noun

The order is number, classifier, noun, describing word: ba cái áo đỏ.

{{ex:vi.s0107}}

No classifier for things in general

When you talk about a kind of thing, not about counted or pointed-at ones, leave the classifier out. Meat is the same: thịt gà is chicken to eat, and con gà is a live bird.

{{ex:vi.s0108}}{{ex:vi.s0109}}

No plural ending

Vietnamese nouns never change for "more than one". The number does the work: một con mèo, hai con mèo.

Most common mistake: counting the English way, with no word between the number and the noun. hai mèo → hai con mèo; and never cái mèo, because animals take con.
