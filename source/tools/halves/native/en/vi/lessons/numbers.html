Vietnamese numbers are built like blocks: learn ten words and three small changes, and you can count to 99.

Every tone mark counts: ba is three, but bà is a grandmother.

Eleven to nineteen

Say "ten" and then the digit: mười một (11), mười hai (12). One change: five after ten becomes lăm, so 15 is mười lăm.

{{ex:vi.s0092}}{{ex:vi.s0093}}

The tens

English has special words: twenty, thirty. Vietnamese says "two ten", "three ten": hai mươi, ba mươi. In the tens, ten loses its tone mark: mười (falling tone) becomes mươi (level tone).

{{ex:vi.s0094}}

Three changes after mươi

  • một becomes mốt: 21 is hai mươi mốt. (11 stays mười một.)
  • năm becomes lăm: 25 is hai mươi lăm.
  • bốn often becomes tư: 24 is hai mươi tư (hai mươi bốn is also fine).

A hundred is một trăm.

How many: bao nhiêu and mấy

bao nhiêu asks for any number. mấy is for small numbers you expect to be under ten, and for children's ages. Like ai and gì, both stay where the answer goes.

{{ex:vi.s0098}}{{ex:vi.s0099}}

The answer has no "have" and no là: just the person, the number and tuổi (years old).

{{ex:vi.s0100}}

Counting things

When you count things, a small word goes between the number and the noun: hai con mèo, two cats. The next lesson is all about these words.

Most common mistake: building the tens the English way, with the plain words for ten and five. hai mười năm → hai mươi lăm (25).
