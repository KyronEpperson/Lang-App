Vietnamese family words do two jobs. They name your relatives, and they are also the everyday words for "you" and "I". You already use anh, chị and em this way; this lesson adds the rest.

The family

WordRelativeAlso "you" to…
bố, mẹfather, mother (South: ba, má)
anh trai, chị gáiolder brother, older sistera man, a woman a little older
em trai, em gáiyounger brother, younger sisteranyone younger

chú, côuncle, aunta man, a woman your parents' age
cháugrandchild; niece or nephew("I", to ông, bà, chú, cô)

Older or younger first

English says brother or sister first. Vietnamese says older or younger first: anh and chị are older, em is younger, either sex. Add trai (male) or gái (female) after the word when you need to say which.

The same words for "you" and "I"

To someone your parents' age, say chú (a man) or cô (a woman), and call yourself cháu. To old people, ông or bà, and you are cháu again. Add ạ at the end for respect.

More than one "you"

Put các in front: các bạn (you, friends), các anh chị (you, older people).

{{ex:vi.s0132}}

Choose by age, not by title

English "Mrs." works for any married woman. bà is for an old woman only: a woman of thirty-five is chị, and a woman your mother's age is cô. When you hesitate between two of these words for a woman, pick the younger one: chị rather than cô, cô rather than bà.

Most common mistake: using bà as "Mrs." for any woman. To a woman of about thirty: Chào bà → Chào chị.
