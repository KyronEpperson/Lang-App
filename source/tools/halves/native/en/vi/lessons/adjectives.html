English needs "is" before a describing word: "The pho is good." Vietnamese adjectives work like verbs, so they need nothing in between: "Pho good." This is why là, which joins two nouns, never goes before an adjective.

No là

{{ex:vi.s0075}}{{ex:vi.s0052}}

Some everyday adjectives: ngon (tasty), đẹp (pretty), mệt (tired), nóng (hot), lạnh (cold), đắt (expensive), rẻ (cheap), to (big), nhỏ (small). Careful: to is not the English word "to". You will hear many of these all day at markets and food stalls.

After the noun

English puts the adjective first: "a pretty shirt". Vietnamese puts the noun first: "shirt pretty". Colors work the same way: đỏ is red. It is the same noun-first order you saw with nationalities and languages.

{{ex:vi.s0076}}{{ex:vi.s0077}}

Because adjectives work like verbs, phở ngon can be a phrase ("good pho") or a whole sentence ("The pho is good"). Context tells you which. Very and so: rất, lắm, quá

Use one of them, not two. rất sounds a little neutral, lắm is chatty, and quá is an exclamation, perfect at a street stall when the food arrives.

Asking about adjectives

An adjective fits straight into có … không, just like a verb. To answer yes, repeat the adjective; to answer no, put không in front of it.

{{ex:vi.s0081}}{{ex:vi.s0082}}

Not: không, without phải

không phải là is only for nouns. For an adjective, just put không in front. It is the same rule you met in the lesson on là, seen from the other side.

{{ex:vi.s0083}}
Most common mistake: adding the English "is". Tôi là mệt. → Tôi mệt. And keep the noun first: đẹp áo → áo đẹp.
