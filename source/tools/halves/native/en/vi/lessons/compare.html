English compares in two ways: add -er (colder) or put "more" in front (more expensive). Vietnamese has one way for every adjective: put hơn after it.

hơn: more … than

Adjective, then hơn, then the other thing. As with every adjective, there is no là.

{{ex:vi.s0133}}{{ex:vi.s0134}}

If the other thing is clear, stop after hơn. Nothing else changes: the same word works for short adjectives and long ones, where English would switch from -er to "more".

{{ex:vi.s0135}}

bằng: as … as

bằng means "equal to". It also goes after the adjective. For "not as … as", put không before the adjective.

{{ex:vi.s0136}}{{ex:vi.s0137}}

nhất: the most

English says "the coldest", "the most expensive". Vietnamese puts nhất after the adjective, with no word for "the".

{{ex:vi.s0138}}

The noun still comes first

English puts the whole description in front: "the best pho place in Hanoi". Vietnamese keeps the noun first and everything else after it: quán phở (pho place), then ngon nhất (tastiest), then the place.

{{ex:vi.s0139}}

Much more

To say "much colder", add nhiều (a lot) at the end: lạnh hơn nhiều. Don't put rất (very) in front of hơn or nhất: they already do its job, so rất lạnh hơn is wrong.

{{ex:vi.s0140}}

Useful adjectives for comparing: to or lớn (big), nhỏ (small), cao (tall), đắt (expensive), rẻ (cheap), nóng (hot), lạnh (cold).

Most common mistake: putting the comparing word in front, the way English puts "more" and "most". hơn lạnh → lạnh hơn; nhất ngon quán → quán ngon nhất.
