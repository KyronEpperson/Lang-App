How Vietnamese writing works

Vietnamese is written in the same Latin letters as English, so you can start reading on day one. The catch: many letters stand for different sounds than in English. Under every Vietnamese phrase in this course there is a pronunciation line in plain English letters. This lesson teaches you to read both.

The spelling is regular: once you know the pieces, you can say any word you see. Each written chunk between spaces is one syllable, built from a first sound (one letter or a pair such as nh, sometimes none), a vowel, maybe a last sound, and a tone: five of the six tones have a mark. These four lessons teach the pieces in order: the letters here, then the tones, then how a syllable is built, then the vowels.

Seven extra letters

The alphabet has 29 letters. f, j, w and z appear only in foreign words. Seven letters carry a vowel mark or a stroke: ă â ê ô ơ ư and đ, the crossed d. Each one is a separate letter, not a decoration, so leaving the mark off writes a different letter.

Here are all 29. Tap a letter to see how it reads. The seven new ones have their own cards below. Most of the others sound close to what an English reader expects; the ones that do not come next.

Reading the pronunciation line

Vietnamese puts a space after every syllable, and the line has one chunk for each written syllable. A hyphen inside a chunk is still one syllable: Việt is vee-uht., one quick glide. There are no capitals in the line, because no syllable is stressed more than another.

{{ex:vi.s0008}}

The symbol at the end of a chunk names the tone, which the next lesson teaches: nothing for the level tone, then / rising, _ falling, ? dipping, ~ broken and . heavy.

Vowel spellings in the line

The line uses fixed vowel spellings: ah (father), a (a short ah), uh (cup), eh (bed), ey (hey), ee (see), aw (law), oh (go), er (her), oo (food) and uu (oo said with a smile). er never has an r sound: it is the vowel of her with the r left off.

{{ex:vi.s0009}}

The line is a crutch. Listen to the audio first, read the line second, and hide it once the letters feel natural.

Read your first words

Now put the letters together. Read a syllable left to right: first sound, vowel, last sound. đ + ỏ is đỏ, daw?: đ is d, o is aw, and the hook is a tone mark (the next lesson teaches the tones; for now, trust the symbol in the line). Every card below is a common word spelled with the letters of this lesson. Read it aloud before you flip it.

Most common mistake: reading d as an English d. da said "dah" → da said "zah". The English d sound belongs to the crossed d: đi.
