At a Vietnamese market you need three things: a way to ask the price, numbers in the thousands, and a few words to react and bargain.

How much? Bao nhiêu tiền?

tiền means money, so bao nhiêu tiền is "how much money". The usual frame names the thing first and asks after it.

{{ex:vi.s0186}}{{ex:vi.s0187}}

The seller's answer usually leaves out đồng (the currency), because everyone knows it. When the context is clear, you can drop tiền too, and put the amount first: Một cân xoài bao nhiêu? is "How much for a kilo of mangoes?".

Prices count thousands

Dong prices are big numbers, so Vietnamese counts in nghìn (thousand). Luckily the order is the same as in English: say the number of thousands, then nghìn. A million is một triệu. In the South you will hear ngàn for nghìn. Price tags and menus often write 50k or 50.000, with a dot where English uses a comma. Read it as the whole price, năm mươi nghìn, not as "fifty".

Too expensive! Bargaining

đắt is expensive and rẻ is cheap. Add quá for "too" or "so". At a market, not in a supermarket, you can ask for a little off with bớt, or name your own price with được không? (is that OK?). Keep it light and friendly: a smile gets you further than a hard line, and when you agree, Tôi lấy cái này (I'll take this one) closes the deal.

Colors come after the noun

English puts the color first: "a red shirt". Vietnamese puts the thing first and the color after it, with or without màu (color): áo đỏ or áo màu đỏ. To point at one, add the classifier in front and này at the end.

{{ex:vi.s0115}}{{ex:vi.s0191}}
Most common mistake: the color first, as in English. đỏ áo → áo đỏ. The thing comes first, then what it looks like.
