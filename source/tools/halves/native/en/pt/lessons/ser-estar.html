You now know both verbs for "be". This lesson puts them side by side. Forget a rule you may have heard, "ser is permanent, estar is temporary": it fails too often. Ask instead what you are describing. Is it what someone or something is, or an event? Then ser. Is it where a person or thing is, or how someone is at the moment? Then estar.

ser: what someone or something is

  • who or what: Ela é médica.
  • where from: Sou de Recife.
  • what someone or something is like: A cidade é bonita.
  • time: É cedo. (It's early.)
  • where an event takes place: A festa é na casa da Ana.

estar: where, and how right now

  • where a person or thing is: A Ana está na praia.
  • how someone feels or is at the moment: Estou nervoso.

One adjective, two messages

Some adjectives work with both verbs, and the verb changes the message. Ela é bonita: she's a pretty person. Ela está bonita: she looks pretty today. A sopa está fria: the soup has gone cold. For being married you will hear both Ele é casado and Ele está casado.

Events, people, buildings

"Where" can take all three verbs, depending on what is there.

What is thereVerbExample
an eventserA reunião é no escritório.
a person or a movable thingestarA Ana está no escritório.
a building or placeficarO escritório fica no centro.
{{ex:pt.s0075}}

ficar: become

Ficar also means "get" or "become": Fico nervoso is "I get nervous". You will hear its past, Fiquei feliz (I was glad), all the time.

{{ex:pt.s0076}}
Most common mistake: using estar for an event because the sentence says where. A festa está na casa da Ana. → A festa é na casa da Ana.
