Asking the way in Portuguese takes a handful of phrases. Start politely with Com licença (excuse me).

Asking where

For buildings, stations and streets, Brazilians ask Onde fica...?, as you learned with estar and ficar. To ask whether something is nearby, use Tem... por aqui? (Is there... around here?).

Understanding the answer

À direita is "on the right" or "to the right", à esquerda is "on the left", em frente is "straight ahead", and na esquina is "on the corner". The à is ‹a› + ‹a›, and it needs its accent mark: a direita without it is just "the right". After à it is always the feminine direita, never direito.

Buses, the metro and rides

English "takes" a bus; Brazilians pegar (literally "catch") a bus, the metro, a taxi or an Uber. To say how you travel, use de: de ônibus, de metrô. In big cities many people simply say they are going by Uber, with the same de. Bus stops are pontos, and the name of the final stop is shown on the front of the bus.

Addresses

A street is a rua and an avenue is a avenida, both feminine, so "on" a street is na. English puts the house number first; Portuguese puts it after the street name, often with the word número. To ask for an address, say Qual é o endereço?

{{ex:pt.s0210}}{{ex:pt.s0211}}

Near or far?

Before you walk, ask whether it is close: perto means near and longe means far. If someone asks you the way and you cannot help, say that you are not from here.
