For something finished ("I worked", "she ate", "we went"), Portuguese uses the pretérito perfeito, the past tense for finished actions. As in the present, the verb ending shows who did it.

Verbs ending in -car, -gar and -çar change their spelling in the eu form to keep the same sound: ficar → fiquei, chegar → cheguei, começar → comecei.

falamos: now or before?

For regular verbs the nós form is the same in the present and the past: falamos is both "we speak" and "we spoke". A time word tells you which: ontem (yesterday), no sábado (on Saturday), semana passada (last week), no fim de semana (on the weekend).

Ir and ser share their past forms, so fui is "I went" or "I was"; the sentence makes it clear. These four verbs come up in almost every story about your day, so learn them first.

Talking about the weekend

On Monday, coworkers ask about your weekend. The question uses the você form fez; your answer uses eu forms, joined with e. Notice that English needs "did" to ask, but Portuguese just uses the past form with a question tone.

"Have you ever...?": já

English uses "have done" for experiences. Portuguese has a form like tenho feito, but it means something else: an action repeated up to now ("I have been doing"). For "ever" and "already", use já + the pretérito perfeito. A short answer is Já (yes, I have) or Ainda não (not yet).
