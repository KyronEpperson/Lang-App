English softens requests with "could you" and "I'd like". Portuguese has its own soft phrases, and por favor goes with all of them.

‹Pode...?› Can you...?

English needs "could you" or "would you" to sound polite. In Portuguese a friendly tone and por favor do most of that work. Pode + infinitive is the everyday way to ask someone to do something. Say Você pode...? or just Pode...?. To an older person, use o senhor or a senhora, with the same ‹ele› form, pode.

‹Me dá...?› at the counter

At a bakery or a snack bar, Brazilians often ask with Me dá...? (literally "give me...?"), with the pronoun first, as in the lesson on object pronouns. With por favor it is friendly, not rude. In Portugal: the order is Dá-me.

{{ex:pt.s0187}}

‹Eu queria...› I'd like

Eu queria (literally "I wanted") is the polite way to order or ask for something, much like English "I'd like". Eu quero is fine with friends and family but sounds blunt to a waiter or a pharmacist.

{{ex:pt.s0188}}

Commands you will hear

You will not need to give orders at this level, but you will hear them. Signs, strangers and staff use forms like Vire (turn), Siga (go on, keep going) and Espere (wait). Friends use the same form as você fala: Fala mais devagar! and Espera aí! (Wait a second!).

{{ex:pt.s0189}}{{ex:pt.s0190}}

In an emergency

Socorro! means "Help!", for real emergencies only. For a specific favor, use Pode...?, and when someone helps you, thank them with obrigado or obrigada, which agrees with you, the speaker.

{{ex:pt.s0191}}
Most common mistake: ordering with ‹quero›. Eu quero um café (blunt to a waiter) → Eu queria um café, por favor.
