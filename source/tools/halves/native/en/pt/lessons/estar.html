Lesson 4 used ser for who you are. Portuguese has a second verb for "be", estar, for where someone is and how they are right now.

The forms

Subject‹estar›
euestou
você, ele, ela, a genteestá
nósestamos
vocês, eles, elasestão

Mind the accent on está: it carries the stress and the meaning. Without it, esta means "this".

Where people and things are

For where a person is, or a thing that can move, use estar. A name often takes o or a in Brazil (a Ana, o Pedro); it is optional.

{{ex:pt.s0055}}{{ex:pt.s0056}}

How you are right now

English says "I'm fine, I'm tired". Portuguese uses estar for how someone is at the moment, and the adjective still agrees: a woman says Estou cansada. "Fine" is bem, which never changes.

To ask, Como você está? works anywhere, though Tudo bem? is more common. English "I'm good" invites a word-for-word copy with bom, but the everyday way to say you are fine is Estou bem or just Tudo bem.

{{ex:pt.s0057}}{{ex:pt.s0058}}

Where places are: ‹ficar›

Buildings and places do not move, and for them Brazilians usually ask with ficar: Onde fica o banco? (Where is the bank?). You will also hear Onde é o banco?. The answer uses ficar too. Fica is the ‹ele› form; for more than one place, say ficam: Onde ficam os banheiros?

{{ex:pt.s0059}}{{ex:pt.s0060}}

What you will hear: ‹tô› and ‹tá›

In speech, estou often shrinks to tô and está to tá: Tô bem, Ela tá aqui. These are short forms of the same verb. Recognize them, and write estou and está in your answers.

Most common mistake: using ser for how you feel right now. Sou cansado. → Estou cansado.
