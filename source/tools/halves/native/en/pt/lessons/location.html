English puts one small word before the place: "near the beach", "on the table". Portuguese uses short phrases that end in de, and that de joins the article after it, just as in the lesson on contractions.

‹de› + the article = one word

English keeps "of" and "the" apart. Portuguese fuses them: de + o = do, de + a = da, de + os = dos, de + as = das. The contraction agrees with the noun after it, so check that noun's gender: perto do mercado (‹o mercado›), perto da farmácia (‹a farmácia›).

When no article follows, de stays alone (perto de Boston), or fuses with the next word: longe daqui (‹de› + ‹aqui›).

‹estar› or ‹ficar›

Some of these phrases already contain a contraction: na frente de is em + a frente, and ao lado de is a + o lado. Learn them as fixed chunks.

Remember: people and things that move use estar (A chave está dentro da bolsa); buildings and fixed places use ficar (O banco fica atrás da igreja).

Here and there

English has "here" and "there". Portuguese has aqui (here, near me), aí (there, near you), and ali and lá (over there, away from both of us; lá is usually farther).

{{ex:pt.s0107}}
Most common mistake: leaving de and the article apart, or giving the contraction the wrong gender. ao lado de o banco → ao lado do banco; perto do farmácia → perto da farmácia.
