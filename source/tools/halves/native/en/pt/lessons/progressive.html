English adds -ing to say what is going on right now: "I'm working". Portuguese does the same with estar plus a verb ending in -ndo.

Making the -ndo form

Take the infinitive, drop the final r, and add -ndo: falar → falando, comer → comendo, abrir → abrindo. Almost every verb follows this rule: even ir gives indo, and fazer gives fazendo. (Grammar books call this form the gerund.)

Only estar changes

The -ndo word never changes for a man, a woman or a group. All the work is done by estar, which agrees with the subject. Você and a gente take está, like ele.

Right now or every day?

English uses "I work" for habits and "I'm working" for now, and Portuguese splits them the same way. The simple present is for routines and facts; estar + -ndo is for this moment, or for "these days".

{{ex:pt.s0149}}{{ex:pt.s0150}}

Plans are different

English also uses -ing for plans: "I'm traveling tomorrow". Portuguese uses ir + infinitive for those: Vou viajar amanhã. The big exception is Estou indo!, the everyday "I'm on my way!" or "I'm leaving now".

Asking what someone is doing

English asks "What are you doing?" Portuguese uses the same pattern, with fazer in the -ndo form. The answer usually repeats estar in the right person, followed by the new verb.

{{ex:pt.s0153}}{{ex:pt.s0154}}

In Portugal: you will hear estar a + infinitive (estou a falar) for the same idea. Brazilians say estou falando.

Most common mistake: keeping the infinitive after estar. Estou falar com ela → Estou falando com ela.
