English often talks about plans with "going to": "I'm going to travel." Portuguese does the same, with the verb ir you already know, and it is the normal way to talk about the future in Brazil.

ir + infinitive

Take the right form of ir and add the infinitive of the main verb. Nothing goes between them: there is no word for "to".

Only ir changes for the person: vou for eu, vai for você, ele, ela and a gente, vamos for nós, vão for vocês, eles and elas. The second verb always stays in the infinitive, whoever the subject is.

The weather works the same way: vai chover (it's going to rain), vai fazer calor (it's going to be hot).

{{ex:pt.s0126}}

Saying when

Useful time words: hoje (today), amanhã (tomorrow), hoje à noite (tonight), no sábado (on Saturday), na segunda (on Monday), semana que vem (next week), mês que vem (next month). Like in English, they can go at the start or at the end of the sentence.

{{ex:pt.s0127}}

Saying no

Put não right before the form of ir.

{{ex:pt.s0128}}

The other future

Portuguese also has a one-word future, such as falarei (I will speak). You will see it in writing and hear it in formal speeches, but in conversation Brazilians say vou falar.

Most common mistake: adding a word between the two verbs, or conjugating both. If you know Spanish, its a doesn't belong here: Vou a viajar → Vou viajar. And only ir changes: Vou viajo → Vou viajar.
