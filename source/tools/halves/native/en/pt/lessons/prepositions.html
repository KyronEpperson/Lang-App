English prepositions like to, for, by, about do not map one to one onto Portuguese. The safest habit is to learn each Portuguese preposition together with the verb or phrase it belongs to.

para: where to, what for, who for

English says "to São Paulo", "to learn" and "for you". Portuguese uses para for all three: the place you are heading, the purpose of something, and the person who gets it.

{{ex:pt.s0137}}{{ex:pt.s0138}}

In speech para often shrinks to pra. You will hear it everywhere; in typed answers para is the safe choice, and pra is accepted too.

por: a short list to memorize

At this level, keep por to a few fixed uses: por favor (please), obrigado por (thanks for), and "through" or "around" a place. Like em and de, por joins the article: por + o = pelo, por + a = pela, and the result agrees with the noun.

{{ex:pt.s0139}}{{ex:pt.s0140}}

de + vehicle, but a pé

English says "by car" and "by bus". Portuguese says de carro, de ônibus, de metrô, de avião, with no article. The one exception is "on foot": a pé. No carro means "in the car", not "by car".

{{ex:pt.s0141}}

com, and comigo

Com is "with": com você, com a Ana. With eu it becomes one word, comigo (with me). Never say com eu.

{{ex:pt.s0142}}

Verbs that bring their own preposition

Some verbs always come with a partner, like gostar de. Three more: precisar de (need), pensar em (think about) and chegar em (arrive at; this is everyday Brazilian speech, and formal writing uses chegar a). Before another verb, precisar usually drops the de: Preciso ir.

{{ex:pt.s0143}}{{ex:pt.s0144}}{{ex:pt.s0145}}
Most common mistake: dropping the partner, or translating it from English. Preciso ajuda → Preciso de ajuda; Obrigado para tudo → Obrigado por tudo.
