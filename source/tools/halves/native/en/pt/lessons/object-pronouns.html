English puts the object after the verb: "help me", "I love you". Brazilian Portuguese puts the short object pronouns before the verb.

Te is the object form used with você in everyday speech, even by people who never say tu. A question like Você me ajuda? is also the everyday way to ask for a favor, and in speech the pronoun can even start the sentence: Me ajuda?

For "us", writing uses nos before the verb, while speech usually puts a gente after it, like a noun. Nos (us) has no accent; nós (we) does.

him, her, them: two systems

In everyday speech Brazilians use ele, ela, eles, elas after the verb, just like English "him". In writing and formal speech the pronoun is o, a, os, as, placed before the verb. They look exactly like the articles, so read carefully: in Eu o conheço, o means "him".

{{ex:pt.s0168}}{{ex:pt.s0169}}

When you hear o or a right before a verb, it is a pronoun, not "the". In short answers Brazilians often leave the object out completely, where English must say "her" or "it".

{{ex:pt.s0170}}

You will also hear Eu vi ele ontem (I saw him yesterday). Vi is the past of ver, which comes in the next lesson.

{{ex:pt.s0171}}

With two verbs

When a verb like poder or ir comes first, the pronoun goes before the second verb, the infinitive: Vou te ligar (I'm going to call you).

{{ex:pt.s0172}}

lhe, comigo, contigo

In formal service you may hear lhe for "you" or "to you". After com, "me" is comigo, and "you" is com você or contigo.

Most common mistake: English word order. Você ajuda me? → Você me ajuda?
