English replaces a thing with "it" or "them" and puts it after the verb: "I'll buy it." Spanish uses lo, la, los, las and puts them before the verb: Lo compro. That is two differences at once, so take them one at a time.

Four words for "it" and "them"

Spanish has no neutral "it". The pronoun agrees in gender and number with the noun it replaces. The same four words also mean "him", "her" and "them" for people.

el suéter, Carlosloit, him
la camisa, Analait, her
los zapatoslosthem
las llaveslasthem (all feminine)

Before the verb

The pronoun goes right before the conjugated verb. With no, the order is no, then the pronoun, then the verb.

People: no a, no ella

With a person, you say Llamo a Ana with the personal a. When you replace Ana, both the a and her name go, and la moves before the verb. Never use ella or él after the verb for "her" or "him".

{{ex:es.s0181}}

With ir a + infinitive: two places

When there is an infinitive, the pronoun can go before the conjugated verb, or be attached to the end of the infinitive as one word. Both mean the same and both are correct.

{{ex:es.s0182}}{{ex:es.s0183}}

In a store: Me lo llevo

To say "I'll take it" in a store, use Me lo llevo. The lo changes to agree with what you are buying: Me la llevo for la camisa, Me los llevo for los zapatos.

{{ex:es.s0184}}
The most common mistake is putting the pronoun after the verb, as in English: Compro la → La compro. The next is using lo for everything: ¿La camisa? Lo compro → ¿La camisa? La compro.
