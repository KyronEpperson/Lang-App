English "to be" has three forms: am, are, is. Spanish has two verbs for "to be". This lesson covers the first, ser, which you use to say who someone is, where they are from and what they do.

Subject pronouns and ‹ser›

The subject is who the sentence is about. Each subject has its own form of the verb.

Subject‹ser›English
yosoyI am
túeresyou are (a friend)
él, ella, ustedeshe is, she is, you are (a stranger)
nosotros, nosotrassomoswe are
ellos, ellas, ustedessonthey are, you all are

Two things to notice. Usted takes the same form as él, and ustedes the same form as ellos. And nosotras and ellas are for groups of women only; any mixed group is nosotros or ellos.

Drop the pronoun

English always needs "I" or "you". In Spanish the verb ending already tells you who, so soy alone means "I am". Native speakers usually leave yo, tú and nosotros out, and keep a pronoun only for contrast or to make clear who is meant.

Where you are from: ‹ser de›

Origin always uses ser de, "to be from". To ask a friend, say ¿De dónde eres?; to ask a stranger, ¿De dónde es usted?

Jobs: no ‹un›, no ‹una›

English says "I'm a nurse". Spanish leaves the article out: Soy enfermera. Many job words change for a man or a woman, just like the people words in the gender lesson: enfermero, enfermera; maestro, maestra. Words ending in -e, like estudiante, stay the same.

Most common mistake: copying the English "a" before a job. Soy un estudiante → Soy estudiante.
