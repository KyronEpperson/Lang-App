Three of the most useful verbs in Spanish are irregular: tener (to have), ir (to go) and hacer (to do, to make). They are also where English speakers copy English most often.

The forms

Tener and hacer are ‹yo›-irregular verbs: the ‹yo› form is odd (tengo, hago). Tener also changes its stem in three forms (tienes, tiene, tienen). You will meet more verbs like that in the next lesson. Ir is irregular everywhere, but its endings still follow the subject.

‹tener› for age, hunger and cold

English says "I am 25" and "I am hungry". Spanish says "I have 25 years" and "I have hunger": tener plus a noun. The same goes for thirst, cold and heat in your body: tengo sed, tengo frío, tengo calor.

‹hacer› for the weather

English says "it is cold". For the weather, Spanish says "it makes cold": hace frío, hace calor, hace sol, hace buen tiempo. Keep it apart from tengo frío, which is about how your body feels.

{{ex:es.s0105}}{{ex:es.s0106}}

Hacer is also the verb in everyday questions about plans: ¿Qué haces? means "What are you doing?"

{{ex:es.s0107}}

‹ir a› plus a place

To say where someone goes, use ir with a (to) and the place. The present can also mean a plan for soon, as in English "I'm going to the beach on Saturday."

English speakers say "I am" where Spanish says "I have". Soy veinticinco años → Tengo veinticinco años. Estoy hambre → Tengo hambre. For the weather: Es frío → Hace frío.
