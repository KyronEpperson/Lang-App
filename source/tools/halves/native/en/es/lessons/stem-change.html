English verbs keep the same middle: want, wants. Some very common Spanish verbs change the vowel in their stem when that vowel is stressed. The endings stay regular, so you already know half the work.

Other common ones: preferir and pensar (‹e› → ‹ie›), dormir and costar (‹o› → ‹ue›). Textbooks also call these "boot verbs", because in a full table the changing forms make the shape of a boot.

‹nosotros› keeps the stem

The ‹nosotros› form does not change: its stress falls on the ending, not the stem. So it is queremos, podemos, pedimos, exactly like the infinitive. The ‹ustedes› form is the same as the ‹ellos› form, so it does change: ustedes pueden.

{{ex:es.s0113}}

Verb plus infinitive

As in English, querer, poder and preferir are often followed by a second verb in the infinitive: quiero descansar (I want to rest), puedo ir (I can go). Only the first verb changes for the person.

Asking a stranger for help

To a stranger, use the ‹usted› form, which is the same as the ‹él› form: ¿Puede…? (Can you…?). This is the most useful question in an emergency.

{{ex:es.s0114}}{{ex:es.s0115}}

‹pedir› or ‹preguntar›?

English uses "ask" for two things. Spanish splits them: pedir means to ask for something (help, a coffee, a doctor), and preguntar, a regular verb, means to ask a question. In a restaurant, pedir is also the verb for ordering: pido unos tacos.

{{ex:es.s0116}}{{ex:es.s0117}}
Don't change the ‹nosotros› form. Nosotros quieremos → Nosotros queremos. And keep "ask for" apart: Pregunto ayuda → Pido ayuda.
