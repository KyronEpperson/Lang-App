English verbs barely change: I work, you work, she works. Spanish changes the end of the verb for every person, and that ending tells you who is doing the action.

Stem plus ending

Every verb has an infinitive, like hablar (to speak), comer (to eat) or vivir (to live). Take off -ar, -er or -ir and you have the stem: habl-, com-, viv-. Then add the ending that matches the subject.

‹hablar›‹comer›‹vivir›
yohablocomovivo
túhablascomesvives
él, ella, ustedhablacomevive
nosotroshablamoscomemosvivimos
ellos, ellas, ustedeshablancomenviven

In Spain you will also hear vosotros habláis for "you all".

The -er and -ir endings are the same except in the ‹nosotros› form: comemos, but vivimos.

You can drop the subject pronoun

English needs "I" or "we" in every sentence. In Spanish the ending already says who, so people usually leave out yo, tú and nosotros. Saying yo every time sounds heavy. Keep the pronoun for contrast: Yo estudio, ella trabaja.

{{ex:es.s0072}}

‹usted› and ‹ustedes›

English has one "you". Spanish has three. To a friend, use the ‹tú› form: hablas. To a stranger, use usted, which takes the same form as él: habla. For more than one person, friends or strangers, use ustedes, which takes the ‹ellos› form: hablan.

{{ex:es.s0073}}{{ex:es.s0074}}

One form for "I study" and "I'm studying"

English has two presents. Spanish uses the simple present for both: Estudio español means "I study Spanish" and also "I'm studying Spanish" these days. Estoy estudiando exists, but it is for this very moment, not for your routine or this year's plans.

The most common mistake is one form for everyone. Nosotros trabaja → Nosotros trabajamos. Match the ending to the subject every time.
