Hola works with anyone, at any hour. To sound warmer or more polite, add the greeting for the time of day.

Morning, afternoon, evening

English says "good morning". Spanish says "good days": buenos días. After about noon it is buenas tardes, and after dark buenas noches, which means both "good evening" and "good night".

{{ex:es.s0013}}{{ex:es.s0014}}

Why buenos in one and buenas in the others? Días is a masculine word and tardes and noches are feminine, and the word for "good" changes to match. This is your first taste of agreement, which runs through all of Spanish.

Two ways to say "you"

English has one "you". Spanish has two for one person. Use tú with friends, family, children and people your age in relaxed settings. Use usted with strangers, older people, customers and staff. The verb changes too: estás goes with tú, está with usted.

When in doubt, start with usted. If the other person wants tú, they will switch, and you can follow.

Names

To give your name, say me llamo, literally "I call myself". It already contains "my name is", so nothing else goes in between. When you meet someone, say mucho gusto, "nice to meet you".

Small polite words

Por favor is "please", gracias is "thank you", and the answer is de nada, "you're welcome". To leave, say adiós or hasta luego, "see you later".

{{ex:es.s0020}}

In Spain you will also hear vosotros for "you all". Latin America uses ustedes for any group, and so does this course.

Most common mistake: one form of "good" for every greeting. Buenos tardes → buenas tardes. Only días takes buenos.
