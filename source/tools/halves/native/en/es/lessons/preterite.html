English has one simple past: "I worked, I ate, I went." Spanish has more than one past, and the preterite is the one for finished actions: what you did yesterday, last night or last weekend.

Regular endings

Take off -ar, -er or -ir and add these endings. The verb ending matches the subject, as in the present. -er and -ir verbs share one set.

The accent mark changes the meaning

In hablo the stress is on HA, and it means "I speak". In habló the stress moves to the end, and it means "he or she spoke". The written accent is the only difference on paper, so always write it, and say it with the stress at the end.

{{ex:es.s0188}}{{ex:es.s0189}}

Three irregular verbs: ‹fui›, ‹hice›, ‹tuve›

Three very common verbs have their own forms, with no accent marks: ir (yo fui, tú fuiste, él fue), hacer (hice, hiciste, hizo) and tener (tuve, tuviste, tuvo).

{{ex:es.s0190}}{{ex:es.s0191}}

Useful time words: ayer (yesterday), anoche (last night), el fin de semana pasado and la semana pasada, where pasado agrees with its noun.

‹Ya›: "have you…?" in Latin America

Where English says "Have you eaten yet?" or "I've been to Cancún", Latin American Spanish usually uses the preterite, often with ya (already, yet).

{{ex:es.s0192}}

What you will hear in Spain: ‹he comido›

In Spain, people say he comido ("I have eaten") for things done today. In Latin America you'll hear it mostly for "lately" or "so far". Just recognize it for now.

{{ex:es.s0193}}
The mistake to watch for is leaving off the accent mark, or keeping the present: Ayer hablo con Ana → Ayer hablé con Ana.
