Asking the way is one of the first real conversations you will have, and it is usually with a stranger, so it happens in usted. You already know ¿Dónde está…? from the lesson on estar. This lesson gives you the answers.

Right, left and straight ahead

derecha is "right" and izquierda is "left", and both come with a la: a la derecha, a la izquierda. derecho means "straight ahead". English speakers mix up derecho and derecha because they look alike, so remember: -a turns, -o goes straight.

One place or several

The verb agrees with the place you ask about: one place takes está, two or more take están.

{{ex:es.s0164}}

What a stranger will say to you

People giving directions use ‹usted› commands: verb forms that tell you to do something. You don't need to make them yet, just catch them. Three cover most directions: siga (keep going), gire (turn) and cruce (cross). In Mexico you will also hear dé vuelta for "turn".

Asking politely

Start with Disculpe (the ‹usted› form of "excuse me"), then ask. If you don't know whether there is one nearby, use hay. When someone answers, say Muchas gracias. If you didn't catch it, ask ¿Cómo? and listen for the three command words above, plus derecha, izquierda and derecho.

‹derecho› goes straight and ‹derecha› turns right: Está a la derecho → Está a la derecha. And join the little words: al lado de el banco → al lado del banco.
