English has one "to be". Spanish splits it in two, and you now know both. The choice between them is the most famous trap for English speakers, so this lesson gives you one clear test.

The test

Ask yourself what the sentence is about. Is it what someone or something is: who, what kind, from where, what job, when or where an event takes place? Use ser. Is it where something is, or how someone is right now? Use estar.

‹ser›: what it is‹estar›: where and how it is
Es Carlos. Es enfermero.Está en el hospital.
Somos de Puebla.Estamos en Chicago.
Ana es alta.Ana está cansada.
Es tarde.Estoy bien.

Forget "permanent vs temporary"

You may have heard that ser is for permanent things and estar for temporary ones. That rule breaks too often. Being a student is not permanent, yet it is Soy estudiante. A city never moves, yet it is Monterrey está en México. Use the test above instead.

Events take ‹ser›

One exception to "location takes ‹estar›": when you say where a party, a class or a meeting takes place, use ser. The building is somewhere; the event happens somewhere.

{{ex:es.s0058}}{{ex:es.s0059}}

Feelings take ‹estar›

How you feel right now is a state, so it takes estar, and the adjective agrees with the person: contento, contenta (happy), triste (sad), cansado, cansada (tired), nervioso, nerviosa (nervous).
