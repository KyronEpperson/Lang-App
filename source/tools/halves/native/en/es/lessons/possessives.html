English possessives follow the owner: his book, her book. Spanish possessives follow the thing owned. They agree with it in number, and nuestro agrees in gender too.

Tu (your) has no accent mark. Tú with an accent mark means "you".

The thing decides, not the owner

Ask "how many things?", not "how many owners?". One man with many books: sus libros. A whole family with one house: su casa. Nuestro also matches the gender of the thing: nuestra casa, nuestros amigos. When you hear "their" or "our" in English, don't reach for the plural form automatically: look at the noun first.

‹su›: his, her, your, their

Su can mean his, her, its, their, and also "your" when you speak to someone as usted or ustedes. The situation usually makes it clear. So English "your" has two Spanish forms: tu to a friend, su to a stranger. If su is not clear, Spanish names the owner instead, with de; you will see how in a later lesson.

‹este› and ‹ese›: this and that

English has this, these, that and those. Spanish has four forms of each, because they agree with the noun in gender and number.

Use este for things near you and ese for things a little farther away. Like adjectives, they agree with the noun, but they go before it: esta camisa, not "‹camisa esta›". Watch the accent mark: esta means "this", but está means "is".
