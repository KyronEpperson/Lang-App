Small words like "to", "of", "in" and "for" rarely match one to one between languages. Learn each Spanish preposition with its typical uses rather than as one English word.

‹a›: to, and ‹al›

A marks where you go: Voy a la estación. When a meets el, they join into al: al aeropuerto, never "‹a el›". With la, los and las nothing joins.

{{ex:es.s0133}}

The personal ‹a›

English says "I see Ana". Spanish puts a before a person who receives the action: Veo a Ana, Llamo a mi mamá. It has no English translation. Things don't take it: Veo el tren.

{{ex:es.s0134}}

‹de›: from, of, and whose

De means "from" and "of". Spanish has no "'s", so it uses de for whose things are: "Ana's car" is el carro de Ana, "the car of Ana". De plus el becomes del.

{{ex:es.s0135}}{{ex:es.s0136}}

‹en›: in, on, at, and by

En covers "in", "on" and "at" for places, and "by" for transport: en autobús, en carro, en tren. The exception is on foot: a pie.

{{ex:es.s0137}}

‹con›: with, and ‹conmigo›

Con means "with". With me and with you (a friend) are single words: conmigo, contigo.

{{ex:es.s0138}}

‹por› and ‹para›: start with a few fixed uses

Both can mean "for". For now, use para for a purpose (para viajar, in order to travel) and for who gets something (para ti). Use por in fixed phrases: gracias por, por favor, por la mañana (in the morning), por aquí (around here).

{{ex:es.s0139}}{{ex:es.s0140}}
Two mistakes come straight from English. Veo Ana → Veo a Ana, because a person gets the personal ‹a›. Gracias para todo → Gracias por todo, because thanks always take por.
