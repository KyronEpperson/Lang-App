In English, question words jump to the front: "Where do you live?". In Cantonese they stay exactly where the answer would go. Say the answer sentence, then swap the answer for the question word. This one habit fixes most question mistakes.

The question words

WordJyutpingMeaning
咩me1what

邊度bin1 dou6where

What and who

Because nothing moves, a question is built from a statement in one step. There is no "do" to add and no change in word order. 咩 goes where the thing would go: 你食咩呀？ is "you eat what?". 邊個 goes where the person would go: compare 佢係我老師 (she is my teacher) with 佢係邊個呀？ (who is she?). Only one word changes, and 呀 joins the end.

When: before the verb

Time words go before the verb in Cantonese, and 幾時 is a time word, so it sits there too. English says "I'm going to Hong Kong tomorrow"; Cantonese says "I tomorrow go Hong Kong", with 聽日 ting1 jat6 (tomorrow) before 去, and asks "you when go Hong Kong?".

Why and how

點解 (why) comes first, or right after the subject, and the answer usually starts with 因為 jan1 wai6 (because). 點 (how) goes before the verb: 你點去呀？ is "how are you getting there?".

{{ex:yue.s0078}}

The ending: 呀 or 呢

End a question-word question with 呀 to keep it friendly, or with 呢 ne1 to make it softer, a little wondering. Both are fine; a bare question can sound abrupt. On its own, 你呢？ asks the same question back. 邊度 also works after 喺 (at, in): 洗手間喺邊度呀？ sai2 sau2 gaan1 hai2 bin1 dou6 aa3? is "where is the restroom?".

Most common mistake: putting the question word first, as in English. 邊度你住？ → 你住喺邊度呀？
