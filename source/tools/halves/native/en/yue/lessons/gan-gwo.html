English builds "I am eating" with "am" and "-ing", and "I have been" with "have". Cantonese does both with one small word after the verb, and the verb itself never changes.

緊: in the middle of it

Put 緊 gan2 right after the verb for something happening right now. There is no "am": never put 係 in front. 緊 is for actions only; a state such as "I'm tired" stays 我好攰.

{{ex:yue.s0199}}{{ex:yue.s0200}}{{ex:yue.s0201}}

Like 咗, 緊 sits between the verb and the thing, never at the end of the sentence: 食緊飯, 搭緊地鐵. On the phone, this is how you say where you are.

{{ex:yue.s0202}}

A time word such as 而家 ji4 gaa1 (now) goes before the verb as usual: 我而家食緊飯.

過: ever done it

Put 過 gwo3 right after the verb for something you have done at some point in your life: "I've been", "I've tried".

{{ex:yue.s0203}}

過 here is the same character as in 高過我 (taller than me), but after a verb it means "have done it before".

To ask "Have you ever…?", add 未 at the end, as you did with 咗. Answer with the verb and 過 for yes; for no, say 未去過 (not yet) or 冇去過 (never).

{{ex:yue.s0204}}{{ex:yue.s0205}}

Never: 未…過 and 冇…過

Put 未 mei6 or 冇 mou5 before the verb and keep 過 after it. 未 adds "so far", as if it might still happen; 冇 is a plain "never".

{{ex:yue.s0206}}{{ex:yue.s0207}}

過 or 咗?

English "went" can be either, so choose by meaning. 咗 tells what happened on one occasion, or that someone went and is there now. 過 tells that it is part of your experience. So ask "Have you been to Hong Kong?" with 過, and "Did you go yesterday?" with 咗 or 冇.

CantoneseMeaning
佢去咗中環。She's gone to Central (she is there now).
佢去過中環。She's been to Central (at some point).

Most common mistake: copying English "am" with 係. 我係食飯 → 我食緊飯.
