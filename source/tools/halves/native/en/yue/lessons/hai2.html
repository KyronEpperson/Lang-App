English uses "am" and "is" both for what you are ("I am a teacher") and for where you are ("I am in Hong Kong"). Cantonese uses two different words, and they differ only in tone: 係 hai6 (to be) and 喺 hai2 (at, in). They are tone twins, so say the tone carefully: hai6 is low and level, hai2 rises. Both come up in almost every conversation, so it pays to hear the difference early.

Where something is: thing + 喺 + place

To say where someone or something is, put 喺 between the thing and the place. No other verb is needed.

{{ex:yue.s0110}}{{ex:yue.s0111}}

Where you do something: before the verb

English puts the place at the end: "I work at a bank", "I eat at home". Cantonese puts 喺 + place before the verb, in the middle of the sentence. Who, then where, then what you do. Think of it as setting the scene first, then saying what happens. Questions keep the same order: 你喺邊度做嘢呀？ asks where someone works.

{{ex:yue.s0112}}{{ex:yue.s0113}}

An exception: 住喺

With 住 zyu6 (to live) the place comes after the verb, as in English: 住喺 + place. You met it when introducing yourself.

{{ex:yue.s0114}}

Place words come after the noun

English says "inside the house", "next to the bank": the place word comes first. Cantonese says the noun first, then the place word: "the bank's next-to".

屋企入面inside the home
枱上面on the table
銀行隔籬next to the bank
超市對面across from the supermarket

Notice 本書 in the first one: classifier + noun, "the book", from the classifiers lesson.

Most common mistake: putting the place at the end, as in English. 我做嘢喺銀行 → 我喺銀行做嘢. And watch the tone: 我係香港 (hai6) → 我喺香港 (hai2).
