English "brother", "sister" and "grandma" leave a lot unsaid. Cantonese family words always say more: older or younger, and for grandparents, whether they are your father's or your mother's parents. There is no everyday word for just "brother".

Brothers and sisters

CantoneseMeaning
哥哥older brother
家姐older sister
細佬younger brother
細妹younger sister

Counting them works like any other noun: number + 個 + family word, and two is 兩.

{{ex:yue.s0104}}{{ex:yue.s0105}}

To say whose they are, use owner + family word from the last lesson, with no 嘅: 我哥哥 (my older brother), 我家姐 (my older sister).

Mom and dad: changed tones

媽媽 maa4 maa1 (mom) and 爸爸 baa4 baa1 (dad) repeat a syllable, and the second one rises to tone 1. This is a changed tone: the Jyutping line always shows it as people say it.

{{ex:yue.s0102}}

Grandparents on both sides

Father's sideMother's side
爺爺 (grandpa)公公 (grandpa)
嫲嫲 (grandma)婆婆 (grandma)

So "my grandma" depends on whose mother she is: 嫲嫲 maa4 maa4 is your father's mother, 婆婆 po4 po2 your mother's mother.

Older people outside the family, such as a friend's parents or a neighbor, are often called 阿姨 (auntie) and 叔叔 (uncle).

{{ex:yue.s0106}}

Husband and wife

The everyday words are 老公 lou5 gung1 (husband) and 老婆 lou5 po4 (wife). They literally contain "old", but they are warm, normal words, not rude ones.

How many in your family?

屋企 uk1 kei2 means both "home" and "family". To say how many people are in it, use 有 (there is, have) with a number and a classifier.

{{ex:yue.s0107}}

Age: no 係

English says "I am thirty". Cantonese leaves out "am": just the person, then the number + 歲 seoi3 (years old). 今年 gam1 nin2 (this year) often goes in front of the number.

{{ex:yue.s0108}}{{ex:yue.s0109}}
Most common mistake: adding 係 to an age, the way English adds "am". 我係三十歲 → 我三十歲 or 我今年三十歲.
