Lesson 5 used 唔 m4 in 唔係. It is the everyday "not", and it goes straight before a verb or an adjective, never after it the way English puts "not" after "is". Cantonese verbs never change, so English "don't", "doesn't" and "isn't" are all just 唔.

{{ex:yue.s0063}}{{ex:yue.s0064}}

With an adjective there is no 係 and no 好: just 唔 + adjective, as in 唔貴 m4 gwai3, "not expensive".

Yes/no questions: verb, 唔, verb

English asks "Are you going?" by moving words around. Cantonese keeps the order and says the verb, then 唔, then the verb again: "you go not go?". This is a verb-not-verb question. It already holds the "yes or no", so nothing else is needed: no "do", and no rising voice at the end.

{{ex:yue.s0065}}{{ex:yue.s0066}}{{ex:yue.s0067}}

It works with adjectives too (貴唔貴, 好唔好), and 呀 at the end keeps it friendly. In fast speech 係唔係 often shrinks to 係咪 hai6 mai6, with the same meaning.

{{ex:yue.s0068}}

No word for yes or no

Cantonese answers by repeating the verb: "go" or "not go". To 你去唔去呀？ say 去 for yes or 唔去 for no. To 貴唔貴呀？ the answer is 貴 or 唔貴. The short answer is complete on its own. Only a 係 question takes 係 or 唔係 as the answer.

{{ex:yue.s0069}}

Not … either

都 (also) from lesson 5 comes before 唔: 我都唔去 ngo5 dou1 m4 heoi3 is "I'm not going either". English puts "either" at the end; Cantonese puts 都 right after the subject, then 唔, then the verb.

{{ex:yue.s0070}}

嗎, and a first look at 冇

You may see questions ending in 嗎, such as 你係學生嗎？ They are understood but sound formal or written; in speech, use verb-not-verb. And one verb never takes 唔: 有 (to have). Its "not" is 冇 mou5, never 唔有 (lesson 14).

{{ex:yue.s0071}}
Most common mistake: answering every question with 係, like English "yes". 你去唔去呀？ 係 → 去.
