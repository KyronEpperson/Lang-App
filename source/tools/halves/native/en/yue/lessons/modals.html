English has "want", "can", "will" and "like". Cantonese has a small set of words that do the same jobs. They all go right before the main verb, and the main verb never changes: no "to", no "-ing", no "-s". Each one has its own job, so English "can" splits into two different words.

想 and 要: would like, want, need

想 soeng2 is the soft one: "would like to", "feel like". 要 jiu3 is firmer: "want", and with a verb "need to" or "have to". You already order food with 要.

{{ex:yue.s0162}}{{ex:yue.s0163}}

Which one? 想 is softer and sounds polite when you talk about wishes and plans. 要 is direct: fine for ordering and for things you must do. When in doubt about a wish, choose 想.

Notice the order: time first, then 要, then the verb. Words like 想 and 要 never move the time or place to the end.

識: know how to

English says "I can swim" for a skill you learned. Cantonese uses 識 sik1 for that: know how to. Its negative is 唔識 m4 sik1. With a person or a place instead of a verb, 識 means "know": 我識佢 (I know him).

{{ex:yue.s0062}}{{ex:yue.s0164}}

可以: may, allowed to, possible

可以 ho2 ji5 is "can" in the sense of permission or possibility, not skill. To ask "Can I…?", use the verb-not-verb question 可唔可以 ho2 m4 ho2 ji5.

{{ex:yue.s0165}}{{ex:yue.s0166}}

會: will

會 wui5 says what will happen or is likely to happen. It is about the future, not about skills. In a question, 會唔會 asks "will it…?".

{{ex:yue.s0167}}

鍾意: like

鍾意 zung1 ji3 takes a thing or an action, with no extra word between them: English "like to eat" or "like eating" is just 鍾意食. Add 好 for "really like".

{{ex:yue.s0168}}

Saying no, and "no need"

Put 唔 in front: 唔想 m4 soeng2, 唔識, 唔可以 m4 ho2 ji5, 唔鍾意. One trap: "don't need to" is not 唔要 (that means "don't want" a thing). It is 唔使 m4 sai2, the same 唔使 as in 唔使客氣.

{{ex:yue.s0169}}

應該: should (just recognize it)

You will also hear 應該 jing1 goi1, "should", in the same slot before the verb. Recognize it for now.

{{ex:yue.s0170}}
Most common mistake: using 可以 for a skill you have learned. 我可以講廣東話 → 我識講廣東話.
