Ordering in Hong Kong is short and direct. Call the server with 唔該, say what you want with a number and a classifier, and you are done. Long polite English phrases ("Could I please have…") only slow things down.

唔該: to call, to order, to thank

唔該 m4 goi1 gets the server's attention, starts the order, and thanks them when the food arrives. Save 多謝 for gifts and compliments; saying it for every dish sounds odd.

{{ex:yue.s0138}}

要: I'll have

要 jiu3 means "want" or "I'll have". The food keeps its number and classifier: 我要 + number + classifier + food. You can also just say 唔該 and the order. For two or more, change the number and keep the classifier: 兩杯凍奶茶.

Classifiers for food

Food is counted by what it comes in.

ClassifierComes inExample
杯a cup or glass一杯奶茶
籠a steamer basket (dim sum)一籠蝦餃
碟a plate一碟炒飯
碗a bowl一碗雲吞麵

No ice, less sweet

走 means "leave out": 走冰 zau2 bing1 is "no ice". 少甜 siu2 tim4 is "less sweet". Add them after the drink, or say them on their own.

{{ex:yue.s0143}}

Dim sum and the cha chaan teng

飲茶 jam2 caa4, literally "drink tea", means going out for a dim sum meal with tea. A 茶餐廳 caa4 caan1 teng1 is a Hong Kong café serving milk tea, noodles and toast. At the counter you may hear 堂食定外賣？, "for here or to go?" Answer 堂食 (here) or 外賣 (to go).

The check

When you are done, say 唔該埋單！ m4 goi1 maai4 daan1! to any server.

Not 請

English "please" is not 請. 請 is for inviting (請坐, please sit). For a request, use 唔該.

Most common mistake: dropping the classifier, or starting with 請. 我要兩奶茶 → 我要兩杯奶茶, and 請畀我一杯茶 → 唔該，一杯茶.
