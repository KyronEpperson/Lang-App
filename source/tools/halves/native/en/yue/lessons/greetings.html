Cantonese greetings are short, and there are two different thank-yous. Get those two right and you sound polite at once.

Hello and good morning

你好 nei5 hou2 is "hello" when you meet someone new. With friends it sounds stiff: people just say the name, or 早晨 zou2 san4 (good morning) until about noon. There is no everyday "good afternoon". "How are you?" copied word for word, 你好嗎, is understood but sounds translated; friends ask 最近點呀？ zeoi3 gan6 dim2 aa3? (how have you been?).

Two ways to say thank you

English has one "thank you"; Cantonese has two. 唔該 m4 goi1 is for a service or a small favor: the server who brings your tea, someone who holds a door. It also means "please", and "excuse me" when you call a server. 多謝 do1 ze6 is for a gift, money or kind words. Add 晒 to either one for "thanks so much".

The answer to both is 唔使客氣 m4 sai2 haak3 hei3, "you're welcome", literally "no need to be polite".

Sorry, and goodbye

唔好意思 m4 hou2 ji3 si3 is the small "sorry" or "excuse me": being late, squeezing past. 對唔住 deoi3 m4 zyu6 is a real apology, when you have done something wrong. To leave, say 拜拜 baai1 baai3, from English "bye-bye"; at night, 早抖 zou2 dau2 means "good night, get some rest".

Ordering a drink

To order, say 唔該, then the number, then a small word for the container, then the drink. English says "a milk tea"; Cantonese says "one cup milk tea", 一杯奶茶 jat1 bui1 naai5 caa4, with 杯 (cup) between the number and the drink. That small word is a classifier, and lesson 10 is all about them.

{{ex:yue.s0035}}
Most common mistake: thanking a server with 多謝 for every dish. 多謝 to the server → 唔該. Keep 多謝 for gifts and kind words.
