English changes its melody to sound friendly, unsure or pushy: "Coming?" with a rise, "Come on!" with a fall. Cantonese cannot do that, because the melody of each syllable is its tone. Instead it adds a small word at the end of the sentence, a particle (a "sentence-final particle"). Without one, many sentences sound curt, like an order or an interrogation.

呀: the friendly default

呀 aa3 softens questions and statements. When in doubt, end a question with it. On a statement, it makes your answer sound warmer and less clipped.

{{ex:yue.s0058}}{{ex:yue.s0123}}

啦: requests and agreeing

啦 laa1 goes on requests and urging, and on saying yes to a suggestion. It turns a bare command into something friendlier.

{{ex:yue.s0124}}{{ex:yue.s0125}}

吖: offers and invitations

吖 aa1 makes an offer or an invitation: "come on, let's", "have some".

{{ex:yue.s0126}}{{ex:yue.s0127}}

呢: "and you?"

呢 ne1 after a person asks the same question back. It can also soften a question that has a question word. For example, 你去邊度呢？ is a gentle "so, where are you going?"

{{ex:yue.s0128}}

Listen for these

You will hear more particles. Recognize them for now; you do not need to use them yet.

ParticleWhat it addsExample
㗎"really, that's how it is"係㗎。

喎something new or reported

No English rise

Each particle has its own fixed tone: 呀 is tone 3, mid and level, and 啦 and 吖 are tone 1, high. Do not add an English question rise on top; the particle already does the asking.

Most common mistake: leaving the particle off, so a question sounds blunt or an invitation sounds like an order. 你叫咩名？ → 你叫咩名呀？, and 一齊去？ → 一齊去吖！
