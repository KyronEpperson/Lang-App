English shows an owner in two ways: a word like "my", "your", "his", or 's after a name (Anna's phone). Cantonese uses one small link word for all of them: 嘅 ge3. The owner comes first, then 嘅, then the thing.

Owner + 嘅 + thing

"My" is 我嘅 ngo5 ge3, "your" is 你嘅 nei5 ge3, "his" or "her" is 佢嘅 keoi5 ge3, "our" is 我哋嘅 ngo5 dei6 ge3. A name or a job works the same way: the teacher's book is 老師嘅書 lou5 si1 ge3 syu1.

The owner always comes first

English can put the owner after the thing: "the number of the hotel", "a friend of mine". Cantonese never does this inside a noun phrase. Whoever owns it goes in front, and 嘅 is not a copy of 's that can trail after the noun: 書我嘅 is not Cantonese, 我嘅書 is.

"Mine", "yours": 嘅 at the end

To say "it's mine", end the sentence with the owner + 嘅. Here 嘅 stands for the thing itself, like English "mine" or "Anna's". To ask "whose?", use 邊個嘅 bin1 go3 ge3, literally "who's".

Family and home: usually no 嘅

With people close to you and with your home, Cantonese usually drops 嘅: 我媽媽 (my mom), 我朋友 (my friend), 我屋企 ngo5 uk1 kei2 (my home, my family). 我嘅媽媽 is not wrong, just less usual in everyday speech.

Two ways to say "my phone"

For things, you now have two ways to say "my": 我嘅手機, and from the last lesson, owner + classifier + noun, 我部手機. Both are correct and both are common. If you are not sure of a noun's classifier, 嘅 is always a safe choice.

Most common mistake: copying the English order, with the owner after the thing. 書我嘅 → 我嘅書, and 電話安娜嘅 → 安娜嘅電話.
