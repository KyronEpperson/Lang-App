English puts the details of your day at the end: "I eat at home in the evening." Cantonese puts them up front, before the action. Learn the order once and you can describe your whole day.

The fixed line

A Cantonese sentence about your day runs in this order: who, when, where or how, and only then the verb and what you do. When and where go before the verb, never after it.

WhoWhenWhere / howVerb + what
我朝早搭地鐵返工
我夜晚喺屋企食飯

The time word may also open the sentence, before the person: 夜晚我喺屋企食飯。 je6 maan5 ngo5 hai2 uk1 kei2 sik6 faan6. is fine too. What never happens is time or place after the verb.

Parts of the day

朝早 ziu1 zou2 is morning, 晏晝 aan3 zau3 is the middle of the day, and 夜晚 je6 maan5 is evening and night. Add a clock time from the last lesson after it: big to small, as with dates.

{{ex:yue.s0155}}

English says "at seven". Cantonese needs no word for "at" before a clock time: the time simply sits before the verb. Keep 喺 hai2 for places, as in 喺屋企 (at home).

Words for a working day

返工 faan1 gung1 is "go to work" (literally "go back to work", but it is the everyday word), and 放工 fong3 gung1 is "get off work". 食晏 sik6 aan3 is to have lunch, 沖涼 cung1 loeng4 is to take a shower, and 瞓覺 fan3 gaau3 is to go to bed or sleep.

How often

有時 jau5 si4 (sometimes) and 成日 seng4 jat6 (often, all the time) sit in the same place as other time words: after the person, before the verb. 每日 mui5 jat6 (every day) goes there too.

Asking about someone's day

Question words take the slot of the answer, as you learned in lesson 8. 幾點 gei2 dim2 (what time) goes in the time slot, 邊度 bin1 dou6 (where) in the place slot.

Most common mistake: copying the English order and saving the time and place for the end. 我返工搭地鐵朝早 → 我朝早搭地鐵返工.
