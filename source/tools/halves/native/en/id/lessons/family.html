Family comes up early in any conversation in Indonesia: whether you are married, whether you have children, how many brothers and sisters you have. The words are easy; the surprise is how they sort people.

The core words

IndonesianEnglish
ayah, bapakfather
ibumother
orang tuaparents
suami, istrihusband, wife
anakchild; son or daughter
kakek, nenekgrandfather, grandmother

The owner still comes after the noun: ibu saya (my mother), suami saya (my husband). The short endings work too: ibuku, istrinya (his wife).

Older or younger, not brother or sister

English sorts siblings by sex: brother, sister. Indonesian sorts them by age. kakak is an older brother or sister, adik a younger one. There is no single word that means only "brother". To say which, add laki-laki (male) or perempuan (female) after the word, before the owner.

Family words as "you"

Several family words double as polite ways to say "you". You already know Bapak and Ibu. Kak (from kakak) is a friendly "you" for a young person, common in shops and online. Mas and Mbak also mean older brother and older sister.

The questions you will be asked

Indonesians often ask Sudah menikah? (Are you married?, literally "already married?"). If you are not, the expected answer is Belum, "not yet", even if you have no plans. A flat Tidak sounds blunt. For children, people often put the number last: Anak saya dua, "my children (are) two".
