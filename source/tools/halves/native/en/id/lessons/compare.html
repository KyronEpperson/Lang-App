English changes the describing word itself: cheap, cheaper, cheapest. Indonesian never changes it. It puts a small word in front instead, and that is all.

lebih: more, -er

lebih + describing word means "more …" or "…-er". To say "than", add dari or daripada; daripada is a little more careful, and both are correct.

{{ex:id.s0180}}{{ex:id.s0181}}{{ex:id.s0182}}

paling: most, -est

paling + describing word means "the most …" or "the …-est". With a noun, link it with yang: toko yang paling murah, "the cheapest shop". Do not use sangat here: it only means "very".

{{ex:id.s0183}}{{ex:id.s0184}}

The same: sama … -nya, se-

For "the same size" or "the same price", say sama + describing word + -nya. For "as … as", put se- on the describing word.

{{ex:id.s0185}}{{ex:id.s0186}}

kurang and terlalu

kurang means "less" or "not … enough". You already know it from bargaining: Bisa kurang? asks for less. terlalu means "too", as in "too expensive".

{{ex:id.s0187}}{{ex:id.s0188}}

Which one?

To ask which of two things wins, start with Mana yang ("which one is…"): Mana yang lebih murah? The shopkeeper can answer with yang ini or yang itu. For "better", say lebih baik; for "the best", paling baik or, for food, paling enak.

{{ex:id.s0189}}

Still no "to be"

A comparison is a describing word with a helper in front, so the old rule holds: nothing stands for "is". Baju ini lebih murah is the whole sentence.

Most common mistake: leaving out lebih, because "than" seems to be enough. Ini murah dari itu → Ini lebih murah dari itu.
