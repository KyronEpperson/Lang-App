English adds -s to almost every plural: book, books. Indonesian usually adds nothing at all. buku is "book" or "books", and the situation makes it clear.

Numbers and quantity words do the work

When the number matters, say it: a number or a word such as banyak (many), beberapa (some, a few) or semua (all) goes before the noun, and the noun stays as it is.

{{ex:id.s0208}}{{ex:id.s0209}}

Repeating the word

To stress that there are several, or many different ones, repeat the noun with a hyphen: anak-anak, buku-buku. You will hear this often with people: anak-anak is the usual way to say "the kids".

{{ex:id.s0210}}{{ex:id.s0211}}

Never after a number

A number or a quantity word already says "more than one", so the noun is not repeated as well: tiga buku, banyak orang. English speakers often say both, because English marks the plural twice ("three books"). The pattern is always the same: the number or quantity word, then the plain noun, then any describing word: dua kopi panas.

The hyphen is part of the spelling

A repeated word is one word, written with a hyphen. In text messages you will see anak2; that is fine with friends, but not in careful writing or in this course's answers.

Repeated words that are not plurals

Some words only exist in repeated form, and some change meaning when repeated. These are not plurals at all.
