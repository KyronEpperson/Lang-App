English adds a description after a noun with "who", "which" or "that": "the person who lives here", "the book that I bought". Indonesian uses one word for all three: yang. Like every describer, the yang phrase comes after the noun.

One word for who, which and that

Put yang right after the noun, then the description. It does not matter whether the noun is a person or a thing.

{{ex:id.s0166}}{{ex:id.s0167}}

Notice beli, not membeli, in that last one: when the person doing it comes right before the verb inside a yang phrase, the verb appears as the bare root.

A long describer needs yang

English can sometimes drop "that" ("the book I bought"). Indonesian cannot drop yang before a phrase with a verb or several words. Without it, the listener hears two separate sentences.

{{ex:id.s0168}}

A one-word describer: yang is optional

With a single describing word you already know the pattern: baju merah. Adding yang (baju yang merah) picks one out from others: "the shirt that is red", not the blue one. Both are correct; don't put yang before every describing word out of habit.

{{ex:id.s0169}}

yang on its own: "the … one"

Leave out the noun and yang + describer means "the … one". This is everyday shopping language.

{{ex:id.s0170}}{{ex:id.s0171}}

Where ini and itu go

ini and itu go at the very end, after the whole description. However long the phrase gets, the order never changes: first the noun, then yang and the description, then ini or itu. An owner sits right after the noun: teman saya yang tinggal di Bali.

{{ex:id.s0172}}
Most common mistake: dropping yang before a longer description, as English drops "that". buku saya beli kemarin → buku yang saya beli kemarin.
