This is the rule you will use in almost every Indonesian sentence: the noun comes first, and whatever describes it follows.

Describing words go after the noun

English says "a big house", with the describing word in front. Indonesian says rumah besar, literally "house big". Colors, sizes and opinions all follow the noun.

Two nouns: the main one first

English "plane ticket" is a kind of ticket, so the main noun, "ticket", comes last. Indonesian turns it around: the main noun first, then the one that says what kind.

Kamar mandi is literally "room bathe". In the same way, orang Indonesia is "an Indonesian", literally "person Indonesia".

Very: sangat before, sekali after

There are two common ways to say "very". Sangat goes before the describing word; sekali goes after it. Use one, not both.

A phrase or a sentence?

Add itu or ini, and the order tells you whether you have a phrase or a full sentence. When the describing word comes before itu, it is part of the phrase "that big house". When it comes after itu, it says something about the house, and you have a sentence: "That house is big". There is still no word for "is".

More than one describer

English stacks describers in a fixed order before the noun: "my new red car". Indonesian lines them up after it, starting with the noun, and the owner usually comes last: mobil baru saya, "my new car".

Most common mistake: putting the describing word first, as in English. besar rumah → rumah besar.
