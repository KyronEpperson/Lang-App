Many Indonesian verbs are a root word with a prefix (a few letters stuck on the front). English does a little of this ("re-do", "un-pack"); Indonesian does it all the time. The first prefix to know is ber-.

What ber- verbs do

A ber- verb mostly says what someone does or is doing, and it is usually complete without an object (a thing that the action is done to): you just work, swim or walk. A few, like belajar and bermain, can be followed by what you study or play. The verb still never changes for time.

ber- verbEnglish
bekerjato work
belajarto study, to learn
berenangto swim
bermainto play
berbicarato talk, to speak
bertanyato ask
berjalanto walk

Learn the spelling with the word

The prefix is not always spelled ber-. Three very common verbs lose letters: bekerja (from kerja), belajar and berenang (from renang). Learn these as whole words rather than building them.

{{ex:id.s0138}}

ber- with a noun: have, use, be

Added to a noun, ber- often means "have", "use" or "be": umur (age) gives berumur, "be … years old"; sepeda (bicycle) gives bersepeda, "ride a bike"; nama gives bernama, "be named".

{{ex:id.s0139}}

In everyday speech

In casual talk people often drop the prefix: Saya kerja di Jakarta, main bola. That is normal speech, and you will hear it all day. In this course, answers use the full ber- form, which is right everywhere.

One warning: ber- is not a general "verb maker". Cooking, reading and writing take a different prefix, me-, in the next lesson.

Most common mistake: keeping the r in front of kerja. berkerja → bekerja.
