Indonesian uses the same letters as English, and its spelling is very regular: a handful of rules let you read almost any word. Under every Indonesian phrase in this course there is a pronunciation line in English letters.

The letters

All 26 letters are used. These sound much as in English, except that a final k or h works differently (see "How words end"). q, v, x and z turn up only in borrowed words, and v is usually said as f. The sections below cover the vowels, the letters that differ from English, and how words end. Tap a letter to hear it.

Six short, pure vowels

English vowels slide: say "no" slowly and your lips end on a w. Indonesian vowels stay put. The line writes them ah, ee, oo, oh, plus two sounds for one letter. The letter e is either the strong e, eh as in "bed", or the weak e, uh as in "about". The spelling never shows which, so learn it with the word; the pronunciation line always tells you. An e at the very end of a word is usually strong.

Capitals mean a slight lift

One syllable per word is in CAPITALS. It gets a slight lift, nothing like an English stress. Say every syllable evenly and keep every vowel full: never swallow the others the way English swallows the first a in "banana".

Before ee and eh, the line writes a hard g as gh, so that you never read it like the g of "gem": pagi (morning) is PAH-ghee. The letter t is always a plain t, never "ch" and never "d". And p, t, k have no puff of air after them.

How words end

A final k is a catch in the throat, like the break in "uh-oh" (a glottal stop). The line writes it as q. Don't pop the k. A final h is breathed out, never dropped: the line adds an h after the vowel, ahh, eeh.

ai and au at the end of a word are one sound, as in "Thai" and "how". Before a final consonant they split into two syllables.

The noun comes first

Many cards come as two-word phrases, and they show the language's biggest rule. English says "hot coffee"; Indonesian says kopi panas, literally "coffee hot". The noun comes first and the describing word follows.

Read whole words

Now put it together. Read a word one piece at a time, left to right: each vowel short and full, each letter or letter group with its Indonesian sound, and the ending as you learned it. So kucing is k + u + c + i + ng: KOO-cheeng. Then give the syllable in capitals a slight lift. Each card's note breaks the word into its pieces. Say the word aloud before you flip it.

Listen first, then read. The line is a crutch: hide it once the spelling feels natural.

Most common mistake: reading c as k. cantik said "kantik" → cantik said with ch, as in "church".
