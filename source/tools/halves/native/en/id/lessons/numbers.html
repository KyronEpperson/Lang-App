Indonesian numbers are built like Lego: learn ten words and a few building blocks, and you can say any price. You will need big numbers fast, because prices in rupiah run into the thousands.

One to ten

satu, dua, tiga, empat, lima, enam, tujuh, delapan, sembilan, sepuluh. Zero is nol; in phone numbers people often say kosong (empty) instead.

The building blocks

English has special words like "twelve" and "twenty". Indonesian just adds a block: belas makes the teens, puluh means tens, ratus hundreds, ribu thousands, juta millions.

se- means "one"

For "one" in front of a block, Indonesian uses se- joined to it, not satu: sepuluh (10), sebelas (11), seratus (100), seribu (1,000). For a million you hear both satu juta and sejuta.

Prices

Prices are written with a dot for thousands: Rp25.000 is twenty-five thousand, not twenty-five. Read the number in chunks before the dot, then say ribu. To ask a price, say Berapa harganya? (literally "how much its price?").

Numbers go before the noun

Here is a surprise after all those noun-first lessons: numbers and words like banyak (many) come BEFORE the noun, just as in English. Describing words still follow the noun, so a number and a describing word end up on opposite sides. After a number the noun stays plain, never repeated.

When you order food you will also hear the number after the dish, as a kind of shorthand. In an ordinary sentence the number comes first.

Most common mistake: using satu in front of a block. satu ratus → seratus, satu ribu → seribu.
