Ini means "this" and itu means "that". They are the words you need most at a market, and where you put them changes the meaning.

Before the noun: a sentence

Put ini or itu first and you have a whole sentence: "This is…", "That is…". There is no word for "is", and you need no word for "a". You already met this in Ini teman saya, "This is my friend".

{{ex:id.s0050}}{{ex:id.s0051}}

After the noun: "this book"

English puts "this" and "that" in front: "this book". Indonesian puts them after the noun: buku ini, literally "book this". That is just a phrase, not a sentence, so you can go on and say something about it. Indonesian rarely marks plurals, so buku ini can also mean "these books".

itu as "the"

Indonesian has no word for "the". When both of you know which thing you mean, itu after the noun often does the job. Use ini for something near you, and itu for something farther away or already mentioned.

{{ex:id.s0055}}

This one, that one

To choose without naming the thing, say yang ini (this one) or yang itu (that one). Yang is a small linking word you will meet again later; here it works like English "one". Mau means "want". At a market stall, point, say Yang ini, and the seller knows exactly what you mean.

{{ex:id.s0056}}{{ex:id.s0057}}

At a market, point and ask Berapa ini?, "How much is this?". Here ini stands alone, so it simply means "this".
