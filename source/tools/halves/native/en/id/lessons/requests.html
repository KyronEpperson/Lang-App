English makes almost every request with "please" or "could you". Indonesian has a different small word for each kind of request, and it goes at the start.

tolong: please do something for me

tolong + a verb asks someone to do something. After tolong the verb usually appears as the bare root, without meN-: tolong tutup, tolong bantu. A title at the end makes it warmer.

minta: may I have

minta + a thing is the polite way to ask for something: in a warung, a shop or at someone's home. It is what Indonesians say to a waiter; a string of Saya mau… sounds blunt. When ordering, the number often comes after the dish.

{{ex:id.s0159}}{{ex:id.s0160}}{{ex:id.s0161}}

The little ya at the end works like "OK?" and softens any request. You will also meet -lah on the end of a verb, which softens a command in a similar way: Makanlah ("Go on, eat"). You do not need it to sound polite.

silakan: please, go ahead

silakan is the other "please": it offers or invites. Hosts, waiters and shop staff use it all the time. You never use it to ask for something for yourself.

coba and ayo

coba means "try": Coba ini, enak! ("Try this, it's good!"). ayo means "let's" or "come on", and includes you.

{{ex:id.s0164}}

jangan: don't

English makes "don't" from "do not". Indonesian has a separate word for commands: jangan. tidak only describes ("I don't smoke"); it cannot tell someone not to do something. Put tolong in front for a polite sign or request: Tolong jangan merokok.

Most common mistake: using tidak for "don't" in a command. Tidak pergi! → Jangan pergi!
