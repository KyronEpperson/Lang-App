English changes the verb to show time: eat, ate, eating, will eat, have eaten. Indonesian never touches the verb. makan is always makan. When the time matters, a small time word goes in front of the verb.

sudah: done, already

sudah says something is finished. It covers English "have eaten", "already ate" and often plain "ate".

{{ex:id.s0128}}

sedang: in the middle of it

sedang is English "-ing" for something happening right now. If you are on the phone and someone asks what you are doing, this is the word you need.

{{ex:id.s0129}}

akan: will

akan points to the future. In conversation it is often left out when a word like besok (tomorrow) already shows the time.

{{ex:id.s0130}}

belum: not yet

belum is the negative of sudah. Indonesians use it far more than English uses "not yet": the polite answer to Sudah makan? is Belum, not Tidak, because it leaves the door open.

{{ex:id.s0131}}

pernah and masih

pernah means "ever, at some time", for experiences. belum pernah is "never (yet)". masih means "still", and it works before a place too, with no word for "is".

Words for when

Words such as kemarin (yesterday), tadi (earlier today), sekarang (now), nanti (later) and besok (tomorrow) can carry the time on their own. You can add sudah as well, but you do not have to. Just do not mix a future word with a past one.

{{ex:id.s0134}}
Most common mistake: mixing future and past, as if akan were a tense ending. Saya akan makan kemarin → Saya makan kemarin.
