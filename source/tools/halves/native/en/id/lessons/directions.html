To ask the way, start with Permisi ("Excuse me") and a title, then the place and di mana. The answer will be built from a handful of words you can learn in one sitting.

{{ex:id.s0190}}

Turn and go straight

belok is "turn": belok kiri (left), belok kanan (right). lurus is "straight", and terus means "keep going". The two side words sound alike, so say them slowly until they stick: kiri is left, kanan is right.

Where it is

Place words start with di: di depan (in front of), di seberang (across from), di sebelah kiri (on the left). dekat is near and jauh is far; "from here" is dari sini. As always, there is no word for "is" before a place.

{{ex:id.s0193}}{{ex:id.s0194}}{{ex:id.s0195}}

jalan: street, and walking

jalan is a street (Jalan Sudirman, often written Jl.), but it is also the verb "walk, go". jalan kaki means "on foot". The sentence shows which one is meant. Street names keep the noun first, like every other name of a place: Jalan Sudirman, never "Sudirman Jalan".

{{ex:id.s0196}}

Check that you understood

Repeat the key words back with a rising voice, as a question. People will happily confirm with Ya, betul ("Yes, right") or put you straight. If the answer comes too fast, ask them to say it slowly: Bisa pelan-pelan?

{{ex:id.s0197}}

When someone gives you directions, a simple Terima kasih, Pak or Terima kasih, Bu closes the exchange.

Most common mistake: adding "is" to the question. Stasiun adalah di mana? → Stasiun di mana?
