Describing your day needs no new grammar. The verbs stay exactly as they are, jam + a number says when, and time words such as sudah and belum say whether it has happened.

A day in order

IndonesianEnglish
bangunwake up, get up
mandiwash, shower
sarapanhave breakfast
berangkatleave, set off
pulanggo home
tidursleep, go to bed

Link the steps with lalu ("then"). In everyday speech no word for "at" is needed before a clock time.

Usually, every day

To talk about habits, put biasanya ("usually") at the start, or setiap hari ("every day") at the start or the end. The verb stays the same for habits, for today and for yesterday: only the time words around it change.

{{ex:id.s0201}}

mandi is not a long soak

mandi is the everyday wash, usually twice a day: a shower, or water scooped from a tub. It is not "take a bath" in the American sense, so Saya sudah mandi is just "I've showered".

Yesterday and today

For yesterday, add kemarin; the verb does not change. belum means "not yet".

Shopping at a warung or market

At a market stall or a street seller you ask the price, bargain a little, and choose. Bargaining is normal there, but not in a minimarket or any shop with printed prices. Keep it friendly, and stop when the price feels fair: a few thousand rupiah means more to the seller than to you.
