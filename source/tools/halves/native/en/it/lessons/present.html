English verbs barely change: I work, you work, she works. Only "he / she / it" adds an -s. An Italian verb has six forms, one for each subject, and the ending tells you who is doing it.

Three groups

The infinitive (the "to" form) ends in -are, -ere or -ire. Take off that ending to get the stem (lavor-, scriv-, dorm-) and add the ending for the subject.

The io form always ends in -o, the tu form in -i, and the noi form in -iamo. Only the vowel of the voi form and the lui / lei and loro forms changes from group to group. In the loro form the stress stays on the same syllable as in the io form, not on the ending: lavoro, lavorano; scrivo, scrivono.

Lei and voi

Lei takes the lui / lei form, so "Do you work here?" to a stranger is Lei lavora qui? or just Lavora qui?. For a group, use the voi form.

-isc- verbs

Some -ire verbs add -isc- in four forms: capire (understand), finire (finish), preferire (prefer).

One tense, three English meanings

Studio means "I study", "I'm studying" and, with a time word, "I'm going to study". Italian has no separate "-ing" form for this.

{{ex:it.s0056}}

Keeping c and g hard

In cercare (look for) and pagare (pay), an h keeps the hard sound before i: cerchi, cerchiamo, paghi, paghiamo.

Most common mistake: one form for every subject, or a tu ending with Lei. Lei parli inglese? → Lei parla inglese?
