The two most common verbs in Italian are essere (to be) and avere (to have). Both are irregular, so learn their forms as they are.

Sono means both "I am" and "they are"; the rest of the sentence tells you which. Use essere for who and where someone is from. Nationalities are written in lowercase and agree like other words: a man is americano, a woman americana.

Watch the accent: è means "is", while e without an accent means "and".

Yes-or-no questions

To ask a yes-or-no question, keep the same words and let your voice rise at the end: Hai fame? English adds "do" or swaps the words; Italian does neither.

avere: to have

The h in ho, hai, ha, hanno is silent, but you must write it: o means "or", a means "to", and anno means "year".

Where English says "be", Italian says "have"

English uses "be" for age and for many feelings in the body. Italian uses avere: literally, "I have thirty years", "I have hunger".

These words are nouns, so they never change for a man or a woman: ho fame for everybody. But adjectives after essere do change: sono stanco (a man), sono stanca (a woman).

Most common mistake: copying English "be". Sono freddo → Ho freddo. Sono freddo means "I'm a cold person".
