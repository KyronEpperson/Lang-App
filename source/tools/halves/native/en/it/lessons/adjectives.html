English adjectives never change and come before the noun: "the red shirt", "the red shoes". Italian adjectives do two things differently. Most come after the noun, and they change their ending to agree with it.

After the noun

Say the thing first, then describe it: la camicia bianca, literally "the shirt white". Colors and nationalities always go after the noun.

Four forms

An adjective ending in -o has four forms: -o and -a for masculine and feminine, -i and -e for the plurals. Check the noun first, then pick the ending. Follow the gender, not the letters: il maglione (the sweater) ends in -e but is masculine, so it's il maglione nero.

OneMore than one
Masculineil maglione neroi pantaloni neri
Femininela gonna nerale scarpe nere

Adjectives in -co and -ca add an h in the plural to keep the hard sound: bianco, bianchi; bianca, bianche.

Two forms

Adjectives ending in -e are the same for masculine and feminine. They have one form for one thing and -i for more than one.

OneMore than one
la borsa verdele borse verdi
il cappotto verdei cappotti verdi

After essere

The adjective agrees even after the verb: it looks back at the subject. A mixed group of men and women takes the masculine plural.

A few go first

Some very common adjectives usually go before the noun: bello (beautiful), buono (good), piccolo (small). Before the noun bello and buono can shorten, as in buongiorno; you only need to recognize this for now.

{{ex:it.s0050}}

Useful colors: rosso (red), bianco (white), nero (black), giallo (yellow), verde (green). For prices: caro (expensive).

Most common mistake: keeping the adjective masculine. la camicia bianco → la camicia bianca.
