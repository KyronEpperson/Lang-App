In a shop, English says "I'll take it" and puts "it" after the verb. Italian uses lo, la, li, le and puts them before the verb: Lo prendo. These are direct object pronouns. Two things are new, so take them one at a time.

Four words for "it" and "them"

Italian has no neutral "it". The pronoun agrees in gender and number with the noun it replaces, just as an article does. The same words also mean "him", "her" and "them" for people.

The nounThe pronounEnglish
il maglione, Marcoloit, him
la giacca, Giulialait, her
i pantalonilithem
le scarpelethem (all feminine)

Before the verb

The pronoun goes right before the conjugated verb. With non, the order is non, then the pronoun, then the verb.

It works the same with every verb you know: lo vedo (I see it), la mangio (I eat it), li compro (I buy them). Think of the pronoun as glued to the front of the verb, and choose it by looking back at the noun it stands for.

Watch out for i pantaloni: English "pants" is plural too, so "them" feels natural, but if you say "it" in English you still need li in Italian.

People

For a person, use the same words: lo for a man, la for a woman.

{{ex:it.s0145}}

Before a vowel: ‹l'›

Before a verb that starts with a vowel or ‹h›, lo and la often lose their vowel: l'aspetto. Lo aspetto is also correct. Li and le never do this.

{{ex:it.s0146}}

With an infinitive: two places

After posso, devo or voglio, the pronoun can join the end of the infinitive, like mi in alzarmi. It can also go before the conjugated verb. Both are right.

{{ex:it.s0147}}{{ex:it.s0148}}
Most common mistake: putting the pronoun after the verb, as in English: Prendo lo → Lo prendo. The next one is using ‹lo› for everything: La borsa? Lo prendo → La borsa? La prendo.
