English says "there is" for one thing and "there are" for more. Italian works the same way, with two short phrases: c'è and ci sono. Here ci means "there", and è and sono are the verb essere you already know.

c'è for one, ci sono for more

C'è is ci + è: the i drops and an apostrophe takes its place. With more than one thing, use ci sono. The verb agrees with the thing that is there, not with "there".

One thingMore than one
C'è un bar.Ci sono due bar.
C'è una farmacia.Ci sono due farmacie.
{{ex:it.s0067}}{{ex:it.s0068}}

Due means "two". An Italian bar is a café, where people stop for an espresso. Words like bar and hotel, borrowed from other languages, do not change in the plural.

Asking and saying no

To ask, keep the same words and raise your voice. Qui vicino means "near here". To say there isn't one, put non in front.

c'è or è?

The two do different jobs. C'è introduces something new: there is one somewhere. That is why it usually comes with un, una or a number. È says where a particular thing is, one you have already named with il or la.

Something new: c'èA particular one: è
C'è una farmacia in centro.La farmacia è in centro.
There's a pharmacy downtown.The pharmacy is downtown.
{{ex:it.s0072}}{{ex:it.s0073}}

To ask where a particular place is, say Dov'è…?, which is dove + è. A typical exchange uses both: first C'è una farmacia qui vicino?, then Dov'è la farmacia?

{{ex:it.s0074}}

Never drop ci

Without ci, the meaning changes. È un bar means "It's a bar", not "There's a bar".

Most common mistake: using c'è for more than one thing, as in casual English "there's two bars". C'è due bar → Ci sono due bar.
