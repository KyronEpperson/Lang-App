Japanese numbers are very regular up to 99. After that they group digits differently from English. Prices and clock times use the same numbers with a small word attached.

One to ten, then building up

12345678910
いちにさんよんごろくななはちきゅうじゅう

Four and seven also have a second word each, し and しち. You hear them when people count off one to ten and in a few fixed words such as しちじ (7:00); in bigger numbers use よん and なな (よんじゅう, 40). Above ten, numbers are built like sums: じゅういち is 10 + 1, にじゅう is 2 × 10, and にじゅうご is 25. Zero is ゼロ, borrowed from English.

Hundreds and thousands

ひゃく is 100 and せん is 1,000. A few combinations change their sound, and they are worth learning as words: さんびゃく (300), ろっぴゃく (600), はっぴゃく (800), さんぜん (3,000), はっせん (8,000).

{{ex:ja.s0053}}

Counting in ten-thousands

English groups digits by thousands, so 10,000 is "ten thousand". Japanese has a word for ten thousand, まん, and groups by four digits. So 10,000 is いちまん, 20,000 is にまん and 50,000 is ごまん. Never say じゅうせん.

{{ex:ja.s0054}}

Prices: えん and いくらですか

Put えん (yen) right after the number: ひゃくごじゅうえん is ¥150, about the price of a rice ball. Longer prices are read exactly like the numbers, biggest part first: せんごひゃくえん is ¥1,500, one thousand and five hundred. Ask any price with いくらですか.

Telling the time

Add じ for "o'clock", ふん or ぷん for minutes (ごふん is five minutes, じゅっぷん is ten), and はん for "half past". Three hours need care: よじ (4:00), しちじ (7:00, also ななじ) and くじ (9:00). Ask the time with なんじですか. For "a.m." and "p.m.", put ごぜん or ごご before the time, not after it: ごご さんじ is 3 p.m.

{{ex:ja.s0057}}{{ex:ja.s0058}}

"At" a time: に

English says "at seven". Japanese puts the time particle に after the time instead: しちじに. Learn おきます (get up) as a word for now; verbs get a lesson of their own in lesson 8.
