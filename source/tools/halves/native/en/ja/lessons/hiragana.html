Japanese is written with three scripts. Hiragana spells Japanese words and grammar, katakana spells loanwords and foreign names, and kanji are characters borrowed from Chinese. Almost everything in this course is written in hiragana, so it comes first.

One letter, one beat

An English letter is usually one sound. A hiragana letter is a whole beat: a vowel, or a consonant plus a vowel. か is ka, す is su. The 46 letters sit in a grid of five vowels times a row for each consonant, and each letter always sounds the same.

The first four rows: あ, か, さ, た

Start with the vowels. English names its vowels "ay, ee, eye, oh, you". Japanese vowels sound as in Spanish or Italian: あ "ah", い "ee", う "oo" with relaxed lips, え "eh", お "oh". So え is never "ee", and い is never "eye".

Three letters in these rows do not follow their row: し is shi, ち is chi, and つ is tsu. Watch the look-alikes さ and ち. Tap a letter to hear it and see how it reads.

The other six rows: な to ん

Two more letters break the pattern: ふ is fu, said with both lips and no teeth, and ん is a plain n with no vowel. The y row has only three letters and the w row only two. Watch the look-alikes: ぬ and め, わ, れ and ね, る and ろ, は and ほ.

Read your first words

You now know all 46 letters. A word is read one letter at a time, one beat each, with no letter louder than the others: ね + こ is neko, two beats. The cards below are real words spelled with the letters you just learned. Read each one aloud before you flip it.

Two small marks

Two dots ゛ change a sound: か ka becomes が ga, さ sa becomes ざ za, た ta becomes だ da, は ha becomes ば ba. A small circle ゜ turns は into ぱ pa. (In Japanese they are called dakuten and handakuten.) Now you can read です and ください. The dotted letters get their own cards in the next lesson.

は, を and へ as particles

English puts small words like "to" before a noun. Japanese puts small words called particles after it. Three particles keep an old spelling: は is said wa, を is said o, and へ is said e. The greeting こんにちは ends in the same は.

Spaces and romaji

Real Japanese has no spaces between words. While this course is written in kana, it puts a space after each phrase so you can see where one ends. The line in English letters under each phrase is romaji. Lean on it this week: from lesson 4 it is hidden until you tap it, so read the kana first.

Most common mistake: spelling a particle the way it sounds. これわ すしです → これは すしです.
