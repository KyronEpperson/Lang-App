English joins actions with "and": "I get up, eat breakfast and go to school." Japanese does not put と ("and") between verbs. Instead, every verb except the last changes into the て form, and only the last verb carries the polite ending and the tense. It is called the て form because it always ends in て or で. You will meet it again in the next two lessons, so it pays to learn it well now.

ru-verbs and the two irregular verbs

For ru-verbs, replace ます with て: たべます → たべて, みます → みて, おきます → おきて. The irregular verbs: します → して, きます → きて.

u-verbs: listen to the sound before ます

u-verbs change according to the sound just before ます. You cannot always tell a u-verb from a ru-verb by its ます form (みます is a ru-verb, のみます is a u-verb), so keep learning each verb with its group. The table looks long, but it is really one idea: the sound before ます decides whether you get って, んで, いて, いで or して.

い ち りってかいます → かって, まちます → まって, かえります → かえって

One exception to learn by heart: いきます → いって, not いいて.

Joining actions in order

Put the actions in the order they happen. The て form means "and then". You can chain as many as you like; just keep the last verb polite.

The tense goes only on the last verb. For yesterday, the て forms stay exactly the same and the last verb takes ました. The same goes for a negative or a question: only the end changes.

Joining adjectives

Adjectives join the same way. An い-adjective drops い and adds くて; a な-adjective adds で. いい becomes よくて. Japanese uses と for "and" between nouns (すしと てんぷら), and the て form for actions and descriptions.

{{ex:ja.s0143}}{{ex:ja.s0144}}
Most common mistake: using と for "and" between verbs, as you do between nouns. たべます と のみます → たべて、のみます. And remember the exception: いきて → いって. Read the kana first, then the romaji.
