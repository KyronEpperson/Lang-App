English makes a request with "please" and a verb: "Please wait." Japanese uses the て form you just learned, followed by ください. After a thing, ください means "please give me". After a て form, it means "please do". It is polite enough for shop staff, strangers and colleagues, and it is the request you will use most in Japan.

て ください: please do

{{ex:ja.s0145}}{{ex:ja.s0146}}

Compare the two: みずを ください is "Water, please" (give me some). みずを のんで ください asks someone to drink it.

{{ex:ja.s0147}}

To get someone's attention first, start with すみません. It turns a request to a stranger into a polite one, much like "excuse me" before a question in English. Ending with ください is already polite, so you do not need a separate word for "please".

{{ex:ja.s0148}}

ても いいですか: may I?

To ask permission, add も to the て form, then いいですか. Word for word it is "even if I do it, is it all right?" The answer for yes is はい、どうぞ; for no, you will hear the polite すみません、ちょっと….

{{ex:ja.s0149}}{{ex:ja.s0150}}

This form asks about what you are going to do. To ask someone else to do something, go back to て ください. Permission is about your action; a request is about theirs.

ては いけません: you must not

For rules, add は to the て form, then いけません, roughly "it won't do". You will see it on signs and hear it from teachers. Said to someone's face it sounds strict, so use it for rules, not for advice.

{{ex:ja.s0151}}{{ex:ja.s0152}}

The three patterns share one shape: the て form, then a separate word. Get the て form right and all three follow.

Most common mistake: asking permission when you mean to ask for help. To get someone to open the window, まどを あけても いいですか (may I open it?) → まどを あけて ください. Read the kana first, then the romaji.
