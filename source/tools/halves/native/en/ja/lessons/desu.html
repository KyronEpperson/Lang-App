English needs "am", "is" or "are" and a subject in every sentence. Japanese says it with one word at the very end, です, and often leaves the subject out.

X は Y です

The basic pattern is "X は Y です": X is what you are talking about, Y is what you say about it. は is the topic particle, like "as for…". So わたしは エミリーです is "as for me, (I) am Emily". は does not mean "is"; です does, and it comes last.

Leave out "I" and "you"

Once the topic is clear, Japanese drops it. After you have said your name, がくせいです is enough for "I'm a student". For "you", use the person's name + さん, or nothing at all. The dictionary word あなた sounds cold, even rude, to someone you have just met, so this course avoids it.

{{ex:ja.s0037}}

さん is for other people

Put さん after someone else's family or given name: たなかさん, エミリーさん. It works for men and women alike. Never put it after your own name. A teacher gets せんせい instead: やまだせんせい.

{{ex:ja.s0038}}

Questions with か

English flips the words for a question: "you are" becomes "are you". Japanese keeps the order and adds か at the end.

{{ex:ja.s0039}}

Saying "not"

The negative of です is じゃないです. じゃありません means the same and sounds a little more formal. Answer "no" with いいえ.

{{ex:ja.s0040}}{{ex:ja.s0041}}

A full introduction

Begin with はじめまして ("nice to meet you", only at a first meeting), give your name and one fact about yourself, and finish with よろしく おねがいします. It has no exact English; think "I hope we get along". To get someone's attention or to apologize, say すみません, "excuse me" or "sorry".
