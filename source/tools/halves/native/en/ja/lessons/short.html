So far every sentence has ended in です or ます. That polite style is right with strangers, staff, teachers and colleagues. Between friends and family, Japanese uses short forms (also called plain forms): the same words without the polite ending.

The dictionary form

The short form of a ます verb is its dictionary form, the form a dictionary lists. ru-verbs swap ます for る. u-verbs change the sound before ます from an i-sound to a u-sound. The two irregular verbs are する and くる.

PoliteShortEnglish
たべますたべるeat (ru-verb)
のみますのむdrink (u-verb)
いきますいくgo (u-verb)
かいますかうbuy (u-verb)
しますするdo
きますくるcome

The negative: ない

English adds "don't". Japanese changes the end of the verb to ない. ru-verbs drop る and add ない. u-verbs change the u-sound to an a-sound, and a verb ending in う takes わ. する becomes しない, くる becomes こない, and ある ("there is") is just ない.

Adjectives and nouns

For an い-adjective, just drop です: たかいです becomes たかい, and たかくないです becomes たかくない. For a noun or な-adjective, です becomes だ, and じゃないです becomes じゃない. In conversation, だ is often left off.

Casual questions

Casual questions usually drop か. Say the short form with a rising voice: いく？ The answer is うん (yeah) or ううん (nope). Never end a question with だ. The rising voice does the job that か does in polite speech.

Which style when?

Use short forms with friends, family and children. With anyone you have just met, anyone older or senior, and anyone serving you, stay with です and ます. When in doubt, stay polite. Short forms are not only for friends, though: from the next lesson on, they also appear inside polite sentences, before endings such as と おもいます.

Most common mistake: short forms with a stranger. To a shop clerk, いく？ → いきますか。 Also watch "there isn't": あらない → ない, and casual questions: がくせいだ？ → がくせい？ Read the kana first, then check the romaji.
