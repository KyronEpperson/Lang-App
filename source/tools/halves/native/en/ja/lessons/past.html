English shows the past inside the verb: eat becomes ate, go becomes went. Japanese is far more regular. Only the polite ending at the end of the sentence changes, and it changes the same way for every verb.

Verbs: ました and ませんでした

Swap ます for ました and you have the past. For "didn't", start from the negative ません and add でした: ませんでした. There are no irregular past forms here: します and きます follow the same pattern. Because the ending comes last, listen to the very end of a Japanese sentence: that is where you find out whether something happened or not.

Noたべませんたべませんでした

Nouns: でした and じゃなかったです

The polite "be", です, has its own past: でした, "was" or "were". "Wasn't" is じゃなかったです. You will also hear じゃありませんでした, which means the same and sounds a little more formal.

Time words and days of the week

Words like きのう (yesterday), せんしゅう (last week) and きょねん (last year) take no particle, just like まいにち. Days of the week work like clock times: they take the time particle に.

Asking and answering

Add か as usual: いきましたか, "did you go?" English answers "Yes, I did". Japanese has no "did" to repeat, so you answer with the verb itself, in the past: はい、いきました for yes, and the negative past for no. Leave out the subject, as in any answer where it is clear who you mean.

One warning for later: words like たのしい (fun) are adjectives, and adjectives do not simply add でした. You will learn their past in lesson 14.

Most common mistake: building "didn't" the English way, with "not" after the past. たべましたじゃない → たべませんでした. The negative comes first, then the past. And read the kana before you peek at the romaji.
