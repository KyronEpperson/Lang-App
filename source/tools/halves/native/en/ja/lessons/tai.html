English "want to" is a separate verb in front of the action. Japanese adds たい to the action verb itself: いきます becomes いきたいです, "I want to go". The verb and たい make one word, and the sentence still ends politely with です.

Making the たい form

Take the ます form, drop ます, and add たいです. It works for every verb, the irregular ones too. Everything else in the sentence stays the same: きょうとに いきます becomes きょうとに いきたいです, with the same particle に.

ます formWant to
いきますいきたいです
たべますたべたいです
みますみたいです
しますしたいです
きますきたいです

たい works like an い-adjective

たい ends in い, and it changes exactly like たかい: たくないです for "don't want to", たかったです for "wanted to", and たくなかったです for "didn't want to".

{{ex:ja.s0198}}{{ex:ja.s0199}}

を or が

With たい, the thing you want to eat, see or buy can take を or が. Both are natural. A place, a person or a time keeps its usual particle, though: に stays に, and で stays で.

{{ex:ja.s0200}}

Wanting a thing: ほしいです

For a thing rather than an action, use ほしいです, with が on the thing. ほしい is an い-adjective too: ほしくないです, ほしかったです. Do not join ほしい to a verb: "I want to buy" is かいたいです.

{{ex:ja.s0201}}

Only for yourself

Japanese does not say flatly what someone else wants, because you cannot see inside their head. In English "Ken wants to go" is normal; in Japanese it sounds as if you were reading Ken's mind. To pass on another person's wish, quote it with と いって いました.

{{ex:ja.s0202}}

Asking a person directly with たいですか is fine with friends, but it can sound blunt with others. To invite someone, use the ませんか form instead. For your own needs, though, たいです works well: at a station, すみません、きっぷを かいたいです tells the staff exactly what you need.

{{ex:ja.s0203}}
Most common mistake: たいです about other people. けんさんは いきたいです → けんさんは いきたいと いって いました. And ほしい with a verb: かう ほしいです → かいたいです. Read the kana first, then check the romaji.
