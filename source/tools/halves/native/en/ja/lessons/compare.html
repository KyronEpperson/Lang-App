English changes the adjective to compare: "big, bigger, biggest", or adds "more". Japanese adjectives never change for comparing. Instead, small words around them do the work. This lesson has three patterns: comparing two things, asking which of two, and naming the one that is the most.

A は B より ～です

English says "A is bigger than B". Japanese says "A は, B より, big": より is the comparison particle, "than", and it goes right after B, the thing you compare against. The adjective stays exactly as it is.

{{ex:ja.s0182}}{{ex:ja.s0183}}

You do not need a word for "more". You may hear もっと, but it means "even more" or "more" with no "than" (もっと ゆっくり, "more slowly"), so leave it out of A は B より sentences for now.

Which one? どちらが

To ask "which is ~, A or B?", list the two with と…と and ask with どちらが. When a question word is the subject, as どちら is here, it takes が, never は. To answer, use の ほうが, "the side of…".

{{ex:ja.s0184}}{{ex:ja.s0185}}

どちら is only for two things. For three or more, use どれ, なに, どこ or だれ with いちばん. In the answer, the winner takes が, because it answers a question word.

The most: いちばん

いちばん means "number one", and it turns any adjective into "the most…" or "the -est". Name the group first with の なかで ("among"), then the winner with が. A place works as the group too: にほんで, "in Japan".

Tenses still work

The adjective at the end keeps its normal polite endings: きのうは きょうより さむかったです, "yesterday was colder than today".

Most common mistake: putting より after the wrong noun, as if it meant "more than". For "Tokyo is bigger than Osaka", とうきょうより おおさかは おおきいです (that says Osaka is bigger!) → とうきょうは おおさかより おおきいです. より goes after the thing you compare against. Read the kana first, then check the romaji.
