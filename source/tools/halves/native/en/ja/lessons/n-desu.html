English explains with tone of voice or with phrases like "you see" and "the thing is". Japanese has a small ending for this: んです.

Explaining with んです

いたいです simply states a fact: "it hurts". いたいんです explains a situation: "the thing is, it hurts". Use it when you give a reason, excuse yourself or describe a problem, for example at a pharmacy or a front desk.

{{ex:ja.s0211}}{{ex:ja.s0212}}

With か, it asks for an explanation of something you have noticed. どうしたんですか is what people ask when you look unwell or upset.

{{ex:ja.s0213}}

How to attach it

Verbs and い-adjectives go before んです in the short form, present or past. Nouns and な-adjectives add な.

Word+ んです
いくいくんです
いかないいかないんです
したしたんです
いたいいたいんです
ひまひまなんです
しごとしごとなんです
{{ex:ja.s0214}}

Because: ので

English puts "because" in front of the reason. Japanese puts ので after it: reason ので, result. The same な rule applies: nouns and な-adjectives take な before ので.

{{ex:ja.s0215}}{{ex:ja.s0216}}{{ex:ja.s0217}}

The reason always comes first and the result last. English can say "I'll go home because my head hurts", with the reason at the end. In a Japanese sentence, the part with ので comes first, and the main verb, as always, comes last.

You may also hear から for "because". ので sounds softer and more polite, so it is the safer choice for requests and excuses.

Don't overdo it

んです adds "the thing is". When nothing needs explaining, plain です and ます are better: to "Where are you from?", answer アメリカじんです, and keep んです for reasons, problems and surprises. Used in every sentence, it sounds pushy or emotional, as if you were always defending yourself.

Most common mistake: forgetting な after a noun. やすみので → やすみなので, and しごとんです → しごとなんです. Also, don't put んです on every sentence. Read the kana first, then check the romaji.
