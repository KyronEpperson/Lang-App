English has small words in front of the verb: "must", "have to", "should". Japanese builds them from short forms you already know, the short negative (いかない) and the past short form (いった), and puts the meaning at the end.

Must: なければ いけません

Japanese says "must go" as "if I don't go, it won't do". Take the short negative, change ない to なければ, and add いけません.

Short negativeMust…
いかないいかなければ いけません
たべないたべなければ いけません
しないしなければ いけません
こないこなければ いけません

You will also hear なければ なりません, which is more formal, and ないと いけません in everyday speech. They mean the same; this course uses なければ いけません.

Don't have to: なくても いいです

For "don't have to", change ない to なくても and add いいです: "even if you don't, it's fine". Add か to ask for permission not to do something. Both patterns start from the same short negative, so once you can say いかない, you can say "must go" and "don't have to go".

Advice: ほうが いいです

For "you'd better" or "you should", give advice with the past short form + ほうが いいです, "the side of having done it is good". For "you'd better not", use the short negative: ない ほうが いいです. The past form here does not mean the past: いった ほうが いいです is advice about what to do next.

Careful with people above you

ほうが いいです is direct advice, fine for friends and for someone who asked you. With a teacher or a boss it can sound pushy, so ask a question or soften it instead. なければ いけません is for rules and your own duties; said to someone senior, it sounds like an order. For yourself, it is always fine: くすりを のまなければ いけません explains your own duty.

Most common mistake: building なければ from the ます form. いきなければ いけません → いかなければ いけません: start from the short negative いかない. For advice, the past form (やすんだ ほうが いいです) is the usual choice; you will also hear the dictionary form. Read the kana first, then check the romaji.
