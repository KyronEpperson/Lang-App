English says "there is" for a book, a cat and a person alike. Japanese has two verbs: あります for things and plants, and います for people and animals.

あります or います?

Ask yourself: can it move on its own? A bag, a station or a tree: あります. A friend, a teacher or a dog: います. The negatives are ありません and いません. ありますか is also the everyday way to ask a shop whether they have something, and the answer may be すみません、ありません: "Sorry, we don't have any."

"There is a … in …"

To say what is somewhere, start with the place and the location particle に, then the thing with the subject particle が, then the verb. Where something is takes に, not で: で is only for where an action happens (lesson 9).

{{ex:ja.s0085}}{{ex:ja.s0086}}

"The … is in …"

When the thing is already what you are talking about ("Where's my wallet?"), start with it and the topic particle は, then the place. To ask, put どこに in the place slot. A rough guide: something you mention for the first time takes が, and something both of you already have in mind takes は.

On, under, next to

English says "on the desk". Japanese says "the desk's top": つくえの うえ. The thing you measure from comes first, then の, then the position word, then に.

WordMeaning
うえon, above
したunder, below
なかin, inside
となりnext to
まえin front of
うしろbehind

So "next to the bank" is ぎんこうの となり, never となりの ぎんこう, which would mean "the bank next door". The same words work for people and animals too; only the verb changes to います.

Most common mistake: あります for an animal or a person. へやに いぬが あります → へやに いぬが います. Read the kana first, and tap the romaji only to check.
