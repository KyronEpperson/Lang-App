English says "I think (that) it's good": the thinking comes first. Japanese puts the thought first and the verb of thinking last: おいしいと おもいます, "delicious と I think". The particle と marks what you think or say, like quotation marks.

Short form + と おもいます

The part before と is in the short form from the last lesson. Only おもいます at the very end is polite, so the whole sentence is still polite. Verbs and い-adjectives go straight before と.

Nouns and な-adjectives need だ

A noun or a な-adjective adds だ before と. Think of だ as the short form of です: がくせいです becomes がくせいだ. An い-adjective already ends in its own い, so it never takes だ.

StatementI think…
たかいですたかいと おもいます
しずかですしずかだと おもいます
がくせいですがくせいだと おもいます

"I don't think…"

English puts "not" on "think": "I don't think it's expensive". Japanese puts it inside the quote and keeps おもいます as it is: "I think it is not expensive". Use the short negative you already know: たかくない, こない, がくせいじゃない.

{{ex:ja.s0173}}{{ex:ja.s0174}}

Reporting: と いって いました

To pass on what someone said, use the same と with いって いました, "was saying". This is the everyday way to say "Ken said that…". The quoted part is a short form, just as with おもいます. Japanese also keeps the tense of the original words: Ken said "I'm going", so the quote stays いく, where English shifts to "he was going".

{{ex:ja.s0175}}

Your own thoughts

と おもいます is for what you yourself think. It also softens a statement: たかいです sounds certain, while たかいと おもいます sounds softer and less pushy, which suits polite conversation. For what someone else thinks, report what they said with と いって いました.

Most common mistake: だ after an い-adjective. おいしいだと おもいます → おいしいと おもいます. And for "I don't think it's tasty", put the negative in the quote: おいしいと おもいません → おいしくないと おもいます. Read the kana first, then check the romaji.
