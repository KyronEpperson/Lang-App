English adjectives never change: expensive, was expensive, isn't expensive. Japanese adjectives come in two kinds, and one kind carries its own polite endings, just like a verb.

Two kinds

い-adjectives end in い: たかい (expensive), おおきい (big), おいしい (tasty). な-adjectives take な before a noun: きれい (pretty, clean), しずか (quiet), ゆうめい (famous). Careful: きれい and ゆうめい end in い but are な-adjectives, so learn each word with its kind. Many word lists for learners show a な-adjective with its な: しずかな, ゆうめいな. The cards in this course do the same.

Before a noun

Both kinds go in front of the noun, as in English. An い-adjective goes straight in; a な-adjective adds な. Nothing else changes: a big park is おおきい こうえん, a famous park is ゆうめいな こうえん.

Is, isn't, was, wasn't

A な-adjective works like a noun: add です, じゃないです, でした or じゃなかったです. An い-adjective changes its own ending instead: drop the last い and add くないです (isn't) or かったです (was). The です at the end never changes: after an い-adjective it only makes the sentence polite, and the tense lives in the adjective itself. That is why たかいでした, with the past in the wrong place, is a mistake.

い-adjectiveな-adjective
isたかいですきれいです
isn'tたかくないですきれいじゃないです
wasたかかったですきれいでした
wasn'tたかくなかったですきれいじゃなかったです

You will also hear くありません for "isn't" (たかくありません). It means the same thing.

いい is irregular

いい (good) switches to an older form, よい, for every ending except いいです itself: よくないです (isn't good), よかったです (was good).

{{ex:ja.s0120}}

Very, and not very

とても means "very". あまり means "not very", and it always goes with a negative ending. With a positive ending it sounds broken, so never say あまり たかいです.

Most common mistake: adding でした to an い-adjective, as you would to a noun. たかいでした → たかかったです. And the reverse: きれいくないです → きれいじゃないです. Read the kana first, then the romaji.
