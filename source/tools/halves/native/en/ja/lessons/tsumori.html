This lesson has two useful patterns: one for what you plan to do, and one for how things change. Both use forms you already know, the short forms from the last few lessons and the adjective endings, so there is very little new to build.

Plans: dictionary form + つもりです

English says "I'm planning to go" or "I intend to go". Japanese says いく つもりです: the dictionary form, then つもり ("intention"), then です. The verb before つもり is always short, and the polite ending comes only at the very end.

{{ex:ja.s0189}}{{ex:ja.s0190}}

Not planning to: ない つもりです

For a plan not to do something, put the short negative before つもり: かわない つもりです, "I don't intend to buy it". つもり itself stays the same.

{{ex:ja.s0191}}

つもり is a firm plan you have already decided on. For something you only hope to do, the next lesson gives you たい.

To ask about someone else's plans, the plain ます form is the safe choice: しゅうまつは なにを しますか. A question with つもりですか can sound as if you are checking up on the person.

Changes: なります

English uses "get" or "become": "it got cold", "she became a doctor". Japanese uses なります ("become") and changes the word in front of it.

Word+ なりますEnglish
さむいさむく なりますget cold
たかいたかく なりますget expensive
いいよく なりますget better
げんきげんきに なりますget well
いしゃいしゃに なりますbecome a doctor

An い-adjective changes its last い to く. A noun or な-adjective adds に. いい is irregular, as always: よく.

Tense lives in なります

なります takes the usual polite endings: なりました for "got" or "became", なりません for "doesn't get". The word in front never changes for tense, so "it will get cold" and "it got cold" differ only in the ending of なります.

Most common mistake: つもり with a ます form. いきます つもりです → いく つもりです. And なります with nothing in between: さむい なりました → さむく なりました. Read the kana first, then check the romaji.
