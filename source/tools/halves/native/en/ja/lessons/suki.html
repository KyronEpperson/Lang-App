In English, "like" is a verb, and the thing you like is its object: "I like cats." In Japanese, すき is not a verb at all. It is a な-adjective that means roughly "liked" or "pleasing". So the sentence is closer to "As for me, cats are liked."

すき takes が

Because すき is not a verb, the thing you like does not take the object particle を. It takes the subject particle が. The person who likes it is the topic, with は, and is often left out when it is you. To ask, add か: ねこが すきですか. Answer with the adjective itself: はい、すきです.

The same pattern works for people, but be careful: said about a person, すきです can sound like a confession of love.

Love, dislike, and not like

だいすき is "really like" or "love" (for things and hobbies too). きらい is "dislike", and it sounds strong. Many people prefer the softer すきじゃないです, "I don't really like it", which uses the な-adjective endings you already know.

{{ex:ja.s0125}}{{ex:ja.s0126}}

Good at, bad at

じょうず (good at) and へた (bad at) work exactly like すき: the skill takes が. Use じょうず to praise other people. About yourself it sounds like bragging, so people say へた or nothing.

What kind do you like?

どんな means "what kind of" and goes right before a noun. Because すき is a な-adjective, すきな before a noun means "favorite". There is no separate word for it: "my favorite color" is simply "the liked color". Answer a どんな question with a noun, or with an adjective before a noun.

{{ex:ja.s0129}}{{ex:ja.s0130}}

For the past, use the な-adjective endings: すきでした, すきじゃなかったです.

Most common mistake: treating すき like the English verb, with the object particle. すしを すきです → すしが すきです. The same goes for きらい, じょうず and へた. Read the kana first, then the romaji.
