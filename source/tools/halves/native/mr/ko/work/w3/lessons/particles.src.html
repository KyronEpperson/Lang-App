Both 에 and 에서 attach to places, and English often translates both as "at". The difference is the verb. 에 goes with going, coming and being somewhere — and with times. 에서 goes with doing something at a place, and with "from".

에: where you go

Destination + 에 with 가요 (go) and 와요 (come).

{{ex:ko.s0120}}{{ex:ko.s0121}}{{ex:ko.s0122}}

에: where things are, and when

Location + 에 with 있어요 / 없어요, and time words + 에: 주말에 (on the weekend), 아침에 (in the morning).

{{ex:ko.s0056}}{{ex:ko.s0123}}{{ex:ko.s0124}}

에서: where you do something, and "from"

Place + 에서 when an action happens there: eating, studying, resting, working. Also "from" with 왔어요.

Topic vs subject, briefly

은/는 sets the frame ("as for me…"); 이/가 points at the one specific thing, especially new information or the answer to "who?".

Quick test: look at the verb. 가요, 와요, 있어요, 없어요 → 에. Anything you actively do there (먹어요, 공부해요, 쉬어요) → 에서.
Common mistakes
  • 에서 for a destination — 집에서 가요 ✗ → 집에 가요 ✓
  • 에 for an action — 카페에 공부해요 ✗ → 카페에서 공부해요 ✓
  • 에서 with 있어요 — 집에서 있어요 ✗ → 집에 있어요 ✓
  • Particle before the noun — 에 집 ✗ → 집에 ✓
