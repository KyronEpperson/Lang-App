English is Subject-Verb-Object: I eat rice. Korean is Subject-Object-Verb: I rice eat. Everything else can move around, but the verb closes the sentence. The thing the action is done to — the object — gets its own tag, 을/를.

{{ex:ko.s0109}}{{ex:ko.s0110}}

The object marker 을/를

Put 을 after a consonant and 를 after a vowel on whatever is eaten, drunk, seen, studied or bought.

{{ex:ko.s0111}}{{ex:ko.s0112}}{{ex:ko.s0113}}

Describing verbs take 이/가, not 을/를

좋아요 and 커요 describe their noun; nothing is being done to it, so there is no object.

{{ex:ko.s0072}}{{ex:ko.s0073}}

Drop what's obvious

Korean leaves out the subject whenever context makes it clear, and in casual speech 을/를 drops too. Real conversations are mostly just objects and verbs.

{{ex:ko.s0114}}{{ex:ko.s0115}}{{ex:ko.s0116}}

Questions

No word reordering. In 요-form, a question is the same sentence with rising intonation. Question words slot in where the answer would go: 뭐 (what), 어디 (where), 언제 (when), 누구 (who), 왜 (why), 어떻게 (how).

When you are unsure whether to drop 을/를, keep it. A sentence with every particle is always correct, just a little careful-sounding.
Common mistakes
  • English order — 저는 먹어요 밥을 ✗ → 저는 밥을 먹어요 ✓
  • Wrong form — 밥를 먹어요 ✗ → 밥을 먹어요 ✓ · 커피을 ✗ → 커피를 ✓
  • 을/를 with a describing verb — 가방을 커요 ✗ → 가방이 커요 ✓
