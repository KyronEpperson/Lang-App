Short sentences are fine, but joining them is what makes you sound fluent. Each of these endings replaces the 요 ending of the first sentence.

-고 = and

Stem + 고. Use it for two facts, or two actions in a row.

{{ex:ko.s0214}}{{ex:ko.s0215}}

-지만 = but

Stem + 지만.

{{ex:ko.s0216}}{{ex:ko.s0217}}

-아/어서 = so, because

Take the 요-form, swap 요 for 서. The reason comes first.

{{ex:ko.s0218}}{{ex:ko.s0219}}

-아/어서 = go and then…

With 가다 and 오다 it links two steps of one trip.

{{ex:ko.s0220}}{{ex:ko.s0221}}

Tense goes on the last verb

-아/어서 never takes the past; the final verb tells the time.

{{ex:ko.s0222}}
Between two full sentences, use the stand-alone words instead: 그리고 (and), 하지만 or 그런데 (but), 그래서 (so). The next lesson builds a story with them.
Common mistakes
  • 바빴어서 ✗ → 바빠서 ✓ (no past before 서)
  • 싸요고 ✗ → 싸고 ✓ (attach to the stem)
  • 비싸요지만 ✗ → 비싸지만 ✓
  • Mixing them up — 비싸서 맛있어요 means "it's tasty because it's expensive"
