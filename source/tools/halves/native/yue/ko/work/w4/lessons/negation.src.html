To say you don't do something, put 안 right in front of the verb. That one move covers most negative sentences. There is a longer version, -지 않아요, with the same meaning. Two cases work differently: nouns use 아니에요, and the negative of 있어요 is its own verb, 없어요.

안 + verb

안 goes directly before the verb — after the object, never before it.

{{ex:ko.s0130}}{{ex:ko.s0131}}{{ex:ko.s0132}}{{ex:ko.s0133}}

하다 verbs split

For noun + 하다, 안 goes between the noun and 해요. 좋아해요 is not noun + 하다, so it stays whole.

{{ex:ko.s0134}}{{ex:ko.s0135}}{{ex:ko.s0136}}

-지 않아요

Stem + 지 않아요. Same meaning as 안; it sounds a little more careful and is common in writing. Past: 지 않았어요.

{{ex:ko.s0137}}{{ex:ko.s0138}}{{ex:ko.s0139}}

Nouns and 있어요 have their own negatives

"Is not a …" is 이/가 아니에요. "There isn't / don't have" is 없어요 — never 안 있어요.

{{ex:ko.s0140}}{{ex:ko.s0141}}{{ex:ko.s0142}}
In conversation 안 is far more common than -지 않아요. Learn to produce 안 first and recognise -지 않아요 when you read it.
Common mistakes
  • 안 before the noun of a 하다 verb — 안 공부해요 ✗ → 공부 안 해요 ✓
  • 안 before the object — 안 커피를 마셔요 ✗ → 커피를 안 마셔요 ✓
  • 안 with 있어요 — 시간이 안 있어요 ✗ → 시간이 없어요 ✓
  • 안 with a noun — 학생 안 이에요 ✗ → 학생이 아니에요 ✓
