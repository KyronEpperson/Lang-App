You have nouns, verbs and particles. Almost every beginner sentence is one of four shapes — and the verb is always last.

1 · A is B

Noun + 이에요 / 예요.

{{ex:ko.s0059}}

2 · A is (like this)

Noun + 이/가 + describing verb.

{{ex:ko.s0156}}

3 · Do something to A

Noun + 을/를 + action verb.

{{ex:ko.s0112}}

4 · Go to / be at / do at

Place + 에 with 가요, 와요, 있어요. Place + 에서 when an action happens there.

{{ex:ko.s0157}}{{ex:ko.s0126}}

Grow it one piece at a time

Start from the verb and add to the front. Usual order: who · when · with whom · where · what · verb.

{{ex:ko.s0158}}{{ex:ko.s0112}}{{ex:ko.s0159}}{{ex:ko.s0160}}

Swap the parts

Keep the frame, change one word. This is how one sentence becomes fifty.

{{ex:ko.s0161}}{{ex:ko.s0162}}{{ex:ko.s0163}}{{ex:ko.s0164}}
Stuck mid-sentence? Say the nouns you know, then end with the verb. Korean listeners wait for the verb anyway.
Common mistakes
  • English order — 저는 마셔요 커피를 ✗ → 저는 커피를 마셔요 ✓
  • 을/를 with a describing verb — 커피를 맛있어요 ✗ → 커피가 맛있어요 ✓
  • 에 for where you do something — 카페에 공부해요 ✗ → 카페에서 공부해요 ✓
