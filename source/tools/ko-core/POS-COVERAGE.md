# Korean: parts of speech in the 33 lessons

Read from `tools/halves/native/en/ko/lessons.jsonl`, the lesson pages in `tools/halves/native/en/ko/lessons/*.html`, and the pattern cards in `patterns.jsonl`. "Taught" means a lesson explains it as grammar. "Touched" means it appears in a lesson or as a pattern card without being explained as a class. "Missing" means nothing covers it.

## Proposed lessons

Outlines only. Nothing has been written or changed.

### A. Adverbs: how often, how much, and the ones that need "not"
- **Teaches:** adverbs go right before the verb (after the object). There are frequency (항상 > 자주 > 가끔 > 별로 안 > 전혀 안), degree (너무, 정말, 아주, 조금) and time (벌써, 아직, 곧, 이제) adverbs, and adverbs are made from adjectives with -게 (쉽게, 맛있게) or -히 (조용히, 천천히, 열심히).
- **English-speaker trap:** 별로 and 전혀 need a negative verb (별로 안 좋아해요, never *별로 좋아해요*). Also, 너무 means "so" as often as "too".
- **Examples:** 저는 아침을 자주 안 먹어요. / 매운 음식은 별로 안 좋아해요. / 천천히 말해 주세요.
- **Links to:** Top adverbs.

### B. Interjections: reacting like a Korean
- **Teaches:** the 15 most common reactions by situation: surprise (어, 헉, 세상에), pleasure (와, 우와, 아싸), pain and mistakes (아야, 앗, 아차), relief (휴), thinking (음, 글쎄요), teasing (에이), the phone (여보세요). Also who says what: 어머 and 아이참 skew female and older, and most are casual.
- **English-speaker trap:** 여보세요 is not "hello" in person, and 글쎄요 is usually a polite no, not "maybe".
- **Examples:** 아, 그래요? 몰랐어요. / 글쎄요, 잘 모르겠어요. / 아차, 우산을 두고 왔어요.
- **Links to:** Top interjections.

### C. More particles: to a person, from … until, by, like
- **Teaches:** 한테 / 에게 / 께 (to a person) vs 에 (to a place); 한테서 (from a person) vs 에서 (from a place); 부터 … 까지; (으)로 for means and direction (vowel or ㄹ → 로); 처럼; 마다; 밖에 + negative.
- **English-speaker trap:** using 에 for people (*친구에 줬어요* ✗ → 친구한테 줬어요 ✓), and 밖에 with a positive verb.
- **Examples:** 엄마한테 전화했어요. / 아홉 시부터 여섯 시까지 일해요. / 천 원밖에 없어요.
- **Links to:** Top particles.

### D. More connecting endings: because (with requests), while, before, after, when
- **Teaches:** -(으)니까 (a reason that can take a command or suggestion), -(으)면서, -기 전에, -(으)ㄴ 후에, -(으)ㄹ 때, -(으)려고, -아/어도, and -는데 as "background / but".
- **English-speaker trap:** -아서 cannot be followed by a command or suggestion (*비가 와서 우산 가져가세요* ✗ → 비가 오니까 우산 가져가세요 ✓).
- **Examples:** 늦었으니까 택시 타요. / 음악 들으면서 공부해요. / 자기 전에 이를 닦아요.
- **Links to:** Top connectors.

### E. Irregular verbs: ㄷ, 르, ㅅ, ㅎ, ㅡ and ㄹ-drop
- **Teaches:** one table per class with the 해요 form, past and modifier: 듣다 → 들어요, 부르다 → 불러요, 낫다 → 나아요, 그렇다 → 그래요 / 그런, 바쁘다 → 바빠요, 살다 → 사세요 / 사는. Also the regular look-alikes (닫다, 입다, 웃다).
- **English-speaker trap:** treating every ㄷ / ㅂ / ㅅ stem as irregular: 닫아요, 입어요 and 웃어요 are regular.
- **Examples:** 매일 음악을 들어요. / 노래방에서 노래를 불렀어요. / 감기가 다 나았어요.
- **Links to:** Top verbs, Top adjectives.

### F. Which, what kind, every: question words before a noun
- **Teaches:** 어느 (which, from a set), 무슨 (what kind / what), 어떤 (what sort, what is it like), 몇 (how many) + counter; then 모든, 여러, 다른, 새, 첫.
- **English-speaker trap:** using 뭐 before a noun (*뭐 음식* ✗ → 무슨 음식 ✓), and 어느 for open questions.
- **Examples:** 어느 나라 사람이에요? / 무슨 음식 좋아해요? / 다른 거 있어요?
- **Links to:** Top words.

### G. Endings that soften: -네요, -죠, -군요
- **Teaches:** -네요 for reacting to something you just noticed (맛있네요), -죠 for checking agreement or a softened statement (맛있죠? / 제가 할게요 → 제가 하죠), and -군요 for "oh, I see" (recognise only). These are the core of compliments and small talk.
- **English-speaker trap:** using -네요 about yourself or about facts you already knew (*제 이름은 민수네요* ✗).
- **Examples:** 한국어 정말 잘하시네요. / 날씨 좋죠? / 아, 그렇군요.
- **Links to:** Compliments, Flirting words.
