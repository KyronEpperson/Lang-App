# Worksheet: en-yue

## LESSON jyutping — 1 · Jyutping: initials and finals — deck "Jyutping sounds"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
This course teaches Cantonese as it is spoken in Hong Kong, written in traditional characters the way people write it in messages and ads, with Jyutping to show the sounds.
 One character, one syllable
 English letters spell sounds. A Cantonese character stands for one syllable with a meaning, so every Cantonese phrase in this course has a Jyutping line under it: one chunk per character, each ending in a number from 1 to 6. The number is the tone, and the next lesson is all about it. 美國人 mei5 gwok3 jan4 (American) is three characters, three syllables, three numbers.
 EX: 美國人 | mei5 gwok3 jan4 | American (a person)
CARDS NOW: 美國人 = American (a person)

### SECTION 1   (has 5)
Initials: the consonant at the start
 In Cantonese, b , d and g have no puff of air, while p , t and k have a strong one. Hold your hand in front of your mouth to feel it. Several other letters do not mean what English readers expect:
 Jyutping ; Sounds like ; Example ; 
 j ; the y in yes ; 有 ; 
 z ; the ds in beds , no puff ; 早 ; 
 c ; the ts in cats , with a puff ; 草 ; 
 ng ; the ng in singer , even at the start ; 我 ; 
 gw, kw ; the gu in Guam ; the qu in quick ; 貴, 誇 ; 
 EX: 有 | jau5 | to have
 EX: 早 | zou2 | early
 EX: 草 | cou2 | grass
 EX: 我 | ngo5 | I; me
CARDS NOW: 有 = to have [card] || 早 = early [card] || 草 = grass [card] || 我 = I; me [card] || 貴 = expensive [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 5)
Finals: the rest of the syllable
 aa is long, as in father ; a is short, like the u in but .
 eo and oe are vowels English lacks: say the u of fur with rounded lips. oe is the longer one.
 yu is "ee" with rounded lips, never the word you .
 eu is a rare final that slides from "eh" to "oo"; oi is the oy of boy , and ui runs "oo" into "ee".
 Final p , t and k are stopped, not released: close the mouth and stop, with no puff and no extra vowel.
 EX: 三 | saam1 | three
 EX: 心 | sam1 | heart
 EX: 去 | heoi3 | to go
 EX: 書 | syu1 | book
 EX: 十 | sap6 | ten
CARDS NOW: 三 = three [card] || 心 = heart [card] || 去 = to go [card] || 書 = book [card] || 十 = ten [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Syllables with no vowel
 m and ng can be whole syllables. 唔 m4 (not) is a low hum with closed lips, not "um". 五 ng5 (five) is the ng of sing on its own.
 EX: 唔 | m4 | not
 EX: 五 | ng5 | five
CARDS NOW: 唔 = not [card] || 五 = five [card]

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
Lazy sounds, and Yale
 Many Hong Kong speakers say lei5 for 你 (you), o5 for 我 and m5 for 五. These "lazy sounds" are very common, but the course writes the dictionary forms, which everyone understands: nei5 , ngo5 , ng5 . If you learned the older Yale system: Jyutping j is Yale y, z and c are Yale j and ch, and eo and oe are Yale eu.
 The Jyutping line is a crutch: listen to the audio first, and hide the line once the characters feel familiar.
 Most common mistake: reading j as in jam . 有 read "jow" → 有 jau5 , starting like yes .
CARDS NOW: (none)

## LESSON tones — 2 · The six tones, numbered 1 to 6 — deck "Tone twins"
### SECTION 0   (has 10)
English uses pitch for feelings: your voice goes up at the end of a question. In Cantonese, pitch is part of the word itself. Say 買 maai5 and you buy; say 賣 maai6 , with another tone, and you sell.
 EX: 我想買嘢。 | ngo5 soeng2 maai5 je5. | I want to buy some things.
 EX: 我想賣嘢。 | ngo5 soeng2 maai6 je5. | I want to sell some things.
 Six tones, numbered 1 to 6
 Tone ; Shape ; Word ; Jyutping ; Meaning ; 
 1 ; high and level ; 詩 ; si1 ; poem ; 
 2 ; rises from the middle ; 史 ; si2 ; history ; 
 3 ; middle and level ; 試 ; si3 ; to try ; 
 4 ; low and falling ; 時 ; si4 ; time ; 
 5 ; rises from low ; 市 ; si5 ; market; city ; 
 6 ; low and level ; 事 ; si6 ; matter; thing ; 
 These six words are tone twins: the same letters with a different tone make a different word. Think of three level tones, high (1), middle (3) and low (6); two rising tones, from the middle (2) and from low (5); and one falling tone (4), the lowest of all.
 EX: 詩 | si1 | poem
 EX: 試 | si3 | to try
 EX: 時 | si4 | time; hour
 EX: 事 | si6 | matter; thing
CARDS NOW: 買 = to buy [card] || 賣 = to sell [card] || 我想買嘢。 = I want to buy some things. || 我想賣嘢。 = I want to sell some things. || 詩 = poem [card] || 史 = history [card] || 試 = to try [card] || 時 = time; hour [card] || 市 = city; market [card] || 事 = matter; thing to do [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
Pairs that are easy to mix up
 Tones 3 and 6 are both level, one step apart. Tones 2 and 5 both rise, from different heights; some younger speakers barely separate them, but dictionaries and this course keep them apart. Famous pairs: 四 sei3 (four) and 死 sei2 (to die); 係 hai6 (to be) and 喺 hai2 (at, in).
 EX: 四 | sei3 | four
 EX: 死 | sei2 | to die
CARDS NOW: 四 = four [card] || 死 = to die [card] || 係 = to be [card] || 喺 = at; in [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Syllables ending in p, t or k
 A syllable that ends in p , t or k is short and clipped, and it only comes in tone 1, 3 or 6: 七 cat1 (seven), 八 baat3 (eight), 六 luk6 (six).
 EX: 八 | baat3 | eight
CARDS NOW: 八 = eight [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Changed tones
 In some words the last syllable rises. 話 waa6 on its own has tone 6, but in 電話 din6 waa2 (phone) it rises to tone 2. In 媽媽 maa4 maa1 (mom) the second syllable goes up to tone 1. The Jyutping line always writes the tone people actually say. It also keeps the dictionary forms of the lazy sounds from lesson 1: nei5 for 你, even if you hear lei5 .
 EX: 電話 | din6 waa2 | phone
 EX: 媽媽 | maa4 maa1 | mom
CARDS NOW: 電話 = phone; phone number [card] || 媽媽 = mom [card]

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
No English question rise
 Every syllable keeps its own tone, even at the end of a question. Do not push the last word up the English way: a small word at the end, such as 呀 aa3 , does the asking (lesson 6).
 Most common mistake: pulling every syllable up to a high, flat tone 1. ngo1 soeng1 maai1 → 我想買 ngo5 soeng2 maai5 : low rising, middle rising, low rising.
CARDS NOW: (none)

DECK CARDS IN NO SECTION (add by front, as a string, only where a section teaches them): 九 | gau2 | nine || 夠 | gau3 | enough || 舊 | gau6 | old (of things) || 粉 | fan2 | rice noodles; powder || 瞓 | fan3 | to sleep || 馬 | maa5 | horse

## LESSON greetings — 3 · Hello, thanks and sorry: 唔該 and 多謝 — deck "Greetings and courtesy"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Cantonese greetings are short, and there are two different thank-yous. Get those two right and you sound polite at once.
 Hello and good morning
 你好 nei5 hou2 is "hello" when you meet someone new. With friends it sounds stiff: people just say the name, or 早晨 zou2 san4 (good morning) until about noon. There is no everyday "good afternoon". "How are you?" copied word for word, 你好嗎 , is understood but sounds translated; friends ask 最近點呀？ zeoi3 gan6 dim2 aa3? (how have you been?).
 EX: 你好！ | nei5 hou2! | Hello!
 EX: 早晨！ | zou2 san4! | Good morning!
CARDS NOW: 你好！ = Hello! [card] || 早晨！ = Good morning! [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
Two ways to say thank you
 English has one "thank you"; Cantonese has two. 唔該 m4 goi1 is for a service or a small favor: the server who brings your tea, someone who holds a door. It also means "please", and "excuse me" when you call a server. 多謝 do1 ze6 is for a gift, money or kind words. Add 晒 to either one for "thanks so much".
 EX: 唔該！ | m4 goi1! | Thanks! (for a service)
 EX: 多謝！ | do1 ze6! | Thank you! (for a gift or kind words)
 EX: 唔該晒！ | m4 goi1 saai3! | Thanks a lot! (for your help)
 The answer to both is 唔使客氣 m4 sai2 haak3 hei3 , "you're welcome", literally "no need to be polite".
 EX: 唔使客氣。 | m4 sai2 haak3 hei3. | You're welcome.
CARDS NOW: 唔該！ = Thanks! / Excuse me! (for a service) [card] || 多謝！ = Thank you! (for a gift or kind words) [card] || 唔該晒！ = Thanks so much! (for help) [card] || 唔使客氣。 = You're welcome. [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
Sorry, and goodbye
 唔好意思 m4 hou2 ji3 si3 is the small "sorry" or "excuse me": being late, squeezing past. 對唔住 deoi3 m4 zyu6 is a real apology, when you have done something wrong. To leave, say 拜拜 baai1 baai3 , from English "bye-bye"; at night, 早抖 zou2 dau2 means "good night, get some rest".
 EX: 唔好意思。 | m4 hou2 ji3 si3. | Excuse me. / Sorry. (small things)
 EX: 對唔住。 | deoi3 m4 zyu6. | I'm sorry.
 EX: 拜拜！ | baai1 baai3! | Bye!
CARDS NOW: 唔好意思。 = Excuse me. / Sorry. (small things) [card] || 對唔住。 = I'm sorry. [card] || 拜拜！ = Bye! [card] || 早抖！ = Good night! (get some rest) [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Ordering a drink
 To order, say 唔該 , then the number, then a small word for the container, then the drink. English says "a milk tea"; Cantonese says "one cup milk tea", 一杯奶茶 jat1 bui1 naai5 caa4 , with 杯 (cup) between the number and the drink. That small word is a classifier, and lesson 10 is all about them.
 EX: 唔該，一杯奶茶。 | m4 goi1, jat1 bui1 naai5 caa4. | One milk tea, please.
 Most common mistake: thanking a server with 多謝 for every dish. 多謝 to the server → 唔該 . Keep 多謝 for gifts and kind words.
CARDS NOW: 唔該，一杯奶茶。 = One milk tea, please.

(for a gift) || 唔緊要。 | m4 gan2 jiu3. | It's OK. / Never mind. || 冇問題。 | mou5 man6 tai4. | No problem. || 好耐冇見！ | hou2 noi6 mou5 gin3! | Long time no see! || 生日快樂！ | saang1 jat6 faai3 lok6! | Happy birthday! || 請坐。 | cing2 co5. | Please have a seat. || 喂？ | wai2? | Hello? ## LESSON characters — 4 · First characters: strokes and radicals — deck "First characters"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
Cantonese is written in characters, not with an alphabet. This course does not ask you to write them by hand: the goal is to recognize about 250 by the end, enough to read menus, signs and family messages.
 Pictures
 Some characters began as drawings. 人 is a person walking, 口 an open mouth, 日 the sun, 月 the moon, 山 three peaks, 水 flowing water and 木 a tree.
 EX: 人 | jan4 | person
 EX: 口 | hau2 | mouth
 EX: 日 | jat6 | sun; day
 EX: 山 | saan1 | mountain
 EX: 水 | seoi2 | water
CARDS NOW: 人 = person [card] || 口 = mouth [card] || 日 = sun; day [card] || 月 = moon; month [card] || 山 = mountain; hill [card] || 水 = water [card] || 木 = wood; tree [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
Parts that combine
 Many characters are built from two parts. Two trees make 林 lam4 (woods). A person leaning on a tree makes 休 jau1 (to rest). The sun and the moon make 明 ming4 (bright). A woman, 女 , next to a child, 子 , makes 好 hou2 (good).
 EX: 明 | ming4 | bright
 EX: 好 | hou2 | good
CARDS NOW: 林 = woods [card] || 休 = to rest [card] || 明 = bright [card] || 好 = good [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
Strokes and radicals
 Each character is written with strokes, the lines, dots and hooks of the pen. A part that turns up in many characters is a radical, and it often hints at the meaning: 亻 is a squeezed 人 (person), as in 休 and 你; 氵 is water; 女 is woman.
CARDS NOW: (none)

### SECTION 3   (has 4)
The 口 of spoken Cantonese
 Cantonese has everyday words that Mandarin does not, and many of their characters carry 口 (mouth) on the left, as if to say "this is a spoken word": 嘅 , 咗 , 啲 , 嘢 , 呢 , 嚟 , 喺 . When you see 口 on the left, think "Cantonese word".
 EX: 嘢 | je5 | thing; stuff
 EX: 喺 | hai2 | at; in
CARDS NOW: 嘅 = 's; of (links owner and noun) [card] || 咗 = (marks a finished action) [card] || 嘢 = thing; stuff [card] || 喺 = at; in [card]

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Signs, and simplified characters
 Hong Kong uses traditional characters; mainland China uses simplified ones, with fewer strokes. Signs, menus and the news are often in standard written Chinese, a formal style that differs from speech: 出口 ceot1 hau2 on a door means "exit". This course teaches what people say, so "he is" is 佢係 keoi5 hai6 , even though a newspaper writes 他是.
 EX: 出口 | ceot1 hau2 | exit
 Most common mistake: copying the written style into speech. 他是美國人 → 佢係美國人 keoi5 hai6 mei5 gwok3 jan4 , "He is American", the way people say it.
CARDS NOW: 出口 = exit

DECK CARDS IN NO SECTION (add by front, as a string, only where a section teaches them): 大 | daai6 | big || 小 | siu2 | small || 門 | mun4 | door; gate || 手 | sau2 | hand

## LESSON hai — 5 · 係 (to be) and the pronouns 我, 你, 佢 — deck "People and family"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
English says "I am a student". Cantonese uses the same order: 我係學生 ngo5 hai6 hok6 saang1 . The verb 係 joins two nouns: who someone is, and what they are.
 EX: 我係學生。 | ngo5 hai6 hok6 saang1. | I'm a student.
 EX: 佢係老師。 | keoi5 hai6 lou5 si1. | She's a teacher.
 No "a", and no changes
 There is no word for "a" here, and 係 never changes: I am, you are, she is and they are are all just 係, and it does not change for time either: the same 係 covers "was" and "were". Nouns do not change either: English adds -s for "students", but 學生 is one student or many.
CARDS NOW: 我係學生。 = I'm a student. || 佢係老師。 = She's a teacher. || 學生 = student [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 6)
I, you, he, she, they
 One ; Jyutping ; More than one ; Jyutping ; 
 我 ; ngo5 (I, me) ; 我哋 ; ngo5 dei6 (we, us) ; 
 你 ; nei5 (you) ; 你哋 ; nei5 dei6 (you all) ; 
 佢 ; keoi5 (he, she, it) ; 佢哋 ; keoi5 dei6 (they, them) ; 
 哋 makes a pronoun plural. 佢 covers he, she and it, so only the context tells you which. There is only one "you": 你 works for your boss and your grandma alike, and politeness comes from other words. Newspapers write 他 and 她 for he and she; in speech it is always 佢.
 EX: 佢哋係學生。 | keoi5 dei6 hai6 hok6 saang1. | They're students.
 EX: 我哋係朋友。 | ngo5 dei6 hai6 pang4 jau5. | We're friends.
CARDS NOW: 我哋 = we; us [card] || 你哋 = you (more than one) [card] || 佢哋 = they; them [card] || 朋友 = friend [card] || 佢哋係學生。 = They're students. || 我哋係朋友。 = We're friends.

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Not: 唔係
 Put 唔 right before 係: 唔係 m4 hai6 means "am not, is not, are not". 唔 is the everyday "not", and lesson 7 does much more with it.
 EX: 我唔係老師。 | ngo5 m4 hai6 lou5 si1. | I'm not a teacher.
CARDS NOW: 唔係 = no; not (be) [card] || 我唔係老師。 = I'm not a teacher. [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
My friend, and "too"
 The owner goes before the noun, and for people close to you nothing goes in between: 我朋友 ngo5 pang4 jau5 is "my friend". For "too" or "also", put 都 dou1 right after the subject, before 係. English puts "too" at the end; Cantonese never does.
 EX: 佢係我朋友。 | keoi5 hai6 ngo5 pang4 jau5. | He's my friend.
 EX: 我都係學生。 | ngo5 dou1 hai6 hok6 saang1. | I'm a student too.
CARDS NOW: 佢係我朋友。 = He's my friend. [card] || 我都係學生。 = I'm a student too.

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
No 係 with adjectives
 English uses "is" with adjectives too: "she is tired". Cantonese does not. Use 好 (literally "very") instead: 佢好攰 keoi5 hou2 gui6 . Lesson 22 comes back to this.
 EX: 佢好攰。 | keoi5 hou2 gui6. | She's tired.
 Most common mistake: putting 係 before an adjective, as English does with "is". 佢係攰 → 佢好攰 .
CARDS NOW: 佢好攰。 = She's tired.

## LESSON intro — 6 · Introducing yourself: 我叫…, 我係美國人 — deck "About me"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
Introducing yourself takes a few short sentences. They keep the English order, with one twist: a question does not move anything to the front.
 My name is…
 Cantonese says "I am called": 我叫 ngo5 giu3 + your name. Do not copy "my name is" word for word; 我叫 already says it.
 EX: 我叫安娜。 | ngo5 giu3 on1 naa4. | My name is Anna.
 EX: 我叫馬克。 | ngo5 giu3 maa5 hak1. | My name is Mark.
 Many Hong Kong people also use an English given name and write it in English letters. In this course the two learners write their names in characters: 安娜 (Anna) and 馬克 (Mark).
 For your family name, use 姓 sing3 . You may hear the polite question 你貴姓呀？ nei5 gwai3 sing3 aa3? , "what's your family name?". Answer with 我姓…, never with 貴: that is only for the other person's name.
 EX: 我姓陳。 | ngo5 sing3 can4. | My last name is Chan.
CARDS NOW: 我叫安娜。 = My name is Anna. [card] || 我叫馬克。 = My name is Mark. || 我姓陳。 = My last name is Chan. [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Asking someone's name
 English moves "what" to the front. Cantonese leaves the question word where the answer goes: 我叫安娜 becomes 你叫咩名呀？ nei5 giu3 me1 meng2 aa3? , literally "you are called what name?". The 呀 at the end makes the question soft and friendly; without it, it can sound curt. To ask the same question back, say 你呢？ nei5 ne1? , "and you?".
 EX: 你叫咩名呀？ | nei5 giu3 me1 meng2 aa3? | What's your name?
 EX: 你呢？ | nei5 ne1? | And you?
CARDS NOW: 你叫咩名呀？ = What's your name? [card] || 你呢？ = And you? [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Where you are from
 A country + 人 (person) gives the nationality: 美國人 (American), 香港人 (from Hong Kong), 加拿大人 (Canadian). Join it with 係, as in lesson 5. To ask, say 你係邊度人呀？ nei5 hai6 bin1 dou6 jan4 aa3? , "where are you from?": again, the question word sits where the answer goes.
 EX: 我係美國人。 | ngo5 hai6 mei5 gwok3 jan4. | I'm American.
CARDS NOW: 你係邊度人呀？ = Where are you from? [card] || 我係美國人。 = I'm American. [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Where you live
 住喺 zyu6 hai2 means "live in". Here the place comes after the verb, as in English; with most other verbs, 喺 + place goes before the verb (lesson 13). 三藩市 is San Francisco. To ask, keep the same order and swap in the question word: 你住喺邊度呀？ (where do you live?).
 EX: 我住喺三藩市。 | ngo5 zyu6 hai2 saam1 faan4 si5. | I live in San Francisco.
CARDS NOW: 我住喺三藩市。 = I live in San Francisco. [card]

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
The languages you speak
 識講 sik1 gong2 means "can speak": you know how to. The describer goes before the noun: 少少廣東話 , "a little Cantonese".
 EX: 我識講少少廣東話。 | ngo5 sik1 gong2 siu2 siu2 gwong2 dung1 waa2. | I can speak a little Cantonese.
 Most common mistake: copying "my name is" word for word. 我名係安娜 → 我叫安娜 .
CARDS NOW: 我識講少少廣東話。 = I can speak a little Cantonese. [card]

## LESSON m — 7 · 唔 (not) and yes/no questions: 係唔係, 好唔好 — deck "Questions and particles"
### SECTION 0   (has 6)
Lesson 5 used 唔 m4 in 唔係. It is the everyday "not", and it goes straight before a verb or an adjective, never after it the way English puts "not" after "is". Cantonese verbs never change, so English "don't", "doesn't" and "isn't" are all just 唔.
 EX: 我唔去。 | ngo5 m4 heoi3. | I'm not going.
 EX: 我唔攰。 | ngo5 m4 gui6. | I'm not tired.
 With an adjective there is no 係 and no 好: just 唔 + adjective, as in 唔貴 m4 gwai3 , "not expensive".
 Yes/no questions: verb, 唔, verb
 English asks "Are you going?" by moving words around. Cantonese keeps the order and says the verb, then 唔, then the verb again: "you go not go?". This is a verb-not-verb question. It already holds the "yes or no", so nothing else is needed: no "do", and no rising voice at the end.
 EX: 你去唔去呀？ | nei5 heoi3 m4 heoi3 aa3? | Are you going?
 EX: 你係唔係學生呀？ | nei5 hai6 m4 hai6 hok6 saang1 aa3? | Are you a student?
 EX: 貴唔貴呀？ | gwai3 m4 gwai3 aa3? | Is it expensive?
 It works with adjectives too (貴唔貴, 好唔好), and 呀 at the end keeps it friendly. In fast speech 係唔係 often shrinks to 係咪 hai6 mai6 , with the same meaning.
 EX: 你係咪美國人呀？ | nei5 hai6 mai6 mei5 gwok3 jan4 aa3? | Are you American?
CARDS NOW: 我唔去。 = I'm not going. || 我唔攰。 = I'm not tired. || 你去唔去呀？ = Are you going? [card] || 你係唔係學生呀？ = Are you a student? || 貴唔貴呀？ = Is it expensive? || 你係咪美國人呀？ = Are you American?

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
No word for yes or no
 Cantonese answers by repeating the verb: "go" or "not go". To 你去唔去呀？ say 去 for yes or 唔去 for no. To 貴唔貴呀？ the answer is 貴 or 唔貴. The short answer is complete on its own. Only a 係 question takes 係 or 唔係 as the answer.
 EX: 唔去。 | m4 heoi3. | No, I'm not going.
CARDS NOW: 唔去。 = No, I'm not going.

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Not … either
 都 (also) from lesson 5 comes before 唔: 我都唔去 ngo5 dou1 m4 heoi3 is "I'm not going either". English puts "either" at the end; Cantonese puts 都 right after the subject, then 唔, then the verb.
 EX: 我都唔去。 | ngo5 dou1 m4 heoi3. | I'm not going either.
CARDS NOW: 我都唔去。 = I'm not going either.

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
嗎, and a first look at 冇
 You may see questions ending in 嗎 , such as 你係學生嗎？ They are understood but sound formal or written; in speech, use verb-not-verb. And one verb never takes 唔: 有 (to have). Its "not" is 冇 mou5 , never 唔有 (lesson 14).
 EX: 我冇錢。 | ngo5 mou5 cin2. | I don't have any money.
 Most common mistake: answering every question with 係, like English "yes". 你去唔去呀？ 係 → 去 .
CARDS NOW: 我冇錢。 = I don't have any money. [card]

## LESSON questions — 8 · Question words: 咩, 邊個, 邊度, 幾時, 點解 — deck "Questions and particles"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
In English, question words jump to the front: "Where do you live?". In Cantonese they stay exactly where the answer would go. Say the answer sentence, then swap the answer for the question word. This one habit fixes most question mistakes.
 EX: 你住喺邊度呀？ | nei5 zyu6 hai2 bin1 dou6 aa3? | Where do you live?
 EX: 我住喺紐約。 | ngo5 zyu6 hai2 nau2 joek3. | I live in New York.
 The question words

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
What and who
 Because nothing moves, a question is built from a statement in one step. There is no "do" to add and no change in word order. 咩 goes where the thing would go: 你食咩呀？ is "you eat what?". 邊個 goes where the person would go: compare 佢係我老師 (she is my teacher) with 佢係邊個呀？ (who is she?). Only one word changes, and 呀 joins the end.
 EX: 你食咩呀？ | nei5 sik6 me1 aa3? | What are you having?
 EX: 佢係邊個呀？ | keoi5 hai6 bin1 go3 aa3? | Who is she?
CARDS NOW: 咩呀？ = What? [card] || 你食咩呀？ = What are you having? || 邊個呀？ = Who is it? [card] || 佢係邊個呀？ = Who is she? [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
When: before the verb
 Time words go before the verb in Cantonese, and 幾時 is a time word, so it sits there too. English says "I'm going to Hong Kong tomorrow"; Cantonese says "I tomorrow go Hong Kong", with 聽日 ting1 jat6 (tomorrow) before 去, and asks "you when go Hong Kong?".
 EX: 我聽日去香港。 | ngo5 ting1 jat6 heoi3 hoeng1 gong2. | I'm going to Hong Kong tomorrow.
 EX: 你幾時去香港呀？ | nei5 gei2 si4 heoi3 hoeng1 gong2 aa3? | When are you going to Hong Kong?
CARDS NOW: 聽日 = tomorrow [card] || 我聽日去香港。 = I'm going to Hong Kong tomorrow. || 你幾時去香港呀？ = When are you going to Hong Kong?

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Why and how
 點解 (why) comes first, or right after the subject, and the answer usually starts with 因為 jan1 wai6 (because). 點 (how) goes before the verb: 你點去呀？ is "how are you getting there?".
 EX: 點解你唔去呀？ | dim2 gaai2 nei5 m4 heoi3 aa3? | Why aren't you going?
CARDS NOW: 因為 = because [card] || 點解你唔去呀？ = Why aren't you going?

### SECTION 4   (has 3)
The ending: 呀 or 呢
 End a question-word question with 呀 to keep it friendly, or with 呢 ne1 to make it softer, a little wondering. Both are fine; a bare question can sound abrupt. On its own, 你呢？ asks the same question back. 邊度 also works after 喺 (at, in): 洗手間喺邊度呀？ sai2 sau2 gaan1 hai2 bin1 dou6 aa3? is "where is the restroom?".
 EX: 你叫咩名呢？ | nei5 giu3 me1 meng2 ne1? | And what's your name?
 Most common mistake: putting the question word first, as in English. 邊度你住？ → 你住喺邊度呀？
CARDS NOW: 你呢？ = And you? [card] || 洗手間喺邊度呀？ = Where's the restroom? [card] || 你叫咩名呢？ = And what's your name?

## LESSON numbers — 9 · Numbers: 一 to 十, 百, 千, 萬 — deck "Numbers and money"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
Cantonese numbers are very regular: learn one to ten and you can build every number up to 99 with no new words. The same numbers later give you the months too: 一月 jat1 jyut6 is January, "month one".

### SECTION 1   (has 6)
Eleven to 99
 Build the rest like a sum: 十二 sap6 ji6 is "ten two", 12; 二十 ji6 sap6 is "two ten", 20; 二十五 is 25. In everyday speech the twenties are often shortened with 廿 jaa6 : 廿五 for 25. Both are correct.
 EX: 十二 | sap6 ji6 | twelve
 EX: 二十五 | ji6 sap6 ng5 | twenty-five
 EX: 九十九 | gau2 sap6 gau2 | ninety-nine
CARDS NOW: 十二 = twelve [card] || 二十 = twenty [card] || 十五 = fifteen [card] || 二十五 = twenty-five || 廿五 = twenty-five (spoken form) [card] || 九十九 = ninety-nine [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 5)
Hundreds, thousands and 萬
 百 is a hundred and 千 a thousand. An empty tens place takes 零 (zero): 一百零五 is 105. Without 零, 一百五 is short for 一百五十, 150. Then comes a word English lacks: 萬 maan6 , ten thousand. Big numbers are counted in 萬, so 100,000 is 十萬 sap6 maan6 , "ten ten-thousands". English groups digits by thousands; Cantonese groups them by ten thousands, so a million is 一百萬 jat1 baak3 maan6 , "a hundred ten-thousands".
 EX: 一百零五 | jat1 baak3 ling4 ng5 | a hundred and five
 EX: 一百五 | jat1 baak3 ng5 | a hundred and fifty
 EX: 十萬 | sap6 maan6 | a hundred thousand
CARDS NOW: 零 = zero [card] || 一百零五 = a hundred and five [card] || 一百五 = a hundred and fifty [card] || 十萬 = a hundred thousand || 一百萬 = one million [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 4)
兩 or 二
 English has one "two"; Cantonese has two. In counting, inside numbers like 二十 and 十二, and in phone numbers, it is 二 . Right before a classifier, the small word between a number and a noun (lesson 10), it is 兩 loeng5 . 個 is the everyday classifier for people, so "two people" is 兩個人. Only a lone 2 right before the classifier becomes 兩: twenty-two people is 二十二個人 ji6 sap6 ji6 go3 jan4 . Before 百 and 千 both are said: 兩百 or 二百, 兩千 or 二千.
 EX: 兩個人 | loeng5 go3 jan4 | two people
 EX: 二十個人 | ji6 sap6 go3 jan4 | twenty people
CARDS NOW: 兩個 = two (of something) [card] || 兩個人 = two people [card] || 兩千 = two thousand [card] || 二十個人 = twenty people

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Phone numbers
 Phone numbers are read one digit at a time, with 二 for 2 and 零 for 0. Hong Kong numbers have eight digits, usually said in two groups of four. Prices use the same numbers, with 蚊 for "dollars" in speech (lesson 16).
 EX: 九二三四五六七八 | gau2 ji6 saam1 sei3 ng5 luk6 cat1 baat3 | 9234 5678 (a phone number)
 Most common mistake: 二 right before a classifier. 二個人 → 兩個人 .
CARDS NOW: 九二三四五六七八 = 9234 5678 (a phone number)

## LESSON classifiers — 10 · Classifiers: 個, 本, 杯, 張, and 'my' with 我本書 — deck "Classifiers"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
English counts some nouns straight away (three books) and others with a unit word in between (three cups of tea, two pieces of paper). Cantonese puts a unit word in front of every counted noun. That word is the classifier (you may have met the term "measure word").
 Number + classifier + noun
 The order never changes: number, then classifier, then noun. 三本書 saam1 bun2 syu1 is "three books"; 三書 is not Cantonese. And remember from the numbers lesson: two is 兩 loeng5 before a classifier, never 二.
 EX: 三本書 | saam1 bun2 syu1 | three books
 EX: 兩杯茶 | loeng5 bui1 caa4 | two cups of tea
CARDS NOW: 三本書 = three books [card] || 兩杯茶 = two cups of tea

### SECTION 1   (has 6)
Which classifier?
 Each noun has its usual classifier, often linked to its shape. Learn every new noun together with it.
 Classifier ; Used for ; Example ; 
 個 ; people and many everyday things ; 一個人 ; 
 本 ; books, magazines ; 一本書 ; 
 杯 ; a cup or glass of a drink ; 一杯咖啡 ; 
 張 ; flat things: tickets, tables, sheets of paper ; 一張枱 ; 
 隻 ; most animals ; 一隻貓 ; 
 件 ; tops and jackets ; 一件衫 ; 
 條 ; long, thin things: pants, roads, fish ; 一條褲 ; 
 架 ; cars and other vehicles ; 一架車 ; 
 部 ; machines: phones, computers ; 一部手機 ; 
 間 ; buildings, shops, restaurants ; 一間餐廳 ; 
 個 go3 is the everyday fallback, and plenty of nouns really do take it. But where a noun has its own classifier, 個 is wrong: one book is 一本書, not 一個書.
 EX: 一隻貓 | jat1 zek3 maau1 | a cat
CARDS NOW: 一個人 = one person; a person [card] || 一張枱 = a table [card] || 一隻貓 = a cat [card] || 一件衫 = a top; a piece of clothing [card] || 一條褲 = a pair of pants [card] || 一架車 = a car [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 5)
This, that, which
 English says "this book". Cantonese says "this + classifier + book": 呢本書 ni1 bun2 syu1 is "this book", 嗰本書 go2 bun2 syu1 is "that book" and 邊本書 bin1 bun2 syu1 is "which book". 呢 (this), 嗰 (that) and 邊 (which) all sit in front of the classifier, never straight in front of the noun.
 EX: 呢本書幾多錢呀？ | ni1 bun2 syu1 gei2 do1 cin2 aa3? | How much is this book?
 EX: 嗰個人係邊個呀？ | go2 go3 jan4 hai6 bin1 go3 aa3? | Who is that person?
 幾 gei2 (how many) works the same way in a question: 幾 + classifier + noun. The answer keeps the classifier too: 三本.
 EX: 你有幾本書呀？ | nei5 jau5 gei2 bun2 syu1 aa3? | How many books do you have?
CARDS NOW: 呢本書 = this book [card] || 嗰個人 = that person [card] || 呢本書幾多錢呀？ = How much is this book? || 嗰個人係邊個呀？ = Who is that person? || 你有幾本書呀？ = How many books do you have?

### SECTION 3   (has 3)
The classifier as "the" and "my"
 Cantonese has no word for "the". A classifier + noun with nothing in front of it points at one thing you both know about: 本書 means "the book".
 EX: 本書喺邊度呀？ | bun2 syu1 hai2 bin1 dou6 aa3? | Where's the book?
 Put a person in front and the thing is theirs: 我部手機 is "my phone", 你本書 is "your book". Nothing else is needed. The next lesson shows the other way to say "my", with 嘅.
 EX: 我部手機喺邊度呀？ | ngo5 bou6 sau2 gei1 hai2 bin1 dou6 aa3? | Where's my phone?
 Most common mistake: leaving the classifier out because English has none. 三書 → 三本書 , and 呢書 → 呢本書 .
CARDS NOW: 本書喺邊度呀？ = Where's the book? [card] || 我部手機 = my phone [card] || 我部手機喺邊度呀？ = Where's my phone?

## LESSON ge — 11 · 嘅: my, your, his (我嘅, 佢嘅) — deck "About me"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
English shows an owner in two ways: a word like "my", "your", "his", or 's after a name (Anna's phone). Cantonese uses one small link word for all of them: 嘅 ge3 . The owner comes first, then 嘅, then the thing.
 Owner + 嘅 + thing
 "My" is 我嘅 ngo5 ge3 , "your" is 你嘅 nei5 ge3 , "his" or "her" is 佢嘅 keoi5 ge3 , "our" is 我哋嘅 ngo5 dei6 ge3 . A name or a job works the same way: the teacher's book is 老師嘅書 lou5 si1 ge3 syu1 .
 EX: 我嘅手機 | ngo5 ge3 sau2 gei1 | my phone
 EX: 老師嘅書 | lou5 si1 ge3 syu1 | the teacher's book
 EX: 安娜嘅電話號碼 | on1 naa4 ge3 din6 waa2 hou6 maa5 | Anna's phone number
CARDS NOW: 我嘅 = mine; my [card] || 你嘅 = yours; your [card] || 佢嘅 = his; hers [card] || 我哋嘅 = ours; our [card] || 老師嘅書 = the teacher's book || 我嘅手機 = my phone || 安娜嘅電話號碼 = Anna's phone number

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
The owner always comes first
 English can put the owner after the thing: "the number of the hotel", "a friend of mine". Cantonese never does this inside a noun phrase. Whoever owns it goes in front, and 嘅 is not a copy of 's that can trail after the noun: 書我嘅 is not Cantonese, 我嘅書 is.
CARDS NOW: (none)

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
"Mine", "yours": 嘅 at the end
 To say "it's mine", end the sentence with the owner + 嘅. Here 嘅 stands for the thing itself, like English "mine" or "Anna's". To ask "whose?", use 邊個嘅 bin1 go3 ge3 , literally "who's".
 EX: 呢本書係我嘅。 | ni1 bun2 syu1 hai6 ngo5 ge3. | This book is mine.
 EX: 嗰部手機係邊個嘅呀？ | go2 bou6 sau2 gei1 hai6 bin1 go3 ge3 aa3? | Whose phone is that?
CARDS NOW: 呢本書係我嘅。 = This book is mine. || 嗰部手機係邊個嘅呀？ = Whose phone is that?

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Family and home: usually no 嘅
 With people close to you and with your home, Cantonese usually drops 嘅: 我媽媽 (my mom), 我朋友 (my friend), 我屋企 ngo5 uk1 kei2 (my home, my family). 我嘅媽媽 is not wrong, just less usual in everyday speech.
 EX: 我媽媽係老師。 | ngo5 maa4 maa1 hai6 lou5 si1. | My mom is a teacher.
 EX: 我朋友係香港人。 | ngo5 pang4 jau5 hai6 hoeng1 gong2 jan4. | My friend is from Hong Kong.
CARDS NOW: 我媽媽係老師。 = My mom is a teacher. || 我朋友係香港人。 = My friend is from Hong Kong.

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Two ways to say "my phone"
 For things, you now have two ways to say "my": 我嘅手機 , and from the last lesson, owner + classifier + noun, 我部手機 . Both are correct and both are common. If you are not sure of a noun's classifier, 嘅 is always a safe choice.
 Most common mistake: copying the English order, with the owner after the thing. 書我嘅 → 我嘅書 , and 電話安娜嘅 → 安娜嘅電話 .

## LESSON hai2 — 13 · 喺 (at, in), not 係: 我喺香港 — deck "Around town and directions"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
English uses "am" and "is" both for what you are ("I am a teacher") and for where you are ("I am in Hong Kong"). Cantonese uses two different words, and they differ only in tone: 係 hai6 (to be) and 喺 hai2 (at, in). They are tone twins, so say the tone carefully: hai6 is low and level, hai2 rises. Both come up in almost every conversation, so it pays to hear the difference early.
 Where something is: thing + 喺 + place
 To say where someone or something is, put 喺 between the thing and the place. No other verb is needed.
 EX: 我喺香港。 | ngo5 hai2 hoeng1 gong2. | I'm in Hong Kong.
 EX: 洗手間喺邊度呀？ | sai2 sau2 gaan1 hai2 bin1 dou6 aa3? | Where's the restroom?

Where you do something: before the verb
 English puts the place at the end: "I work at a bank", "I eat at home". Cantonese puts 喺 + place before the verb, in the middle of the sentence. Who, then where, then what you do.  EX: 我喺銀行做嘢。 | ngo5 hai2 ngan4 hong4 zou6 je5. | I work at a bank.

## LESSON jau-mou — 14 · 有 and 冇: have, and there is (never 唔有) — deck "Traps for English speakers"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
English needs two expressions: "I have a phone" and "there is a bank nearby". Cantonese uses one verb for both: 有 jau5 . And its negative is its own word, 冇 mou5 .
 有: have
 有 works like any verb: who, then 有, then the thing. Counted things keep their number and classifier.
 EX: 我有一部手機。 | ngo5 jau5 jat1 bou6 sau2 gei1. | I have a phone.
 EX: 我屋企有四個人。 | ngo5 uk1 kei2 jau5 sei3 go3 jan4. | There are four people in my family.

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)

## LESSON particles — 15 · Sentence-final particles: 呀, 啦, 吖, 呢 — deck "Questions and particles"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
English changes its melody to sound friendly, unsure or pushy: "Coming?" with a rise, "Come on!" with a fall. Cantonese cannot do that, because the melody of each syllable is its tone. To ask a price, Cantonese asks "how much money?": 幾多錢呀？ gei2 do1 cin2 aa3? . Notice the tone on 錢: in this question it is cin2, a changed tone, rising at the end.
 Asking the price

## LESSON food — 17 · Ordering food and drink: 唔該, 要, 埋單 — deck "Food and drink"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Ordering in Hong Kong is short and direct. Call the server with 唔該, say what you want with a number and a classifier, and you are done. Long polite English phrases ("Could I please have…") only slow things down.
 唔該: to call, to order, to thank
 唔該 m4 goi1 gets the server's attention, starts the order, and thanks them when the food arrives. Save 多謝 for gifts and compliments; saying it for every dish sounds odd.
 EX: 唔該！ | m4 goi1! | Excuse me! (to call the server)
CARDS NOW: 唔該！ = Thanks! / Excuse me! (for a service) [card] || 多謝！ = Thank you! (for a gift or kind words) [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
要: I'll have
 要 jiu3 means "want" or "I'll have". The food keeps its number and classifier: 我要 + number + classifier + food. You can also just say 唔該 and the order. For two or more, change the number and keep the classifier: 兩杯凍奶茶.
 EX: 唔該，我要一杯凍奶茶。 | m4 goi1, ngo5 jiu3 jat1 bui1 dung3 naai5 caa4. | An iced milk tea, please.
 EX: 唔該，一杯茶。 | m4 goi1, jat1 bui1 caa4. | A tea, please.
CARDS NOW: 凍奶茶 = iced milk tea [card] || 唔該，我要一杯凍奶茶。 = An iced milk tea, please. || 唔該，一杯茶。 = A tea, please.

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
Classifiers for food
 Food is counted by what it comes in.

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
No ice, less sweet

CARDS NOW: 唔該，走冰。 = No ice, please. [card]

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Dim sum and the cha chaan teng
 飲茶 jam2 caa4 , literally "drink tea", means going out for a dim sum meal with tea. A 茶餐廳 caa4 caan1 teng1 is a Hong Kong café serving milk tea, noodles and toast. At the counter you may hear 堂食定外賣？ , "for here or to go?" Answer 堂食 (here) or 外賣 (to go).
CARDS NOW: 飲茶 = to have dim sum (yum cha) [card]

### SECTION 5   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
The check

 Most common mistake: dropping the classifier, or starting with 請. 我要兩奶茶 → 我要兩杯奶茶 , and 請畀我一杯茶 → 唔該，一杯茶 .

## LESSON time — 18 · Clock time and dates: 幾點？, 三點半, 星期一 — deck "Time, days and dates"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
Clock time, days and dates in Cantonese are built from numbers you already know. The big difference from English is where the time goes in a sentence.
 What time is it?
 而家 ji4 gaa1 is "now", and 點 dim2 is "o'clock": 三點 is three o'clock. To ask, put 幾 (how many) in front: 幾點. The other hours just change the number: 一點, 十二點. Two o'clock is 兩點, because 點 behaves like a classifier here.
 EX: 而家幾點呀？ | ji4 gaa1 gei2 dim2 aa3? | What time is it now?
 EX: 而家三點。 | ji4 gaa1 saam1 dim2. | It's three o'clock.
CARDS NOW: 而家 = now [card] || 而家幾點呀？ = What time is it now? [card] || 而家三點。 = It's three o'clock.

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Half past and minutes
 半 is half: 三點半 is half past three. Minutes are number + 分 : 三點十分 is 3:10. You will also hear the clock face counted in steps of five minutes, each step a 字: 三點兩個字 is 3:10 too.
 EX: 三點半 | saam1 dim2 bun3 | half past three
 EX: 三點十分 | saam1 dim2 sap6 fan1 | ten past three
CARDS NOW: 三點半 = half past three [card] || 三點十分 = ten past three [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
Today, tomorrow, yesterday
 今日 gam1 jat6 is today, 聽日 ting1 jat6 tomorrow and 琴日 kam4 jat6 yesterday. In 聽日 the first character is ting1, not teng1 as in the verb "listen".
CARDS NOW: 今日 = today [card] || 聽日 = tomorrow [card] || 琴日 = yesterday [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 3)
Days of the week
 Monday to Saturday are 星期 + one to six: 星期一 is Monday, 星期六 Saturday. Sunday is 星期日. 禮拜 means the same as 星期, so 禮拜一 is also Monday.
 EX: 星期一 | sing1 kei4 jat1 | Monday
 EX: 禮拜日 | lai5 baai3 jat6 | Sunday
CARDS NOW: 星期 = week [card] || 星期一 = Monday [card] || 禮拜日 = Sunday

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Dates: month first
 English often says the day first or mixes it ("the third of December", "December 3"). Cantonese always goes from big to small, the same way every time: month first, then day. 月 is month and 號 is the day of the month.
 EX: 十二月三號 | sap6 ji6 jyut6 saam1 hou6 | December 3
 EX: 今日係十二月三號。 | gam1 jat6 hai6 sap6 ji6 jyut6 saam1 hou6. | Today is December 3.
CARDS NOW: 十二月三號 = December 3 [card] || 今日係十二月三號。 = Today is December 3.

### SECTION 5   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Time words go before the verb
 English puts "tomorrow" or "on Saturday" at the end: "I'm going to Hong Kong tomorrow". In Cantonese the time word goes before the verb, never after it. It can come right after the person, or at the very start of the sentence.
 EX: 我聽日去香港。 | ngo5 ting1 jat6 heoi3 hoeng1 gong2. | I'm going to Hong Kong tomorrow.
 EX: 我哋星期六飲茶。 | ngo5 dei6 sing1 kei4 luk6 jam2 caa4. | We're having dim sum on Saturday.
 Most common mistake: putting the time at the end, as in English. 我去香港聽日 → 我聽日去香港 . And for dates, 三號十二月 → 十二月三號 .
CARDS NOW: 我聽日去香港。 = I'm going to Hong Kong tomorrow. || 我哋星期六飲茶。 = We're having dim sum on Saturday.

## LESSON routine — 19 · My day: time and place before the verb — deck "Time, days and dates"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
English puts the details of your day at the end: "I eat at home in the evening ." Cantonese puts them up front, before the action. Learn the order once and you can describe your whole day.
 The fixed line
 A Cantonese sentence about your day runs in this order: who, when, where or how, and only then the verb and what you do. When and where go before the verb, never after it.
 Who ; When ; Where / how ; Verb + what ; 
 我 ; 朝早 ; 搭地鐵 ; 返工 ; 
 我 ; 夜晚 ; 喺屋企 ; 食飯 ; 
 EX: 我朝早搭地鐵返工。 | ngo5 ziu1 zou2 daap3 dei6 tit3 faan1 gung1. | I take the MTR to work in the morning.
 EX: 我夜晚喺屋企食飯。 | ngo5 je6 maan5 hai2 uk1 kei2 sik6 faan6. | I eat at home in the evening.
 The time word may also open the sentence, before the person: 夜晚我喺屋企食飯。 je6 maan5 ngo5 hai2 uk1 kei2 sik6 faan6. is fine too. What never happens is time or place after the verb.
CARDS NOW: 朝早 = morning [card] || 返工 = to go to work [card] || 夜晚 = night; evening [card] || 我朝早搭地鐵返工。 = I take the MTR to work in the morning. || 我夜晚喺屋企食飯。 = I eat at home in the evening.

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Parts of the day
 朝早 ziu1 zou2 is morning, 晏晝 aan3 zau3 is the middle of the day, and 夜晚 je6 maan5 is evening and night. Add a clock time from the last lesson after it: big to small, as with dates.
 EX: 我朝早七點起身。 | ngo5 ziu1 zou2 cat1 dim2 hei2 san1. | I get up at seven in the morning.
 English says "at seven". Cantonese needs no word for "at" before a clock time: the time simply sits before the verb. Keep 喺 hai2 for places, as in 喺屋企 (at home).
CARDS NOW: 我朝早七點起身。 = I get up at seven in the morning. [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
Words for a working day
 返工 faan1 gung1 is "go to work" (literally "go back to work", but it is the everyday word), and 放工 fong3 gung1 is "get off work". 食晏 sik6 aan3 is to have lunch, 沖涼 cung1 loeng4 is to take a shower, and 瞓覺 fan3 gaau3 is to go to bed or sleep.
 EX: 我十二點半食晏。 | ngo5 sap6 ji6 dim2 bun3 sik6 aan3. | I have lunch at twelve thirty.
 EX: 我夜晚十一點瞓覺。 | ngo5 je6 maan5 sap6 jat1 dim2 fan3 gaau3. | I go to bed at eleven at night.
CARDS NOW: 沖涼 = to shower; to bathe [card] || 瞓覺 = to go to sleep [card] || 我十二點半食晏。 = I have lunch at twelve thirty. || 我夜晚十一點瞓覺。 = I go to bed at eleven at night.

### SECTION 3   (has 4)
How often
 有時 jau5 si4 (sometimes) and 成日 seng4 jat6 (often, all the time) sit in the same place as other time words: after the person, before the verb. 每日 mui5 jat6 (every day) goes there too.
 EX: 我有時喺公司食晏。 | ngo5 jau5 si4 hai2 gung1 si1 sik6 aan3. | Sometimes I have lunch at the office.
 EX: 佢成日喺屋企食飯。 | keoi5 seng4 jat6 hai2 uk1 kei2 sik6 faan6. | He often eats at home.
CARDS NOW: 有時 = sometimes [card] || 成日 = always; all the time [card] || 我有時喺公司食晏。 = Sometimes I have lunch at the office. || 佢成日喺屋企食飯。 = He often eats at home.

### SECTION 4   (has 3)
Asking about someone's day
 Question words take the slot of the answer, as you learned in lesson 8. 幾點 gei2 dim2 (what time) goes in the time slot, 邊度 bin1 dou6 (where) in the place slot.
 EX: 你幾點返工呀？ | nei5 gei2 dim2 faan1 gung1 aa3? | What time do you go to work?

## LESSON modals — 20 · Want, can, like: 想, 可以, 識, 會, 鍾意, 要 — deck "Work and study"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
English has "want", "can", "will" and "like". Cantonese has a small set of words that do the same jobs. They all go right before the main verb, and the main verb never changes: no "to", no "-ing", no "-s". Each one has its own job, so English "can" splits into two different words.
 想 and 要: would like, want, need
 想 soeng2 is the soft one: "would like to", "feel like". 要 jiu3 is firmer: "want", and with a verb "need to" or "have to". You already order food with 要.
 EX: 我想飲杯咖啡。 | ngo5 soeng2 jam2 bui1 gaa3 fe1. | I'd like a coffee.
 EX: 我聽日九點要返工。 | ngo5 ting1 jat6 gau2 dim2 jiu3 faan1 gung1. | I have to go to work at nine tomorrow.
 Which one? 想 is softer and sounds polite when you talk about wishes and plans. 要 is direct: fine for ordering and for things you must do. When in doubt about a wish, choose 想.
 Notice the order: time first, then 要, then the verb. Words like 想 and 要 never move the time or place to the end.
CARDS NOW: 返工 = to go to work [card] || 我想飲杯咖啡。 = I'd like a coffee. || 我聽日九點要返工。 = I have to go to work at nine tomorrow.

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
識: know how to
 English says "I can swim" for a skill you learned. Cantonese uses 識 sik1 for that: know how to. Its negative is 唔識 m4 sik1 . With a person or a place instead of a verb, 識 means "know": 我識佢 (I know him).
 EX: 我識講少少廣東話。 | ngo5 sik1 gong2 siu2 siu2 gwong2 dung1 waa2. | I can speak a little Cantonese.
 EX: 我唔識游水。 | ngo5 m4 sik1 jau4 seoi2. | I can't swim.
CARDS NOW: 我識講少少廣東話。 = I can speak a little Cantonese. [card] || 我唔識游水。 = I can't swim.

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
可以: may, allowed to, possible
 可以 ho2 ji5 is "can" in the sense of permission or possibility, not skill. To ask "Can I…?", use the verb-not-verb question 可唔可以 ho2 m4 ho2 ji5 .
 EX: 我可唔可以坐呢度呀？ | ngo5 ho2 m4 ho2 ji5 co5 ni1 dou6 aa3? | Can I sit here?
 EX: 呢度唔可以食嘢。 | ni1 dou6 m4 ho2 ji5 sik6 je5. | You can't eat here.
CARDS NOW: 可以 = can; may [card] || 我可唔可以坐呢度呀？ = Can I sit here? || 呢度唔可以食嘢。 = You can't eat here.

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
會: will
 會 wui5 says what will happen or is likely to happen. It is about the future, not about skills. In a question, 會唔會 asks "will it…?".
 EX: 聽日會落雨。 | ting1 jat6 wui5 lok6 jyu5. | It will rain tomorrow.
CARDS NOW: 聽日會落雨。 = It will rain tomorrow.

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
鍾意: like
 鍾意 zung1 ji3 takes a thing or an action, with no extra word between them: English "like to eat" or "like eating" is just 鍾意食. Add 好 for "really like".
 EX: 我好鍾意食點心。 | ngo5 hou2 zung1 ji3 sik6 dim2 sam1. | I really like dim sum.
CARDS NOW: 鍾意 = to like [card] || 我好鍾意食點心。 = I really like dim sum.

### SECTION 5   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Saying no, and "no need"
 Put 唔 in front: 唔想 m4 soeng2 , 唔識, 唔可以 m4 ho2 ji5 , 唔鍾意. One trap: "don't need to" is not 唔要 (that means "don't want" a thing). It is 唔使 m4 sai2 , the same 唔使 as in 唔使客氣.
 EX: 你唔使畀錢。 | nei5 m4 sai2 bei2 cin2. | You don't need to pay.
CARDS NOW: 你唔使畀錢。 = You don't need to pay.

### SECTION 6   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
應該: should (just recognize it)
 You will also hear 應該 jing1 goi1 , "should", in the same slot before the verb. Recognize it for now.
 EX: 你應該休息。 | nei5 jing1 goi1 jau1 sik1. | You should rest.

## LESSON ding-dou — 21 · Choices and 'also': A 定 B？ and 都 — deck "Food and drink"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
At a Hong Kong café the server will ask you to choose: iced or hot? Here to eat or to take out? This lesson gives you the two words for "or", the word for "and" between things, and the word for "also".
 Two words for "or"
 English has one "or". Cantonese has two. In a question that offers a choice, use 定 ding6 : A 定 B？ The particle 呀 at the end keeps it friendly.

## LESSON zo — 23 · 咗: done; 冇 (didn't) and 未 (not yet) — deck "Health and help"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
English changes the verb for the past: eat, ate, have eaten. Cantonese verbs never change. Instead, a small word after the verb shows that the action is done. This lesson also covers the two ways to say "not" about the past: didn't, and not yet.
 咗: it's done
 Put 咗 zo2 right after the verb (it is an "aspect marker", if you like grammar words). It covers both "I ate" and "I've eaten". It sits between the verb and the thing: 買咗兩本書, never 買兩本書咗. Time and place still go before the verb.
 EX: 我食咗飯。 | ngo5 sik6 zo2 faan6. | I've eaten.

 EX: 我感冒咗。 | ngo5 gam2 mou6 zo2. | I've caught a cold.

## LESSON go-come — 25 · 去 and 嚟: going and coming; 搭車, 坐地鐵 — deck "Getting around"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
English says "go to Central" and "come to my place", with "to" before the place and "by bus" at the end. Cantonese needs no "to", and the "by bus" part moves to the front. The verb itself also depends on where the speaker stands.
 去 and 嚟
 去 heoi3 is "go", away from where you are. 嚟 lei4 is "come", toward the speaker. The place follows directly, with no word for "to".
 EX: 我聽日去中環。 | ngo5 ting1 jat6 heoi3 zung1 waan4. | I'm going to Central tomorrow.
 EX: 你幾時嚟香港呀？ | nei5 gei2 si4 lei4 hoeng1 gong2 aa3? | When are you coming to Hong Kong?
 Where the speaker is decides it: someone in Hong Kong asks 你幾時嚟香港呀？, while a friend in New York asks 你幾時去香港呀？ nei5 gei2 si4 heoi3 hoeng1 gong2 aa3? 
 An invitation to come over uses 嚟 and the particle 吖.
 EX: 嚟我屋企食飯吖！ | lei4 ngo5 uk1 kei2 sik6 faan6 aa1! | Come over to my place for dinner!
CARDS NOW: 我聽日去中環。 = I'm going to Central tomorrow. || 你幾時嚟香港呀？ = When are you coming to Hong Kong? [card] || 嚟我屋企食飯吖！ = Come over to my place for dinner!

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
返: going back where you belong
 返 faan1 is for going to a place that is yours: home, work, school. That is why 返工 and 返學 simply mean "go to work" and "go to school".
 EX: 我六點返屋企。 | ngo5 luk6 dim2 faan1 uk1 kei2. | I go home at six.
CARDS NOW: 我六點返屋企。 = I go home at six.

### SECTION 2   (has 5)
How you travel goes first
 English puts "by MTR" at the end. Cantonese puts how you travel before the verb of going: 搭 daap3 or 坐 co5 + the vehicle, then 去, 嚟 or 返 + the place. Both 搭 and 坐 work for the MTR, buses and taxis. On foot is 行路 haang4 lou6 .
 EX: 我搭地鐵去中環。 | ngo5 daap3 dei6 tit3 heoi3 zung1 waan4. | I take the MTR to Central.
 EX: 我坐巴士返工。 | ngo5 co5 baa1 si2 faan1 gung1. | I go to work by bus.
 EX: 我行路去。 | ngo5 haang4 lou6 heoi3. | I'll walk there.
 The vehicles you need most are 地鐵 (MTR), 巴士 (bus), 小巴 (minibus) and 的士 (taxi). 搭 is literally "ride" or "catch", and 坐 is "sit".
 The order matters: 我去中環搭地鐵 means "I'm going to Central to take the MTR". To ask how, put 點 dim2 (how) in the same slot.
 EX: 你點去機場呀？ | nei5 dim2 heoi3 gei1 coeng4 aa3? | How do you get to the airport?
 To ask where someone is going, the question word takes the place slot after 去, as you learned in lesson 8.
 EX: 你去邊度呀？ | nei5 heoi3 bin1 dou6 aa3? | Where are you going?
CARDS NOW: 我搭地鐵去中環。 = I take the MTR to Central. || 我坐巴士返工。 = I go to work by bus. || 我行路去。 = I'll walk there. [card] || 你點去機場呀？ = How do you get to the airport? || 你去邊度呀？ = Where are you going?

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Getting on and off
 上車 soeng5 ce1 is to get on and 落車 lok6 ce1 is to get off, for buses, trains and taxis alike. On a minibus, call out 前面有落！ cin4 min6 jau5 lok6! and the driver stops.
 EX: 我喺中環落車。 | ngo5 hai2 zung1 waan4 lok6 ce1. | I get off at Central.
 EX: 前面有落！ | cin4 min6 jau5 lok6! | Getting off ahead, please!
 Most common mistake: putting how you travel at the end, as English does. 我去中環搭地鐵 → 我搭地鐵去中環.
CARDS NOW: 前面有落！ = Getting off ahead, please! [card] || 我喺中環落車。 = I get off at Central.

## LESSON directions — 26 · Directions: 左邊, 右邊, 直行, 對面 — deck "Around town and directions"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Asking the way in Hong Kong takes a few fixed phrases. The one real trap for English speakers is the order of "across from the bank": Cantonese says it the other way round.
 Asking
 Start with 請問 cing2 man6 (excuse me, may I ask), then the place, 喺 (at) and 邊度 (where), and end with 呀. The question word stays where the answer will go. 請問 is the polite opener for any question to a stranger, and 唔該 thanks them afterwards.
 EX: 請問，地鐵站喺邊度呀？ | cing2 man6, dei6 tit3 zaam6 hai2 bin1 dou6 aa3? | Excuse me, where is the MTR station?
 Or name where you want to go and ask how: 請問，中環點去呀？ cing2 man6, zung1 waan4 dim2 heoi3 aa3? (excuse me, how do I get to Central?).
CARDS NOW: 請問，地鐵站喺邊度呀？ = Excuse me, where is the MTR station?

### SECTION 1   (has 5)
Following directions
 Cantonese ; Meaning ; 
 直行 ; go straight ; 
 轉左 ; turn left ; 
 轉右 ; turn right ; 
 過馬路 ; cross the road ; 
 直行 zik6 haang4 is "go straight", 轉左 zyun3 zo2 and 轉右 zyun3 jau6 are "turn left" and "turn right", and 過馬路 gwo3 maa5 lou6 is "cross the road". 去到 heoi3 dou3 means "when you get to". Directions are just the steps in order, joined by commas, with no word for "then" needed.
 EX: 直行，去到紅綠燈轉右。 | zik6 haang4, heoi3 dou3 hung4 luk6 dang1 zyun3 jau6. | Go straight, and turn right at the traffic lights.
 EX: 過馬路，轉左。 | gwo3 maa5 lou6, zyun3 zo2. | Cross the road and turn left.
 In 直行, 行 is haang4 (walk). It is the same character as in 銀行 (bank), where it is read hong4 .
CARDS NOW: 直行 = go straight [card] || 轉左 = turn left [card] || 過馬路 = cross the road [card] || 直行，去到紅綠燈轉右。 = Go straight, and turn right at the traffic lights. || 過馬路，轉左。 = Cross the road and turn left.

### SECTION 2   (has 9)
Where it is: the landmark first
 English says "across from the bank", "next to the supermarket". Cantonese says the landmark first and the place word after it: 銀行對面 ngan4 hong4 deoi3 min6 , literally "bank across". The same goes for 隔籬 gaak3 lei4 (next to), 前面 cin4 min6 (in front of), 後面 hau6 min6 (behind), 左邊 zo2 bin1 and 右邊 jau6 bin1 . Put 喺 in front. Used alone, a place word points from where you stand: 喺對面 is "across the road".
 EX: 地鐵站喺銀行對面。 | dei6 tit3 zaam6 hai2 ngan4 hong4 deoi3 min6. | The MTR station is across from the bank.
 EX: 酒店喺超市後面。 | zau2 dim3 hai2 ciu1 si5 hau6 min6. | The hotel is behind the supermarket.
 EX: 洗手間喺左邊。 | sai2 sau2 gaan1 hai2 zo2 bin1. | The restroom is on the left.
 When you do something there, the whole 喺 phrase goes before the verb: 我喺銀行對面等你 (I'll wait for you across from the bank).
CARDS NOW: 隔籬 = next to; next door [card] || 前面 = in front; ahead [card] || 後面 = behind; at the back [card] || 左邊 = left side [card] || 右邊 = right side [card] || 喺銀行對面 = across from the bank [card] || 地鐵站喺銀行對面。 = The MTR station is across from the bank. || 酒店喺超市後面。 = The hotel is behind the supermarket. || 洗手間喺左邊。 = The restroom is on the left.

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
How far?
 Ask 遠唔遠呀？ jyun5 m4 jyun5 aa3? (is it far?). The answer is often 好近 (very close) or 好遠 (very far), or it counts minutes on foot with 行 and 分鐘.
 EX: 遠唔遠呀？ | jyun5 m4 jyun5 aa3? | Is it far?
 EX: 行五分鐘就到。 | haang4 ng5 fan1 zung1 zau6 dou3. | It's a five-minute walk.
 Most common mistake: keeping the English order for "across from the bank". 對面銀行 → 銀行對面.
CARDS NOW: 遠唔遠呀？ = Is it far? [card] || 行五分鐘就到。 = It's a five-minute walk. [card]

## LESSON suggest — 27 · Suggestions and invitations: 不如…？, 一齊…吖 — deck "Food and drink"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
In English, "Let's get dim sum!" sounds friendly. In Cantonese, a bare 去飲茶！ sounds like an order. Suggestions and invitations need a soft opening, a particle at the end, or both. This last lesson gives you the language of making plans together.
 How about…? 不如…吖
 不如 bat1 jyu4 at the start is "how about…?" or "why don't we…?". Literally it means "not as good as", as if to say nothing beats the plan that follows. Finish with 吖 aa1 , the particle for offers and invitations, which you met in lesson 15.
 EX: 不如去飲茶吖？ | bat1 jyu4 heoi3 jam2 caa4 aa1? | How about going for dim sum?
 Time and place go where they always go, before the verb, so a whole plan fits in one line: when, where, then what.
 EX: 不如聽日去行山吖？ | bat1 jyu4 ting1 jat6 heoi3 haang4 saan1 aa1? | How about going hiking tomorrow?
 EX: 不如七點喺中環見吖？ | bat1 jyu4 cat1 dim2 hai2 zung1 waan4 gin3 aa1? | How about meeting in Central at seven?
 Listen for the pitch of the particle: 吖 is high and level, while the 呀 of ordinary questions is lower. Say it without an English rise at the end; the particle already does the asking.
CARDS NOW: 飲茶 = to have dim sum (yum cha) [card] || 不如 = how about; why don't we [card] || 不如去飲茶吖？ = Why don't we go for dim sum? [card] || 不如聽日去行山吖？ = How about going hiking tomorrow? || 不如七點喺中環見吖？ = How about meeting in Central at seven?

Together: 一齊
 一齊 jat1 cai4 (together) also goes before the verb. With 吖 at the end it is a warm "let's". It combines with 不如 too: 不如一齊去吖？ (how about we all go?).
 EX: 一齊食飯吖！ | jat1 cai4 sik6 faan6 aa1! | Let's eat together!
CARDS NOW: 一齊 = together [card] || 一齊食飯吖？ = Want to have dinner together? [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Let's: 我哋…啦
 To get a group moving, use 我哋 ngo5 dei6 + verb + 啦 laa1 .
 EX: 我哋走啦。 | ngo5 dei6 zau2 laa1. | Let's go.
CARDS NOW: 我哋 = we; us [card] || 我哋走啦。 = Let's go.

### SECTION 3   (has 3)
Saying yes, or not this time
 好吖！ hou2 aa1! is a happy "sure!", and 好啦 hou2 laa1 is "OK then". To say no politely, give a reason and offer 下次啦 haa6 ci3 laa1 (next time). A flat 唔去 on its own can sound blunt.
 EX: 好吖！ | hou2 aa1! | Sure!
 EX: 今日唔得，下次啦。 | gam1 jat6 m4 dak1, haa6 ci3 laa1. | I can't today. Next time!
CARDS NOW: 好吖！ = Sure! || 好啦。 = OK, sure. [card] || 今日唔得，下次啦。 = I can't today. Next time!

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Is that OK? 得唔得呀？
 得 dak1 means "OK, can do". Ask with 得唔得呀？ to check a time or a plan. The answer is 得 (fine) or 唔得 (no good), often with a new suggestion.
 EX: 七點得唔得呀？ | cat1 dim2 dak1 m4 dak1 aa3? | Is seven OK?
CARDS NOW: 七點得唔得呀？ = Is seven OK?

### SECTION 5   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Don't: 唔好 + verb
 唔好 m4 hou2 before a verb means "don't". It is the friendly way to reassure someone too, and 啦 at the end makes it warmer still: 唔好擔心啦.
 EX: 唔好擔心。 | m4 hou2 daam1 sam1. | Don't worry.
 Most common mistake: inviting with a bare command and no particle, as English "Let's…" allows. 去飲茶！ → 不如去飲茶吖？
CARDS NOW: 唔好 = don't (do it) [card] || 唔好擔心。 = Don't worry.
