# Worksheet: en-zh

## LESSON pinyin — 1 · Pinyin: initials and finals — deck "Pinyin sounds"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
This course teaches Mandarin Chinese, the standard language of mainland China, written in simplified characters with pinyin. Taiwan uses the same spoken language with traditional characters and some different words. Cantonese is a different language.
 One character, one syllable
 English letters spell sounds. A Chinese character stands for a whole syllable and a meaning, so the course shows a pronunciation line in pinyin under every Chinese phrase. Pinyin groups the syllables of one word together: 美国人 Měiguórén (American) is three characters and three syllables, written as one word.
 EX: 美国人 | Měiguórén | American (a person)
CARDS NOW: 美国人 = American (a person)

### SECTION 1   (has 10)
Initials: the consonant at the start
 Each syllable has an initial (the consonant at the start) and a final (the rest). Many initials look English but are not. In Chinese, b , d and g have no puff of air, and p , t and k have a strong one. Hold your hand in front of your mouth to feel it.
 EX: 八 | bā | eight
 EX: 怕 | pà | to be afraid
 Pinyin ; Sounds like ; Example ; 
 q ; the ch in cheese ; 七 ; 
 x ; sh, said with a smile ; 谢 ; 
 j ; the j in jeep , said with a smile ; 九 ; 
 zh, ch, sh ; j, ch, sh with the tongue curled back ; 中, 吃, 是 ; 
 r ; close to the s in measure ; 日 ; 
 z, c ; the ds in kids ; the ts in cats ; 字, 菜 ; 
 EX: 七 | qī | seven
 EX: 菜 | cài | a dish; vegetables
CARDS NOW: 八 = eight [card] || 怕 = to be afraid [card] || 七 = seven [card] || 谢 = to thank [card] || 中 = middle [card] || 吃 = to eat [card] || 是 = to be (am, is, are) [card] || 日 = sun; day [card] || 字 = written character [card] || 菜 = dish (of food); vegetables [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
Finals that trick English readers
 e sounds like "uh": 喝 hē (to drink).
 ü is "ee" with rounded lips. After j, q, x and y it is written without the dots: 去 qù (to go) has a ü sound.
 After z, c, s, zh, ch, sh and r, i is not "ee": the vowel just buzzes, as in 四 sì (four).
 ian sounds like "yen", ui like "way", iu like "yo".
 EX: 绿 | lǜ | green
 EX: 去 | qù | to go
 EX: 六 | liù | six
CARDS NOW: 喝 = to drink [card] || 去 = to go [card] || 绿 = green [card] || 六 = six [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
Tone marks
 The little marks over the vowels are tones: ā high and level, á rising, ǎ low, à falling. No mark means the neutral tone, short and light. The next lesson is all about them. When you type pinyin, press a tone key on the accent bar right after the syllable: the mark lands on the right vowel.
CARDS NOW: (none)

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
The apostrophe
 An apostrophe shows where a new syllable starts with a, o or e: 西安 Xī'ān is two syllables, while xiān would be one.
 EX: 西安 | Xī'ān | Xi'an (a city)
 The pinyin line is a crutch: listen to the audio first, and hide the line once the characters feel familiar.
 Most common mistake: reading q and x the English way. 去 read "kwoo" → 去 qù , like "chü"; 谢 read "ksyeh" → 谢 xiè , like "shyeh".
CARDS NOW: 西安 = Xi'an (a city)

## LESSON tones — 2 · Four tones and the neutral tone — deck "Tone pairs"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
English uses pitch for feeling: your voice rises on a question and falls when you are sure. Chinese uses pitch to make words. The same syllable with a different tone is a different word, so the tone is as much a part of the word as its letters.
 Four tones
 Tone ; Mark ; How it sounds ; Example ; 
 first tone ; ā ; high and level, like singing one note ; 妈 ; 
 second tone ; á ; rises, like a surprised "what?" ; 麻 ; 
 third tone ; ǎ ; low; said alone it dips and comes back up ; 马 ; 
 fourth tone ; à ; falls sharply, like a firm "No!" ; 骂 ; 
 EX: 妈 | mā | mom
 EX: 麻 | má | numb; hemp
 EX: 马 | mǎ | horse
 EX: 骂 | mà | to scold
 Four words, one syllable. Say them slowly, then faster. Make each tone go all the way: the first tone stays high, and the fourth tone drops right to the bottom of your voice.
CARDS NOW: 妈 = mom (as in 妈妈) [card] || 麻 = numb; tingly [card] || 马 = horse [card] || 骂 = to scold [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
Words that change with the tone
 Mixing up tones changes what you say. 买 mǎi is to buy, and 卖 mài is to sell. 汤 tāng is soup, and 糖 táng is sugar.
 EX: 买 | mǎi | to buy
 EX: 卖 | mài | to sell
 EX: 汤 | tāng | soup
 EX: 糖 | táng | sugar
CARDS NOW: 买 = to buy [card] || 卖 = to sell [card] || 汤 = soup [card] || 糖 = sugar; candy [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
The neutral tone
 Some syllables have no tone mark. They are short and light, and lean on the syllable before them. In 妈妈 māma (mom), the second syllable is neutral.
 EX: 妈妈 | māma | mom
CARDS NOW: 妈妈 = mom [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Tone pairs
 Most words have two syllables, so practice tones in pairs. 很快 hěn kuài (very fast) is a third tone and then a fourth tone: stay low on the first syllable, then fall from high.
 EX: 很快 | hěn kuài | very fast
CARDS NOW: 很快 = very fast

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
Questions keep their tones
 English speakers often let their voice rise at the end of every question. In Chinese, that rise would turn a fourth tone into a second tone, and change the word. Keep each tone as it is: Chinese asks questions with words, which you will meet soon.
 Most common mistake: saying every syllable flat, or the fourth tone only half way down. 卖 said low, like mǎi → 卖 mài , falling all the way. Said low, you are buying instead of selling.
CARDS NOW: (none)

## LESSON tone-change — 3 · Tone changes: 不, 一, third tone — deck "Tone pairs"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
A few very common syllables change their tone depending on the syllable after them. This is called tone change (linguists say "tone sandhi"). English has something similar: the t in water changes in American speech, and nobody spells it differently. There are three rules to learn.
 不 (not): bù or bú
 不 means "not". On its own it has the fourth tone, bù . Before another fourth tone it rises instead: 不是 bú shì (is not). Before every other tone it stays bù . In this course the pinyin line shows the tone you actually say.
 EX: 不是 | bú shì | is not; no
 EX: 不客气 | bú kèqi | you're welcome
 EX: 不好 | bù hǎo | not good
CARDS NOW: 不是 = no (it isn't) [card] || 不客气。 = You're welcome. [card] || 不好 = not good

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
一 (one): yī, yí or yì
 一 keeps its first tone when you count, yī, èr, sān , at the end of a bigger number ( 十一 shíyī , eleven), in dates, and in an order, as in 第一 dìyī (first). Before a word, it changes. Before a fourth tone it rises: 一个人 yí ge rén (one person). Before the other tones it falls: 一天 yì tiān (one day).
 EX: 一个人 | yí ge rén | one person
 EX: 一天 | yì tiān | one day
 EX: 第一 | dìyī | first
 Look at 一个人 again. English says "one person"; Chinese puts a little word, 个 , between the number and the noun. It is called a measure word, and you will learn more of them later. 个 itself is written with no tone mark, but it counts as a fourth tone, so 一 before it is yí .
CARDS NOW: 十一 = eleven [card] || 第一 = first [card] || 一个人 = one person; a person [card] || 一天 = one day

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Two third tones in a row
 When a third tone comes right before another third tone, the first one is said like a second tone. 你好 (hello) is said "ní hǎo". The pinyin line still writes nǐ hǎo , the tones each word has in the dictionary, so you always see the real tone of 你 .
 EX: 你好 | nǐ hǎo | hello
 EX: 很好 | hěn hǎo | very good
CARDS NOW: 你好！ = Hello! [card] || 很好 = very good

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
The half third tone
 Before a first, second or fourth tone, a third tone stays low and does not come back up: 很快 hěn kuài . The full dip is heard mostly when a third tone is said alone or at the end of a phrase.
 Most common mistake: forgetting the change in 不 before a fourth tone. 不是 said bù shì → 不是 bú shì . The opposite mistake is changing too much: a third tone changes only before another third tone, not before every tone.
CARDS NOW: (none)

## LESSON characters — 4 · How characters work: strokes and radicals — deck "First characters"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
English has 26 letters that spell sounds. Chinese has no alphabet: each character is one syllable with a meaning. That sounds like a lot to learn, but characters are built from a small set of parts, and many parts come back again and again. This course aims at recognizing about 300 common characters by the end. You will read them, not write them by hand.
 Pictures
 The oldest characters began as drawings. 人 rén is a person walking, 口 kǒu an open mouth, 木 mù a tree with roots, 山 shān three peaks, 日 rì the sun and 月 yuè the moon.
 EX: 人 | rén | person
 EX: 木 | mù | tree; wood
 EX: 山 | shān | mountain
CARDS NOW: 人 = person [card] || 口 = mouth [card] || 木 = tree; wood [card] || 山 = mountain [card] || 月 = moon; month [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
Two parts, one meaning
 Many characters join two parts whose meanings add up. Two trees make 林 lín (woods). A person leaning on a tree, 休 xiū , is to rest. The sun and the moon make 明 míng , bright. A woman with her child makes 好 hǎo , good.
 EX: 林 | lín | woods
 EX: 明 | míng | bright
 EX: 好 | hǎo | good
CARDS NOW: 林 = woods [card] || 休 = to rest [card] || 明 = bright [card] || 好 = good [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
Radicals: hints, not spellings
 A radical is the part of a character that hints at its meaning. Three dots on the left (氵) mean water, as in 汤 tāng (soup). A small 口 on the left often marks a mouth word or a sound. 亻, a narrow form of 人, means a person, as in 休. 女 means a woman.
 Another part often hints at the sound. 妈 mā (mom) is 女 (woman) for the meaning and 马 mǎ (horse) for the sound. The hint is rough: you cannot sound out a character letter by letter, so learn each one with its pinyin.
 EX: 汤 | tāng | soup
 EX: 妈 | mā | mom
CARDS NOW: 汤 = soup [card] || 女 = woman; female [card] || 妈 = mom (as in 妈妈) [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
Strokes
 Characters are made of strokes: lines, dots and hooks. Knowing the basic strokes helps you see the parts and look characters up. This course does not drill stroke order.
CARDS NOW: (none)

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Simplified and traditional
 Mainland China uses simplified characters, which this course teaches. Taiwan and Hong Kong use traditional ones, with more strokes: 门 is written 門 there.
 Most common mistake: trying to sound out a character from its parts. 妈 read as "woman-horse", or as mǎ → 妈 mā . The parts are hints for memory; the pinyin tells you the sound.
CARDS NOW: 门 = door; gate [card]

DECK CARDS IN NO SECTION (add by front, as a string, only where a section teaches them): 森 | sēn | forest || 子 | zǐ | child || 上 | shàng | up; on top || 下 | xià | down; below || 雨 | yǔ | rain || 火 | huǒ | fire || 水 | shuǐ | water

## LESSON greetings — 5 · Hello, thank you, sorry: Chinese courtesy — deck "Greetings and courtesy"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Hello
 你好 nǐ hǎo is "hello", literally "you good". It is safe with anyone your age, at any hour.
 EX: 你好！ | Nǐ hǎo! | Hello!
CARDS NOW: 你好！ = Hello! [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
The polite you: 您
 English has one "you". Chinese has 你 nǐ for friends, classmates and people your age, and 您 nín to show respect: to older people, to a teacher, and from staff to a customer. Using 您 with a friend sounds stiff, never rude.
 EX: 您好！ | Nín hǎo! | Hello! (polite)
 EX: 王老师，您好！ | Wáng lǎoshī, nín hǎo! | Hello, Teacher Wang!
CARDS NOW: 您好！ = Hello! (polite) [card] || 王老师，您好！ = Hello, Teacher Wang!

### SECTION 2   (has 6)
Thank you and sorry
 You say ; They answer ; 
 谢谢！ (thank you) ; 不客气。 (you're welcome) ; 
 对不起。 (sorry) ; 没关系。 (that's OK) ; 
 EX: 谢谢！ | Xièxie! | Thank you!
 EX: 不客气。 | Bú kèqi. | You're welcome.
 EX: 对不起。 | Duìbuqǐ. | I'm sorry.
 EX: 没关系。 | Méi guānxi. | That's OK.
 对不起 is a real apology, for a mistake you made. For a small inconvenience, like squeezing past someone or asking a favor, the lighter 不好意思 bù hǎoyìsi is more natural. To start a question to a stranger, say 请问 qǐngwèn , "may I ask". Among family and close friends, Chinese says thank you less often than English does.
CARDS NOW: 谢谢！ = Thank you! [card] || 不客气。 = You're welcome. [card] || 对不起！ = I'm sorry! [card] || 没关系。 = That's OK. / No problem. [card] || 不好意思。 = Excuse me. / Sorry about that. [card] || 请问 = Excuse me, may I ask… [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 4)
Names
 To give your name, say 我叫 wǒ jiào and your name. Chinese names put the family name first: in 王明 , Wang is the surname. To ask a stranger's surname politely, say 您贵姓？ , literally "your honorable surname?". Answer with 我姓 and your surname. 贵 is only for the other person. Friends often call each other by the full name, like 王明 ; in Chinese that sounds friendly, not cold.
 EX: 请问，您贵姓？ | Qǐngwèn, nín guìxìng? | Excuse me, what's your last name?
 EX: 我姓王。 | Wǒ xìng Wáng. | My last name is Wang.
 EX: 我叫王明。 | Wǒ jiào Wáng Míng. | My name is Wang Ming.
CARDS NOW: 您贵姓？ = What's your surname? (polite) [card] || 请问，您贵姓？ = Excuse me, what's your last name? || 我姓王。 = My last name is Wang. [card] || 我叫王明。 = My name is Wang Ming.

### SECTION 4   (has 5)
What to call people
 Titles come after the surname: 王老师 (Teacher Wang), 王先生 Wáng xiānsheng (Mr. Wang), 李女士 Lǐ nǚshì (Ms. Li). Call your teacher 老师 , a driver 师傅 shīfu , and a server in a restaurant 服务员 fúwùyuán .
 EX: 服务员！ | Fúwùyuán! | Excuse me! (calling a server)
 To leave, say 再见 zàijiàn , "see you again". One more trap: 你好吗？ Nǐ hǎo ma? is a real question about how someone is doing. For a casual "hi", just say 你好 .
 EX: 再见！ | Zàijiàn! | Goodbye!
 Most common mistake: calling a server 小姐 xiǎojiě (miss). In mainland China it now suggests a sex worker. 小姐！ → 服务员！
CARDS NOW: 老师 = teacher [card] || 服务员！ = Excuse me! (calling a server) [card] || 再见！ = Goodbye! [card] || 你好吗？ = How are you? (a real question) [card] || 小姐 = Miss (avoid as an address) [card]

## LESSON pronouns — 6 · Pronouns and 们 — deck "People and family"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
I, you, he, she
 Chinese pronouns never change. English says I and me, he and him; Chinese says 我 wǒ for both, and 他 for both he and him.
 English ; Chinese ; Pinyin ; 
 I, me ; 我 ; wǒ ; 
 you ; 你 / 您 (polite) ; nǐ / nín ; 
 he, him ; 他 ; tā ; 
 she, her ; 她 ; tā ; 
 it ; 它 ; tā ; 
 他 , 她 and 它 all sound the same: tā . Only the written character shows the difference, so in speech the context tells you who is meant. 它 is for animals and things, and you will mostly read it: Chinese often leaves "it" out.
 Pronouns are often left out when the meaning is clear. Asked whether you are busy, you can simply answer 很忙 hěn máng , with no 我, where English must say "I am".
 EX: 他叫王明。 | Tā jiào Wáng Míng. | His name is Wang Ming.
 EX: 她姓李。 | Tā xìng Lǐ. | Her last name is Li.
CARDS NOW: 他叫王明。 = His name is Wang Ming. || 她姓李。 = Her last name is Li.

### SECTION 1   (has 7)
们 makes people plural
 Add 们 men to a pronoun and it means more than one: 我们 wǒmen (we, us), 你们 nǐmen (you all), 他们 tāmen (they, them). For a group of women only, write 她们 tāmen . The polite 您 has no everyday plural: to a group, say 你们 or 大家 (everyone).
 EX: 你们好！ | Nǐmen hǎo! | Hello, everyone! (to a group)
 EX: 大家好！ | Dàjiā hǎo! | Hello, everybody!
CARDS NOW: 我们 = we; us [card] || 你们 = you (more than one) [card] || 他们 = they; them [card] || 她们 = they (all women) [card] || 大家 = everyone [card] || 你们好！ = Hello, everyone! (to a group) || 大家好！ = Hello, everyone! [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
Nouns do not need a plural
 English adds -s to nouns. Chinese nouns stay the same: 学生 xuésheng is "student" or "students". 们 can go on some nouns for people ( 学生们 , the students), but never after a number. A number takes a measure word instead, like the 个 you met in 一个人 .
 EX: 五个学生 | wǔ ge xuésheng | five students
CARDS NOW: 学生 = student [card] || 一个人 = one person; a person [card] || 五个学生 = five students

### SECTION 3   (has 4)
My friend, their teacher
 English says "my friend". Chinese puts the owner before the noun too, linked by 的 de , which works like English 's: 我的朋友 wǒ de péngyou . With close people, like friends and family, you can drop it: 我朋友 . Lesson 12 covers 的 fully.
 EX: 我的朋友 | wǒ de péngyou | my friend
 EX: 我朋友叫安娜。 | Wǒ péngyou jiào Ānnà. | My friend's name is Anna.
 EX: 我们的老师姓王。 | Wǒmen de lǎoshī xìng Wáng. | Our teacher's last name is Wang.
 Most common mistake: adding 们 after a number, as English adds -s. 五个学生们 → 五个学生 . The number already says there are several.
CARDS NOW: 朋友 = friend [card] || 我的朋友 = my friend || 我朋友叫安娜。 = My friend's name is Anna. || 我们的老师姓王。 = Our teacher's last name is Wang.

## LESSON shi — 7 · 是: A is B — deck "About me"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
One verb for am, is and are
 English changes "be" for every person: I am, she is, they are. Chinese has one word, 是 shì , and it never changes. It joins two nouns: A 是 B.
 EX: 我是美国人。 | Wǒ shì Měiguórén. | I'm American.
 EX: 她是老师。 | Tā shì lǎoshī. | She's a teacher.
 EX: 我是学生。 | Wǒ shì xuésheng. | I'm a student.
 There is no word for "a" or "the" either: "a teacher" is just 老师 . With a name, 我是王明 wǒ shì Wáng Míng means "I'm Wang Ming": people say it when they introduce themselves or answer the phone, while 我叫王明 simply tells your name.
CARDS NOW: 我是美国人。 = I'm American. [card] || 她是老师。 = She's a teacher. || 我是学生。 = I'm a student. || 老师 = teacher [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Not: 不是
 Put 不 before 是 to say "is not". Remember the tone change from lesson 3: before a fourth tone, 不 rises, so it is bú shì .
 EX: 他不是中国人。 | Tā bú shì Zhōngguórén. | He isn't Chinese.
CARDS NOW: 他不是中国人。 = He isn't Chinese.

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
Countries, people and languages
 Add 人 (person) to a country to name its people: 美国 (America) gives 美国人 Měiguórén , 中国 (China) gives 中国人 Zhōngguórén . The language is a different word: 中文 Zhōngwén is Chinese, 英语 Yīngyǔ is English.
 EX: 王明是中国人。 | Wáng Míng shì Zhōngguórén. | Wang Ming is Chinese.
CARDS NOW: 中国 = China [card] || 中文 = Chinese (the language) [card] || 英语 = English (the language) [card] || 王明是中国人。 = Wang Ming is Chinese.

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
The describer comes first
 As in English, a word that describes a noun goes before it: 美国学生 is an American student, 中文老师 a Chinese teacher. Chinese keeps this order even where English does not, so learn it now.
 EX: 他是美国学生。 | Tā shì Měiguó xuésheng. | He's an American student.
 EX: 李老师是中文老师。 | Lǐ lǎoshī shì Zhōngwén lǎoshī. | Teacher Li is a Chinese teacher.
CARDS NOW: 他是美国学生。 = He's an American student. || 李老师是中文老师。 = Teacher Li is a Chinese teacher.

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Also: 也

## LESSON adj — 8 · Adjectives with 很 (no 是) — deck "Describing things"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
Adjectives work like verbs
 English needs "be" before an adjective: she is tall. In Chinese, an adjective is already a full predicate, like a verb, so there is no 是. Instead, a plain statement usually takes 很 hěn .
 EX: 她很高。 | Tā hěn gāo. | She's tall.

 EX: 我不忙。 | Wǒ bù máng. | I'm not busy.

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)

 Here are adjectives you can use right away: 忙 busy, 累 tired, 高 tall, 贵 expensive, 好 good, 大 dà big, 小 xiǎo small, 冷 lěng cold, 热 rè hot. Each one works the same way: subject, then 很, 不 or 太, then the adjective.

## LESSON ma — 9 · Questions with 吗 and 呢 — deck "Questions and connectors"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
A statement plus 吗
 English turns words around to ask: "You are a teacher" becomes "Are you a teacher?". Chinese keeps the statement exactly as it is and adds 吗 ma at the end. That is all.
 EX: 你是老师吗？ | Nǐ shì lǎoshī ma? | Are you a teacher?
 EX: 你忙吗？ | Nǐ máng ma? | Are you busy?
 EX: 你也是学生吗？ | Nǐ yě shì xuésheng ma? | Are you a student too?
 Everything else stays in place: 也 still goes before the verb, and an adjective still needs no 是. This works with any statement you already know. 你好吗？ Nǐ hǎo ma? is built the same way, which is why it is a real question about how someone is doing, not a casual "hi". Adding 吗 never changes the order of the words before it. In writing, the question ends with the full-width question mark ？. One limit: 吗 is only for yes/no questions. A question with a word like "what" or "where" never takes it, as the next lesson shows.
CARDS NOW: 你是老师吗？ = Are you a teacher? || 你忙吗？ = Are you busy? [card] || 你也是学生吗？ = Are you a student too? || 你好吗？ = How are you? (a real question) [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
Let 吗 do the asking
 English speakers raise their voice at the end of a question. In Chinese the tones must stay as they are, so a rising voice can change a word. 吗 already makes it a question; it has the neutral tone, short and light.

### SECTION 2   (has 5)

 The safe answer repeats the verb or adjective of the question. To 你忙吗？ , say 很忙 hěn máng or 不忙 , not 是.

## LESSON bu-mei — 11 · Negation: 不 and 没 (not, didn't) — deck "Traps for English speakers"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
English has one word for "not" and builds the rest with helpers: don't, doesn't, didn't, won't, haven't. Chinese has no helpers. It has two words for "not", and you choose by meaning: 不 bù and 没 méi . Both go right before the verb or adjective, and the verb itself never changes.
 不: not now, not usually, not going to
 Use 不 for habits, likes, states and plans: what you don't do, aren't, or won't do. English needs don't, isn't or won't; Chinese uses 不 for all three.
 EX: 我不喝酒。 | Wǒ bù hē jiǔ. | I don't drink (alcohol).

## LESSON de — 12 · 的: possession and describing — deck "Describing things"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
English can put the owner or the description before the noun ("my teacher's book", "a red car") or after it ("the book of my teacher", "a car that is red"). Chinese has only one place: everything that describes a noun goes before it. The little word that joins them is 的 de .
 Owner + 的 + thing
 的 works like English 's. The owner comes first, then 的 , then the thing. A pronoun works the same way: 我的 wǒ de is "my", 你的 nǐ de is "your".
 EX: 这是我的书。 | Zhè shì wǒ de shū. | This is my book.
 EX: 老师的手机很贵。 | Lǎoshī de shǒujī hěn guì. | The teacher's phone is expensive.
 You can stack owners, biggest first, just as in English: 我老师的书 wǒ lǎoshī de shū is "my teacher's book". Never "the book of my teacher": there is no "of" word to put after the noun.
CARDS NOW: 我的 = my; mine [card] || 你的 = your; yours [card] || 这是我的书。 = This is my book. || 老师的手机很贵。 = The teacher's phone is expensive.

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
Describer + 的 + thing
 The same 的 joins a description to a noun. In English, "a car that is very expensive" puts the description after the noun; in Chinese it must move in front.
 EX: 我喜欢红色的车。 | Wǒ xǐhuan hóngsè de chē. | I like red cars.
 EX: 这是很贵的衣服。 | Zhè shì hěn guì de yīfu. | These are very expensive clothes.
 Color words ending in 色 usually take 的 too: 白色的狗 báisè de gǒu (a white dog). You can also drop the 色 : 白狗 bái gǒu .
 When an adjective has 很 in front of it, 的 is needed: 很贵的衣服 , never 很贵衣服 . A short one-syllable adjective often joins the noun directly: 好朋友 hǎo péngyou (a good friend).
 EX: 他是我的好朋友。 | Tā shì wǒ de hǎo péngyou. | He's my good friend.

## LESSON word-order — 20 · Word order: who · when · where · verb — deck "Time, days and dates"
### SECTION 0   (has 6)
English builds a sentence like a train: who, the verb, what, and then the details trail behind: "I'm eating at home tomorrow ." Chinese does the opposite. The details come first, and the action waits until the end. Once you know the line-up, you can build long sentences without guessing.
 The line-up
 Who ; When ; Where ; Verb + what ;

## LESSON le — 22 · 了: done, and changed (not a past tense) — deck "Travel and transport"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
English marks time on the verb: go, went, gone. Chinese verbs never change. 我去北京 can be yesterday, today or tomorrow, and a time word tells you which. So 了 le is not a past tense. It does two other jobs.
 Job 1: the action is done
 了 right after a verb says the action happened and is complete. It sounds most natural when the object has a number.
 EX: 我买了两张票。 | Wǒ mǎi le liǎng zhāng piào. | I bought two tickets.
 EX: 他喝了三杯咖啡。 | Tā hē le sān bēi kāfēi. | He drank three cups of coffee.
 When the object has no number, put 了 at the end of the sentence instead. Verb-object words such as 吃饭 chīfàn (sometimes called separable verbs) do the same: 我吃饭了 , "I've eaten".
 EX: 我昨天去上海了。 | Wǒ zuótiān qù Shànghǎi le. | I went to Shanghai yesterday.

## LESSON progress — 23 · 在 and 正在: doing it right now — deck "Work and study"

English shows an action in progress with "am / is / are" and -ing: "He is watching TV." Chinese has no -ing, and it does not use 是 for "is" here. It puts a small word before the verb instead.
 在 + verb

## LESSON serial — 24 · Two verbs in a row: 去 + verb — deck "Around town and directions"

"Or" in a statement: 或者
 When you are not asking, but saying that either one is fine or possible, use 或者 huòzhě .
 EX: 我星期六或者星期天去。 | Wǒ xīngqīliù huòzhě xīngqītiān qù. | I'll go on Saturday or Sunday.
 或者 is also how you give someone options without asking them to choose right now.
 EX: 你可以坐地铁或者打车。 | Nǐ kěyǐ zuò dìtiě huòzhě dǎchē. | You can take the subway or a taxi.

 tea and coffee ; 茶和咖啡 ; 
 Tea or coffee? (asking) ; 茶还是咖啡？ ;

 Most common mistake: using 和 between two sentences. 我很累和想睡觉 → 我很累，想睡觉 . A second one: 或者 when you want the other person to choose. 你要茶或者咖啡？ is heard in casual speech, but the standard way is → 你要茶还是咖啡？
