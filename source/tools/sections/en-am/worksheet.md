# Worksheet: en-am

## LESSON orders — 1 · How the fidel works — deck "How the fidel works"
### SECTION 0   (has 10)
Amharic is written in the fidel, left to right. This course teaches Addis Ababa Amharic, the standard of the city. A fidel is an abugida: one character is a consonant plus a vowel, not an English letter on its own. There are no capital letters.
 One consonant, seven orders
 Each consonant has seven orders. The consonant stays and the vowel changes. Here is b. The English words are a rough guide for your ear, not a spelling to write.
 Order ; Shape ; Listen for ; Written ; 
 first ; በ ; u in but, short ; e ; 
 second ; ቡ ; oo in food, short ; u ; 
 third ; ቢ ; ee in see, short ; i ; 
 fourth ; ባ ; a in father ; a ; 
 fifth ; ቤ ; e in bed ; é ; 
 sixth ; ብ ; a vowel, or none ; i, or nothing ; 
 seventh ; ቦ ; o in go, no final w ; o ; 
 Keep each vowel short. The o does not slide into a w. A few letters, named later, use the father vowel in the first order too. በ does not.
 EX: በ | be | b with the vowel of but
 EX: ባ | ba | b with the vowel of father
 EX: ቤት | bét | house
 EX: ቡና | bunna | coffee
 EX: ቡና ቤት | bunna bét | a coffee house

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
The sixth order
 The sixth order may be a short vowel, and that vowel is not the ee of see, or it may be no vowel. Word endings often drop it. The spelling will not tell you which reading a word uses. Listen, and learn the word.
 EX: በር | ber | door
CARDS NOW: በር = door [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
A long consonant is not written
 The fidel writes a consonant once, long or short. The length can change the meaning. ገና with a short n is still. ገና with a long n is Christmas. Same letters. You will not add a dot. Listen.
 EX: ገና | gena | still. With a long n, genna, the same letters are Christmas
CARDS NOW: ገና = still; yet [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 5)
The English letters under each word
 Every card and example also shows the word in English letters, so you can read it before you know the fidel. It is a reading aid, not a second spelling. The Written column above gives the vowels. The first order is written e, but say it short, between the e of bed and the u of but. The fifth order is written é, like the e of café. The letter i stands for two sounds: the third order, the ee of see said short, and the sixth order, a quick weak vowel like the i of bit. When the sixth order is silent, nothing is written.
 A doubled letter is a long consonant: hold it, as in the middle of book case. ቡና is bunna, with a long n. The fidel does not show this, but the English letters do. An apostrophe after a consonant marks an ejective, a popped sound: t', k', p', ch', ts'. ቀ is k'e and ከ is ke. An apostrophe after a vowel is a short catch in the throat, as in ሰዓት , se'at.
 The other letters sound the way they do in English: sh, ch, j as in jam, y as in yes, ny as in canyon, zh as the s in measure. Some words look like English words. They are not read that way: ነው new is ne plus w, ሰው sew is se plus w, ዋልክ walk is wa plus l plus k, and ግን gin has a hard g, as in get. The English letters come from a checked word list, so a word you meet later may surprise you. Trust the audio when it differs.
 EX: ጠዋት | t'ewat | morning, with the ejective t'
CARDS NOW: ሰዓት = hour; o'clock; watch [card] || ነው = is (copula, he / it form) [card] || ሰው = a person [card] || ግን = but [card] || ጠዋት = morning [card]

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
What the rows will add
 Some consonants are ejectives: a catch in the throat, and a different consonant from the plain one. In Addis Ababa, different letters can also share one sound. Keep each word's own spelling. The row lessons take both of these up.
CARDS NOW: (none)

### SECTION 5   (has 3)
A verb ending in every practice
 English has one you and one are. Amharic changes the verb ending for the person and the gender. ደህና means fine. ደህና ነህ is you are fine, to a man. ደህና ነሽ is you are fine, to a woman. Greetings will say who gets which form, including a stranger. For now the practice names the person in English.
 EX: ደህና ነህ | dehna neh | you are fine (to a man)
 EX: ደህና ነሽ | dehna nesh | you are fine (to a woman)
 Most common mistake: reading በ as the English letter name bee. በ read as bee → በ as b plus the vowel of but. A long consonant is still written once in fidel, and doubled in the English letters.
CARDS NOW: ደህና = fine; well; safe [card] || ደህና ነህ = you are fine (to a man) || ደህና ነሽ = you are fine (to a woman)

## LESSON fidel-1 — 2 · Fidel rows: ሀ to ቸ — deck "Fidel rows: ሀ to ቸ"
### SECTION 0   (has 13)
Each row below is a consonant. The seven orders work the way they did for በ . The shape in the list is the first order. For most of these letters, that order has the vowel of but. Learn the row, then trust the audio for the words.

 ሀ ; h, as in house. Same sound as ሐ ; 
 ለ ; l ;

 ሀ and ሐ, ሠ and ሰ
 In Addis Ababa, ሀ and ሐ are one h. ሠ and ሰ are one s. They are not two sounds that you choose by ear. The spelling is part of the word, so you learn which letter a word uses. Do not swap them to taste.
 ሀ and ሐ are also the vowel exception. Their first order uses the vowel of father, the same vowel as their fourth order. So ሀ and ሃ are the same vowel. A few words break even that pattern. You learn those as whole words.

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
ቀ is an ejective
 ቀ is an ejective, a popped consonant, with a catch in the throat. It is not a plain k. The plain k letter is ከ , and it comes in the next lesson. If you say an ordinary k, you have said a different consonant. Do not call ቀ a k. Listen for that catch. The English letters write it k'e: the apostrophe is the pop.
 EX: ቀ | k'e | an ejective, not a plain k
 The man form and the woman form stay in the practice. You can now see the last letter of each ending. ህ is the sixth order of ሀ . ሽ is the sixth order of ሸ . The ነ row comes next. Until then, treat ነህ and ነሽ as whole shapes: man form, woman form.
 EX: ደህና ነህ | dehna neh | you are fine (to a man)
 EX: ደህና ነሽ | dehna nesh | you are fine (to a woman)
 Most common mistake: calling ቀ a plain k. ቀ said as an ordinary k → ቀ as its own ejective. The plain k letter is still ahead of you.
CARDS NOW: ደህና ነህ = you are fine (to a man) || ደህና ነሽ = you are fine (to a woman)

 Four letters, one h
 You now have ሀ , ሐ , ኀ and ኸ . In Addis Ababa they are one h. English speakers often invent four h sounds because they see four letters. There are not four. There is one sound, and four spellings. The word remembers which letter it uses. You do too.
 ኀ and ኸ , like ሀ and ሐ , use the father vowel in the first order. አ and ዐ do the same. A few words keep a different vowel on these letters. ኧረ is one of them. Learn that kind of word whole. Do not build a private rule for it.

The vowel carriers
 አ and ዐ do not start with a consonant. They write a vowel by itself. In Addis Ababa they do the same job. A word that begins with a vowel uses one of them, and the dictionary spelling is the one you copy. You can read a name now: አበበች . Hello to her is two words.

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
Plain k, and n
 ከ is a plain k. ቀ , from the last lesson, is the ejective. They are different consonants. Saying ከ when the word is spelled with ቀ gives a different word. ነ is n. You can now read the first letter of the endings ነህ and ነሽ .
 EX: ከ | ke | plain k, not the ejective ቀ

 Most common mistake: four h sounds for four letters. ሀ , ሐ , ኀ and ኸ as four h sounds → one h in Addis Ababa, with the spelling still kept apart.

## LESSON reading — 5 · Letters that sound the same, and reading words — deck "Reading words"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
You have the rows. Reading is the part the rows cannot do alone: letters that share a sound, a shape with a w built in, and a long consonant that never appears on the page.
 Same sound, different letter
 In Addis Ababa these sets are each one sound. You still spell the word the way it is spelled. Swapping a homophone letter is a wrong spelling, not a shortcut.
 Letters ; One sound ; 
 ሀ ሐ ኀ ኸ ; h ;

 Beside the seven orders, some consonants have a -wa form: a w sound built into the shape. Learn these four first. ቧ is on b. ሏ is on l. ኳ is on plain k, the ከ row, not on the ejective ቀ . ጓ is on g. Other consonants have one too. Learn the shape with the word.

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
One spelling, two lengths
 Do not give a spelling one pronunciation and keep it forever. ገና with a short n is still. ገና with a long n is Christmas. አለ with a short l is he said. አለ with a long l is there is. Same letters each time. Context and the audio tell you which one you heard. You cannot mark the long consonant in writing, and you should not try.
 EX: ገና | gena | still. With a long n, genna, the same letters are Christmas
 EX: አለ | alle | there is. With a short l, ale, the same letters are he said
CARDS NOW: ገና = still; yet [card] || አለ = there is; exists [card]

## LESSON greetings — 6 · Greetings: አንተ, አንቺ and እርስዎ — deck "Greetings and courtesy"
### SECTION 0   (has 6)
English has one you. Amharic does not. From this lesson on, a friend or a peer who is a man is አንተ . A friend or a peer who is a woman is አንቺ . A stranger, a shopkeeper, or an older person is እርስዎ . When you are not sure, use እርስዎ .
 Any hour
 ሰላም is hello. It works with anyone, at any hour. It is also the word for peace. ጤና ይስጥልኝ is the formal hello for any person and any time of day. Use it with a stranger or an older person, and any time you want to be formal. You can answer with the same phrase. ጤና is health, which you can already read.

## LESSON definite — 9 · The and plurals — deck "Classroom and home"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
English puts the before a noun, and a or an when the noun is new. Amharic has no separate word for a. ቤት is house, or a house. What English does with the, Amharic does with a definite ending on the noun. A specific noun, one you and the listener can pick out, needs that ending. Leaving it off is the common miss.
 The house

Do not add an English s. ቤትስ is not a word. A specific group is ቤቶቹ , the houses. The plural definite ending does not switch for a group of women. It stays this u ending.

## LESSON this-that — 10 · This, that, and the classroom — deck "Classroom and home"
### SECTION 0   (has 10)
English has one word, this, for a book and for a woman. Amharic looks at the noun. Near you, a masculine noun takes ይህ and a feminine noun takes ይህች . Farther away, a masculine noun takes ያ and a feminine noun takes ያች . One English word cannot cover those four.
 The noun, then "to be"

## LESSON have — 14 · Having: አለኝ — deck "Classroom and home"
### SECTION 0   (has 20)
English puts have in the middle: I have a book. Amharic does not. It says the thing, then a form of አለ that means "there is" pointed at a person. መጽሐፍ አለኝ is a book exists-for-me: I have a book. The thing comes first. The verb comes last.

## LESSON want — 19 · The infinitive and wanting — deck "Likes and free time"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
English puts "want" in the middle: "I want coffee", "I want to go". Amharic puts the thing first and the verb last. Coffee I-want is the shape, not I-want coffee. The sentences in this lesson leave off እኔ, አንተ, and the other subject pronouns. The ending already shows who wants it, so type the short form.
 EX: ቡና እፈልጋለሁ | bunna ifelligallehu | I want coffee.

 The to-form, then want

## LESSON prices — 22 · Prices at the market — deck "Numbers, time and prices"
### SECTION 0   (has 11)
At a shop in Addis Ababa you ask the price with ዋጋው ስንት ነው . How much is it? English can say "how much are they" about a price. Amharic keeps one verb, ነው . The price is not a person, so it is not ነህ or ነሽ , and it is not the plural ናቸው .
 EX: ዋጋው ስንት ነው | wagaw sint new | How much is the price?
 EX: አራት ብር ነው | arat birr new | It is four birr.
 The definite ending on the price
 ዋጋ is price. ዋጋው is the price, the one you are both looking at. Picture a pencil, or a cup of coffee, on the counter between you and the seller. That shared thing is why the definite ending is there. The -ው is the same ending you already use on ቤቱ . Leaving it off is the common miss. You are not asking about prices in general. You are asking about this one.
 The shop's answer is a number plus ብር (birr), then ነው . Four birr is አራት ብር ነው . Twenty birr is ሃያ ብር ነው . One hundred birr is መቶ ብር ነው . Put the thing first when you want to name it.
 EX: ሃያ ብር ነው | haya birr new | It is twenty birr.

## LESSON likes — 23 · Likes, and kind remarks — deck "Compliments"
### SECTION 0   (has 11)
Like works the same way as want. The thing comes first, the verb last. "I like coffee" is ቡና እወዳለሁ . "I don't like coffee" is ቡና አልወድም . በጣም means very, and it sits in front of the verb: ቡና በጣም እወዳለሁ , I like coffee a lot. Want and like are not the same verb. እፈልጋለሁ means you want it. እወዳለሁ means you like it. You can want a coffee and also like coffee, but you cannot swap the words.
 EX: ቡና እወዳለሁ | bunna iweddallehu | I like coffee.
 EX: ቡና አልወድም | bunna alweddim | I don't like coffee.
 EX: ቡና በጣም እወዳለሁ | bunna bet'am iweddallehu | I like coffee a lot.

That same spelling is also "she doesn't like". The words around it tell you which. The woman form is different: አትወጅም .

## LESSON restaurant — 25 · At a restaurant — deck "Food and drink"
