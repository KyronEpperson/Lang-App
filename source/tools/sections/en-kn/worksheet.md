## LESSON greetings — 6 · Greetings: ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ, ನೀವು and ನೀನು — deck "Greetings and courtesy"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
English greetings change with the time of day. Kannada greetings change with the person in front of you.
 Hello: ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ 
 ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ namaskāra is a respectful hello at any hour, morning or night. ನಮಸ್ತೆ namaste is the same. On the phone people say ಹಲೋ .
 EX: ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ. | namaskāra. | Hello.
CARDS NOW: ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ = hello (respectful) [card] || ಹಲೋ = hello? (on the phone) [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 5)
Two words for "you"
 English has one "you". Kannada has two. ನೀವು nīvu is for strangers, elders, shopkeepers, teachers and colleagues: it is the safe default. ನೀನು nīnu is for close friends your age and for children; to an elder it sounds rude. The verb changes too, because a Kannada verb must agree with its subject. When in doubt, use ನೀವು : nobody minds a little extra respect.
 ; To a stranger ( ನೀವು ) ; To a friend ( ನೀನು ) ; 
 How are you? ; ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಾ? ; ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀಯಾ? ; 
 And you? ; ನೀವು? ; ನೀನು? ; 
 EX: ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ, ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಾ? | namaskāra, hēgiddīrā? | Hello, how are you?
 EX: ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀಯಾ? | hēgiddīyā? | How are you? (to a friend)
CARDS NOW: ನೀವು = you (polite; also you all) [card] || ನೀನು = you (one friend, a child) [card] || ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಾ? = How are you? [card] || ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀಯಾ? = How are you? (to a friend) [card] || ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ, ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಾ? = Hello, how are you?

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
I'm fine
 The answer is ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೀನಿ chennāgiddīni . Its ending, -ಈನಿ , means "I", so no separate word for "I" is needed. Do not echo the question's ending: ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೀರಿ means "you are fine".
 EX: ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೀನಿ, ನೀವು? | chennāgiddīni, nīvu? | I'm fine, and you?
CARDS NOW: ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೀನಿ. = I'm fine. [card] || ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೀನಿ, ನೀವು? = I'm fine, and you? [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 5)
Thank you, sorry, no problem
 ಧನ್ಯವಾದಗಳು dhanyavādagaḷu is "thank you", a little formal; in everyday speech you will hear ಥ್ಯಾಂಕ್ಸ್ thyānks all the time. Kannada has no set reply like "you're welcome": say ಪರವಾಗಿಲ್ಲ paravāgilla ("it's OK, no problem") or just smile. For "sorry" or "excuse me", say ಕ್ಷಮಿಸಿ kshamisi , or everyday ಸಾರಿ sāri . ಪರವಾಗಿಲ್ಲ answers a "sorry" too.
 EX: ಧನ್ಯವಾದಗಳು. | dhanyavādagaḷu. | Thank you.
 EX: ಪರವಾಗಿಲ್ಲ. | paravāgilla. | It's OK. No problem.
CARDS NOW: ಧನ್ಯವಾದಗಳು = thank you [card] || ಥ್ಯಾಂಕ್ಸ್ = thanks [card] || ಪರವಾಗಿಲ್ಲ. = It's OK. No problem. || ಕ್ಷಮಿಸಿ = excuse me; sorry (formal) [card] || ಸಾರಿ. = Sorry. [card]

### SECTION 4   (has 4)
Yes, no, OK
 ಹೌದು haudu is "yes" and ಇಲ್ಲ illa is "no". ಸರಿ sari is "OK, right", and ಆಯ್ತು āytu is "OK, done", also said when you agree to do something. You will meet ಇಲ್ಲ again in lesson 9, where it also means "there isn't".
 EX: ಹೌದು, ಸರಿ. | haudu, sari. | Yes, right.
CARDS NOW: ಹೌದು. = Yes. [card] || ಇಲ್ಲ. = No. [card] || ಸರಿ. = OK. [card] || ಹೌದು, ಸರಿ. = Yes, right.

### SECTION 5   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Goodbye: I'll go and come back
 Kannada has no everyday word for "goodbye". The person leaving says ಹೋಗಿ ಬರ್ತೀನಿ hōgi bartīni , "I'll go and come back"; the host answers ಹೋಗಿ ಬನ್ನಿ hōgi banni , "go and come back". To a friend or a child the host says ಹೋಗಿ ಬಾ .
 EX: ಹೋಗಿ ಬರ್ತೀನಿ. | hōgi bartīni. | Goodbye. (I'll be back.)
 EX: ಹೋಗಿ ಬನ್ನಿ. | hōgi banni. | Goodbye, come again.
 Most common mistake: the friend form to an elder. To your friend's father: ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀಯಾ? → ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಾ?
CARDS NOW: ಹೋಗಿ ಬರ್ತೀನಿ. = Goodbye (I'll be back). [card] || ಹೋಗಿ ಬನ್ನಿ. = Goodbye (to someone leaving). [card]

DECK CARDS IN NO SECTION (add by front, as a string, only where a section teaches them): ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ ಸರ್. | namaskāra sar. | Hello, sir. || ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು | dayaviṭṭu | please || ಆಯ್ತು, ಮಾಡ್ತೀನಿ. | āytu, māḍtīni. | OK, I'll do it. || ಬನ್ನಿ, ಕೂತ್ಕೊಳ್ಳಿ. | banni, kūtkoḷḷi. | Come in, have a seat. || ಶುಭ ರಾತ್ರಿ. | shubha rātri. | Good night.

## LESSON no-is — 7 · Pronouns, and no word for "is" — deck "About me"
### SECTION 0   (has 6)
English needs "am", "is" or "are" in every sentence. Kannada does not, when you say who or what something is.
 The pronouns
 Kannada ; English ; For ; 
 ನಾನು ; I ; ; 
 ನೀನು / ನೀವು ; you ; a friend / anyone else ; 
 ಅವನು ; he ; a boy, a friend ; 
 ಅವಳು ; she ; a girl, a friend ; 
 ಅವರು ; he, she (with respect); they ; any adult you respect, or more than one person ; 
 ಅದು / ಇದು ; it, that / this ; animals and things ; 
 ನಾವು ; we ; ; 
 English picks "he" or "she" for everyone. Kannada also asks about respect: your teacher, your parents and anyone older are ಅವರು avaru ; ಅವನು avanu and ಅವಳು avaḷu are for children and for people you would call ನೀನು .
CARDS NOW: ನಾನು = I [card] || ನೀನು = you (one friend, a child) [card] || ಅವನು = he (familiar) [card] || ಅವಳು = she (familiar) [card] || ಅವರು = he; she (respectful); they [card] || ನಾವು = we [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 5)
No word for "is"
 "I am a doctor" is simply ನಾನು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ nānu ḍākṭar : "I doctor". There is no word for "am", and none for "a" either. Point at something near you and say ಇದು , "this"; for something farther away, or for "it", say ಅದು .
 EX: ನಾನು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್. | nānu ḍākṭar. | I'm a doctor.
 EX: ಇದು ಪುಸ್ತಕ. | idu pustaka. | This is a book.
 EX: ಅದು ಕಾರು. | adu kāru. | That's a car.
CARDS NOW: ನಾನು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್. = I'm a doctor. [card] || ಇದು = this (thing) [card] || ಅದು = it; that [card] || ಇದು ಪುಸ್ತಕ. = This is a book. || ಅದು ಕಾರು. = That's a car.

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
"Not": ಅಲ್ಲ 
 To say what someone or something is not, put ಅಲ್ಲ alla at the end. Kannada has another "not", ಇಲ್ಲ , which says that something isn't there (lesson 9). For who or what something is, it is always ಅಲ್ಲ .
 EX: ನಾನು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ ಅಲ್ಲ. | nānu ḍākṭar alla. | I'm not a doctor.
 EX: ಇದು ಕಾಫಿ ಅಲ್ಲ. | idu kāfi alla. | This isn't coffee.
CARDS NOW: ಅಲ್ಲ = not (it is not) [card] || ಇಲ್ಲ. = No. [card] || ನಾನು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ ಅಲ್ಲ. = I'm not a doctor. [card] || ಇದು ಕಾಫಿ ಅಲ್ಲ. = This isn't coffee. [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 4)
Where from: this word agrees
 To say where someone is from, Kannada adds an ending to the country: ಅಮೆರಿಕ amerika gives ಅಮೆರಿಕದವನು amerikadavanu (a man from America), ಅಮೆರಿಕದವಳು amerikadavaḷu (a woman) and ಅಮೆರಿಕದವರು amerikadavaru (with respect, or more than one). With no verb in the sentence, this word does the agreeing: masculine -ವನು , feminine -ವಳು , and the respect plural -ವರು . India is ಭಾರತ bhārata .
 ಅವನು ಅಮೆರಿಕದವನು. ; He's American. ; 
 ಅವಳು ಅಮೆರಿಕದವಳು. ; She's American. ; 
 ಅವರು ಭಾರತದವರು. ; He's / She's Indian (respectful). They're Indian. ; 
 ನಾವು ಅಮೆರಿಕದವರು. ; We're American. ; 
 EX: ಅವಳು ಅಮೆರಿಕದವಳು. | avaḷu amerikadavaḷu. | She's American.
 EX: ಅವರು ಭಾರತದವರು. | avaru bhāratadavaru. | He's Indian. (respectful)
 Here, and only here so far, even "I" shows whether a man or a woman is speaking.
 EX: ನಾನು ಅಮೆರಿಕದವನು. | nānu amerikadavanu. | I'm American. (a man speaking)
 EX: ನಾನು ಅಮೆರಿಕದವಳು. | nānu amerikadavaḷu. | I'm American. (a woman speaking)
 Most common mistake: adding a verb for "am". ನಾನು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ ಇದ್ದೀನಿ → ನಾನು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ . ( ಇದ್ದೀನಿ means "I am somewhere": lesson 9.)
CARDS NOW: ಅವಳು ಅಮೆರಿಕದವಳು. = She's American. || ಅವರು ಭಾರತದವರು. = He's Indian. (respectful) || ನಾನು ಅಮೆರಿಕದವನು. = I'm American (man speaking). [card] || ನಾನು ಅಮೆರಿಕದವಳು. = I'm American (woman speaking). [card]

## LESSON questions — 8 · Questions: -ಆ, ಏನು, ಯಾರು, ಎಲ್ಲಿ — deck "Questions and connectors"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
English questions move words around: "You are a doctor" becomes "Are you a doctor?". Kannada keeps the order and changes one ending, or swaps in one word.
 Yes-no questions: add -ಆ 
 Put a long -ಆ on the last word. A word that ends in a consonant simply takes it: ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ becomes ಡಾಕ್ಟರಾ ḍākṭarā . After a final -ಅ , a v slips in: ಪುಸ್ತಕ becomes ಪುಸ್ತಕವಾ pustakavā . A final -ಉ makes way for it: ಅಮೆರಿಕದವರು becomes ಅಮೆರಿಕದವರಾ amerikadavarā . A rising voice alone does not make a question. Written Kannada uses -ಏ instead ( ಪುಸ್ತಕವೇ? ); you will see it on signs and in messages, and the course accepts it in your answers, but in speech say -ಆ .
 EX: ನೀವು ಡಾಕ್ಟರಾ? | nīvu ḍākṭarā? | Are you a doctor?
 EX: ಇದು ಪುಸ್ತಕವಾ? | idu pustakavā? | Is this a book?
 EX: ಅವರು ಅಮೆರಿಕದವರಾ? | avaru amerikadavarā? | Is he American? (respectful)
CARDS NOW: ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ = doctor [card] || ನೀವು ಡಾಕ್ಟರಾ? = Are you a doctor? [card] || ಇದು ಪುಸ್ತಕವಾ? = Is this a book? || ಅವರು ಅಮೆರಿಕದವರಾ? = Is he American? (respectful)

### SECTION 1   (has 5)
Answering
 Yes is ಹೌದು and no is ಇಲ್ಲ . To deny who or what something is, follow ಇಲ್ಲ with the ಅಲ್ಲ sentence from the last lesson. For yes, you can likewise repeat the sentence: ಹೌದು , ನಾನು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ .
 EX: ಇಲ್ಲ, ನಾನು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ ಅಲ್ಲ. | illa, nānu ḍākṭar alla. | No, I'm not a doctor.
CARDS NOW: ಹೌದು. = Yes. [card] || ಇಲ್ಲ. = No. [card] || ಅಲ್ಲ = not (it is not) [card] || ನಾನು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್. = I'm a doctor. [card] || ಇಲ್ಲ, ನಾನು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ ಅಲ್ಲ. = No, I'm not a doctor.

### SECTION 2   (has 13)
Question words stay in place
 English moves "what" and "who" to the front. Kannada puts the question word where the answer goes: ಇದು ಪುಸ್ತಕ , "this is a book", becomes ಇದು ಏನು? , "this is what?".
 ಏನು ; what ; ಯಾರು ; who ; 
 ಎಲ್ಲಿ ; where ; ಯಾವಾಗ ; when ; 
 ಹೇಗೆ ; how ; ಯಾಕೆ ; why ; 
 ಎಷ್ಟು ; how much, how many ; ಯಾವ ; which ; 
 EX: ಇದು ಏನು? | idu ēnu? | What is this?
 EX: ಅವರು ಯಾರು? | avaru yāru? | Who is he? (respectful)
 EX: ಇದು ಎಷ್ಟು? | idu eshṭu? | How much is this?
 ಯಾವ comes before a noun, just like English "which".
 EX: ಯಾವ ಪುಸ್ತಕ? | yāva pustaka? | Which book?
 Point at anything in a shop or a market and ask ಇದು ಏನು? or ಇದು ಎಷ್ಟು? : two of the most useful questions you will learn. A question word already makes the sentence a question, so it takes no -ಆ . The one fixed exception is the greeting ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಾ? .
CARDS NOW: ಇದು ಏನು? = What is this? [card] || ಏನು? = what? [card] || ಯಾರು? = who? [card] || ಎಲ್ಲಿ? = where? [card] || ಯಾವಾಗ? = when? [card] || ಹೇಗೆ? = how? [card] || ಯಾಕೆ? = why? [card] || ಎಷ್ಟು = how much; how many [card] || ಯಾವ = which (before a noun) [card] || ಅವರು ಯಾರು? = Who is he? (respectful) || ಇದು ಎಷ್ಟು? = How much is this? [card] || ಯಾವ ಪುಸ್ತಕ? = Which book? || ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಾ? = How are you? [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 4)
Where from? It agrees
 ಎಲ್ಲಿಯವರು elliyavaru asks "from where?" and agrees with the person, just like ಅಮೆರಿಕದವರು : -ವರು for ನೀವು , ಅವರು and more than one person, -ವನು for a man you know well, -ವಳು for a woman.
 EX: ನೀವು ಎಲ್ಲಿಯವರು? | nīvu elliyavaru? | Where are you from?
 EX: ಅವಳು ಎಲ್ಲಿಯವಳು? | avaḷu elliyavaḷu? | Where is she from?
 Later lessons put the same -ಆ on verbs: "Will you come?" and "Do you want it?" work exactly this way.
 Most common mistake: asking with your voice alone. ನೀವು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್? with a rising tone → ನೀವು ಡಾಕ್ಟರಾ?
CARDS NOW: ನೀವು = you (polite; also you all) [card] || ಅವರು = he; she (respectful); they [card] || ನೀವು ಎಲ್ಲಿಯವರು? = Where are you from? [card] || ಅವಳು ಎಲ್ಲಿಯವಳು? = Where is she from?

## LESSON ide — 9 · There is, there isn't: ಇದೆ, ಇಲ್ಲ — deck "Home and daily things"
### SECTION 0   (has 6)
In the last two lessons, "is" disappeared: ನಾನು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ . That is only for who or what something is. To say where something is, or that it exists at all, Kannada does use a verb.
 There is: ಇದೆ 
 ಇದೆ ide means "there is" or "it is (somewhere)". With ಇಲ್ಲಿ illi (here) and ಅಲ್ಲಿ alli (there) you can already point things out. The place word can come first or after the thing: ಇಲ್ಲಿ ಬ್ಯಾಂಕ್ ಇದೆ and ಬ್ಯಾಂಕ್ ಇಲ್ಲಿ ಇದೆ are both fine. The verb stays at the end.
 EX: ಇಲ್ಲಿ ಬ್ಯಾಂಕ್ ಇದೆ. | illi byānk ide. | There's a bank here.
 EX: ಕಾಫಿ ಇದೆ. | kāfi ide. | There's coffee.
 A useful word for your first walk: in Karnataka a ಹೋಟೆಲ್ hōṭel is usually a place to eat, not a place to sleep.

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
There isn't: ಇಲ್ಲ 
 Swap ಇದೆ for ಇಲ್ಲ illa . Remember the other "not", ಅಲ್ಲ : it says what something is not. ಇಲ್ಲ says it isn't there. And since a question with -ಆ is answered by repeating its verb, "Is there coffee?" gets a one-word answer: ಇದೆ (yes, there is) or ಇಲ್ಲ (no, there isn't).
 EX: ಇಲ್ಲಿ ಬ್ಯಾಂಕ್ ಇಲ್ಲ. | illi byānk illa. | There's no bank here.
CARDS NOW: ಇಲ್ಲ. = No. [card] || ಅಲ್ಲ = not (it is not) [card] || ಇಲ್ಲಿ ಬ್ಯಾಂಕ್ ಇಲ್ಲ. = There's no bank here.

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
Is there…? Where is…?
 Add -ಆ for a yes-no question: ಇದೆಯಾ ideyā . With a question word there is no -ಆ , and ಎಲ್ಲಿ + ಇದೆ usually run together as ಎಲ್ಲಿದೆ ellide ; both spellings are correct.

### SECTION 3   (has 5)
People: the verb agrees
 ಇದೆ is the neuter form, for things and animals. For a person, the verb takes a person ending that agrees with the subject, just as ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಾ? did in the greetings.

 Parents and anyone you respect take ಇದ್ದಾರೆ . Take ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ maneyalli , "at home", as a set phrase for now; lesson 18 explains its ending. In speech you will often hear it shortened to ಮನೇಲಿ manēli .
 EX: ಅಮ್ಮ ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ ಇದ್ದಾರೆ. | amma maneyalli iddāre. | Mom is at home.
 The negative is easy: ಇಲ್ಲ is the same for everyone and everything.

 Most common mistake: ಅಲ್ಲ for "isn't there". ಇಲ್ಲಿ ಬ್ಯಾಂಕ್ ಅಲ್ಲ → ಇಲ್ಲಿ ಬ್ಯಾಂಕ್ ಇಲ್ಲ .

## LESSON intro — 10 · Introducing yourself: ನನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು … — deck "About me"
### SECTION 0   (has 8)
This lesson puts what you know together into a short self-introduction: your name, where you are from, your work, and the languages you speak. The romanisation is a crutch: read the Kannada first and glance down only to check.
 My name is …
 English says "My name is Sarah". Kannada says "my name Sarah": ನನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು nanna hesaru , then the name, and nothing for "is". ನನ್ನ is "my", the form ನಾನು takes before a noun. To ask someone, use ನಿಮ್ಮ nimma , the polite "your"; to a friend or a child, ನಿನ್ನ ninna .
 EX: ನನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು ಸಾರಾ. | nanna hesaru sārā. | My name is Sarah.
 EX: ನಿಮ್ಮ ಹೆಸರು ಏನು? | nimma hesaru ēnu? | What's your name?
 Never put ಅವರು after your own name. After someone else's name it shows respect ( ರಾವ್ ಅವರು , Mr. Rao), and you do not show respect to yourself.

 My, your, his, her

## LESSON like-want — 15 · Liking, wanting, knowing: ನನಗೆ … ಇಷ್ಟ / ಬೇಕು / ಗೊತ್ತು — deck "Likes and free time"
### SECTION 0   (has 8)
In English, "I" does the liking, the wanting and the knowing: I like coffee . Kannada turns this around. The thing is the subject, and the person takes the dative ending you learned in the last lesson: "to me, coffee is liked". So these sentences start with ನನಗೆ , ನಿಮಗೆ , ಅವಳಿಗೆ , never with ನಾನು .
 Liking: ಇಷ್ಟ
 ಇಷ್ಟ ishṭa means "liking". Put the person first, then the thing, then ಇಷ್ಟ . To ask, add -ಆ: after the -ಅ of ಇಷ್ಟ a linking v comes in, as in ಪುಸ್ತಕವಾ? , giving ಇಷ್ಟವಾ? ishṭavā? . "I don't like it" is ಇಷ್ಟ ಇಲ್ಲ .
 EX: ನನಗೆ ಕಾಫಿ ಇಷ್ಟ. | nanage kāfi ishṭa. | I like coffee.
 EX: ನಿಮಗೆ ಟೀ ಇಷ್ಟವಾ? | nimage ṭī ishṭavā? | Do you like tea?
 EX: ನನಗೆ ಇಷ್ಟ ಇಲ್ಲ. | nanage ishṭa illa. | I don't like it.

### SECTION 1   (has 8)
Wanting: ಬೇಕು and ಬೇಡ
 ಬೇಕು bēku is "wanted, needed", and its opposite is ಬೇಡ bēḍa , "not wanted". When someone offers you something, answer with ಬೇಡ , the polite "no, thanks". ಇಲ್ಲ is the "no" of "is there…?" questions; on its own it does not say "I don't want it". The question is ಬೇಕಾ? . ### SECTION 2   (has 4)

### SECTION 3   (has 4)
Other people
 Here is some good news: ಇಷ್ಟ , ಬೇಕು and ಗೊತ್ತು have no person endings. They stay exactly the same for everyone, because the dative already tells you who likes, wants or knows. Just swap the person: ಅವನಿಗೆ , ಅವಳಿಗೆ , ಅವರಿಗೆ , ನಮಗೆ .

## LESSON locative — 18 · Where and from: ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ, ಬೆಂಗಳೂರಿನಿಂದ, ಜೊತೆ — deck "Getting around"
### SECTION 0   (has 6)
English uses small words before the noun: in Bengaluru, from Mysuru, with Mom. Kannada works the other way round: a case ending after the noun, or a separate word after it. You know the pattern from -ಗೆ, "to".
 "In, at": -ಅಲ್ಲಿ
 You have been saying ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ , "at home", since lesson 9. It is ಮನೆ plus the ending -ಅಲ್ಲಿ, with a linking ಯ in between. The linking sound depends on the noun, much as with -ಗೆ:

### SECTION 2   (has 8)
"With", "on", "under", "near"
 These are separate words that come after the noun, and the noun before them takes its "of" form, like ನನ್ನ : ಅಮ್ಮನ ಜೊತೆ ammana jote , "with Mom", literally "Mom's company".  EX: ನಾನು ಅಮ್ಮನ ಜೊತೆ ಇದ್ದೀನಿ. | nānu ammana jote iddīni. | I'm with Mom.

## LESSON present — 19 · Present and future: ಮಾಡ್ತೀನಿ, ಮಾಡಲ್ಲ — deck "Time, days and routine"
### SECTION 0   (has 14)
English has "I go" and "I will go". Spoken Kannada uses one form for both: ಹೋಗ್ತೀನಿ hōgtīni means "I go (usually)" and "I'll go". A word such as ದಿನಾ dinā (every day) or ನಾಳೆ nāḷe (tomorrow) tells you which one is meant.
 EX: ನಾನು ದಿನಾ ಆಫೀಸಿಗೆ ಹೋಗ್ತೀನಿ. | nānu dinā āfīsige hōgtīni. | I go to the office every day.
 EX: ನಾನು ನಾಳೆ ಮೈಸೂರಿಗೆ ಹೋಗ್ತೀನಿ. | nānu nāḷe maisūrige hōgtīni. | I'll go to Mysuru tomorrow.

## LESSON time — 21 · Clock time, days and routine — deck "Time, days and routine"
### SECTION 0   (has 8)
To ask the time, Kannada asks "how much is the hour?": ಗಂಟೆ ಎಷ್ಟು? gaṇṭe eshṭu? . The answer is a number, written as a word, plus ಗಂಟೆ gaṇṭe (hour, o'clock).
 EX: ಗಂಟೆ ಎಷ್ಟು? | gaṇṭe eshṭu? | What time is it?
 EX: ಈಗ ಐದು ಗಂಟೆ. | īga aidu gaṇṭe. | It's five o'clock now.
 Half past: -ಊವರೆ
 ವರೆ vare means "and a half". The number's last -ಉ becomes a long -ಊ and joins it, all as one word: ಏಳು → ಏಳೂವರೆ ēḷūvare (7:30), ಎರಡು → ಎರಡೂವರೆ (2:30), ಹತ್ತು → ಹತ್ತೂವರೆ (10:30). Clocks and tickets show digits; say the Kannada words.
 EX: ಈಗ ಎರಡೂವರೆ. | īga eraḍūvare. | It's two thirty now.
 The same half works away from the clock: ಒಂದೂವರೆ is "one and a half" and ಎರಡೂವರೆ "two and a half", for hours, kilos or liters.

## LESSON must-can — 22 · Must, may and can: ಮಾಡಬೇಕು, ಮಾಡಬಹುದು, ಮಾಡೋಕೆ ಆಗುತ್ತೆ — deck "Health and help"
### SECTION 0   (has 9)
English uses separate little words: must, may, can. Kannada adds an ending to the verb, and these endings do not change for the person: ನಾನು , ನೀವು and ಅವಳು all take the same form.
 Must, have to: -ಬೇಕು
 Take the stem, add -ಅ, then ಬೇಕು : ಹೋಗು → ಹೋಗಬೇಕು hōgabēku , ಮಾಡು → ಮಾಡಬೇಕು , ಕುಡಿ → ಕುಡಿಯಬೇಕು . You know ಬೇಕು as "want" in ನನಗೆ ಕಾಫಿ ಬೇಕು ; after a verb it means "have to", and the person is plain ನಾನು .
 EX: ನಾನು ಆಫೀಸಿಗೆ ಹೋಗಬೇಕು. | nānu āfīsige hōgabēku. | I have to go to the office.
 EX: ನೀವು ತುಂಬಾ ನೀರು ಕುಡಿಯಬೇಕು. | nīvu tumbā nīru kuḍiyabēku. | You have to drink lots of water.
 The same form works for anyone: ಅವಳು ನಾಳೆ ಬರಬೇಕು , "she has to come tomorrow"; ನಾವು ಹೋಗಬೇಕು , "we have to go". To ask "do I have to?", put -ಆ on the end: ನಾನು ಬರಬೇಕಾ? A doctor or a teacher will use this form a lot, so it is worth knowing well.

 ಮಾಡಬಾರದು māḍabāradu is "must not", the form of rules and signs. Compare the request ಹೋಗಬೇಡಿ , "please don't go", said to someone, with ಹೋಗಬಾರದು , "one must not go", a rule.
 EX: ಇಲ್ಲಿ ಸಿಗರೇಟ್ ಸೇದಬಾರದು. | illi sigarēṭ sēdabāradu. | You must not smoke here.
CARDS NOW: ಇಲ್ಲಿ ಸಿಗರೇಟ್ ಸೇದಬಾರದು. = You must not smoke here. [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
May: -ಬಹುದು
 ಮಾಡಬಹುದು māḍabahudu gives permission ("you may") or a guess ("it may happen"). In speech it often sounds like ಮಾಡ್ಬೋದು . Add -ಆ to ask permission.
 EX: ನೀವು ಒಳಗೆ ಬರಬಹುದು. | nīvu oḷage barabahudu. | You may come in.
 EX: ನಾನು ಒಳಗೆ ಬರಬಹುದಾ? | nānu oḷage barabahudā? | May I come in?
CARDS NOW: ನೀವು ಒಳಗೆ ಬರಬಹುದು. = You may come in. || ನಾನು ಒಳಗೆ ಬರಬಹುದಾ? = May I come in? [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 6)
Can: the ನನಗೆ pattern again
 For "can", Kannada says "to me, coming happens". The person takes the dative, like ನನಗೆ ಗೊತ್ತು : ನನಗೆ , ನಿಮಗೆ , ಅಮ್ಮನಿಗೆ . The verb takes -ಓಕೆ: ಬರೋಕೆ , ಹೋಗೋಕೆ , ಮಾಡೋಕೆ . Then comes ಆಗುತ್ತೆ āgutte (it happens) or ಆಗಲ್ಲ āgalla (it won't happen).
 EX: ನನಗೆ ನಾಳೆ ಬರೋಕೆ ಆಗಲ್ಲ. | nanage nāḷe barōke āgalla. | I can't come tomorrow.
 EX: ನನಗೆ ಸಂಜೆ ಬರೋಕೆ ಆಗುತ್ತೆ. | nanage sanje barōke āgutte. | I can come in the evening.
 When it is clear who you mean, ನನಗೆ can be left out: ನಾಳೆ ಬರೋಕೆ ಆಗಲ್ಲ . This is also the polite way to say no to an invitation: it says the coming "won't happen", which sounds softer than ನಾನು ಬರಲ್ಲ , "I won't come". You will also hear ಬರಕ್ಕೆ in place of ಬರೋಕೆ ; both are fine.
CARDS NOW: ನನಗೆ ಬರೋಕೆ ಆಗಲ್ಲ. = I can't come. [card] || ನನಗೆ = to me; for me [card] || ನನಗೆ ಗೊತ್ತು. = I know. [card] || ನಿಮಗೆ = to you; for you (polite) [card] || ನನಗೆ ನಾಳೆ ಬರೋಕೆ ಆಗಲ್ಲ. = I can't come tomorrow. || ನನಗೆ ಸಂಜೆ ಬರೋಕೆ ಆಗುತ್ತೆ. = I can come in the evening.

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
Spoken and written
 Fast speech drops a vowel: ಹೋಗ್ಬೇಕು , ಮಾಡ್ಬಾರ್ದು . Print writes ಬರಲು ಆಗುವುದಿಲ್ಲ for ಬರೋಕೆ ಆಗಲ್ಲ . Typed answers accept these forms.

 Most common mistake: starting "I can't" with ನಾನು. ನಾನು ಬರೋಕೆ ಆಗಲ್ಲ → ನನಗೆ ಬರೋಕೆ ಆಗಲ್ಲ .

## LESSON directions — 23 · Directions and transport — deck "Getting around"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
To ask the way, name the place and add ಎಲ್ಲಿದೆ? ellide? ("where is it?"). A place is a thing, so it takes the "it" form ಇದೆ .
 EX: ಬ್ಯಾಂಕ್ ಎಲ್ಲಿದೆ? | byānk ellide? | Where is the bank?
 Left, right, straight
 English says "turn left". Kannada puts the "to" ending on the direction: ಬಲ bala (right) and ಎಡ eḍa (left) end in -ಅ, so they take -ಕ್ಕೆ: ಬಲಕ್ಕೆ , ಎಡಕ್ಕೆ . ನೇರವಾಗಿ nēravāgi is "straight on". Add a polite verb: ಹೋಗಿ (go), ತಿರುಗಿ (turn). ಆಮೇಲೆ āmēle means "then".
 EX: ನೇರವಾಗಿ ಹೋಗಿ, ಆಮೇಲೆ ಬಲಕ್ಕೆ ತಿರುಗಿ. | nēravāgi hōgi, āmēle balakke tirugi. | Go straight, then turn right.
CARDS NOW: ಬ್ಯಾಂಕ್ ಎಲ್ಲಿದೆ? = Where is the bank? || ಬಲಕ್ಕೆ ಹೋಗಿ. = Go right. [card] || ಎಡಕ್ಕೆ ತಿರುಗಿ. = Turn left. [card] || ನೇರವಾಗಿ = straight; directly [card] || ಆಮೇಲೆ = then; after that [card] || ನೇರವಾಗಿ ಹೋಗಿ, ಆಮೇಲೆ ಬಲಕ್ಕೆ ತಿರುಗಿ. = Go straight, then turn right. || ನೇರವಾಗಿ ಹೋಗಿ. = Go straight ahead. [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Near, in front, behind
 English puts these words first: "near the bank". In Kannada they come after the place, which takes its "of" ending: ಆಸ್ಪತ್ರೆಯ ಹತ್ತಿರ (near the hospital), ಶಾಲೆಯ ಮುಂದೆ (in front of the school), ಬ್ಯಾಂಕಿನ ಹಿಂದೆ (behind the bank). In quick speech you will also hear the plain noun: ಆಸ್ಪತ್ರೆ ಹತ್ತಿರ .
 EX: ಬ್ಯಾಂಕ್ ಆಸ್ಪತ್ರೆಯ ಹತ್ತಿರ ಇದೆ. | byānk āspatreya hattira ide. | The bank is near the hospital.
 EX: ಬಸ್ ಸ್ಟ್ಯಾಂಡ್ ಶಾಲೆಯ ಮುಂದೆ ಇದೆ. | bas sṭyāṇḍ shāleya munde ide. | The bus stand is in front of the school.
CARDS NOW: ಬ್ಯಾಂಕ್ ಆಸ್ಪತ್ರೆಯ ಹತ್ತಿರ ಇದೆ. = The bank is near the hospital. || ಬಸ್ ಸ್ಟ್ಯಾಂಡ್ ಶಾಲೆಯ ಮುಂದೆ ಇದೆ. = The bus stand is in front of the school.

### SECTION 2   (has 6)
Near or far?
 On their own, ಹತ್ತಿರ hattira is "near" and ದೂರ dūra is "far". "From here" is ಇಲ್ಲಿ plus the "from" ending: ಇಲ್ಲಿಂದ illinda .
 EX: ಇಲ್ಲಿಂದ ಎಷ್ಟು ದೂರ? | illinda eshṭu dūra? | How far is it from here?
 EX: ತುಂಬಾ ಹತ್ತಿರ ಇದೆ. | tumbā hattira ide. | It's very close.
CARDS NOW: ಹತ್ತಿರ = near; with (someone has) [card] || ದೂರ = far; away [card] || ಇಲ್ಲಿ = here [card] || ಇಲ್ಲಿಂದ ಎಷ್ಟು ದೂರ? = How far is it from here? || ಎಷ್ಟು ದೂರ? = How far is it? [card] || ತುಂಬಾ ಹತ್ತಿರ ಇದೆ. = It's very close.

### SECTION 3   (has 3)
In an auto
 In Bengaluru you ask an auto driver whether he will "come" to your destination, with the "to" ending on the place: ಜಯನಗರ ends in -ಅ, so it becomes ಜಯನಗರಕ್ಕೆ . Then ask for the meter, and say where to stop.
 EX: ಜಯನಗರಕ್ಕೆ ಬರ್ತೀರಾ? | jayanagarakke bartīrā? | Will you go to Jayanagar?
 EX: ಮೀಟರ್ ಹಾಕಿ. | mīṭar hāki. | Please put the meter on.
 EX: ಇಲ್ಲಿ ನಿಲ್ಲಿಸಿ. | illi nillisi. | Stop here.
 If the driver does not want the trip, he may just say ಬರಲ್ಲ , "I won't come": the present negative you already know. Then try the next auto.
CARDS NOW: ಜಯನಗರಕ್ಕೆ ಬರ್ತೀರಾ? = Will you go to Jayanagar? [card] || ಮೀಟರ್ ಹಾಕಿ. = Please put the meter on. [card] || ಇಲ್ಲಿ ನಿಲ್ಲಿಸಿ. = Stop here. [card]

## LESSON past — 24 · The past: ಮಾಡಿದೆ, ಹೋದೆ, ಬಂದೆ — deck "Time, days and routine"
### SECTION 0   (has 9)
English makes the past with -ed or a new word: "go, went". Kannada also has a past stem, and then the person ending comes after it, as in the present.
 The past stem
 Many verbs add -ಇದ-: ಮಾಡು → ಮಾಡಿದೆ māḍide (I did), ನೋಡು → ನೋಡಿದೆ (I saw), ಕುಡಿ → ಕುಡಿದೆ (I drank). The most common short verbs change more, so learn them as they are:
 Verb ; I …ed ; 
 ಹೋಗು (go) ; ಹೋದೆ ; 
 ಬರು (come) ; ಬಂದೆ ; 
 ತಿನ್ನು (eat) ; ತಿಂದೆ ; 
 ಕೊಡು (give) ; ಕೊಟ್ಟೆ ; 
 EX: ನಾನು ನಿನ್ನೆ ಮೈಸೂರಿಗೆ ಹೋದೆ. | nānu ninne maisūrige hōde. | I went to Mysuru yesterday.
 EX: ನಾನು ಎರಡು ದೋಸೆ ತಿಂದೆ. | nānu eraḍu dōse tinde. | I ate two dosas.
 EX: ನಾನು ಅಮ್ಮನಿಗೆ ಫೋನ್ ಕೊಟ್ಟೆ. | nānu ammanige fōn koṭṭe. | I gave Mom the phone.
 EX: ನಾನು ಬೆಳಿಗ್ಗೆ ಕಾಫಿ ಕುಡಿದೆ. | nānu beḷigge kāfi kuḍide. | I drank coffee in the morning.
 Everything else in the sentence stays as before: the place still takes its ending ( ಮೈಸೂರಿಗೆ ), the person who receives still takes the "to" form ( ಅಮ್ಮನಿಗೆ ), and the verb still comes last. Only the verb changes for the past.

[card] || ನಾನು ನಿನ್ನೆ ಮೈಸೂರಿಗೆ ಹೋದೆ. = I went to Mysuru yesterday. || ನಿನ್ನೆ = yesterday [card] || ನಾನು ಎರಡು ದೋಸೆ ತಿಂದೆ. = I ate two dosas. || ನಾನು ಅಮ್ಮನಿಗೆ ಫೋನ್ ಕೊಟ್ಟೆ. = I gave Mom the phone. || ನಾನು ಬೆಳಿಗ್ಗೆ ಕಾಫಿ ಕುಡಿದೆ. = I drank coffee in the morning.

## LESSON past-neg — 25 · Past negative: ಮಾಡಲಿಲ್ಲ — deck "Traps for English speakers"
### SECTION 0   (has 11)
English says "I didn't go", "she didn't go", "they didn't go": "didn't" never changes. Kannada works the same way. The past negative is one form for every person: the stem plus -ಅಲಿಲ್ಲ.
 EX: ನಾನು ಹೋಗಲಿಲ್ಲ. | nānu hōgalilla. | I didn't go.

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Won't or didn't?
 The two words differ by one syllable, ಲಿ. ಹೋಗಲ್ಲ hōgalla is "won't go" or "don't go"; ಹೋಗಲಿಲ್ಲ hōgalilla is "didn't go". Listen for that ಲಿ, and say it clearly yourself.
 EX: ಅವನು ನಾಳೆ ಬರಲ್ಲ. | avanu nāḷe baralla. | He won't come tomorrow.
 EX: ಅವನು ನಿನ್ನೆ ಬರಲಿಲ್ಲ. | avanu ninne baralilla. | He didn't come yesterday.
 This matters in real life. If a friend asks whether you went to the party and you answer ಹೋಗಲ್ಲ , you have said "I won't go", about a party that is already over. ## LESSON and-then — 26 · Two actions in a row: ಊಟ ಮಾಡಿ ಹೋದೆ — deck "Food and drink"
### SECTION 0   (has 9)
English joins actions with "and": "I ate and left." Kannada uses a special form of the first verb, the -ಇ form, and no "and": ಊಟ ಮಾಡಿ ಹೋದೆ ūṭa māḍi hōde . Only the last verb shows the time and the person.
 EX: ನಾನು ಊಟ ಮಾಡಿ ಹೋದೆ. | nānu ūṭa māḍi hōde. | I ate and left.

The short verbs with a special past ( ಬಂದೆ , ತಿಂದೆ , ಕೊಟ್ಟೆ ), and verbs that end in -ಇ or -ಎ ( ಕುಡಿ , ತೊಳೆ ), use the past stem plus -ಉ:

## LESSON compare — 27 · Comparing with -ಕ್ಕಿಂತ — deck "Shopping and the market"
### SECTION 0   (has 6)
English changes the adjective: "big, bigger". Kannada leaves the adjective alone and marks the thing you compare with: ಇದು ಅದಕ್ಕಿಂತ ದೊಡ್ಡದು idu adakkinta doḍḍadu , "This is bigger than that", literally "this, than that, a big one".
 EX: ಇದು ಅದಕ್ಕಿಂತ ದೊಡ್ಡದು. | idu adakkinta doḍḍadu. | This is bigger than that.
 "Than" is an ending

 EX: ಮೈಸೂರು ಬೆಂಗಳೂರಿಗಿಂತ ಚಿಕ್ಕದು. | maisūru bengaḷūriginta chikkadu. | Mysuru is smaller than Bengaluru.

More and less: ಜಾಸ್ತಿ, ಕಡಿಮೆ
 ಜಾಸ್ತಿ jāsti is "more, too much" and ಕಡಿಮೆ kaḍime is "less". At the market they go with ಬೆಲೆ bele (price).
 EX: ಇದು ಅದಕ್ಕಿಂತ ಕಡಿಮೆ ಬೆಲೆ. | idu adakkinta kaḍime bele. | This is cheaper than that.
 EX: ಬೆಲೆ ತುಂಬಾ ಜಾಸ್ತಿ. | bele tumbā jāsti. | The price is too high.

The best: ಎಲ್ಲಕ್ಕಿಂತ
 "Than everything", ಎಲ್ಲಕ್ಕಿಂತ ellakkinta , gives "the most": the best, the biggest, the cheapest.
 EX: ಇದು ಎಲ್ಲಕ್ಕಿಂತ ಒಳ್ಳೆಯದು. | idu ellakkinta oḷḷeyadu. | This is the best.
CARDS NOW: ಇದು ಎಲ್ಲಕ್ಕಿಂತ ಒಳ್ಳೆಯದು. = This one is the best. [card]

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
At the shop
 Put it together with words you know. Point and ask ಇದು ಎಷ್ಟು? ; if it is too much, say ಬೆಲೆ ತುಂಬಾ ಜಾಸ್ತಿ and ask for something cheaper. When you compare two people, remember the ending: "she is younger than me" is ಅವಳು ನನಗಿಂತ ಚಿಕ್ಕವಳು .
 The romanisation is a crutch: read the Kannada first and glance down only to check.
 Most common mistake: putting "than" on the wrong word. For "This is bigger than that": ಅದು ಇದಕ್ಕಿಂತ ದೊಡ್ಡದು → ಇದು ಅದಕ್ಕಿಂತ ದೊಡ್ಡದು .
CARDS NOW: ಇದು ಎಷ್ಟು? = How much is this? [card]
