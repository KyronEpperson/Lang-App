# Worksheet: en-no

## LESSON alphabet — 1 · The alphabet: æ, ø, å — deck "Sounds and spelling"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
Norwegian uses the English alphabet and then three more letters: æ, ø, å . They are letters, with their own keys, not accents on a or o. There are 29 in all. Their names, in order, are: a, be, se, de, e, eff, ge, hå, i, je, kå, ell, em, en, o, pe, ku, err, ess, te, u, ve, dobbelt-ve, eks, y, sett, æ, ø, å. Vowels are named as their sound. c is se, q is ku, w is dobbelt-ve, and z is sett.
 Letter ; In the aid ; Do not read it as ; 
 æ ; ae ; the English letters a and e ; 
 ø ; oe ; an o you can skip the mark on ; 
 å ; oh ; a plain a ; 
 EX: båt | boh:t | a boat
 EX: være | VAE:-ruh | to be
 How to read the line under a phrase
 Under every phrase there is a pronunciation line in English letters. Listen first, then use the line to check.
 CAPITALS mark the stressed syllable. Most words stress the first syllable. A one-syllable word is all lowercase.
 A colon means hold the vowel, with no English glide. Stay on the vowel.
 The tokens are ah, eh, ee, oo, oh, ue, ew, ae, oe, and uh for an unstressed e. oo is a long o, as in bok . oh is å, and also a short o. ue is u, the end of "new", tighter, with no glide. ew is y, an ee with rounded lips. ae is æ. oe is ø.
 EX: bok | boo:k | a book
 EX: tak | tah:k | a roof
 EX: takk | tahk | thanks
 EX: hei | hai | hi
 EX: student | stue-DEHNT | a student
 tak holds the a, and takk does not: a roof, and thanks. hei writes ei as ai. student stresses the last syllable, the one in capitals.
CARDS NOW: båt = a boat [card] || være = to be [card] || bok = a book [card] || tak = a roof [card] || takk = thanks [card] || Hei! = Hi! [card] || student = a student

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
hy, sh, and what the line leaves out
 hy is the kj-sound, the h of "huge". It is not a k, and it is not sh. sh is skj, sj, and rs. j is the y of "yes". r is a tap, not the American r, and the line still writes r. Pitch, the two word tones, lives only in the audio. The line never marks it.
 EX: kjøpe | HYOE:-puh | to buy
 EX: norsk | nohshk | Norwegian
CARDS NOW: kjøpe = to buy [card] || norsk = Norwegian [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
Two patterns to keep
 Two blocks come back all through the course, so copy them now. bok is feminine: a book is ei bok . båt is masculine: a boat is en båt . A statement that starts with today puts the verb next: I dag jobber jeg , today I work. i dag is the first element, and jobber is the second. That is verb second.
 EX: ei bok | ai boo:k | a book
 EX: I dag jobber jeg. | ee: dah:g YOHB-uhr yai | Today I work.
 Most common mistake: reading å as a plain a, and æ as the English letters a and e. bat for a boat → båt . The three extra letters are letters. Missing one is wrong, not "almost".
CARDS NOW: ei bok = a book || I dag jobber jeg. = Today I'm working. [card] || i dag = today [card]

## LESSON vowels — 2 · Long and short vowels — deck "Sounds and spelling"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
English stretches a vowel to show feeling. Norwegian length is part of the word. Hold a vowel and you may have said something else. The pronunciation line marks a long vowel with a colon, and it never adds an English glide. You stay on the vowel until the consonant.
 One consonant, or two
 A stressed vowel followed by one consonant is usually long. A double consonant, or two different consonants, keeps it short. The line writes the double consonant once: the shortness is already in the missing colon.
 EX: tak | tah:k | a roof
 EX: takk | tahk | thanks
 EX: vin | vee:n | wine
 EX: vinn | veen | win
 tak is a roof, and the a is long. takk is thanks, and the a is short. vin is wine, long i. vinn is win, short i. If you say every vowel short, the way a careful English reader often does, tak and takk fall together.
CARDS NOW: tak = a roof [card] || takk = thanks [card] || vin = wine [card] || vinn = win [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
The letter o
 This is the vowel English speakers misread first. A long o is oo, a pure "oh" with rounded lips and no w at the end. bok , a book, is boo:k. It is not the short o of English "bok". A short o is usually oh, the same token as the letter å. komme , to come, has a short o, and the double m is what keeps it short.
 EX: bok | boo:k | a book
 EX: komme | KOHM-uh | to come
CARDS NOW: bok = a book [card] || komme = to come [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 5)
u, y, and å
 u has no English letter. The token ue is the end of "new", tighter, and with no glide. hus is a house. y is ew: say ee and round your lips. ny is new, and syk is ill. å is oh, a different letter from a. tå is a toe. ta is take. The app will treat a missing å as if it were a plain a, so when å is the difference, it has to be exact.
 EX: hus | hue:s | a house
 EX: ny | new: | new
 EX: syk | sew:k | ill
 EX: tå | toh: | a toe
 EX: ta | tah: | take
CARDS NOW: hus = house [card] || ny = new [card] || syk = ill [card] || tå = a toe || ta = take [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 3)
The small word in front
 hus is neuter, so a house is et hus . You already have ei bok (feminine) and en båt (masculine). The practice keeps those, and it keeps the verb-second sentence I dag jobber jeg .
 EX: et hus | eht hue:s | a house
 Most common mistake: making every vowel short, and reading bok with the English short o. takk said for a roof → tak , with the a held. English "bok" → bok with a long oo.
CARDS NOW: et hus = a house [card] || båt = a boat [card] || I dag jobber jeg. = Today I'm working. [card]

## LESSON consonants — 3 · Consonants: kj, skj and silent letters — deck "Sounds and spelling"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
Most consonants match English. kjøpe and norsk do not, and the line shows what to say instead.
 hy and sh
 kj is hy, the h of "huge". So is k before i, y, ei or øy, and so is tj. kjøpe , to buy, and kino , a cinema, both start with hy. skj and sj are sh. So is sk before those same front vowels, and so is rs. skjema , a form, starts with sh. norsk ends with sh, because rs is one sound. j by itself is y, as in ja .
 EX: kjøpe | HYOE:-puh | to buy
 EX: kino | HYEE:-noo | a cinema
 EX: skjema | SHEH:-mah | a form
 EX: norsk | nohshk | Norwegian
 Many younger speakers say kjøpe with sh. You will hear it. When you speak, keep hy. The line keeps kj and sh apart.
CARDS NOW: kjøpe = to buy [card] || norsk = Norwegian [card] || kino = a cinema || skjema = a form [card] || ja = yes [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 9)
Silent h and silent d
 h goes quiet in hv and hj . hva is what, and it starts with v. hjelpe , to help, starts with the y of j. A d after a vowel at the end of a word is often quiet: med , with. In ld , nd and rd at the end, the d goes quiet too. kald , cold, ends in l. vind , wind, ends in n and sounds like vinn , the short one, not like vin . bord , a table, keeps a long vowel and drops the d: the line still shows the r.
 EX: hva | vah: | what
 EX: hjelpe | YEHL-puh | to help
 EX: med | meh: | with
 EX: kald | kahl | cold
 EX: bord | boo:r | a table
 EX: vind | veen | wind
 g before i, y or ei is that same y. gi means to give, and it does not start with a hard g.
 EX: gi | yee: | to give
CARDS NOW: hva = what [card] || hjelpe = to help [card] || med = with [card] || kald = cold [card] || vind = wind || vinn = win [card] || vin = wine [card] || bord = a table [card] || gi = give [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
What the line does not try to show
 The definite ending on bil , a car, is -en: bilen , the car. The line gives two syllables, the careful form. Fast speech often shrinks it. When r at the end of a word meets t, d, n, l or s on the next word, the sounds pull together. The line does not show that join.
 EX: bilen | BEE:-luhn | the car
 bord is neuter, so a table is et bord . kino is masculine, so a cinema is en kino . The verb-second sentence is still I dag jobber jeg .
 EX: et bord | eht boo:r | a table
 EX: en kino | ehn HYEE:-noo | a cinema
 Most common mistake: starting kjøpe with a k, and saying norsk with an r plus an s. kjøpe with a k → kjøpe with hy. norsk with r plus s → norsk ending in sh.
CARDS NOW: bilen = the car || et bord = a table || en kino = a cinema || I dag jobber jeg. = Today I'm working. [card]

## LESSON greetings — 4 · Greetings: hei, takk, ha det — deck "Greetings and courtesy"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
Hei works with anyone, at any hour. It is hi. To match the time of day, add the greeting that names it. You use the same words with a friend, a stranger and the person behind a counter.
 Morning, afternoon, evening, bed
 English says "good morning". Norwegian says God morgen . God dag is good afternoon, for the middle of the day. In the evening, when you arrive, you say God kveld . God natt is good night, for when you or someone else is going to bed. It is not the greeting for walking in the door.
 EX: Hei! | hai | Hi!
 EX: God morgen. | goo: MOHR-guhn | Good morning.
 EX: God dag. | goo: dah:g | Good afternoon.
 EX: God kveld. | goo: kvehl | Good evening.
 EX: God natt. | goo: naht | Good night.
 You can put a name after the greeting. Hei, Anna and God morgen are enough. You do not add a title, and you do not switch greetings to sound more polite. Polite here is the ordinary greeting, said to the person in front of you.
CARDS NOW: Hei! = Hi! [card] || God morgen. = Good morning. [card] || God dag. = Good afternoon. [card] || God kveld. = Good evening. [card] || God natt. = Good night. [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 6)
Thanks, please, excuse me, goodbye
 Takk is thanks. Vær så snill is please. Unnskyld is excuse me, and it is also sorry when you bump into someone. To leave, say Ha det bra , bye. The short form is Ha det . Both are friendly. You can say them to staff as easily as to a friend.
 EX: Takk. | tahk | Thanks.
 EX: Vær så snill. | vae:r soh: sneel | Please.
 EX: Unnskyld. | UEN-shewl | Excuse me.
 EX: Ha det bra. | hah: deh: brah: | Bye.
CARDS NOW: Takk. = Thanks. [card] || Bra, takk. = Fine, thanks. [card] || Vær så snill. = Please. [card] || Unnskyld. = Excuse me. [card] || Ha det bra. = Bye. [card] || Ha det. = See you. [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 5)
du, and the you you only read
 English has one "you". The you you will say, to one person, is du . That includes a stranger, a shop assistant and a boss. It is ordinary, not rude. dere is you for several people, and it waits until the present tense. When a prompt means one person, it does not say "formal" or "informal". It just means du.
 In formal writing you may see De , Dem , Deres , with a capital. They still exist when a writer wants extra distance, and they can sound stiff. You recognize them. You do not answer with them, and you do not put them on your own sentences. You answer with du.
 The two blocks from the first lessons stay in the practice: ei bok (feminine), en båt (masculine), and I dag jobber jeg (verb second).
 Most common mistake: a formal you on every line, and good night at the door. De to a shop assistant → du . God natt when you arrive in the evening → God kveld .
CARDS NOW: du = you (one person) [card] || dere = you (several people) [card] || de = they [card] || deres = your (several); their [card] || I dag jobber jeg. = Today I'm working. [card]

## LESSON intro — 5 · Introducing yourself — deck "About me"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
Four sentences carry a first conversation. You say your name, where you come from, where you live, and what you do. The people in the examples are Anna, Lars and Kari, and the cities are Oslo, Bergen and Minneapolis. You use du if you need "you", including with staff. There is no formal version of these lines.
 Your name
 Jeg heter Anna means my name is Anna. heter already means "am called", so you do not put er in the middle. The same sentence works for any name: Jeg heter Lars .
 EX: Jeg heter Anna. | yai HEH:-tuhr AHN-ah | My name is Anna.
 When you meet someone, Hyggelig å hilse på deg is nice to meet you. å before a verb is "to", and deg is the object form of du. Learn the whole phrase as a block. The pieces come back later.
 EX: Hyggelig å hilse på deg. | HEWG-uh-lee oh: HEEL-suh poh: dai | Nice to meet you.
CARDS NOW: du = you (one person) [card] || Jeg heter Anna. = My name is Anna. [card] || Jeg heter Lars. = My name is Lars. [card] || Hyggelig å hilse på deg. = Nice to meet you. [card] || deg = you (object, one person) [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 6)
From, and in
 Where you are from is kommer fra . Where you live is bor i . A city takes i for "in" and fra for "from". You do not swap them. In the middle of a sentence, i is lowercase. It is not a capital the way English "I" is. When the sentence starts with it, the first letter is a capital, like any sentence.
 EX: Jeg kommer fra Oslo. | yai KOHM-uhr frah: OOS-loo | I'm from Oslo.
 EX: Jeg bor i Minneapolis. | yai boo:r ee: mee-nee-AH-poh-lees | I live in Minneapolis.
 You can put the place first. Then the verb stays in second place, and jeg follows it. I Oslo bor jeg is the same news as Jeg bor i Oslo , with the city as the first element. That is verb second again.
 EX: I Oslo bor jeg. | ee: OOS-loo boo:r yai | I live in Oslo.
CARDS NOW: fra = from [card] || Jeg kommer fra Oslo. = I'm from Oslo. [card] || jeg = I [card] || Jeg bor i Minneapolis. = I live in Minneapolis. [card] || I Oslo bor jeg. = I live in Oslo. || Jeg bor i Oslo. = I live in Oslo. [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 6)
What you do, and the name of the language
 A job with no adjective takes no article. Jeg er student is I'm a student. Jeg er lærer is I'm a teacher. English wants an "a" there. Leave it out. student is masculine when you do use the article, as in the phrase en student , but the job sentence itself does not take it. The full pattern, including what happens when an adjective is there, is in the next lessons.
 EX: Jeg er student. | yai ae:r stue-DEHNT | I'm a student.
 EX: Jeg er lærer. | yai ae:r LAE:-ruhr | I'm a teacher.
 The language is norsk , with a small n. Jeg er norsk means I'm Norwegian. Languages, like days, are lowercase. Norway also writes Nynorsk, and people speak dialects; this course teaches Bokmål.
 EX: Jeg er norsk. | yai ae:r nohshk | I'm Norwegian.
 Most common mistake: putting an article on the job, and a capital on the language. Jeg er en student → Jeg er student . Norsk in the middle of a sentence → norsk .
CARDS NOW: Jeg er student. = I'm a student. [card] || Jeg lærer norsk. = I'm learning Norwegian. [card] || Jeg er lærer. = I'm a teacher. [card] || en student = a student [card] || norsk = Norwegian [card] || Jeg er norsk. = I'm Norwegian. [card]

## LESSON gender — 6 · en, ei, et: three genders — deck "People and family"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
English has one word for "a" and one word for "the", and it treats every noun the same. Norwegian sorts every noun into masculine, feminine or neuter. The gender is part of the noun, not a guess from the meaning. A book is feminine. A car is masculine. A house is neuter. You learn the noun with its article, as one unit.
 a: the indefinite article
 The indefinite article is the word for "a". Call a noun an en-word, an ei-word or an et-word once. The names to keep are masculine, feminine and neuter.
 ; a ; the ; 
 masculine ; en bil ; bilen ; 
 feminine ; ei bok ; boka ; 
 neuter ; et hus ; huset ; 
 EX: en bil | ehn bee:l | a car
 EX: ei bok | ai boo:k | a book
 EX: et hus | eht hue:s | a house
 en goes with masculine nouns, ei with feminine nouns, et with neuter nouns. One article for everything is the mistake. et bok and en hus are wrong.
CARDS NOW: en = a; an (masculine); one [card] || ei = a; an (feminine) [card] || et = a; an (neuter) [card] || en bil = car [card] || ei bok = a book || et hus = a house [card] || huset = the house [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
the: the definite ending
 English puts "the" in front. Norwegian puts an ending on the noun. Masculine takes -en , feminine takes -a , neuter takes -et . The t of the neuter ending is silent, which is why the line under huset has no t.
 EX: bilen | BEE:-luhn | the car
 EX: boka | BOO:-kah | the book
 EX: huset | HUE:-suh | the house
 On a sign, in the news, or from some speakers, a feminine noun may show up as en bok and boken . That is common gender. You can read it. When you type, you still write ei bok and boka .
CARDS NOW: bilen = the car || boka = the book

A big book, a little book
 An adjective in front of the noun does not grow a feminine ending. stor stays stor for masculine and feminine. Neuter adds -t : et stort hus . Do not build ei stora bok . The one exception in this lesson is liten . Feminine uses lita , and neuter uses lite .

## LESSON present — 7 · The present tense in -r — deck "Work and study"
### SECTION 0   (has 13)
English changes the verb for she: she speaks, she goes. Norwegian does not. The present tense has one form, and every person uses it. That is the good news. There is no ending to pick for han, and no -s to add for hun.
 Add -r to the infinitive
 The infinitive is the å form, the form you meet in a word list: å snakke , to speak. Drop the å and add -r . snakke becomes snakker . Do not insert an extra e. gå , to go, becomes går , not gåer. The same step gives jobber from jobbe, bor from bo, kommer from komme, heter from hete, and studerer from studere.
 EX: å snakke | oh: SNAHK-uh | to speak
 EX: Jeg snakker norsk. | yai SNAHK-uhr nohshk | I speak Norwegian.
 EX: Jeg jobber. | yai YOHB-uhr | I work.
 EX: Jeg studerer. | yai stue-DEH:-ruhr | I'm studying.
 Subject ; Present ; 
 jeg, du, han, hun ; snakker ; 
 vi, dere, de ; snakker ; 
 a name ; Anna snakker ; 
 EX: Hun snakker norsk. | huen SNAHK-uhr nohshk | She speaks Norwegian.
 EX: Du snakker norsk. | due: SNAHK-uhr nohshk | You speak Norwegian.
 dere is you for several people. A prompt that means several people says so. One person, staff included, is still du . de means they. At the start of a sentence it takes a capital, like any word. A capital De in formal writing can be the distant you from the greetings lesson. You still do not produce that one. Jeg snakker norsk covers "I speak" and "I'm speaking". There is no separate -ing form.
 EX: Dere snakker norsk. | DEH:-ruh SNAHK-uhr nohshk | You speak Norwegian. (to several people)

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
er and gjør
 Two verbs do not take -r. å være , to be, is er for everyone. å gjøre , to do something, is gjør . Jeg gjør det means I do it. gjøre is not the English helper "do". You will see that clearly when questions arrive. Four more verbs, kan, vil, skal, må , also skip the -r. They wait for a later lesson. Do not build kaner or viler.
 EX: Jeg gjør det. | yai yoe:r deh: | I do it.
CARDS NOW: gjøre = do [card] || Jeg gjør det. = I do it.

### SECTION 2   (has 5)
Jobs
 You have already said Jeg er student . The same shape is Hun er lærer , she is a teacher. With no adjective, leave the article out. With an adjective, the article comes back, and it matches the noun. lærer is masculine, even when hun is the subject, so a good teacher is en god lærer .
 EX: Hun er lærer. | huen ae:r LAE:-ruhr | She is a teacher.
 EX: Hun er en god lærer. | huen ae:r ehn goo: LAE:-ruhr | She is a good teacher.

## LESSON word-order — 8 · Verb second: I dag jobber jeg — deck "Days, dates and weather"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)

 Full of food is mett . Jeg er mett . Jeg er full is not that. full on a person means drunk. On a glass it means full to the top, and the adjective agrees: Glasset er fullt , because glass is neuter.
 EX: Jeg er mett. | yai ae:r meht | I'm full of food.
 After you eat, especially to the person who cooked, say Takk for maten . Thanks for the meal. maten has the definite ending. The person who cooked can answer Vel bekomme . That reply belongs to Takk for maten . It is not what you say after every kind sentence.
 EX: Takk for maten. | tahk fohr MAH:-tuhn | Thanks for the meal.
 EX: Vel bekomme. | vehl buh-KOHM-uh | The host's reply to Takk for maten.

### SECTION 2   (has 11)
The short kind lines
 Det var kjempegodt means that was really good. det is neuter, so -t : the godt from adjectives, and kjempe makes it stronger. One word. var is the past of er . Learn the line as a chunk. Du er så flink means you are so good at this. flink is skill. Så snill du er means how kind you are. snill is kind. Du er så snill is the same words in another correct order.
 EX: Det var kjempegodt. | deh: vah:r HYEHM-puh-goht | That was really good.
 EX: Du er så flink. | due: ae:r soh: fleengk | You're so good at this.
 EX: Så snill du er. | soh: sneel due: ae:r | How kind you are.
 When someone says one of those to you, answer Takk . Thanks for help is a noun, not å plus a verb: Takk for hjelpen . Not Takk for å hjelpe .
 EX: Takk for hjelpen. | tahk fohr YEHL-puhn | Thanks for the help.
 If the sentence starts with today, the verb is still the second element. I dag er jeg mett . I dag vil jeg ha brød .
 EX: I dag er jeg mett. | ee: dah:g ae:r yai meht | Today I'm full of food.
 Most common mistake: the English full, and thanks plus å . Jeg er full → Jeg er mett , when you mean full of food. Takk for å hjelpe → Takk for hjelpen .

## LESSON double-def — 24 · Double definiteness: den store bilen — deck "Shopping and clothes"
### SECTION 0   (has 10)
English says the once: the big car. Norwegian, when an adjective sits inside that phrase, says it twice. A small word comes first, and the definite ending stays on the noun. That pair is the double definite.
 den, det, de, and the ending
 den goes with masculine and with feminine. den store bilen , the big car. den store boka , the big book. den røde jakka , the red jacket. det goes with neuter. det store huset , the big house. det røde huset , the red house. de goes with the plural. de store bilene , the big cars. This de is the plural word, not a formal you.
 EX: den store bilen | dehn STOO:-ruh BEE:-luhn | the big car
 EX: den store boka | dehn STOO:-ruh BOO:-kah | the big book
 EX: det store huset | deh: STOO:-ruh HUE:-suh | the big house
 EX: de store bilene | dee: STOO:-ruh BEE:-luh-nuh | the big cars
 EX: det røde huset | deh: ROE:-duh HUE:-suh | the red house
 The adjective takes -e for every gender in this pattern. Not -t . Compare three houses you already know. A big house, indefinite, is et stort hus , with -t . The house is big, after er , is Huset er stort , with -t again, and no den or det . The big house, with the adjective inside the, is det store huset . store , not stort , and the ending stays.
 EX: et stort hus | eht stoo:rt hue:s | a big house
 EX: Huset er stort. | HUE:-suh ae:r stoo:rt | The house is big.
CARDS NOW: den = it (masculine or feminine noun) [card] || det = it; that; there [card] || de = they [card] || den store bilen = the big car [card] || den store boka = the big book || det store huset = the big house [card] || det røde huset = the red house || de store bilene = the big cars || et stort hus = a big house [card] || Huset er stort. = The house is big. [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 10)
liten becomes lille
 liten changes shape here. In front of the definite noun it becomes lille : den lille bilen , den lille boka , det lille huset . The plural stays små . barn has no plural ending, and the definite plural is barna : de små barna .
 EX: den lille boka | dehn LEEL-uh BOO:-kah | the little book
 EX: de små barna | dee: smoh: BAH:-rnah | the little children
 Leave the ending on. den store bil , det store hus and de store biler are the English pattern, with the ending missing. The ending is not optional. denne , dette and disse can take the same adjective: denne store bilen , this big car. Same ending. You can build the simpler den store bilen first.
 EX: denne store bilen | DEHN-uh STOO:-ruh BEE:-luhn | this big car

### SECTION 2   (has 6)
The verb does not move
 kjøpe means to buy. The present is kjøper . I dag kjøper jeg den store bilen . I dag is the first element, then kjøper , then jeg . ikke stays after the conjugated verb: Jeg liker ikke det store huset . Not Jeg ikke liker , and not det stort huset .
 EX: Jeg liker ikke det store huset. | yai LEE:-kuhr EEK-uh deh: STOO:-ruh HUE:-suh | I don't like the big house.
 EX: I dag kjøper jeg den store bilen. | ee: dah:g HYOE:-puhr yai dehn STOO:-ruh BEE:-luhn | Today I'm buying the big car.
 Most common mistake: the adjective with no ending on the noun. den store bil → den store bilen . Double definite: the word in front, and the definite ending stays.

## LESSON past — 25 · The past tense — deck "Likes and free time"
### SECTION 0   (has 11)
English adds -ed to most verbs. Norwegian has a few patterns, and the past is one form for jeg , du , hun and vi , just like the present. The spelling of the ending is the work.
 The snakke group: -et
 Verbs like snakke take -et . snakke becomes snakket . vaske becomes vasket . lage , to make, becomes laget . jobbe becomes jobbet . You will hear snakka , especially in speech, and you will sometimes read it. This course types snakket . Do not type snakka .
 EX: Jeg snakket norsk. | yai SNAHK-uh nohshk | I spoke Norwegian.
 EX: Jeg vasket huset. | yai VAHS-kuh HUE:-suh | I cleaned the house.
CARDS NOW: jeg = I [card] || du = you (one person) [card] || hun = she [card] || vi = we [card] || snakke = speak; talk [card] || et = a; an (neuter) [card] || vaske = wash; clean [card] || lage = make; cook [card] || jobbe = work [card] || Jeg snakket norsk. = I spoke Norwegian. [card] || Jeg vasket huset. = I cleaned the house.

### SECTION 0   (has 7)

 People and things
