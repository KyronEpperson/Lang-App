## LESSON greetings — 5 · Greetings, आप and तुम — deck "Greetings and courtesy"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
One greeting for the whole day
 English changes its greeting with the clock. Hindi doesn't: नमस्ते namaste means hello and goodbye, morning or night, to anyone. Pressing your palms together is optional. नमस्कार namaskār is the same, a little more formal.
 EX: नमस्ते। | namaste. | Hello.
CARDS NOW: नमस्ते = hello (in the script lesson) [card] || नमस्ते। = Hello. / Goodbye. [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 11)
Three words for "you"
 English has one "you". Hindi has three. आप āp is the polite one: strangers, older people, shopkeepers, teachers, colleagues. When in doubt, use आप . तुम tum is for friends, children and people clearly younger than you. तू tū is only for very close friends and small children; to anyone else it is rude, so just recognize it.
 आप always takes the plural verb हैं haĩ , even for one person. The word for "how" changes with the person you ask: कैसे kaise to a man, कैसी kaisī to a woman.
 EX: आप कैसे हैं? | āp kaise haĩ? | How are you? (to a man)
 EX: आप कैसी हैं? | āp kaisī haĩ? | How are you? (to a woman)
 EX: तुम कैसे हो? | tum kaise ho? | How are you? (to a friend, a man)
 The usual answer is मैं ठीक हूँ maĩ ṭhīk hū̃ , "I'm fine". With मैं maĩ , "am" is always हूँ hū̃ . Then ask back with the same question, or just और आप? aur āp? , "and you?"
 EX: मैं ठीक हूँ। | maĩ ṭhīk hū̃. | I'm fine.
CARDS NOW: आप = you (polite) [card] || तुम = you (to a friend or a child) [card] || तू = you (very close, or rude) [card] || हैं = are [card] || कैसे = how (in what way) [card] || आप कैसे हैं? = How are you? (to a man, polite) [card] || आप कैसी हैं? = How are you? (to a woman, polite) [card] || तुम कैसे हो? = How are you? (to a male friend) [card] || मैं = I [card] || और आप? = And you? (polite) [card] || मैं ठीक हूँ। = I'm fine. [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
Your name and where you're from
 Say मेरा नाम … है : literally "my name … is". The verb comes last.
 EX: मेरा नाम सारा है। | merā nām sārā hai. | My name is Sarah.
 EX: आप कहाँ से हैं? | āp kahā̃ se haĩ? | Where are you from?
 EX: मैं अमरीका से हूँ। | maĩ amrīkā se hū̃. | I'm from America.
 English puts "from" before the place. Hindi puts से se after it: अमरीका से amrīkā se . You will see this pattern everywhere.
CARDS NOW: मेरा नाम सारा है। = My name is Sarah. [card] || आप कहाँ से हैं? = Where are you from? (polite) [card] || से = from; with; by; than [card] || मैं अमरीका से हूँ। = I'm from America. [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
जी
 जी jī after a name or title adds respect: शर्मा जी sharmā jī . Alone it means a polite "yes"; जी हाँ jī hā̃ and जी नहीं jī nahī̃ are a polite yes and no. Never put जी after your own name.
CARDS NOW: जी = yes (polite); pardon? [card]

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Thanks, sorry, goodbye
 शुक्रिया shukriyā is the everyday thank-you; धन्यवाद dhanyavād is more formal, for signs and speeches. Hindi speakers thank less often than English speakers, because आप already sounds polite. To thanks or to "sorry", answer कोई बात नहीं koī bāt nahī̃ , "no problem".
 EX: कोई बात नहीं। | koī bāt nahī̃. | No problem. / You're welcome.
 माफ़ कीजिए māf kījie means "excuse me" and "sorry". To leave, say नमस्ते and फिर मिलेंगे phir milẽge , "see you again".
 EX: फिर मिलेंगे। | phir milẽge. | See you again.
 Most common mistake: copying "you're welcome" word for word. आपका स्वागत है (that means "welcome to our place") → कोई बात नहीं .
CARDS NOW: कोई बात नहीं। = No problem. / You're welcome. [card] || फिर मिलेंगे। = See you again. [card]

(formal) || बहुत-बहुत शुक्रिया। | bahut-bahut shukriyā. | Thank you so much. || माफ़ कीजिए। | māf kījie. | Excuse me. / I'm sorry. || जी हाँ। | jī hā̃. | Yes. ## LESSON hona — 6 · Pronouns and होना: am, is, are — deck "About me"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
English has am, is and are. Hindi has one verb, होना honā , "to be", with four present forms. Which one you use depends only on the person.
 The forms
 Person ; Hindi ; English ; 
 मैं ; हूँ ; I am ; 
 तुम ; हो ; you are (friend) ; 
 आप ; हैं ; you are (polite) ; 
 यह / वह ; है ; he, she, it, this, that is ; 
 हम / ये / वे ; हैं ; we, these, they are ; 
 Notice the dot: है hai is "is", हैं haĩ , with the nasal dot, is "are". Say it through the nose.
CARDS NOW: होना = to be; to happen [card] || मैं = I [card] || तुम = you (to a friend or a child) [card] || आप = you (polite) [card] || हैं = are [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 7)
The verb goes last
 English says "I am a doctor". Hindi says "I doctor am": the verb closes the sentence. Hindi also needs no word for "a". You can add एक ek , "one", but most of the time you leave it out. A place word like यहाँ yahā̃ ("here") or a question word like कहाँ kahā̃ ("where") sits just before the verb.
 EX: मैं डॉक्टर हूँ। | maĩ ḍŏkṭar hū̃. | I'm a doctor.
 EX: तुम यहाँ हो। | tum yahā̃ ho. | You're here. (to a friend)
 EX: आप कहाँ हैं? | āp kahā̃ haĩ? | Where are you?
CARDS NOW: एक = one [card] || यहाँ = here [card] || आप कहाँ से हैं? = Where are you from? (polite) [card] || कहाँ = where [card] || मैं डॉक्टर हूँ। = I'm a doctor. || तुम यहाँ हो। = You're here. (to a friend) || आप कहाँ हैं? = Where are you?

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
यह and वह: he, she, it
 English picks "he", "she" or "it". Hindi doesn't care: यह yah means he, she, it or this, for someone or something near; वह vah means the same for someone or something farther away. In speech they sound like "yeh" and "voh", and वह is often written वो vo .
 EX: यह मेरा घर है। | yah merā ghar hai. | This is my house.
 EX: वह मेरा दोस्त है। | vah merā dost hai. | He's my friend.
CARDS NOW: यह = this; he; she; it (near) [card] || वह = he; she; it; that [card] || यह मेरा घर है। = This is my house. || वह मेरा दोस्त है। = He's my friend.

### SECTION 3   (has 5)
More than one
 हम ham is "we", ये ye "these, they (near)" and वे ve "they (far)". All three take हैं .
 EX: हम दोस्त हैं। | ham dost haĩ. | We're friends.
 EX: वे यहाँ हैं। | ve yahā̃ haĩ. | They're here.
CARDS NOW: हम = we [card] || ये = these; they (near) [card] || वे = they; he or she (respectful) [card] || हम दोस्त हैं। = We're friends. || वे यहाँ हैं। = They're here.

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
The plural of respect
 आप āp takes the plural हैं even when you speak to one person. Hindi does the same for one older or respected person you talk about: your parents, grandparents, teachers. The words for "my" change too, to मेरे mere (more on that later).
 EX: मेरे पिताजी डॉक्टर हैं। | mere pitājī ḍŏkṭar haĩ. | My father is a doctor.
 This plural is about respect, not number: शर्मा जी यहाँ हैं sharmā jī yahā̃ haĩ means "Mr. Sharma is here", one man. A friend, a child or someone younger than you gets the plain है . For a respected person, वे often replaces वह : वे मेरे टीचर हैं ve mere ṭīchar haĩ , "he's my teacher".
 Most common mistake: using है for everyone. मैं डॉक्टर है → मैं डॉक्टर हूँ ; आप कहाँ है? → आप कहाँ हैं?
CARDS NOW: मेरे पिताजी डॉक्टर हैं। = My father is a doctor.

## LESSON gender — 7 · Gender and plurals — deck "Home and daily things"
### SECTION 0   (has 8)
English nouns have no gender: a table is just "it". In Hindi every noun is masculine or feminine, things included. A room, कमरा kamrā , is masculine; a book, किताब kitāb , is feminine. Other words in the sentence change to match, so learn each noun together with its gender.
 The ending is a hint
 Most nouns ending in -ā (written ा ) are masculine. Many nouns ending in -ī (written ी ) are feminine. For other endings there is no rule.
 Masculine ; Feminine ; 
 लड़का (boy) ; लड़की (girl) ; 
 कमरा (room) ; कुर्सी (chair) ; 
 घर (house) ; किताब (book) ; 
 The hint fails often enough to matter. पानी pānī ("water") and आदमी ādmī ("man") end in -ī but are masculine. बात bāt ("matter, thing said") and मेज़ mez ("table") are feminine. And never guess from English or from the meaning: a book is not more feminine than a room.
CARDS NOW: कमरा = room [card] || किताब = book [card] || कुर्सी = chair [card] || घर = house; home [card] || पानी = water [card] || आदमी = man [card] || बात = matter; thing said; talk [card] || मेज़ = table [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
People
 For people, gender usually follows the person: लड़का laṛkā is a boy, लड़की laṛkī a girl. A few words, like दोस्त dost ("friend") and डॉक्टर ḍŏkṭar ("doctor"), work for both; the words around them then show whether you mean a man or a woman.
CARDS NOW: लड़का = boy; young man [card] || लड़की = girl; young woman [card] || दोस्त = friend [card] || डॉक्टर = doctor [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
Seeing gender: मेरा and मेरी
 You already know मेरा merā , "my", from मेरा नाम merā nām . Before a feminine noun it becomes मेरी merī .
 EX: मेरा कमरा | merā kamrā | my room
 EX: मेरी किताब | merī kitāb | my book
CARDS NOW: मेरा = my; mine [card] || मेरा कमरा = my room || मेरी किताब = my book [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 5)
Plurals
 English adds -s. Hindi has four patterns:
 masculine in -ā: -ā becomes -e: लड़का → लड़के laṛke 
 other masculine nouns stay the same: घर ghar → घर 
 feminine in -ī: -ī becomes -iyā̃ (ी → ियाँ): लड़की → लड़कियाँ laṛkiyā̃ 
 other feminine nouns add -ẽ: किताब → किताबें kitābẽ 
 EX: लड़के यहाँ हैं। | laṛke yahā̃ haĩ. | The boys are here.
 EX: लड़कियाँ यहाँ हैं। | laṛkiyā̃ yahā̃ haĩ. | The girls are here.
 EX: ये मेरी किताबें हैं। | ye merī kitābẽ haĩ. | These are my books.
 EX: आदमी यहाँ हैं। | ādmī yahā̃ haĩ. | The men are here.
 A plural subject takes the plural verb हैं haĩ , as you saw in the last lesson. A noun that stays the same, like घर or आदमी , shows its number only through the verb: आदमी यहाँ है ādmī yahā̃ hai is one man, आदमी यहाँ हैं is several.
CARDS NOW: लड़के यहाँ हैं। = The boys are here. || लड़कियाँ यहाँ हैं। = The girls are here. || ये मेरी किताबें हैं। = These are my books. || आदमी यहाँ हैं। = The men are here. || हैं = are [card]

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Water is masculine
 Because पानी is masculine, the word for "cold" takes its masculine form, as the next lesson explains.
 EX: पानी ठंडा है। | pānī ṭhaṇḍā hai. | The water is cold.
 Most common mistake: trusting the -ī ending. पानी ठंडी है → पानी ठंडा है : पानी is masculine.
CARDS NOW: पानी ठंडा है। = The water is cold.

## LESSON adjectives — 8 · Adjectives that agree: अच्छा, अच्छे, अच्छी — deck "Describing things"
### SECTION 0   (has 10)
An English adjective never changes: a good boy, a good girl, good boys. Many Hindi adjectives change their ending to match the noun's gender and number.
 Three endings
 Adjectives that end in -ā in their dictionary form, like अच्छा acchhā ("good"), have three forms:
 Ending ; Used with ; Example ; 
 -ā: अच्छा ; one masculine noun ; अच्छा लड़का ; 
 -e: अच्छे ; masculine plural ; अच्छे लड़के ; 
 -ī: अच्छी ; any feminine noun, one or more ; अच्छी लड़की ; 
 EX: अच्छा लड़का | acchhā laṛkā | a good boy
 EX: अच्छी लड़की | acchhī laṛkī | a good girl
 Look at the feminine column: one form for one and for many. English speakers expect a plural ending there, but अच्छी किताब acchhī kitāb and अच्छी किताबें acchhī kitābẽ both use अच्छी acchhī ; the noun and the verb show the plural. A masculine noun that never changes, like घर ghar , still makes the adjective change: बड़ा घर baṛā ghar , one big house; बड़े घर baṛe ghar , big houses.
 Others like it: बड़ा baṛā (big), छोटा chhoṭā (small), नया nayā (new), पुराना purānā (old), लंबा lambā (tall, long), ठंडा ṭhaṇḍā (cold). New is spelled नया , नए nae , नई naī .
CARDS NOW: अच्छा = good; OK [card] || अच्छा लड़का = a good boy [card] || अच्छी लड़की = a good girl [card] || घर = house; home [card] || बड़ा = big; older [card] || छोटा = small; younger [card] || नया = new [card] || पुराना = old (a thing) [card] || लंबा = tall; long [card] || ठंडा = cold [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 8)
Adjectives that never change
 So when you learn a new adjective, notice one thing: does its dictionary form end in -ā? If it does, it almost always changes (a few never do, such as ज़्यादा zyādā , "much, more", and शादीशुदा shādīshudā , "married"). Adjectives with any other ending stay the same: लाल lāl (red), सुंदर sundar (beautiful), ख़ुश ḵhush (happy), गरम garam (hot), साफ़ sāf (clean).
 EX: लाल किताब | lāl kitāb | a red book
 EX: लड़के ख़ुश हैं। | laṛke ḵhush haĩ. | The boys are happy.
CARDS NOW: ज़्यादा = more; too much [card] || लाल = red [card] || सुंदर = beautiful; pretty [card] || ख़ुश = happy [card] || गरम = hot; warm [card] || साफ़ = clean; clear [card] || लाल किताब = a red book || लड़के ख़ुश हैं। = The boys are happy.

### SECTION 2   (has 6)
Before the noun, or before है
 As in English, the adjective comes before its noun: बड़ा कमरा baṛā kamrā , "a big room". After a noun it sits just before the verb: "the room big is". The agreement stays the same in both places, and this second position is where English speakers forget it.
 EX: मेरा कमरा छोटा है। | merā kamrā chhoṭā hai. | My room is small.
 EX: कमरे बड़े हैं। | kamre baṛe haĩ. | The rooms are big.
 EX: लड़की बहुत लंबी है। | laṛkī bahut lambī hai. | The girl is very tall.
 बहुत bahut means "very". It never changes.
CARDS NOW: मेरा कमरा छोटा है। = My room is small. || वह लड़की लंबी है। = That girl is tall. [card] || कमरे बड़े हैं। = The rooms are big. || बड़े कमरे = big rooms [card] || लड़की बहुत लंबी है। = The girl is very tall. || बहुत = very; a lot [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 5)
आप and respect
 आप āp takes the plural, so an adjective about आप takes -e when you speak to a man, and -ī when you speak to a woman. The verb is हैं haĩ either way.
 EX: आप बहुत अच्छे हैं। | āp bahut acchhe haĩ. | You're very kind. (to a man)
 The word you met in greetings, कैसा kaisā ("how, what kind"), works the same way: कैसे kaise , कैसी kaisī .

## LESSON questions — 9 · Question words: क्या, कौन, कहाँ, कैसा — deck "Questions and connectors"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
English turns a sentence around to ask a question: "you are" becomes "are you?". Hindi never moves the verb. It stays last, and a question needs only a small word and a rising voice.
 Yes-no questions: क्या at the start
 Put क्या kyā in front of an ordinary sentence and raise your voice at the end. Here क्या does not mean "what": it just announces a question.
 EX: क्या आप डॉक्टर हैं? | kyā āp ḍŏkṭar haĩ? | Are you a doctor?

## LESSON oblique — 13 · The oblique form: लड़के को, कमरे में — deck "Home and daily things"
### SECTION 0   (has 6)
In English, "room" stays "room" whatever comes before or after it. In Hindi, some words change shape when a postposition follows them. This shape is called the oblique form (the "oblique case" in grammar books). You have already met it in pronouns: यह + में = इसमें .
 Masculine nouns in -ā: -ā becomes -e
 A masculine noun that ends in -ā changes it to -e before a postposition: कमरा kamrā (room) becomes कमरे में kamre mẽ (in the room), and लड़का laṛkā (boy) becomes लड़के को laṛke ko (to the boy). It looks just like the plural, but here it means one.
 EX: लड़के कमरे में हैं। | laṛke kamre mẽ haĩ. | The boys are in the room.
 In that sentence, लड़के is plural (more than one boy), but कमरे is one room, in its oblique form before में . The verb tells you about the subject: हैं , so more than one boy.

## LESSON like-need — 17 · पसंद, चाहिए: like and need — deck "Shopping"
### SECTION 0   (has 10)
English makes the person the subject: "I like tea", "I need water". Hindi turns these round, literally "to me, tea is pleasing" and "to me, water is needed". ### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Right now, not usually
 English has two presents: "I work" (a habit) and "I am working" (right now). Hindi has the same pair. You already know the habit form, करता हूँ kartā hū̃ . For something happening at this moment, Hindi uses the continuous: कर रहा हूँ kar rahā hū̃ .
 EX: मैं रोज़ चाय पीता हूँ। | maĩ roz chāy pītā hū̃. | I drink tea every day. (a man speaking)
 EX: मैं अभी चाय पी रहा हूँ। | maĩ abhī chāy pī rahā hū̃. | I'm drinking tea right now. (a man speaking)
CARDS NOW: मैं रोज़ चाय पीता हूँ। = I drink tea every day. (a man speaking) || मैं अभी चाय पी रहा हूँ। = I'm drinking tea right now. (a man speaking)

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
How to build it
 Take the stem (the infinitive minus -ना: बना from बनाना ), then add रहा rahā , then the right form of "to be": हूँ , है , हैं or हो . The stem never changes. Each part is a separate word.
 EX: मैं खाना बना रहा हूँ। | maĩ khānā banā rahā hū̃. | I'm cooking. (a man speaking)
CARDS NOW: बनाना = to make; to cook [card] || हैं = are [card] || मैं खाना बना रहा हूँ। = I'm cooking. (a man speaking) [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 7)
रहा, रही, रहे agree
 English "-ing" never changes. Hindi रहा behaves like an adjective such as अच्छा : it agrees with the subject.
 Subject ; Form ; Example ; 
 one man or boy, masculine thing ; रहा ; वह आ रहा है ; 
 several men, mixed groups, आप (to a man) ; रहे ; वे आ रहे हैं ; 
 a woman or girl, feminine thing (one or many) ; रही ; वह आ रही है ; 
 A woman says मैं खाना बना रही हूँ . To a woman you ask आप क्या कर रही हैं? : रही for her, हैं because आप is always plural.
 EX: मैं खाना बना रही हूँ। | maĩ khānā banā rahī hū̃. | I'm cooking. (a woman speaking)
 EX: आप क्या कर रहे हैं? | āp kyā kar rahe haĩ? | What are you doing? (to a man)
 EX: लड़कियाँ आ रही हैं। | laṛkiyā̃ ā rahī haĩ. | The girls are coming.
 Things agree too. बारिश bārish (rain) is feminine, so "it's raining" is बारिश हो रही है .
 EX: बारिश हो रही है। | bārish ho rahī hai. | It's raining.

### SECTION 3   (has 4)
Questions
 Question words stay where the answer goes, as before: आप क्या कर रहे हैं? For a yes-no question, put क्या at the start. With a friend, use तुम and हो : तुम क्या कर रहे हो? to a male friend, तुम क्या कर रही हो? to a female friend. The word अभी abhī , "right now", often goes with the continuous, but the verb form alone already says it.
 EX: क्या आप हिंदी सीख रहे हैं? | kyā āp hindī sīkh rahe haĩ? | Are you learning Hindi? (to a man)
CARDS NOW: क्या = what [card] || तुम = you (to a friend or a child) [card] || अभी = right now; just now [card] || क्या आप हिंदी सीख रहे हैं? = Are you learning Hindi? (to a man)

### SECTION 4   (has 3)
Saying no
 Put नहीं before the verb, as in the habitual. The final हूँ or है is often dropped.
 EX: मैं घर नहीं जा रहा। | maĩ ghar nahī̃ jā rahā. | I'm not going home. (a man speaking)
CARDS NOW: नहीं = no; not [card] || मैं घर नहीं जा रहा। = I'm not going home. (a man speaking) || घर = house; home [card]

### SECTION 5   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Verbs that don't take रहा
 English says "I know" and "I like it", never "I am knowing". Hindi agrees: जानना (to know) stays in the habitual, मैं जानता हूँ , and मुझे पसंद है stays as it is.
 Most common mistake: keeping रहा for everyone. About a woman: वह जा रहा है → वह जा रही है .
CARDS NOW: जानना = to know [card]

## LESSON was — 21 · था, थी, थे: was, and used to — deck "About me"
### SECTION 0   (has 6)
था agrees like an adjective
 English has two words for the past of "to be": "was" and "were". Hindi has four, and they follow the -ā / -e / -ī pattern you know from अच्छा : था thā , थे the , थी thī , and थीं thī̃ for several women or feminine things.
 ; Masculine ; Feminine ; 
 one ; था ; थी ; 
 several, and आप ; थे ; थीं ; 
 It agrees with the subject. So a woman says मैं घर पर थी , and you ask a man आप कहाँ थे? but a woman आप कहाँ थीं? 
 EX: मैं कल घर पर था। | maĩ kal ghar par thā. | I was at home yesterday. (a man speaking)
 EX: सारा कल बीमार थी। | sārā kal bīmār thī. | Sarah was sick yesterday.
 EX: आप कहाँ थे? | āp kahā̃ the? | Where were you? (to a man)
 EX: दुकानें बंद थीं। | dukānẽ band thī̃. | The shops were closed.
 With था , कल means "yesterday". The tense decides.
CARDS NOW: अच्छा = good; OK [card] || आप कहाँ थे? = Where were you? (to a man) || मैं कल घर पर था। = I was at home yesterday. (a man speaking) || कल = yesterday; tomorrow [card] || सारा कल बीमार थी। = Sarah was sick yesterday. || दुकानें बंद थीं। = The shops were closed.

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
How you felt
 "I was tired" is मैं थका हुआ था : थका हुआ thakā huā works like an adjective, so a woman says थकी हुई थी .
 EX: मैं बहुत थकी हुई थी। | maĩ bahut thakī huī thī. | I was very tired. (a woman speaking)
 Unchanging adjectives stay as they are, so only the verb shows who is speaking: मैं ख़ुश था (a man), मैं ख़ुश थी (a woman).
CARDS NOW: मैं बहुत थकी हुई थी। = I was very tired. (a woman speaking)

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Used to: -ता था
 English says "I used to live in Boston". Hindi takes the habitual and swaps हूँ / है for था : रहता था . Both parts agree: रहता था for a man, रहती थी for a woman, रहते थे for several people.
 EX: मैं पहले बोस्टन में रहता था। | maĩ pahle bosṭan mẽ rahtā thā. | I used to live in Boston. (a man speaking)
 EX: वह पहले बहुत चाय पीती थी। | vah pahle bahut chāy pītī thī. | She used to drink a lot of tea.
CARDS NOW: मैं पहले बोस्टन में रहता था। = I used to live in Boston. (a man speaking) [card] || वह पहले बहुत चाय पीती थी। = She used to drink a lot of tea.

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Was doing: रहा था
 The continuous works the same way: रहा हूँ becomes रहा था , "was doing".
 EX: हम टीवी देख रहे थे। | ham ṭīvī dekh rahe the. | We were watching TV.
CARDS NOW: हम टीवी देख रहे थे। = We were watching TV.

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Questions and short answers
 Questions work exactly as in the present: the question word goes where the answer goes, and a yes-no question starts with क्या . In a reply, the subject is often dropped, but the ending still shows who you are: a man answers जी, घर पर था , a woman जी, घर पर थी .
 EX: क्या वह बीमार थी? | kyā vah bīmār thī? | Was she sick?
CARDS NOW: क्या = what [card] || क्या वह बीमार थी? = Was she sick?

### SECTION 5   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Saying no

 One warning: "I went" or "I ate" is a different past, for finished events. It comes in a later lesson. This lesson is about states, habits and actions in progress.
 Most common mistake: using था for everyone. About a woman: वह बीमार था → वह बीमार थी .
CARDS NOW: नहीं = no; not [card]

## LESSON have — 22 · Having: मेरे पास, मेरा एक भाई — deck "Travel and hotels"
### SECTION 0   (has 9)
English uses "have" for everything: a ticket, a brother, a fever. Hindi has no verb "to have". It uses three patterns, depending on what you have.
 Things: मेरे पास
 For things you carry or own, Hindi says "near me is a ticket": मेरे पास टिकट है . के पास ke pās is a compound postposition, so मेरा takes its oblique form मेरे , and आपका becomes आपके .
 EX: मेरे पास टिकट है। | mere pās ṭikaṭ hai. | I have a ticket.
 EX: क्या आपके पास कमरा है? | kyā āpke pās kamrā hai? | Do you have a room?
 With a noun as the owner, the noun goes first and takes its oblique form too: मेरे भाई के पास , "my brother has".
 EX: मेरे भाई के पास गाड़ी है। | mere bhāī ke pās gāṛī hai. | My brother has a car.
 The verb agrees with the thing you have, not with you: one ticket है , two tickets हैं .
 EX: मेरे पास दो टिकट हैं। | mere pās do ṭikaṭ haĩ. | I have two tickets.
 EX: मेरे पास समय नहीं है। | mere pās samay nahī̃ hai. | I don't have time.
CARDS NOW: के पास = near; (in someone's possession) [card] || मेरा = my; mine [card] || आपका = your (polite) [card] || मेरे पास टिकट है। = I have a ticket. [card] || क्या आपके पास कमरा है? = Do you have a room? [card] || मेरे भाई के पास गाड़ी है। = My brother has a car. || हैं = are [card] || मेरे पास दो टिकट हैं। = I have two tickets. || मेरे पास समय नहीं है। = I don't have time.

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
People: मेरा एक भाई है
 Relatives and friends are not possessions, so they don't take के पास . Hindi says "my one brother is": मेरा एक भाई है . The possessive agrees with the person you have, as you learned with का / की / के .
 EX: मेरा एक भाई है। | merā ek bhāī hai. | I have a brother.
 EX: उसकी दो बेटियाँ हैं। | uskī do beṭiyā̃ haĩ. | She has two daughters.
 Friends work the same way: मेरा एक दोस्त है , "I have a friend", and मेरी एक दोस्त है if the friend is a woman.
 In the second example, उसकी is feminine because बेटियाँ is, whether "she" or "he" has them.
 The same pattern describes people's looks: Hindi says "her hair is long", उसके बाल लंबे हैं . बाल (hair) is masculine plural, so उसके and लंबे agree with it.
 EX: उसके बाल लंबे हैं। | uske bāl lambe haĩ. | She has long hair.
CARDS NOW: मेरा एक भाई है। = I have a brother. [card] || उसकी दो बेटियाँ हैं। = She has two daughters. || बाल = hair [card] || उसके बाल लंबे हैं। = She has long hair.

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Illnesses: मुझे … है
 A fever or a cold "is to me": मुझे बुख़ार है . Pain is "in" the body part: मेरे सिर में दर्द है , "there is pain in my head".
 EX: मुझे बुख़ार है। | mujhe buḵhār hai. | I have a fever.
 EX: मेरे सिर में दर्द है। | mere sir mẽ dard hai. | I have a headache.
CARDS NOW: मुझे बुख़ार है। = I have a fever. [card] || मेरे सिर में दर्द है। = I have a headache. [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 3)
Questions and no
 Add क्या at the start for a question, and नहीं before है for no. In the answer, है can drop: नहीं, मेरे पास टिकट नहीं .
 At a hotel desk or a ticket window you will hear आपके पास a lot. The short answer is simply जी हाँ, है ("yes, I do") or जी नहीं ("no, I don't"): Hindi repeats है where English repeats "do".
 EX: आपके पास पासपोर्ट है? | āpke pās pāsporṭ hai? | Do you have a passport?
 Most common mistake: translating "I have" word for word. मैं एक गाड़ी है → मेरे पास एक गाड़ी है . For family, use मेरा एक भाई है .
CARDS NOW: क्या = what [card] || नहीं = no; not [card] || आपके पास पासपोर्ट है? = Do you have a passport?

## LESSON infinitive — 23 · The -ना form: want to, have to, should — deck "Health and help"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
The -ना form as a noun
 You know the infinitive from word lists: सीखना sīkhnā , "to learn". It can also be the subject of a sentence, like English "learning" in "Learning Hindi isn't hard".
 EX: हिंदी सीखना मुश्किल नहीं है। | hindī sīkhnā mushkil nahī̃ hai. | Learning Hindi isn't hard.
CARDS NOW: सीखना = to learn [card] || हिंदी सीखना मुश्किल नहीं है। = Learning Hindi isn't hard.

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
Want to: -ना चाहना
 "I want to learn" is the -ना form plus चाहना chāhnā (to want) in the habitual. The -ना form never changes. चाहता / चाहती agrees with the speaker, like any habitual verb.
 EX: मैं हिंदी सीखना चाहती हूँ। | maĩ hindī sīkhnā chāhtī hū̃. | I want to learn Hindi. (a woman speaking)
 EX: मैं घर जाना चाहता हूँ। | maĩ ghar jānā chāhtā hū̃. | I want to go home. (a man speaking)
CARDS NOW: चाहना = to want [card] || मैं हिंदी सीखना चाहती हूँ। = I want to learn Hindi. (a woman speaking) || मैं घर जाना चाहता हूँ। = I want to go home. (a man speaking)

### SECTION 2   (has 5)
Have to: मुझे -ना है
 English says "I have to go". Hindi says "to me, going is": मुझे जाना है . The person takes the को form, just like मुझे चाहिए : मुझे , आपको , राहुल को .
 EX: मुझे जाना है। | mujhe jānā hai. | I have to go.
 EX: मुझे काम करना है। | mujhe kām karnā hai. | I have to work.
 Compare this with चाहिए on its own: मुझे पानी चाहिए is "I need water", a thing. मुझे जाना है is "I have to go", an action. Both put the person in the को form.
CARDS NOW: मुझे = me; to me [card] || चाहिए = needed; should [card] || आपको = you; to you (polite) [card] || मुझे जाना है। = I have to go. || मुझे काम करना है। = I have to work.

### SECTION 3   (has 3)
Should: -ना चाहिए
 Add चाहिए chāhie instead of है and the meaning becomes "should". Again the person takes the को form.
 EX: आपको आराम करना चाहिए। | āpko ārām karnā chāhie. | You should rest.

### SECTION 1   (has 11)
ने: when the verb takes an object
 Here Hindi does something English never does. With a verb that takes an object (eat something, see something, read something), the subject gets ने ne , a postposition. Pronouns join it and change form: मैंने , आपने , उसने , हमने , उन्होंने unhõne . Names stay as they are: सारा ने .

## LESSON conjunctions — 26 · और, लेकिन, क्योंकि: joining ideas — deck "Questions and connectors"
### SECTION 0   (has 8)
Good news: Hindi joins sentences much as English does. The joining word goes between the two parts, and each part keeps its own verb at the end.
 और, लेकिन, या
 और aur is "and", लेकिन lekin is "but", and या yā is "or". In everyday speech पर also means "but". You know पर as the postposition "on"; when it means "but", it stands between two sentences.
 EX: मुझे चाय पसंद है, और कॉफ़ी भी। | mujhe chāy pasand hai, aur kŏfī bhī. | I like tea, and coffee too.

 EX: मैं थका हुआ हूँ, लेकिन ख़ुश हूँ। | maĩ thakā huā hū̃, lekin ḵhush hū̃. | I'm tired but happy. (a man speaking)
 लेकिन is safe in any situation. पर sounds more casual. Both can join a short afterthought too, as in English "I'll come, but late".
 EX: मैं आऊँगा, पर देर से। | maĩ āū̃gā, par der se. | I'll come, but late. (a man speaking)
 और joins two nouns ( चाय और कॉफ़ी ) or two whole sentences. Between sentences it simply adds one idea to another.

### SECTION 1   (has 5)
Because and so
 These two point in opposite directions, and English speakers often swap them.
 क्योंकि kyõki ("because") introduces the cause. The क्योंकि part usually comes after the main sentence, as in English.
 इसलिए islie ("so, that's why") introduces the result. The cause comes first.
 EX: मैं आज घर पर हूँ क्योंकि मुझे बुख़ार है। | maĩ āj ghar par hū̃ kyõki mujhe buḵhār hai. | I'm at home today because I have a fever.
 EX: मुझे बुख़ार है, इसलिए मैं घर पर हूँ। | mujhe buḵhār hai, islie maĩ ghar par hū̃. | I have a fever, so I'm at home.
 Both sentences say the same thing. The fever always goes with क्योंकि or before इसलिए .
 When someone asks क्यों? ("why?"), the answer usually starts with क्योंकि , just as an English answer starts with "because".

CARDS NOW: तो = then; so [card] || अच्छा, तो कल मिलेंगे। = OK, so we'll meet tomorrow.

## LESSON directions — 27 · Directions and travel — deck "Getting around"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
Asking the way
 Start politely with माफ़ कीजिए or भैया / दीदी , then ask where something is or how far it is.
 EX: स्टेशन कहाँ है? | sṭeshan kahā̃ hai? | Where is the station?
 EX: स्टेशन कितनी दूर है? | sṭeshan kitnī dūr hai? | How far is the station?
CARDS NOW: स्टेशन कहाँ है? = Where is the station? [card] || स्टेशन कितनी दूर है? = How far is the station? || कितनी दूर है? = How far is it? [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
Left, right, straight
 Directions use the polite command form you already know. सीधे sīdhe is "straight", बाएँ bāẽ "left", दाएँ dāẽ "right", and मुड़िए muṛie "turn".
 EX: सीधे जाइए। | sīdhe jāie. | Go straight.
 EX: बाएँ मुड़िए। | bāẽ muṛie. | Turn left.
 To a driver or a stranger, the -इए form is polite enough on its own. कृपया belongs on signs and announcements, not in these everyday requests.
CARDS NOW: दाएँ मुड़िए। = Turn right. [card] || सीधे जाइए। = Go straight. [card] || बाएँ मुड़िए। = Turn left. [card] || कृपया = please (formal) [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 9)
Near, in front of, behind
 English puts these words before the noun: "near the station". Hindi puts them after it, like every postposition: स्टेशन के पास . They all start with के .
 English ; Hindi ; 
 near the station ; स्टेशन के पास ;

 behind the shop ; दुकान के पीछे ; 
 EX: होटल मेट्रो स्टेशन के पास है। | hoṭal meṭro sṭeshan ke pās hai. | The hotel is near the metro station.
 EX: दवाई की दुकान बैंक के सामने है। | davāī kī dukān baink ke sāmne hai. | The pharmacy is across from the bank.

Understanding the answer
 The reply will often use a few words you can learn as chunks: यहाँ से (from here), थोड़ा आगे (a little further on) and दूर (far) with a time. Hindi says "five minutes far" where English says "five minutes away". Many people point while they speak, so watch their hands too, and don't be shy about asking again: फिर से बताइए , "please say that again".

## LESSON routine — 28 · Daily routine and time phrases — deck "Time, days and routine"
### SECTION 0   (has 6)
What time is it?
 English says "o'clock". Hindi uses बजे baje , from बजना (to strike, to ring). Ask कितने बजे हैं? In casual speech people also say क्या टाइम है? 
 EX: कितने बजे हैं? | kitne baje haĩ? | What time is it?
 EX: तीन बजे हैं। | tīn baje haĩ. | It's three o'clock.
 One o'clock is singular, so it agrees in the singular: एक बजा है . Every other hour is plural: दो बजे हैं .
 EX: एक बजा है। | ek bajā hai. | It's one o'clock.
 For "at" a time, just say the time with बजे : सात बजे , "at seven".
 Hindi rarely says "a.m." or "p.m.". It adds the part of the day instead: सुबह सात बजे , शाम सात बजे . For other minutes, say the hour with बजकर and then the minutes: पाँच बजकर दस मिनट , ten past five.
 EX: शाम सात बजे | shām sāt baje | at seven in the evening
CARDS NOW: सुबह आठ बजे = at eight in the morning [card] || बजे = o'clock [card] || कितने बजे हैं? = What time is it? [card] || तीन बजे हैं। = It's three o'clock. || एक बजा है। = It's one o'clock. [card] || शाम सात बजे = at seven in the evening

### SECTION 2   (has 17)
Days and parts of the day
 The days are सोमवार , मंगलवार , बुधवार , गुरुवार , शुक्रवार , शनिवार and रविवार (Monday to Sunday). English says "on Monday"; Hindi uses the postposition को after the day: सोमवार को somvār ko .
 Many people also call Sunday इतवार , and a week is हफ़्ता , a masculine word. In the course we use the names above.
 EX: मैं सोमवार को ऑफ़िस जाता हूँ। | maĩ somvār ko ŏfis jātā hū̃. | I go to the office on Mondays. (a man speaking)
 The parts of the day are सुबह subah (morning), दोपहर dopahar (afternoon), शाम shām (evening) and रात rāt (night). People usually say शाम को and रात को ; सुबह often stands alone.

### SECTION 3   (has 8)
Your day
 A routine is a habit, so use the habitual, agreeing with the speaker.
 EX: मैं रोज़ सात बजे उठती हूँ। | maĩ roz sāt baje uṭhtī hū̃. | I get up at seven every day. (a woman speaking)
 To put the steps in order, use पहले (first) and फिर (then). For how often, use रोज़ (every day), कभी-कभी (sometimes), हमेशा (always) or हर (every): हर सोमवार , "every Monday", works with or without को .
 EX: पहले मैं चाय पीता हूँ, फिर नहाता हूँ। | pahle maĩ chāy pītā hū̃, phir nahātā hū̃. | First I drink tea, then I take a shower. (a man speaking)
 To ask at what time someone does something, use कितने बजे before the verb. To a woman, the verb is उठती हैं .
 EX: आप कितने बजे उठते हैं? | āp kitne baje uṭhte haĩ? | What time do you get up? (to a man)
