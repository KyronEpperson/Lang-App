## LESSON vowels — 1 · Vowels and spelling — deck "Sounds and spelling"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
Every Dutch phrase here has a pronunciation line in English letters. One syllable stays in small letters. A longer word puts the stressed syllable in CAPITALS. The line is an aid, not a measurement.
 Short, long, and no glide
 maan is not the English word name, and been is not the English word seen. A short vowel sits in a closed syllable, one that ends in a consonant: man . In a closed syllable a long vowel is written with two letters: maan .

 aa, or open a ; aah ; maan ; 
 ie ; ee ; fiets ; 
 oo, or open o ; oh ; boom ; 
 oe ; oo ; boek ;

 uu ; ee, rounded ; muur ; 
 EX: de man | duh mahn | the man
 EX: de maan | duh maahn | the moon
 EX: de fiets | duh feets | the bike
 ie is the ee of see. oe is the vowel of food, not the oh of boom . Short u in rug is rounded and forward, not the u of but or book. Long uu in muur is that ee with rounded lips.
 EX: de muur | duh muur | the wall
CARDS NOW: de man = the man [card] || de maan = the moon [card] || de fiets = the bike [card] || de muur = the wall [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 5)
One letter or two
 mannen , the men, doubles the n so the a in man stays short. manen , the moons, is ma-nen: one a in an open syllable, so the a is long. wonen , to live somewhere, has one long o for the same reason. nemen , to take, has one long e: a steady ay, not the ee of see. naam uses that long aa, with no English glide.
 EX: de mannen | duh MAH-nuh | the men
 EX: de manen | duh MAAH-nuh | the moons
 EX: wonen | WOH-nuh | to live (somewhere)
 EX: de naam | duh naahm | the name
CARDS NOW: wonen = to live (somewhere) [card] || nemen = to take [card] || de mannen = the men [card] || de manen = the moons [card] || de naam = the name [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 5)
What the line leaves out
 ui and eu need rounded lips. The line writes uy and euh . The next lesson trains them. p , t and k have no puff of air. r may be a tap, a throat r, or an English-like r after a vowel. Do not start with an English r. The line writes r. w is the lower lip on the teeth, not an English w. At the start of a word, v and z often have little voice in the north and west. The line still writes them. Either way is understood. Unstressed -ig is a light kh. The line drops the n of -en : werken , to work, is WEHR-kuh. The voice may keep the n. That is not an error.
 EX: werken | WEHR-kuh | to work
 de and het both mean "the". The card stores which one a noun takes. de man takes de. het huis takes het.
 EX: het huis | uht huys | the house
 Most common mistake: reading maan like English "name" → maan with a long steady ah and no glide.
CARDS NOW: en = and [card] || werken = to work [card] || de = the (de-words, all plurals) [card] || het = it [card] || het huis = the house [card]

## LESSON sounds — 2 · The hard sounds — deck "Hard sounds"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
A handful of Dutch spellings do not match English at all. The pronunciation line writes them with the same letters every time. Say the Dutch sound, not the English letter name.
 g and ch are one sound
 In this accent, g and ch are the same sound: the kh of Scottish loch , not the g of English good . goed starts with that kh. lachen , to laugh, has it in the middle. acht , eight, ends with it. The line writes kh for both letters.
 EX: goed | khoot | good
 EX: acht | ahkht | eight
 EX: lachen | LAH-khuh | to laugh
 In Flanders and the south of the Netherlands this sound is softer. This course uses the harder sound of the northern news accent. Both are standard.
CARDS NOW: goed = good [card] || lachen = to laugh [card] || acht = eight [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 7)
ui, eu, ij, oe, ou
 ui has no English word. Start open, then round your lips. huis has it, and so does a lot of everyday vocabulary. eu is the vowel of French deux , with rounded lips and no y-glide. deur has it. The line writes ui as uy and eu as euh . Those letters are a reminder, not a picture.
 EX: het huis | uht huys | the house
 EX: de deur | duh deuhr | the door
 ij and ei are the same sound, wider than English eye . The line writes igh . You learn the spelling with the word: klein is ei, and it means small. oe is one sound, the vowel of food, not an oh plus another vowel. de hoed , the hat, has it, and the final d sounds like t. ou and au are the vowel of cow . The line writes ow . koud means cold. blauw means blue.
 EX: klein | klighn | small
 EX: de hoed | duh hoot | the hat
 EX: koud | kowt | cold
CARDS NOW: het huis = the house [card] || de deur = the door [card] || klein = small [card] || de hoed = the hat [card] || Au! = Ouch! [card] || koud = cold [card] || blauw = blue [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 5)
sch, and a quiet ending
 sch is s plus that kh. It is never sk, and it is never sh. school starts that way.
 EX: de school | duh skhohl | the school
 At the end of a word, d sounds like t, and b sounds like p. The spelling stays d or b, because the voiced sound comes back when a vowel follows. hond , the dog, sounds as if it ended in t. honden , the dogs, brings the d back. Do not respell the singular as hont.
 EX: de hond | duh hawnt | the dog
 EX: de honden | duh HAWN-duh | the dogs
 de and het both mean "the". het huis takes het. de deur , de school and de hond take de. The card stores the one that belongs to the noun.
 Most common mistake: saying goed with the g of English "good" → goed with the kh of Scottish "loch".
CARDS NOW: de school = the school [card] || de = the (de-words, all plurals) [card] || de hond = the dog [card] || de honden = the dogs || het = it [card]

## LESSON greetings — 3 · Greetings and how are you — deck "Greetings and courtesy"
### SECTION 0   (has 6)
Hallo works at any hour. Friends also say Hoi . Dag is hello or goodbye. The line stresses Hallo on the first syllable. Many speakers stress the second. Either is understood.
 EX: Hallo. | HAH-loh | Hello.
 EX: Dag. | dahkh | Hello or goodbye.
 Morning, afternoon, evening, night
 Each time-of-day greeting is one word. Goedemorgen is good morning. Goedemiddag is good afternoon, from about noon. Goedenavond is good evening, after dark, as a hello. Goedenacht is a goodbye at night, while you are still up. When someone is going to sleep, you will also hear welterusten . The line often stresses these words at the start. Speech often stresses the time-of-day part. Do not treat the capitals as a measurement.
 EX: Goedemorgen. | KHOO-duh-mawr-khuh | Good morning.
CARDS NOW: Hallo. = Hello. [card] || Dag. = Hello or goodbye. [card] || Goedemorgen. = Good morning. [card] || Goedemiddag. = Good afternoon. [card] || Goedenavond. = Good evening. [card] || Goedenacht. = Good night. [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 8)
How are you?
 Dutch asks how it goes, and puts the verb second, right after Hoe : Hoe gaat het? Not Hoe het gaat? A friend can add met je . A stranger, shop staff, someone older, or the town hall gets Hoe gaat het met u? 
 EX: Hoe gaat het? | hoo khaaht uht | How are you?
 Do not answer Ik ben goed . Dutch uses het gaat . Het gaat goed means I'm fine. Het gaat wel is softer: I'm all right. Goed, dank je is fine, thanks, to a friend.
 EX: Het gaat goed. | uht khaaht khoot | I'm fine.
 EX: Het gaat wel. | uht khaaht wehl | I'm all right.
 EX: Goed, dank je. | khoot dahnk yuh | Fine, thanks.
CARDS NOW: Hoe? = How? [card] || Hoe gaat het? = How are you? [card] || Hoe gaat het met u? = How are you? (formal) [card] || Het gaat goed. = I'm fine. [card] || Het gaat wel. = I'm all right. [card] || Dank je wel. = Thank you. (informal) [card] || Dank u wel. = Thank you. (formal) [card] || Goed, dank je. = Fine, thanks. [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 8)
je for a friend, u when it matters
 English has one you. je is for friends, family, and people your age. jij is that word when you stress it. u is for a stranger, staff, someone older, and any time you are not sure. Even Dutch speakers hesitate. If you are not sure, use u . Bedankt means thank you and does not choose.
 ; To a friend ; To a stranger ; 
 Thank you. ; Dank je wel. ; Dank u wel. ; 
 Please, or here you are. ; Alsjeblieft. ; Alstublieft. ; 
 How are you? ; Hoe gaat het? ; Hoe gaat het met u? ; 
 EX: Dank je wel. | dahnk yuh wehl | Thank you. (to a friend)
 EX: Dank u wel. | dahnk uu wehl | Thank you. (to a stranger)
 EX: Alstublieft. | ahl-stue-BLEEFT | Here you are, or please. (to a stranger)
 Alsjeblieft is the friend form. In normal speech the s and the j run together into sh, and the stress is on the last syllable, as the line shows. Graag gedaan means you're welcome. Pardon is excuse me. Stress it on the second syllable. Tot ziens is a neutral goodbye, and Doei is bye to a friend.
 EX: Alsjeblieft. | ahl-shuh-BLEEFT | Here you are, or please. (to a friend)
 EX: Graag gedaan. | khraahkh khuh-DAAHN | You're welcome.
 Most common mistake: Ik ben goed → Het gaat goed .

(formal) [card] || Graag gedaan. = You're welcome. [card] || Pardon. = Excuse me. [card] || Tot ziens. = Goodbye. [card] || Doei. = Bye. (informal) [card]

## LESSON intro — 4 · Introducing yourself — deck "About me"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
Your name
 English says my name is. Dutch for that introduction is Ik heet , then your name. Ik heet Anna. Ik ben Anna , I'm Anna, also works when you meet someone. You will also hear Mijn naam is Anna. Ask a friend Hoe heet je? Ask a stranger Hoe heet u? The verb already ends in t, so both questions look the same at heet .
 EX: Ik heet Anna. | ihk hayt AH-nah | My name is Anna.
 EX: Hoe heet je? | hoo hayt yuh | What's your name? (to a friend)
 EX: Hoe heet u? | hoo hayt uu | What's your name? (to a stranger)
CARDS NOW: heet = hot (very) [card] || Ik heet Anna. = My name is Anna. [card] || Hoe heet je? = What's your name? (informal) [card] || Hoe heet u? = What's your name? (formal) [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 11)
Where you are from, where you live
 Origin takes uit . Ik kom uit Nederland. English puts the before the Netherlands. Dutch does not. A city works the same way: Ik kom uit Amsterdam. Ask a friend Waar kom je vandaan? The verb is second, right after Waar , and vandaan stays at the end. A stranger keeps a t: Waar komt u vandaan? 
 EX: Ik kom uit Nederland. | ihk kawm uyt NAY-duhr-lahnt | I come from the Netherlands.
 EX: Waar kom je vandaan? | waahr kawm yuh vahn-DAAHN | Where are you from? (to a friend)
 EX: Waar komt u vandaan? | waahr kawmt uu vahn-DAAHN | Where are you from? (to a stranger)
 Where you live takes woon and in : Ik woon in Utrecht. Amsterdam, Rotterdam, Den Haag and Groningen use the same shape. Den Haag is The Hague, two words, with no de or het in front. Ik ben in Utrecht means you are there now, not that you live there. Start with Ik and the verb is next: Ik woon in Utrecht , not Ik in Utrecht woon .
 EX: Ik woon in Utrecht. | ihk wohn ihn UU-trehkht | I live in Utrecht.
 EX: Ik woon in Den Haag. | ihk wohn ihn dehn haahkh | I live in The Hague.
CARDS NOW: uit = out of; from [card] || Ik kom uit Nederland. = I come from the Netherlands. [card] || ik = I [card] || Ik kom uit Amsterdam. = I come from Amsterdam. [card] || Waar kom je vandaan? = Where are you from? (informal) [card] || waar = where [card] || Waar komt u vandaan? = Where are you from? (formal) [card] || Ik woon in Rotterdam. = I live in Rotterdam. [card] || in = in [card] || Ik woon in Utrecht. = I live in Utrecht. [card] || Ik woon in Den Haag. = I live in The Hague. [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 9)
Study and a little Dutch
 Ik studeer means I study. Ik spreek een beetje Nederlands means I speak a little Dutch. een here means a, said uhn. Language names take a capital: Nederlands , Engels . A friend: Spreek je Engels? A stranger: Spreekt u Engels? When je comes after the verb, the verb has no t. With u the t stays. A role takes no article: Ik ben student means I'm a student.
 EX: Ik studeer. | ihk stuu-DAYR | I study.

 EX: Spreek je Engels? | sprayk yuh EHNG-uhls | Do you speak English? (to a friend)

## LESSON zijn-hebben — 5 · zijn and hebben — deck "Feelings and states"
### SECTION 0   (has 9)
English uses to be for almost every feeling. Dutch splits the work. zijn is to be. hebben is to have. With ik , those are ik ben and ik heb . A lot of "I am" sentences in English are ik heb in Dutch.
 ik, je, and u
 ; zijn, to be ; hebben, to have ; 
 ik ; ik ben ; ik heb ; 
 jij, before the verb ; jij bent ; jij hebt ; 
 je, after the verb ; ben je ; heb je ; 
 u ; u bent ; u hebt, or u heeft ; 
 a name ; Anna is ; Anna heeft ; 
 When je follows the verb, the form shortens: ben je , heb je . u does not shorten. For to have, both u hebt and u heeft are used. This course accepts both. A name takes is and heeft : Anna is moe , Anna heeft honger .
 EX: Ben je moe? | behn yuh moo | Are you tired?
 EX: U heeft honger. | uu hayft HAWNG-uhr | You are hungry. (formal)
 EX: Anna is moe. | AH-nah ihs moo | Anna is tired.
CARDS NOW: zijn = to be [card] || hebben = to have [card] || ik = I [card] || Ik ben moe. = I'm tired. [card] || Ik heb honger. = I'm hungry. [card] || je = you (one person, friendly) [card] || Anna is moe. = Anna is tired. || Ben je moe? = Are you tired? || U heeft honger. = You are hungry. (formal)

### SECTION 1   (has 9)
Fine, tired, hungry, cold
 You already answer Hoe gaat het? with Het gaat goed or the softer Het gaat wel . Do not switch that answer to ik ben . Tired uses zijn : Ik ben moe. Sick uses zijn too: Ik ben ziek. Hungry uses hebben : Ik heb honger. Thirsty is the same verb: Ik heb dorst. There is no het in those two.
 EX: Het gaat goed. | uht khaaht khoot | I'm fine.
 EX: Ik ben moe. | ihk behn moo | I'm tired.
 EX: Ik ben ziek. | ihk behn zeek | I'm sick.
 EX: Ik heb honger. | ihk hehp HAWNG-uhr | I'm hungry.
 EX: Ik heb dorst. | ihk hehp dawrst | I'm thirsty.
 Feeling cold is Ik heb het koud. Ik ben koud is not that feeling. It describes a cold manner, or a thing that is cold to the touch. Feeling hot is Ik heb het warm. The cards add more feelings. The note on each card tells you whether that word takes zijn or hebben . Sleepy, for example, is Ik heb slaap , while tired stays Ik ben moe .
 EX: Ik heb het koud. | ihk hehp uht kowt | I'm cold.
 EX: Ik heb het warm. | ihk hehp uht wahrm | I feel hot.
 If you start with Ik , the verb is the next word. Ik ben moe , not Ik moe ben . Ik heb honger , not Ik honger heb . The question puts the verb first: Ben je moe? Heb je honger? 
 Most common mistake: Ik ben koud → Ik heb het koud .
CARDS NOW: Hoe gaat het? = How are you? [card] || het = it [card] || Het gaat goed. = I'm fine. [card] || Het gaat wel. = I'm all right. [card] || Ik ben ziek. = I'm sick. [card] || Ik heb dorst. = I'm thirsty. [card] || Ik heb het koud. = I'm cold. [card] || Ik heb het warm. = I feel hot. [card] || Ik heb slaap. = I'm sleepy. [card]

## LESSON de-het — 6 · de and het — deck "People and family"
### SECTION 0   (has 14)
de and het both mean "the". They do not differ in meaning. Nothing in the meaning, and nothing in the English, tells you which noun takes which. About two thirds of Dutch nouns take de . That still does not tell you the next noun. You memorize the small word with the noun, the way the card stores it.
 People
 A person you can point at is usually a de-word. That is common gender. Men and women both take de , so do not sort people into masculine and feminine. de man , de vrouw , de jongen , de vader , de moeder , de broer , de zus , de zoon and de dochter all take de. zus is the everyday word for sister.
 EX: de vrouw | duh vrow | the woman
 EX: de jongen | duh YAWNG-uh | the boy
 EX: de moeder | duh MOO-duhr | the mother
 Three family words take het . They are neuter, het-words. het kind is the child. het meisje is the girl: a word ending in -je is a small form, and those take het. het gezin is the family at home, the parents and the children. People are not all de-words.
 EX: het kind | uht kihnt | the child
 EX: het meisje | uht MIGH-shuh | the girl
 EX: het gezin | uht khuh-ZIHN | the family at home
CARDS NOW: de = the (de-words, all plurals) [card] || het = it [card] || de man = the man [card] || de vrouw = the woman [card] || de jongen = the boy [card] || de moeder = the mother || de broer = the brother [card] || de zus = the sister [card] || de zoon = the son [card] || de dochter = the daughter [card] || het kind = the child [card] || het meisje = the girl [card] || je = you (one person, friendly) [card] || het gezin = the family at home [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 6)
a, and the number one
 een means a or an, said uhn. een huis is a house. het huis is the house. When you mean a, do not put het in front. The number one is a different word, één , with an accent, said ayn. Bare een is the article. een vriend is a male friend, and the final d sounds like t. een vriendin is a female friend when you mean a. de vriendin is the form when you mean the. Say the small word the card shows.
 EX: een huis | uhn huys | a house
 EX: een vriend | uhn vreent | a (male) friend
 A plural takes de , even when the singular took het. The plural lesson shows the endings. Until then, store the singular article on the card.
 Most common mistake: de huis → het huis .
CARDS NOW: een = a; an [card] || een huis = a house || het huis = the house [card] || één = one (the number) [card] || een vriend = a (male) friend [card] || een vriendin = a (female) friend [card]

## LESSON present — 7 · The present tense — deck "Work and school"
### SECTION 0   (has 16)
The dictionary form ends in -en . werken is to work, wonen is to live, studeren is to study. Drop -en and you have the base. With ik you use that base: ik werk , ik woon , ik studeer . woon keeps a double o, because the o is long and the syllable is now closed.
 The t, and where it drops
 jij , hij and zij for she add t . jij werkt . She is zij werkt . u adds t too, and u never drops it, whether u comes before the verb or after. When je or jij comes after the verb, the t drops. A friend question is Werk je hier? A stranger question is Werkt u hier? Woon je in Utrecht? drops the t. Woont u in Utrecht? keeps it. If the base already ends in t, you do not add a second one: jij heet . The drop is invisible on heet , so watch it on werk .
 EX: Werk je hier? | wehrk yuh heer | Do you work here? (to a friend)
 EX: Werkt u hier? | wehrkt uu heer | Do you work here? (to a stranger)
 EX: Woon je in Utrecht? | wohn yuh ihn UU-trehkht | Do you live in Utrecht? (to a friend)
 EX: Woont u in Utrecht? | wohnt uu ihn UU-trehkht | Do you live in Utrecht? (to a stranger)

[card] || Ik studeer. = I study. [card] || jij = you (stressed) [card] || hij = he [card] || zij = she; they (stressed) [card] || je = you (one person, friendly) [card] || Werk je hier? = Do you work here? [card] || Werkt u hier? = Do you work here? (formal) [card] || Woon je in Utrecht? = Do you live in Utrecht? (to a friend) || Woont u in Utrecht? = Do you live in Utrecht? (to a stranger) || heet = hot (very) [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 13)
More than one person

## LESSON pronouns — 8 · Pronouns and possessives — deck "People and family"
### SECTION 0   (has 22)
English you is one word. Dutch splits the person, and it also splits she from they.

## LESSON adjectives — 26 · Adjective -e — deck "Shopping and clothes"
### SECTION 0   (has 21)
English big never changes. Dutch big sometimes gains an -e , and sometimes it does not. The noun decides. Auto is a de-word. Huis is a het-word. You already know both articles.
 ; het-word: huis ; de-word: auto ;

 Four places to look
 Before a de-word, add -e . That is true with een and with de : een grote auto , de grote auto . Jas is a de-word too, so a red coat is een rode jas and the red coat is de rode jas . Before a het-word with een or geen , there is no -e : een groot huis , geen groot huis . Before a het-word with het , dit or dat , the -e comes back: het grote huis , dit grote huis . After is or zijn , the adjective is not in front of the noun, so there is no -e . One house or many: Het huis is groot , De huizen zijn groot .
 EX: de rode jas | duh ROH-duh yahs | the red coat
 EX: geen groot huis | khayn khroht huys | not a big house
 EX: dit grote huis | diht KHROH-tuh huys | this big house
 EX: De huizen zijn groot. | duh HUY-zuh zighn khroht | The houses are big.
 In front of a plural, the adjective takes -e , and the article is de : de grote huizen . A possessive behaves like het , not like een . Mijn grote huis keeps the -e , even though huis is a het-word.
 EX: de grote huizen | duh KHROH-tuh HUY-zuh | the big houses
 EX: mijn grote huis | mighn KHROH-tuh huys | my big house

With het , the form with -e is always acceptable: het moeilijke boek , the difficult book. After een , the rule does not change: een moeilijk boek , with no -e . This course does not practice the form without -e after het .

## LESSON omdat — 28 · omdat and dat — deck "Questions and connectors"
### SECTION 0   (has 14)
English says because I am tired, and the verb stays in its usual place. Dutch says omdat ik moe ben , and the verb goes to the end. Omdat means because. Dat , in this lesson, means that, the word that starts a thought. The same spelling dat is also the far word for a het-word, as in dat boek . The joining word does not change for de or het. The pointing word does.
 EX: Ik ga naar huis omdat ik moe ben. | ihk khaah naahr huys awm-DAHT ihk moo behn | I'm going home because I am tired.
 EX: Ik denk dat hij in Utrecht woont. | ihk dehnk daht high ihn UU-trehkht wohnt | I think he lives in Utrecht.
 The finite verb at the end
 The verb that moves is the finite one, the one that shows the person: ben , woont , heb , is . It goes to the end of the omdat clause or the dat clause. Denk means think. Ik denk , jij denkt , hij denkt . When je follows the verb, the -t drops, the same way it drops in werk je : denk je . In Ik denk dat hij in Utrecht woont , woont is at the end. The main clause does not change. Ik denk and Ik ga stay in second place.
 EX: Ik denk dat het huis groot is. | ihk dehnk daht uht huys khroht ihs | I think the house is big.
 EX: Ik neem de trein omdat ik geen fiets heb. | ihk naym duh trighn awm-DAHT ihk khayn feets hehp | I'm taking the train because I don't have a bike.
 EX: Ik denk dat de trein te duur is. | ihk dehnk daht duh trighn tuh duur ihs | I think the train is too expensive.
 One finite verb in the omdat clause is enough. Heb in omdat ik geen fiets heb is at the end. Fiets is a de-word, and geen is the word you already use for not a. After is , groot and te duur still take no -e .
