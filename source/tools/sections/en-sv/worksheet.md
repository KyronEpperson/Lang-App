# Worksheet: en-sv

## LESSON vowels — 1 · Vowels: long, short, and å ä ö u y — deck "Sounds and spelling"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Under every Swedish phrase in this course there is a pronunciation line in English letters. The syllable in CAPITALS is the stressed one. A one-syllable word is all lowercase. The line does not mark pitch accent, the rise or fall on a word. Stress, and long versus short, matter more. Do not add tone marks.
 Long vowel, short consonant
 English vowels slide. Swedish vowels stay put. In a stressed syllable, either the vowel is long and the consonant after it is short, or the vowel is short and the consonant is long. A doubled letter is that long consonant.
 EX: mat | maht | food
 EX: matt | mahtt | matte, dull
 mat (food) is a long a and a short t . matt is a short a and a long t . The aid writes ah for both. Length sits in the consonant: maht versus mahtt . A few small words, such as han (he), stay short with one n .
CARDS NOW: mat = food [card] || matt = matte, dull [card]

 här , hår and har are three words. Type the ring and the dots.

## LESSON sounds — 2 · The sj-sound, the tj-sound, and soft k and g — deck "Sounds and spelling"
### SECTION 0   (has 8)
English has one "sh" and pours several Swedish sounds into it. Swedish keeps two. The aid writes them sj and tj . If you merge them, seven and twenty start the same way, and they are not the same word.
 sj is not tj
 The sj-sound is the one in sju (seven), skjorta (shirt) and stjärna (star). The tj-sound is the one in tjugo (twenty), köpa (to buy) and kyrka (church). An English "sh" is close enough to be understood for the sj-sound. It is not an excuse to use it for both.
 EX: sju | sjueh | seven
 EX: tjugo | TJUEH-gaw | twenty
 EX: en skjorta | ayn SJAWRT-ah | a shirt
 EX: en stjärna | ayn SJAERN-ah | a star
 EX: köpa | TJEUH-pah | to buy
 EX: en kyrka | ayn TJEWR-kah | a church
 j by itself is the y of "yes", never the j of "jam". The aid writes that j , not a y .
 EX: ja | yah | yes
 A k at the start of a word, immediately before e , i , y , ä or ö , is usually the tj-sound. That is why köpa and kyrka do not start with a hard k . A g in that same spot is the j of "yes".
 EX: göra | YEUH-rah | to do
CARDS NOW: sju = seven [card] || tjugo = twenty [card] || köpa = to buy [card] || en skjorta = a shirt [card] || en stjärna = a star [card] || en kyrka = a church [card] || ja = yes [card] || göra = to do; to make [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
Hard on purpose
 Two common words do not follow that soft- k habit. kille (a guy) keeps a hard k . kex (a cracker, a thin crisp cookie) is listed with both a hard k and the tj-sound. This course uses the Central Swedish hard k . You will hear the tj-sound too, especially in Götaland, southern Sweden. Both are real Swedish. Do not "correct" a soft kex in someone else's mouth.
 EX: en kille | ayn KIHL-eh | a guy
 EX: ett kex | eht kehks | a cracker
 sk becomes the sj-sound only when a front vowel is the next letter: e , i , y , ä or ö . skola (school) keeps sk , because o is not one of those vowels. The word for station uses the sj-sound in the -tion ending, with the stress on the ooh .
 EX: en skola | ayn SKOOH-lah | a school
 EX: en station | ayn stah-SJOOHN | a station
 Swedish words also have a pitch accent, one rise or one fall. The aid skips it on purpose. Stress and long versus short matter more. You will not drill accent 1 against accent 2.
 The nouns still bring their small word. skjorta , stjärna , kyrka , kille , skola and station take en . kex takes ett .
 Most common mistake: starting sju and tjugo with the same "sh". sju with "shoo" → sjueh . tjugo with that same start → TJUEH-gaw .
CARDS NOW: en kille = a guy [card] || ett kex = a cracker [card] || en skola = a school [card] || en station = a station

## LESSON greetings — 3 · Greetings: hej, tack, hej då — deck "Greetings and courtesy"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
Hej works with anyone, at any hour. It is the ordinary hello. You do not save it for friends, and you do not switch it off after noon.
 EX: Hej! | hey | Hello!
 Morning, evening, night
 When you want the time of day in the greeting, Swedish uses god plus the time word. The vowel in god is ooh , the vowel of "food", not the vowel of English "good". You already met that trap.
 EX: God morgon! | goohd MAWR-gawn | Good morning!
 EX: God kväll! | goohd kvehl | Good evening!
 EX: God natt! | goohd nahtt | Good night!
 God morgon is the morning. God kväll is when you arrive in the evening. God natt is bedtime, good night, not a second way to say good evening. God dag (good day) also exists. This lesson practices morning, evening and night. Hej still covers the rest of the day.
CARDS NOW: Hej! = Hello! [card] || Hej hej! = Hi! (casual) [card] || God morgon! = Good morning! [card] || God kväll! = Good evening! [card] || God natt! = Good night! [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 6)
Thanks, and the answer
 Tack is thank you. Tack så mycket is thank you so much. When you hand something over, or when someone has just said tack , say Varsågod . It covers "here you are" and "you're welcome". You will also see the three words var så god . This course writes Varsågod .
 EX: Tack! | tahkk | Thank you!
 EX: Tack så mycket! | tahkk soh MEWK-eht | Thank you so much!
 EX: Varsågod! | vahsh-aw-GOOHD | Here you are. You're welcome.
 Förlåt is sorry. Ursäkta is excuse me, when you stop someone or bump them. Hej då is goodbye, at any time of day. The då uses the oh of å .
 EX: Förlåt! | fur-LOHT | Sorry!
 EX: Ursäkta. | uhr-SEHK-tah | Excuse me.
 EX: Hej då! | hey doh | Bye!
CARDS NOW: Tack! = Thank you! [card] || Tack så mycket! = Thank you very much! [card] || Varsågod! = Here you are. / You're welcome. [card] || Förlåt! = Sorry! [card] || Ursäkta. = Excuse me. [card] || Hej då! = Bye! [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 6)
du for one person
 English has one "you". Swedish uses du for one person: a friend, a classmate, a waiter, a shop assistant, a boss, a doctor. It is not rude. Do not capitalise it.
 EX: Hur mår du? | huehr mohr dueh | How are you?
 ni is for several people, or for a company. It is not a polite word for one person. A waiter still gets du . Do not capitalise du or ni to be polite. A capital only appears when the word starts the sentence. The short answer to the question is Bra, tack . bra means good.
 EX: Hur mår ni? | huehr mohr nee | How are you all? (or to a company)
 EX: Bra, tack! | brah tahkk | Fine, thanks.
 EX: Ja. | yah | Yes.
 EX: Nej. | ney | No.
 Most common mistake: Hur mår ni to one waiter, and god with the vowel of English "good". ni to one person → du . the vowel of "good" → goohd .
CARDS NOW: Hur mår du? = How are you? [card] || Bra, tack! = Fine, thanks. || Nej, tack. = No, thanks. [card] || Hur mår ni? = How are you all? [card] || ja = yes [card] || nej = no [card]

DECK CARDS IN NO SECTION (add by front, as a string, only where a section teaches them): Trevligt att träffas! | TRAYV-lihgt ahtt TREHF-ahs | Nice to meet you. || Ja, tack. | yah tahkk | Yes, please. || Kan du hjälpa mig? | kahnn dueh YEHL-pah mey | Can you help me? || Vi ses! | vee says | See you! || Ha det bra! | hah deh brah | Take care!

## LESSON intro — 4 · Introducing yourself — deck "About me"
### SECTION 0   (has 10)
English says "my name is". Swedish uses heter , and the person comes first. jag (I) is said yah in ordinary speech. Nothing extra goes between heter and the name.
 EX: Jag heter Anna. | yah HAY-tehr AHNN-ah | My name is Anna.
 EX: Jag heter Erik. | yah HAY-tehr AY-rihk | My name is Erik.
 Jag är Anna is also normal Swedish for "I am Anna". The pattern to learn here is Jag heter Anna . Use it when the lesson asks for heter .
 Where you are from, where you live
 Origin uses kommer från . Jag är från Sverige is also normal. Both mean you are from Sweden. A city uses bor i , "live in", not "live on" and not "live from".
 EX: Jag kommer från Sverige. | yah KAWM-ehr frohn SVAYR-yeh | I'm from Sweden.
 EX: Jag är från Sverige. | yah aer frohn SVAYR-yeh | I'm from Sweden.
 EX: Jag bor i Stockholm. | yah boohr ee STAWK-hawlm | I live in Stockholm.
 EX: Jag bor i Göteborg. | yah boohr ee yur-teh-BAWRY | I live in Göteborg.
 EX: Jag bor i Umeå. | yah boohr ee UEH-meh-oh | I live in Umeå.
 Malmö, Uppsala and Lund take i the same way. The g in Sverige and in Göteborg is the j of "yes". bor has the long o of "food": boohr . Names you will meet often are Anna, Erik and Maja. Cities stay capitalised. du does not.

[card] || Jag är från Sverige. = I'm from Sweden. || Vi bor i Malmö. = We live in Malmö. [card] || De bor i Lund. = They live in Lund. [card] || Jag kommer från Sverige. = I'm from Sweden. [card] || Jag bor i Stockholm. = I live in Stockholm. [card] || Jag bor i Göteborg. = I live in Göteborg. || Jag bor i Umeå. = I live in Umeå.

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
A job has no "a"
 English says "I'm a teacher". Swedish leaves that "a" out. The job word stands by itself after är .
 EX: Jag är lärare. | yah aer LAE-rah-reh | I'm a teacher.
 Jag är student works the same way. The noun on its own still has an article when you mean "a teacher" as a thing you point at: en lärare , en student . The article drops in Jag är lärare . Do not put it back.

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Languages
 pratar is speak. Language names are not capitalised.
 EX: Jag pratar engelska. | yah PRAH-tahr EHNG-ehl-skah | I speak English.
 Jag pratar svenska is "I speak Swedish", with a small s . The same shape gives you the whole introduction: name, country, city, job, language.
 Most common mistake: copying the English "a" in front of a job. Jag är en lärare → Jag är lärare .

## LESSON en-ett — 5 · en and ett — deck "People and family"
### SECTION 0   (has 10)
English uses "a" for every noun. Swedish has two indefinite articles, en and ett . Both mean "a" or "one". They are not masculine and feminine. This course calls en common gender, an en-word, and ett neuter, an ett-word.
 About three quarters of nouns are en-words. About a quarter are ett-words. The ending does not save you yet. You learn the article with the noun, the way you learn the meaning. If you guess en every time, the ett-words come out wrong.
 Family
 Most family words take en . The one English speakers miss is barn .

 en dotter ; a daughter ; en-word ; 
 en flicka ; a girl ; en-word ; 
 en pojke ; a boy ; en-word ; 
 en familj ; a family ; en-word ;

 EX: en mamma | ayn MAHMM-ah | a mom
 EX: en pappa | ayn PAHPP-ah | a dad
 EX: en bror | ayn broohr | a brother
 EX: en syster | ayn SEWS-tehr | a sister
 EX: ett barn | eht bahrn | a child
 ett barn is a child. It is not the English farm building. bror has the long o of "food". syster has the short y . Plurals wait. The definite suffix, the ending that means "the", also waits. Today the noun is indefinite: en or ett plus the word.

### SECTION 1   (has 7)
Things you already need
 The same split is on the first objects. A car, a book, a mom and a coffee take en . A house, a child and a glass take ett . har means have or has, the word from the vowel lesson.
 EX: en bil | ayn beel | a car
 EX: ett hus | eht huehs | a house
 EX: en bok | ayn boohk | a book
 EX: en kaffe | ayn KAHFF-eh | a coffee
 EX: ett glas | eht glahs | a glass
 EX: Jag har ett barn. | yah hahr eht bahrn | I have a child.

 There is no separate word for "a" that ignores the group. en kaffe is a cup of coffee. ett glas is a glass. You will need both as soon as a price comes up.
 Most common mistake: guessing en for every noun. en barn → ett barn . en hus → ett hus .

## LESSON numbers — 6 · Numbers and prices — deck "Numbers, time and prices"
### SECTION 0   (has 28)
Swedish numbers are regular once the teens are out of the way. You count with ett , not en : ett, två, tre . In front of a noun, "one" agrees. An en-word takes en . An ett-word takes ett . That is the same pair you just learned.

 sju still starts with the sj-sound. tjugo still starts with the tj-sound. två and åtta use å . Eleven and twelve are their own words, elva and tolv . The rest of the teens are their own words too, not "ten" plus the unit.

 From 20 up, say the ten, then the unit, as one word. 30 is trettio , 40 fyrtio , 50 femtio , 60 sextio , 70 sjuttio , 80 åttio , 90 nittio . 100 is hundra . 200 is tvåhundra , one word. 21 when you count is tjugoett . Not the one first, and not a hyphen.

## LESSON pronouns — 7 · Pronouns, and how are you — deck "Feelings and states"
### SECTION 0   (has 22)
English "you" and "they" hide several Swedish words. The subject is who does the thing. The object is who it happens to. Learn the pair, not the subject alone.
 English ; Subject ; Object ; 
 I ; jag ; mig ; 
 you (one person) ; du ; dig ; 
 he ; han ; honom ; 
 she ; hon ; henne ; 
 it (an en-word) ; den ; den ; 
 it (an ett-word) ; det ; det ; 
 we ; vi ; oss ; 
 you all, or a company ; ni ; er ; 
 they ; de ; dem ; 
 du is still one person, including staff. ni is several people, or a company. The sentence will say which. One waiter is du . Do not capitalise ni to be polite.
 mig, dig, sig
 mig is me. dig is you, one person, as the object. Do not leave the subject du in the object slot. For he and she, honom and henne are someone else. sig is the same person as the subject: himself, herself, themselves.
 EX: Jag ser dig. | yah sayr dey | I see you.
 EX: Du ser mig. | dueh sayr mey | You see me.
 EX: Anna ser sig. | AHNN-ah sayr sey | Anna sees herself.
 EX: Han ser henne. | hahnn sayr HEHN-eh | He sees her.
 You will hear mig , dig and sig as mey , dey and sey . Spell them mig , dig , sig . de and dem both sound like dawm . Write de for the subject and dem for the object. dom , mej , dej and sej are colloquial spellings. They are not the answer you type.
 hen is a pronoun for a person when you are not saying han or hon . Recognize it. The form is hen , not henom . This course does not drill it.

### SECTION 1   (has 7)
How are you
 Hur mår du? asks one person. mår goes with bra (good). Tired and hungry use är plus the word, which matches English "I am".
 EX: Hur mår du? | huehr mohr dueh | How are you?
 EX: Hur mår ni? | huehr mohr nee | How are you all?
 EX: Jag mår bra. | yah mohr brah | I'm fine.
 EX: Jag är trött. | yah aer trurt | I'm tired.
 EX: Jag är hungrig. | yah aer HUHNG-rihg | I'm hungry.
 den stands in for an en-word, such as en bil . det stands in for an ett-word, such as ett hus . trött has short ö . The aid writes that vowel ur , and the r is not an r .
 EX: Den är här. | dehn aer haer | It is here. (a car, an en-word)
 EX: Det är ett hus. | deh aer eht huehs | It is a house.

 vill means want. It is not the future. Jag vill jobba means I want to work. Tomorrow is ska , or the present with the verb in second place. Imorgon ska jag jobba. Imorgon jobbar jag. 
 EX: Jag vill jobba. | yah vihl YAWB-ah | I want to work.
 EX: Imorgon ska jag jobba. | ih-MAWR-gawn skah yah YAWB-ah | I'm going to work tomorrow.
 EX: Imorgon jobbar jag. | ih-MAWR-gawn YAWB-ahr yah | I'm working tomorrow.
 måste inte means you do not have to. You are allowed to skip it. Must not, not allowed, is får inte . får here means allowed. Jag får gå means I may go.
 EX: Jag måste inte gå. | yah MAWS-teh IHN-teh goh | I don't have to go.
 EX: Jag får inte gå. | yah fohr IHN-teh goh | I must not go.
 eventuellt means possibly, not eventually. Eventuellt kommer Anna means Anna is possibly coming. kommer is the same verb as in Jag kommer från . Eventually, at last, is till sist . You will also see slutligen .
 EX: Eventuellt kommer Anna. | eh-vehn-tuh-EHLT KAWM-ehr AHNN-ah | Anna is possibly coming.
 EX: till sist | tihl sihst | eventually, at last
 One calque from the directions lesson, because it sticks: Jag väntar på dig , not Jag väntar för dig . för is not English for in that sentence.
 EX: Jag väntar på dig. | yah VEHN-tahr poh dey | I'm waiting for you.
 Most common mistake: Jag väntar för dig → Jag väntar på dig . And must not is not måste inte . Jag måste inte gå means you do not have to. Jag får inte gå means you must not.
