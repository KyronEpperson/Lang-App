## LESSON greetings — 6 · Greetings: ਸਤ ਸ੍ਰੀ ਅਕਾਲ, ਜੀ and ਤੁਸੀਂ — deck "Greetings and courtesy"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
English has one "hello" for everyone. In Punjab the greeting depends a little on the community. Sikhs and most Punjabis in India say ਸਤ ਸ੍ਰੀ ਅਕਾਲ sat srī akāl ; many Punjabi Hindus say ਨਮਸਤੇ namaste ; Muslim Punjabis say ਸਲਾਮ salām . If someone greets you first, answer with the same words. ਸਤ ਸ੍ਰੀ ਅਕਾਲ works for hello and goodbye, at any time of day. You will often see it spelled ਸਤਿ , with a sign that is not said.
 EX: ਸਤ ਸ੍ਰੀ ਅਕਾਲ ਜੀ। | sat srī akāl jī. | Hello. (politely)
 ਜੀ : the politeness word
 English softens with "sir", "ma'am" or "please". Punjabi adds ਜੀ jī : after a name or title, after yes and no, at the end of a greeting or request. Never put it after your own name.
 EX: ਹਾਂ ਜੀ। | hā̃ jī. | Yes. (politely)
 EX: ਨਹੀਂ ਜੀ। | náhī̃ jī. | No. (politely)
CARDS NOW: ਸਤ ਸ੍ਰੀ ਅਕਾਲ = hello (the Punjabi greeting) [card] || ਨਮਸਤੇ = hello (to a Hindu friend) [card] || ਸਲਾਮ = hello (to a Muslim friend) [card] || ਸਤ ਸ੍ਰੀ ਅਕਾਲ ਜੀ। = Hello. (politely) || ਜੀ = yes (polite); a word of respect [card] || ਹਾਂ ਜੀ। = Yes. (polite) [card] || ਨਹੀਂ ਜੀ। = No. (polite) [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 10)
Two ways to say "you"
 English has one "you". Punjabi has ਤੁਸੀਂ tusī̃ for anyone you do not know well, anyone older, and also for several people. Use it by default. ਤੂੰ tū̃ is for close friends your age, children and younger family; to a stranger or an elder it sounds rude. A very formal third form, ਆਪ , appears mostly in speeches and writing.
 The verb changes with the word for "you". ਤੁਸੀਂ is plural, even for one person, so it takes the plural ਹੋ . ਤੂੰ is singular and takes ਹੈਂ .
 EX: ਤੁਸੀਂ ਕਿਵੇਂ ਹੋ? | tusī̃ kivẽ ho? | How are you? (polite)
 EX: ਤੂੰ ਕਿਵੇਂ ਹੈਂ? | tū̃ kivẽ haĩ? | How are you? (to a close friend)
 EX: ਕੀ ਹਾਲ ਹੈ? | kī hāl hai? | How are things? (everyday)
 The usual answer is "I'm fine": the ending ਹਾਂ agrees with ਮੈਂ , "I".
 EX: ਮੈਂ ਠੀਕ ਹਾਂ। | maĩ ṭhīk hā̃. | I'm fine.
CARDS NOW: ਤੁਸੀਂ = you (polite; or more than one) [card] || ਤੂੰ = you (very close, or rude) [card] || ਆਪ = you (very formal); oneself [card] || ਹੈਂ? = Huh? / What? [card] || ਤੁਸੀਂ ਕਿਵੇਂ ਹੋ? = How are you? (polite) [card] || ਤੂੰ ਕਿਵੇਂ ਹੈਂ? = How are you? (to a close friend) [card] || ਕੀ ਹਾਲ ਹੈ? = How's it going? [card] || ਹਾਂ = yes [card] || ਮੈਂ = I [card] || ਮੈਂ ਠੀਕ ਹਾਂ। = I'm fine. [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 6)
Thank you, sorry, goodbye
 ਧੰਨਵਾਦ dhànnvād is the standard "thank you"; ਸ਼ੁਕਰੀਆ shukrīā is just as common, and many people simply say ਥੈਂਕ ਯੂ . The answer is ਕੋਈ ਗੱਲ ਨਹੀਂ koī gall náhī̃ , literally "it's nothing": it also answers "sorry". Thanking close family for small things can sound distant.
 EX: ਕੋਈ ਗੱਲ ਨਹੀਂ। | koī gall náhī̃. | No problem. / You're welcome.
 EX: ਮਾਫ਼ ਕਰਨਾ | māf karnā | sorry; excuse me
 EX: ਫਿਰ ਮਿਲਾਂਗੇ। | phir milā̃ge. | See you again.
 A guest at the door hears ਜੀ ਆਇਆਂ ਨੂੰ jī āiā̃ nū̃ , "welcome".
 The romanisation is a crutch: read the Gurmukhi first and glance down only to check.
 Most common mistake: translating "you're welcome" word for word. ਤੁਹਾਡਾ ਸਵਾਗਤ ਹੈ after thanks → ਕੋਈ ਗੱਲ ਨਹੀਂ . ਤੁਹਾਡਾ ਸਵਾਗਤ ਹੈ welcomes a guest; it does not answer thanks.
CARDS NOW: ਧੰਨਵਾਦ = thank you (polite) [card] || ਸ਼ੁਕਰੀਆ = thank you (everyday) [card] || ਕੋਈ ਗੱਲ ਨਹੀਂ। = No problem. / You're welcome. [card] || ਮਾਫ਼ ਕਰਨਾ = sorry; excuse me || ਫਿਰ ਮਿਲਾਂਗੇ। = See you again. [card] || ਜੀ ਆਇਆਂ ਨੂੰ! = Welcome! [card]

DECK CARDS IN NO SECTION (add by front, as a string, only where a section teaches them): ਬਹੁਤ ਧੰਨਵਾਦ ਜੀ। | báhut dhànnvād jī. | Thank you very much. || ਮਾਫ਼ ਕਰਨਾ। | māf karnā. | Sorry. / Excuse me. || ਤੁਹਾਨੂੰ ਮਿਲ ਕੇ ਖ਼ੁਸ਼ੀ ਹੋਈ। | tuhā̀nū̃ mil ke ḵhushī hoī. | Nice to meet you. || ਆਪਣਾ ਖ਼ਿਆਲ ਰੱਖਣਾ। | āpṇā ḵhiāl rakkhṇā. | Take care.

## LESSON hona — 7 · Pronouns and ਹੋਣਾ: am, is, are — deck "About me"
### SECTION 0   (has 11)
English puts "am, is, are" right after the subject: "I am happy". Punjabi puts the verb at the end: "I happy am".
 EX: ਮੈਂ ਖ਼ੁਸ਼ ਹਾਂ। | maĩ ḵhush hā̃. | I am happy.
 One verb, four forms
 The verb "to be" is ਹੋਣਾ hoṇā . In the present it has four forms, and each one agrees with its subject.
 Subject ; Verb ; English ; 
 ਮੈਂ ; ਹਾਂ ; I am ; 
 ਅਸੀਂ ; ਹਾਂ ; we are ; 
 ਤੂੰ ; ਹੈਂ ; you are (close friend) ; 
 ਤੁਸੀਂ ; ਹੋ ; you are (polite, or several people) ; 
 ਇਹ / ਉਹ (one) ; ਹੈ ; he, she, it, this, that is ; 
 ਇਹ / ਉਹ (several) ; ਹਨ ; they, these, those are ; 
 Notice that ਮੈਂ and ਅਸੀਂ share ਹਾਂ , and that ਤੁਸੀਂ , the polite "you", always takes the plural ਹੋ , even when you speak to one person.
 EX: ਅਸੀਂ ਤਿਆਰ ਹਾਂ। | asī̃ tiār hā̃. | We are ready.
 EX: ਤੁਸੀਂ ਡਾਕਟਰ ਹੋ। | tusī̃ ḍākṭar ho. | You are a doctor.
CARDS NOW: ਮੈਂ ਖ਼ੁਸ਼ ਹਾਂ। = I am happy. || ਮੈਂ ਡਾਕਟਰ ਹਾਂ। = I'm a doctor. [card] || ਮੈਂ = I [card] || ਹੋਣਾ = to be; to happen [card] || ਹਾਂ = yes [card] || ਅਸੀਂ = we [card] || ਤੂੰ = you (very close, or rude) [card] || ਹੈਂ? = Huh? / What? [card] || ਤੁਸੀਂ = you (polite; or more than one) [card] || ਅਸੀਂ ਤਿਆਰ ਹਾਂ। = We are ready. || ਤੁਸੀਂ ਡਾਕਟਰ ਹੋ। = You are a doctor.

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
ਇਹ and ਉਹ : no he or she
 English chooses between he, she and it. Punjabi does not: it chooses by distance. ਇਹ íh is someone or something near (this, he, she, it), ਉਹ úh is farther away (that, he, she, it). Both also work for several people or things; then the verb is the plural ਹਨ .
 EX: ਇਹ ਚਾਹ ਹੈ। | íh chā́h hai. | This is tea.
 EX: ਉਹ ਦੋਸਤ ਹਨ। | úh dost han. | They are friends.
 One more use of ਹਨ : Punjabi shows respect with the plural. For a grandparent, a teacher or a guest, you say ਉਹ … ਹਨ even for one person. You will see this again with family words.
CARDS NOW: ਇਹ = this; he; she; it (near) [card] || ਉਹ = he; she; that [card] || ਇਹ ਚਾਹ ਹੈ। = This is tea. || ਉਹ ਦੋਸਤ ਹਨ। = They are friends.

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Friends and the polite you
 With ਤੂੰ the verb is ਹੈਂ , with a bindi; without it, ਹੈ belongs to he, she and it. Keep ਤੂੰ for close friends and children.
 EX: ਤੂੰ ਖ਼ੁਸ਼ ਹੈਂ? | tū̃ ḵhush haĩ? | Are you happy? (to a close friend)
CARDS NOW: ਤੂੰ ਖ਼ੁਸ਼ ਹੈਂ? = Are you happy? (to a close friend)

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Not Hindi
 If you know some Hindi from films, watch the small words. Hindi says ਹੂੰ for "am"; Punjabi says ਹਾਂ . The other forms look alike, so this one slips in easily. Learners who grew up with Hindi films make this swap without noticing, so the practice below keeps testing it.
 EX: ਮੈਂ ਠੀਕ ਹਾਂ। | maĩ ṭhīk hā̃. | I'm fine.
 The romanisation is a crutch: read the Gurmukhi first and glance down only to check.
 Most common mistake: using ਹੈ for everything, the way English overuses "is". ਤੁਸੀਂ ਤਿਆਰ ਹੈ? → ਤੁਸੀਂ ਤਿਆਰ ਹੋ? Are you ready? ਤੁਸੀਂ always takes ਹੋ .
CARDS NOW: ਮੈਂ ਠੀਕ ਹਾਂ। = I'm fine. [card]

## LESSON intro — 8 · Introducing yourself: ਮੇਰਾ ਨਾਂ … ਹੈ — deck "About me"
### SECTION 0   (has 9)
My name is…
 English says "My name is Sarah". Punjabi says the same words in a different order: "My name Sarah is", with the verb at the end. The word for name is ਨਾਂ nā̃ ; you will also see it spelled ਨਾਮ .
 EX: ਮੇਰਾ ਨਾਂ ਸਾਰਾ ਹੈ। | merā nā̃ sārā hai. | My name is Sarah.
 Why ਮੇਰਾ and not ਮੇਰੀ ? Words for "my" change to match the thing owned, and ਨਾਂ is a masculine word, so it takes ਮੇਰਾ merā . A woman and a man both say ਮੇਰਾ ਨਾਂ . Lesson 12 explains this fully; for now, learn ਮੇਰਾ ਨਾਂ as one block. To ask, use the polite ਤੁਹਾਡਾ tuhā̀ḍā , "your".
 EX: ਤੁਹਾਡਾ ਨਾਂ ਕੀ ਹੈ? | tuhā̀ḍā nā̃ kī hai? | What's your name?
 You can also say simply "I am Simran", with ਹਾਂ from the last lesson. Do not add ਜੀ after your own name: it is a mark of respect for others.
 EX: ਮੈਂ ਸਿਮਰਨ ਹਾਂ। | maĩ simran hā̃. | I'm Simran.
 EX: ਤੁਹਾਨੂੰ ਮਿਲ ਕੇ ਖ਼ੁਸ਼ੀ ਹੋਈ। | tuhā̀nū̃ mil ke ḵhushī hoī. | Nice to meet you.
CARDS NOW: ਨਾਂ = name [card] || ਮੇਰਾ ਨਾਂ ਸਾਰਾ ਹੈ। = My name is Sarah. [card] || ਮੇਰਾ = my; mine [card] || ਤੁਹਾਡਾ = your (polite) [card] || ਤੁਹਾਡਾ ਨਾਂ ਕੀ ਹੈ? = What's your name? (polite) [card] || ਹਾਂ = yes [card] || ਜੀ = yes (polite); a word of respect [card] || ਮੈਂ ਸਿਮਰਨ ਹਾਂ। = I'm Simran. || ਤੁਹਾਨੂੰ ਮਿਲ ਕੇ ਖ਼ੁਸ਼ੀ ਹੋਈ। = Nice to meet you. [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
Where are you from?
 English puts "from" before the place. Punjabi puts ਤੋਂ tõ , "from", after it: "America from". Words like this are postpositions; Punjabi has many, and they always follow the noun. The question word ਕਿੱਥੋਂ kitthõ means "from where".
 EX: ਤੁਸੀਂ ਕਿੱਥੋਂ ਹੋ? | tusī̃ kitthõ ho? | Where are you from?
 EX: ਮੈਂ ਅਮਰੀਕਾ ਤੋਂ ਹਾਂ। | maĩ amrīkā tõ hā̃. | I'm from America.
 Hindi says ਸੇ for "from"; Punjabi always says ਤੋਂ .
CARDS NOW: ਤੋਂ = from; than [card] || ਕਿੱਥੋਂ = from where? [card] || ਤੁਸੀਂ ਕਿੱਥੋਂ ਹੋ? = Where are you from? [card] || ਮੈਂ ਅਮਰੀਕਾ ਤੋਂ ਹਾਂ। = I'm from America. [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
I speak a little Punjabi
 This sentence is worth learning as a block, because it opens every conversation. Its verb shows who is speaking: a man says ਬੋਲਦਾ , a woman says ਬੋਲਦੀ . English verbs never change for the speaker; Punjabi verbs do, all the time. ਥੋੜ੍ਹੀ thóṛhī , "a little", ends in -ī because the word ਪੰਜਾਬੀ is feminine.
 EX: ਮੈਂ ਥੋੜ੍ਹੀ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਬੋਲਦਾ ਹਾਂ। | maĩ thóṛhī panjābī boldā hā̃. | I speak a little Punjabi. (a man speaking)
 EX: ਮੈਂ ਥੋੜ੍ਹੀ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਬੋਲਦੀ ਹਾਂ। | maĩ thóṛhī panjābī boldī hā̃. | I speak a little Punjabi. (a woman speaking)
CARDS NOW: ਮੈਂ ਥੋੜ੍ਹੀ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਬੋਲਦਾ ਹਾਂ। = I speak a little Punjabi. (a man speaking) || ਮੈਂ ਥੋੜ੍ਹੀ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਬੋਲਦੀ ਹਾਂ। = I speak a little Punjabi. (a woman speaking) [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Putting it together
 A first meeting, at a family dinner or a gurdwara, usually runs in this order: the greeting from lesson 6, names, where you are from, and then, almost always, a delighted comment that you speak Punjabi. Say your one sentence about speaking a little Punjabi, smile, and let the other person carry on. Use ਤੁਸੀਂ with everyone you meet this way.
 The romanisation is a crutch: read the Gurmukhi first and glance down only to check.
 Most common mistake: putting "from" first, as in English, or using the Hindi word. ਮੈਂ ਸੇ ਅਮਰੀਕਾ ਹਾਂ → ਮੈਂ ਅਮਰੀਕਾ ਤੋਂ ਹਾਂ . The place comes first, then ਤੋਂ .
CARDS NOW: ਤੁਸੀਂ = you (polite; or more than one) [card]

## LESSON questions — 9 · ਨਹੀਂ and question words — deck "Questions and connectors"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Yes-no questions: add ਕੀ 
 English turns "You are ready" around to make a question: "Are you ready?" Punjabi changes nothing in the order. It puts ਕੀ kī at the start and lets the voice rise at the end. In everyday speech the ਕੀ is often dropped, and the rising voice alone makes the question.
 EX: ਕੀ ਤੁਸੀਂ ਡਾਕਟਰ ਹੋ? | kī tusī̃ ḍākṭar ho? | Are you a doctor?

## LESSON gender — 10 · Gender and plurals — deck "Home and daily things"
### SECTION 0   (has 14)
English nouns have no gender: "my room" and "my book" use the same "my". In Punjabi every noun is masculine or feminine , and the small words around it change to match. You have already met one of them: ਮੇਰਾ merā in ਮੇਰਾ ਨਾਂ . With a feminine noun, "my" is ਮੇਰੀ merī .

## LESSON possessive — 12 · ਦਾ / ਦੇ / ਦੀ: my, your, and family — deck "Family and people"
### SECTION 0   (has 10)
English says "Simran's brother", with the owner first and a little 's. Punjabi also puts the owner first, then a word for "of", then the thing: ਸਿਮਰਨ ਦਾ ਭਰਾ simran dā bhàrā . That word is ਦਾ dā , ਦੇ de or ਦੀ dī .

 The oblique form
 Here is the part with no English equivalent. A masculine noun ending in -ā changes its ending to -e when a postposition follows it. This changed shape is the oblique form.
 EX: ਮੁੰਡਾ ਕਮਰੇ ਵਿੱਚ ਹੈ। | muṇḍā kamre vicch hai. | The boy is in the room.
 EX: ਸਿਮਰਨ ਗੁਰਦੁਆਰੇ ਵਿੱਚ ਹੈ। | simran gurduāre vicch hai. | Simran is in the gurdwara.
 ਕਮਰਾ becomes ਕਮਰੇ , ਗੁਰਦੁਆਰਾ becomes ਗੁਰਦੁਆਰੇ . Words that agree with the noun go oblique too: ਮੇਰਾ becomes ਮੇਰੇ , ਵੱਡਾ becomes ਵੱਡੇ , even when the noun itself cannot change.
 EX: ਸਿਮਰਨ ਮੇਰੇ ਘਰ ਵਿੱਚ ਹੈ। | simran mere ghàr vicch hai. | Simran is in my house.
 EX: ਮੇਰਾ ਭਰਾ ਵੱਡੇ ਸ਼ਹਿਰ ਵਿੱਚ ਹੈ। | merā bhàrā vaḍḍe sháhir vicch hai. | My brother is in a big city.
 Feminine nouns, and their ਮੇਰੀ or ਵੱਡੀ , do not change in the singular: ਮੇਰੀ ਕਾਰ ਵਿੱਚ . Remember too that ਦਾ is itself a postposition, so ਮੁੰਡੇ ਦਾ ਫ਼ੋਨ means "the boy's phone".

## LESSON habitual — 15 · Present habitual: ਕਰਦਾ ਹਾਂ — deck "Time, days and routine"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
English "I work", "she works" describes habits and facts. Punjabi builds this from two pieces: a verb form ending in -ਦਾ , and the ਹਾਂ / ਹੈ / ਹੋ / ਹਨ you already know. You met the pattern in ਮੈਂ ਥੋੜ੍ਹੀ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਬੋਲਦਾ ਹਾਂ .
 Use it for routines, for general facts, and for what is true of you these days: where you live, what work you do, which languages you speak. For what is happening at this very moment, a later lesson teaches the continuous.
 Stem + ਦਾ + ਹੋਣਾ 
 Every verb has a dictionary form, the infinitive, ending in - ਣਾ or - ਨਾ : ਕਰਨਾ karnā (to do), ਬੋਲਣਾ bolṇā (to speak). Take off - ਣਾ or - ਨਾ to get the stem ( ਕਰ , ਬੋਲ ), add ਦਾ , then the right form of "to be".
 EX: ਮੈਂ ਕੰਮ ਕਰਦਾ ਹਾਂ। | maĩ kamm kardā hā̃. | I work. (a man speaking)
CARDS NOW: ਦਾ = of; 's (before a masculine noun) [card] || ਹੋਣਾ = to be; to happen [card] || ਨਾ = not (in commands); no [card] || ਕਰਨਾ = to do [card] || ਬੋਲਣਾ = to speak [card] || ਕਰ = do! (to a friend) [card] || ਮੈਂ ਕੰਮ ਕਰਦਾ ਹਾਂ। = I work. (a man speaking)

## LESSON imperative — 16 · Requests and commands: ਬੈਠੋ, ਦੱਸੋ ਜੀ — deck "Health and help"

### SECTION 1   (has 12)
Softening with ਜੀ 
 English adds "please". Punjabi usually adds ਜੀ after the verb. ਕਿਰਪਾ ਕਰਕੇ kirpā karke also means "please", but you will mostly see it on signs and in formal requests.
 EX: ਬੈਠੋ ਜੀ। | baiṭho jī. | Please sit down.

 Notice ਮੈਨੂੰ : the person you tell or give to takes ਨੂੰ , and the noun before it takes the oblique form, as in the postpositions lesson.
 EX: ਡਾਕਟਰ ਨੂੰ ਬੁਲਾਓ! | ḍākṭar nū̃ bulāo! | Call the doctor!

 EX: ਮੇਰੀ ਮਦਦ ਕਰੋ ਜੀ। | merī madad karo jī. | Please help me.

## LESSON like-need — 17 · ਪਸੰਦ, ਚਾਹੀਦਾ: like and need — deck "Shopping and the market"
### SECTION 0   (has 9)
In English the person who likes or needs something is the subject: "I like tea", "I need water". Punjabi turns it around. The person takes ਨੂੰ , and the thing liked or needed is what the sentence is really about: ਮੈਨੂੰ ਚਾਹ ਪਸੰਦ ਹੈ , literally "to me, tea is pleasing". These are called ਮੈਨੂੰ -constructions.
 Liking: ਪਸੰਦ ਹੈ 
 EX: ਮੈਨੂੰ ਚਾਹ ਪਸੰਦ ਹੈ। | mainū̃ chā́h pasand hai. | I like tea.
 ਪਸੰਦ pasand never changes, but the verb agrees with the thing: several things take ਹਨ . The person's form stays the same: never ਮੈਂ here, always ਮੈਨੂੰ .
 EX: ਮੈਨੂੰ ਅੰਬ ਪਸੰਦ ਹਨ। | mainū̃ amb pasand han. | I like mangoes.

## LESSON food — 18 · Ordering food and the market — deck "Food and drink"
### SECTION 0   (has 6)
This lesson puts together what you know (commands with ਜੀ , numbers, agreement and ਮੈਨੂੰ ਚਾਹੀਦਾ ) for the place you will use it most: a dhaba, a market stall or a family kitchen.
 Ordering

## LESSON continuous — 19 · Continuous: ਕਰ ਰਿਹਾ ਹਾਂ — deck "Home and daily things"

 Stem + ਰਿਹਾ + ਹਾਂ / ਹੈ / ਹੋ / ਹਨ 
 Take the stem of the verb (the infinitive minus - ਣਾ / - ਨਾ : ਕਰ kar , ਖਾ khā , ਪੜ੍ਹ páṛh ), add a form of ਰਿਹਾ ríhā , then the usual ਹਾਂ , ਹੈ , ਹੋ or ਹਨ . The stem never changes. The middle word changes to agree with the person doing the action, exactly like an adjective ending in -ā.

## LESSON time — 20 · Clock time and days — deck "Time, days and routine"
### SECTION 0   (has 10)
English says "it's three o'clock". Punjabi says ਤਿੰਨ ਵਜੇ ਹਨ tinn vaje han , literally "three have struck". ਵਜੇ is plural, so the verb is ਹਨ , not ਹੈ . The question is the same shape.
 EX: ਕਿੰਨੇ ਵਜੇ ਹਨ? | kinne vaje han? | What time is it?
 EX: ਤਿੰਨ ਵਜੇ ਹਨ। | tinn vaje han. | It's three o'clock.
 Quarters and halves
 English counts minutes past and to the hour. Punjabi puts a word in front of the hour instead:
 Word ; Meaning ; Example ; 
 ਸਵਾ ; quarter past ; ਸਵਾ ਚਾਰ ਵਜੇ (4:15) ; 
 ਸਾਢੇ ; half past (from 3:30 on) ; ਸਾਢੇ ਚਾਰ ਵਜੇ (4:30) ; 
 ਪੌਣੇ ; quarter to the hour you name ; ਪੌਣੇ ਪੰਜ ਵਜੇ (4:45) ; 
 ਡੇਢ ; half past one ; ਡੇਢ ਵਜੇ (1:30) ; 
 ਢਾਈ ; half past two ; ਢਾਈ ਵਜੇ (2:30) ; 
 Two traps. ਪੌਣੇ pauṇe names the coming hour: 4:45 is ਪੌਣੇ ਪੰਜ , "a quarter less than five". And 1:30 and 2:30 have their own words, ਡੇਢ ḍéḍh and ਢਾਈ ḍhā̀ī ; ਸਾਢੇ starts at three.
 EX: ਸਾਢੇ ਚਾਰ ਵਜੇ ਹਨ। | sā́ḍhe chār vaje han. | It's half past four.
 EX: ਪੌਣੇ ਪੰਜ ਵਜੇ ਹਨ। | pauṇe panj vaje han. | It's quarter to five.
CARDS NOW: ਸਾਢੇ ਤਿੰਨ = half past three [card] || ਵਜੇ = o'clock [card] || ਕਿੰਨੇ ਵਜੇ ਹਨ? = What time is it? [card] || ਤਿੰਨ ਵਜੇ ਹਨ। = It's three o'clock. [card] || ਸਵਾ ਚਾਰ = quarter past four [card] || ਪੌਣੇ ਪੰਜ = quarter to five [card] || ਡੇਢ ਵਜੇ = at one thirty [card] || ਢਾਈ = two and a half [card] || ਸਾਢੇ ਚਾਰ ਵਜੇ ਹਨ। = It's half past four. || ਪੌਣੇ ਪੰਜ ਵਜੇ ਹਨ। = It's quarter to five.

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
At a time
 English needs "at". Punjabi just puts the time before the verb, with no extra word: ਸੱਤ ਵਜੇ is both "seven o'clock" and "at seven".
 EX: ਮੈਂ ਸੱਤ ਵਜੇ ਉੱਠਦਾ ਹਾਂ। | maĩ satt vaje uṭṭhdā hā̃. | I get up at seven. (a man speaking)
 EX: ਤੁਸੀਂ ਕਿੰਨੇ ਵਜੇ ਉੱਠਦੇ ਹੋ? | tusī̃ kinne vaje uṭṭhde ho? | What time do you get up?
 So ਕਿੰਨੇ ਵਜੇ does two jobs: "what time is it?" and "at what time?". Punjabi has no a.m. and p.m. either. When it matters, add the part of the day in front: ਸਵੇਰੇ ਸੱਤ ਵਜੇ is seven in the morning. In everyday speech you will also hear the English loan ਟਾਈਮ , but the clock itself is always told with ਵਜੇ .
CARDS NOW: ਮੈਂ ਸੱਤ ਵਜੇ ਉੱਠਦਾ ਹਾਂ। = I get up at seven. (a man speaking) [card] || ਤੁਸੀਂ ਕਿੰਨੇ ਵਜੇ ਉੱਠਦੇ ਹੋ? = What time do you get up? || ਸਵੇਰੇ = in the morning [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 13)
Days and parts of the day
 The days end in - ਵਾਰ : ਸੋਮਵਾਰ (Monday), ਮੰਗਲਵਾਰ , ਬੁੱਧਵਾਰ , ਵੀਰਵਾਰ , ਸ਼ੁੱਕਰਵਾਰ , ਸ਼ਨੀਵਾਰ (also ਸ਼ਨਿੱਚਰਵਾਰ ), ਐਤਵਾਰ aitvār (Sunday). Where English says "on Monday", Punjabi puts the postposition ਨੂੰ after the day: ਸੋਮਵਾਰ ਨੂੰ . Evening and night work the same way: ਸ਼ਾਮ ਨੂੰ , ਰਾਤ ਨੂੰ . Morning and afternoon have their own forms: ਸਵੇਰੇ savere , ਦੁਪਹਿਰੇ dupáhire .
 EX: ਮੈਂ ਐਤਵਾਰ ਨੂੰ ਕੰਮ ਨਹੀਂ ਕਰਦੀ। | maĩ aitvār nū̃ kamm náhī̃ kardī. | I don't work on Sunday. (a woman speaking)
 EX: ਅਸੀਂ ਰਾਤ ਨੂੰ ਟੀਵੀ ਦੇਖਦੇ ਹਾਂ। | asī̃ rāt nū̃ ṭīvī dekhde hā̃. | We watch TV at night.
 Time words usually come right after the subject, but they can also open the sentence: ਐਤਵਾਰ ਨੂੰ ਮੈਂ ਕੰਮ ਨਹੀਂ ਕਰਦੀ is just as good.

## LESSON have — 21 · Having: ਮੇਰੇ ਕੋਲ … ਹੈ — deck "Shopping and the market"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
English uses one verb for everything: "I have a car", "I have a brother", "I have a fever". Punjabi has no verb "to have". It uses three different patterns, one for each kind of having, and the verb is always a form of ਹੈ .
 Things: ਮੇਰੇ ਕੋਲ ... ਹੈ 
 For things you own or carry, Punjabi says "near me there is": ਮੇਰੇ ਕੋਲ mere kol . ਕੋਲ is a postposition, so ਮੇਰਾ takes the oblique form ਮੇਰੇ , just like ਮੇਰੇ ਘਰ ਵਿੱਚ . The same goes for ਤੁਹਾਡੇ ਕੋਲ and ਸਾਡੇ ਕੋਲ ; for "he" or "she" it is ਉਸ ਕੋਲ .
 EX: ਮੇਰੇ ਕੋਲ ਕਾਰ ਹੈ। | mere kol kār hai. | I have a car.
 EX: ਕੀ ਤੁਹਾਡੇ ਕੋਲ ਟਾਈਮ ਹੈ? | kī tuhā̀ḍe kol ṭāīm hai? | Do you have time?
 The verb agrees with the thing, not with the owner: one thing takes ਹੈ , several take ਹਨ . To say you don't have something, put ਨਹੀਂ before the verb.
 EX: ਮੇਰੇ ਕੋਲ ਤਿੰਨ ਕਿਤਾਬਾਂ ਹਨ। | mere kol tinn kitābā̃ han. | I have three books.
 EX: ਮੇਰੇ ਕੋਲ ਪੈਸੇ ਨਹੀਂ ਹਨ। | mere kol paise náhī̃ han. | I don't have any money.
CARDS NOW: ਕੋਲ = near; with (in someone's possession) [card] || ਮੇਰਾ = my; mine [card] || ਮੇਰੇ ਕੋਲ ਕਾਰ ਹੈ। = I have a car. || ਕੀ ਤੁਹਾਡੇ ਕੋਲ ਟਾਈਮ ਹੈ? = Do you have time? || ਨਹੀਂ = no; not [card] || ਮੇਰੇ ਕੋਲ ਤਿੰਨ ਕਿਤਾਬਾਂ ਹਨ। = I have three books. || ਮੇਰੇ ਕੋਲ ਪੈਸੇ ਨਹੀਂ ਹਨ। = I don't have money. [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
Relatives: ਮੇਰਾ ਇੱਕ ਭਰਾ ਹੈ 
 People are not things you carry around, so relatives and friends use the possessive: "my one brother is". The possessive agrees with the relative, as you learned with family words: ਮੇਰਾ for a brother, ਮੇਰੀ for a sister, ਮੇਰੀਆਂ for sisters.
 EX: ਮੇਰਾ ਇੱਕ ਭਰਾ ਹੈ। | merā ikk bhàrā hai. | I have a brother.
 EX: ਮੇਰੀਆਂ ਦੋ ਭੈਣਾਂ ਹਨ। | merīā̃ do bhaìṇā̃ han. | I have two sisters.
 EX: ਉਸ ਦੀਆਂ ਦੋ ਧੀਆਂ ਹਨ। | us dīā̃ do dhī̀ā̃ han. | She has two daughters.
 To most speakers ਮੇਰੇ ਕੋਲ ਇੱਕ ਭਰਾ ਹੈ sounds odd: ਕੋਲ means "near", as if your brother were standing next to you right now.
CARDS NOW: ਮੇਰਾ ਇੱਕ ਭਰਾ ਹੈ। = I have a brother. [card] || ਮੇਰੀਆਂ ਦੋ ਭੈਣਾਂ ਹਨ। = I have two sisters. || ਉਸ ਦੀਆਂ ਦੋ ਧੀਆਂ ਹਨ। = She has two daughters.

### SECTION 2   (has 5)
Illnesses: ਮੈਨੂੰ ਬੁਖ਼ਾਰ ਹੈ 
 Fevers and colds happen to you, so they take ਮੈਨੂੰ mainū̃ , the same form as in ਮੈਨੂੰ ਚਾਹ ਪਸੰਦ ਹੈ . For someone else, use their form with ਨੂੰ : ਉਸ ਨੂੰ ਬੁਖ਼ਾਰ ਹੈ , "he has a fever" or "she has a fever".
 EX: ਮੈਨੂੰ ਬੁਖ਼ਾਰ ਹੈ। | mainū̃ buḵhār hai. | I have a fever.
 EX: ਮੈਨੂੰ ਜ਼ੁਕਾਮ ਹੈ। | mainū̃ zukām hai. | I have a cold.
CARDS NOW: ਮੈਨੂੰ ਇਹ ਪਸੰਦ ਹੈ। = I like this. [card] || ਮੈਨੂੰ = me; to me [card] || ਨੂੰ = to; for (marks the person) [card] || ਮੈਨੂੰ ਬੁਖ਼ਾਰ ਹੈ। = I have a fever. [card] || ਮੈਨੂੰ ਜ਼ੁਕਾਮ ਹੈ। = I have a cold.

## LESSON directions — 22 · Directions and transport — deck "Getting around"
### SECTION 0   (has 8)
Asking the way in Punjab is easy: people are happy to help, and the answers use a small set of words. You already know the polite command ( ਜਾਓ , ਦੱਸੋ ) and the postpositions; this lesson puts them together.
 Start politely. Get attention with ਵੀਰ ਜੀ (to a man) or ਭੈਣ ਜੀ (to a woman), then ask with the question word in the middle, where the answer will go: ਸਟੇਸ਼ਨ ਕਿੱਥੇ ਹੈ? Listen for the words below, and say ਧੰਨਵਾਦ at the end.
 Left, right, straight
 English says "turn left". Punjabi says the same thing in the same order: ਖੱਬੇ khabbe (left) or ਸੱਜੇ sajje (right), then ਮੁੜੋ muṛo , "turn". "Go straight" is ਸਿੱਧੇ ਜਾਓ síddhe jāo . Add ਜੀ when you ask someone else to do it, like a driver.
 EX: ਸਿੱਧੇ ਜਾਓ। | síddhe jāo. | Go straight.
 EX: ਖੱਬੇ ਮੁੜੋ। | khabbe muṛo. | Turn left.
 EX: ਇੱਥੇ ਰੋਕੋ ਜੀ। | itthe roko jī. | Stop here, please.

Buses, autos and trains

## LESSON si — 23 · ਸੀ, ਸਨ: was, were, and used to — deck "About me"
### SECTION 0   (has 11)
English has "was" and "were". Punjabi has ਸੀ sī and ਸਨ san , and they replace ਹਾਂ , ਹੈ , ਹੋ and ਹਨ when you talk about the past. If you know some Hindi, this is where ਥਾ sneaks in: Punjabi never uses it.
 ਸੀ : one person, and most of everyday speech
 ਸੀ goes with ਮੈਂ , ਤੂੰ and ਉਹ (one person), and it does not change for men and women. In everyday speech it also goes with ਅਸੀਂ and ਤੁਸੀਂ . In books you will meet the written forms ਸਾਂ (with ਮੈਂ and ਅਸੀਂ ) and ਸੋ (with ਤੁਸੀਂ ); you only need to recognize them.
 EX: ਮੈਂ ਘਰ ਸੀ। | maĩ ghàr sī. | I was at home.
 EX: ਉਹ ਬਹੁਤ ਖ਼ੁਸ਼ ਸੀ। | úh báhut ḵhush sī. | She was very happy.
 EX: ਤੁਸੀਂ ਕਿੱਥੇ ਸੀ? | tusī̃ kitthe sī? | Where were you?
 For "was not", put ਨਹੀਂ just before ਸੀ , as you do before ਹੈ : ਮੈਂ ਘਰ ਨਹੀਂ ਸੀ , "I wasn't at home".
CARDS NOW: ਹਾਂ = yes [card] || ਮੈਂ = I [card] || ਤੂੰ = you (very close, or rude) [card] || ਉਹ = he; she; that [card] || ਅਸੀਂ = we [card] || ਤੁਸੀਂ = you (polite; or more than one) [card] || ਸੋ = so; therefore [card] || ਮੈਂ ਘਰ ਸੀ। = I was at home. || ਉਹ ਬਹੁਤ ਖ਼ੁਸ਼ ਸੀ। = She was very happy. || ਤੁਸੀਂ ਕਿੱਥੇ ਸੀ? = Where were you? || ਨਹੀਂ = no; not [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
ਸਨ : several people, or one respected person
 The standard form for ਉਹ meaning "they" is ਸਨ , and so is the respect plural: a grandfather with ਜੀ takes ਸਨ , just as he takes ਹਨ in the present. A group of girls or women takes ਸਨ as well; ਸਨ , like ਸੀ , does not change for men and women.
 EX: ਮੇਰੇ ਦਾਦਾ ਜੀ ਡਾਕਟਰ ਸਨ। | mere dādā jī ḍākṭar san. | My grandfather was a doctor.
 EX: ਉਹ ਦਿੱਲੀ ਵਿੱਚ ਸਨ। | úh dillī vicch san. | They were in Delhi.
 EX: ਕੁੜੀਆਂ ਘਰ ਸਨ। | kuṛīā̃ ghàr san. | The girls were at home.
CARDS NOW: ਜੀ = yes (polite); a word of respect [card] || ਮੇਰੇ ਦਾਦਾ ਜੀ ਡਾਕਟਰ ਸਨ। = My grandfather was a doctor. || ਉਹ ਦਿੱਲੀ ਵਿੱਚ ਸਨ। = They were in Delhi. || ਕੁੜੀਆਂ ਘਰ ਸਨ। = The girls were at home.

## LESSON compare — 26 · Comparing: ਨਾਲੋਂ, ਸਭ ਤੋਂ — deck "Describing things"
### SECTION 0   (has 10)
English changes the adjective: tall, taller, tallest, or adds "more" and "most". Punjabi leaves the adjective alone and adds a postposition after the thing you compare with. So there is only one new pattern to learn, and it works for every adjective you know.
 Than: ਨਾਲੋਂ or ਤੋਂ 
 The pattern is "X Y-than tall is": ਉਹ ਮੇਰੇ ਨਾਲੋਂ ਲੰਮਾ ਹੈ . ਨਾਲੋਂ nālõ means "than", and ਤੋਂ tõ (the "from" you know) works the same way and is just as common. Both are postpositions, so they come after the noun or pronoun, which takes its oblique form: ਮੈਂ becomes ਮੇਰੇ , ਉਹ becomes ਉਸ , ਮੁੰਡਾ becomes ਮੁੰਡੇ .
 EX: ਉਹ ਮੇਰੇ ਨਾਲੋਂ ਲੰਮਾ ਹੈ। | úh mere nālõ lammā hai. | He is taller than me.
 EX: ਚਾਹ ਲੱਸੀ ਨਾਲੋਂ ਸਸਤੀ ਹੈ। | chā́h lassī nālõ sastī hai. | Tea is cheaper than lassi.
 EX: ਇਹ ਕਮਰਾ ਉਸ ਨਾਲੋਂ ਵੱਡਾ ਹੈ। | íh kamrā us nālõ vaḍḍā hai. | This room is bigger than that one.
CARDS NOW: ਨਾਲੋਂ = than [card] || ਤੋਂ = from; than [card] || ਉਹ = he; she; that [card] || ਮੈਂ = I [card] || ਉਸ = that; him; her (before a postposition) [card] || ਮੁੰਡਾ = boy [card] || ਉਹ ਮੇਰੇ ਨਾਲੋਂ ਲੰਮਾ ਹੈ। = He is taller than me. [card] || ਚਾਹ ਲੱਸੀ ਨਾਲੋਂ ਸਸਤੀ ਹੈ। = Tea is cheaper than lassi. || ਇਹ ਕਮਰਾ ਉਸ ਨਾਲੋਂ ਵੱਡਾ ਹੈ। = This room is bigger than that one. || ਵੱਡਾ = big [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
The adjective still agrees
 Nothing about comparing switches off agreement. The adjective agrees with the person or thing being described, not with the one it is compared to. ਚਾਹ is feminine, so it is ਸਸਤੀ , whatever lassi is.
 EX: ਮੇਰੀ ਭੈਣ ਮੇਰੇ ਨਾਲੋਂ ਵੱਡੀ ਹੈ। | merī bhaìṇ mere nālõ vaḍḍī hai. | My sister is older than me.
 Notice ਵੱਡਾ : "big" also means "older" for people, and ਛੋਟਾ , "small", means "younger". ਮੇਰਾ ਵੱਡਾ ਭਰਾ is "my older brother".
CARDS NOW: ਚਾਹ = tea [card] || ਮੇਰੀ ਭੈਣ ਮੇਰੇ ਨਾਲੋਂ ਵੱਡੀ ਹੈ। = My sister is older than me. || ਛੋਟਾ = small; younger [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
The most: ਸਭ ਤੋਂ 
 For "biggest", "best" or "most expensive", put ਸਭ ਤੋਂ sábh tõ , "than all", before the adjective.
 EX: ਇਹ ਸਭ ਤੋਂ ਵੱਡਾ ਕਮਰਾ ਹੈ। | íh sábh tõ vaḍḍā kamrā hai. | This is the biggest room.
 EX: ਸਿਮਰਨ ਸਭ ਤੋਂ ਲੰਮੀ ਹੈ। | simran sábh tõ lammī hai. | Simran is the tallest.
 To ask which one is the most, use ਕਿਹੜਾ or ਕਿਹੜੀ , agreeing with the noun, in the place where the answer will go.
 EX: ਕਿਹੜਾ ਕਮਰਾ ਸਭ ਤੋਂ ਵੱਡਾ ਹੈ? | kíhṛā kamrā sábh tõ vaḍḍā hai? | Which room is the biggest?
CARDS NOW: ਇਹ ਸਭ ਤੋਂ ਵੱਡਾ ਕਮਰਾ ਹੈ। = This is the biggest room. || ਸਿਮਰਨ ਸਭ ਤੋਂ ਲੰਮੀ ਹੈ। = Simran is the tallest. || ਕਿਹੜਾ = which? [card] || ਕਿਹੜਾ ਕਮਰਾ ਸਭ ਤੋਂ ਵੱਡਾ ਹੈ? = Which room is the biggest?

### SECTION 3   (has 3)
Comparing with a noun
 When the thing you compare with is a masculine noun in -ā, it changes before ਨਾਲੋਂ exactly as it does before ਵਿੱਚ or ਨੂੰ .
 EX: ਕੁੜੀ ਮੁੰਡੇ ਨਾਲੋਂ ਲੰਮੀ ਹੈ। | kuṛī muṇḍe nālõ lammī hai. | The girl is taller than the boy.
CARDS NOW: ਵਿੱਚ = in; inside [card] || ਨੂੰ = to; for (marks the person) [card] || ਕੁੜੀ ਮੁੰਡੇ ਨਾਲੋਂ ਲੰਮੀ ਹੈ। = The girl is taller than the boy.

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
More and less
 You do not need a word for "more" with an adjective: ਲੱਸੀ ਨਾਲੋਂ ਮਹਿੰਗੀ already means "more expensive than lassi". ਜ਼ਿਆਦਾ ziādā (more) and ਘੱਟ (less) are used with amounts: ਜ਼ਿਆਦਾ ਪੈਸੇ , more money; ਘੱਟ ਮਿਰਚ , less chili.
 The romanisation is a crutch: read the Gurmukhi first and glance down only to check.
 Most common mistake: translating "more ... than" word for word, in English order. ਉਹ ਜ਼ਿਆਦਾ ਲੰਮਾ ਨਾਲੋਂ ਮੈਂ ਹੈ → ਉਹ ਮੇਰੇ ਨਾਲੋਂ ਲੰਮਾ ਹੈ .
CARDS NOW: ਜ਼ਿਆਦਾ = more; too much [card] || ਘੱਟ = less; little [card]

## LESSON must-can — 27 · Must and can: ਪੈਂਦਾ, ਸਕਦਾ — deck "Health and help"
### SECTION 0   (has 9)
English uses small helper verbs: "I have to go", "I should go", "I can go". Punjabi has three patterns, and the first two use the same ਮੈਨੂੰ form you know from ਮੈਨੂੰ ਚਾਹ ਪਸੰਦ ਹੈ .
 Have to: ਮੈਨੂੰ + infinitive + ਪੈਂਦਾ ਹੈ / ਪਵੇਗਾ 
 Literally "to me, going falls". The person who must do it takes the postposition form ( ਮੈਨੂੰ , ਤੁਹਾਨੂੰ , ਉਸ ਨੂੰ ), and the verb stays in the infinitive ( ਜਾਣਾ , ਕਰਨਾ ). ਪੈਂਦਾ ਹੈ paĩdā hai is for what you have to do as a rule; ਪਵੇਗਾ pavegā is for what you will have to do, which is also how you say "I have to go" when you are leaving.
 EX: ਮੈਨੂੰ ਰੋਜ਼ ਕੰਮ ਕਰਨਾ ਪੈਂਦਾ ਹੈ। | mainū̃ roz kamm karnā paĩdā hai. | I have to work every day.
 EX: ਮੈਨੂੰ ਜਾਣਾ ਪਵੇਗਾ। | mainū̃ jāṇā pavegā. | I'll have to go.
 EX: ਮੈਨੂੰ ਡਾਕਟਰ ਕੋਲ ਜਾਣਾ ਪਵੇਗਾ। | mainū̃ ḍākṭar kol jāṇā pavegā. | I'll have to go to the doctor.
CARDS NOW: ਮੈਨੂੰ ਜਾਣਾ ਪੈਂਦਾ ਹੈ। = I have to go. (as a rule) [card] || ਮੈਨੂੰ = me; to me [card] || ਤੁਹਾਨੂੰ = you; to you (polite) [card] || ਜਾਣਾ = to go [card] || ਕਰਨਾ = to do [card] || ਮੈਨੂੰ ਰੋਜ਼ ਕੰਮ ਕਰਨਾ ਪੈਂਦਾ ਹੈ। = I have to work every day. || ਮੈਨੂੰ ਜਾਣਾ ਪਵੇਗਾ। = I'll have to go. || ਮੈਨੂੰ ਡਾਕਟਰ ਕੋਲ ਜਾਣਾ ਪਵੇਗਾ। = I'll have to go to the doctor. || ਡਾਕਟਰ = doctor [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
Should: ਮੈਨੂੰ + infinitive + ਚਾਹੀਦਾ ਹੈ 
 You met ਚਾਹੀਦਾ chā́hīdā as "need" with a thing. With an infinitive it means "should": advice from a doctor, a parent or a friend.
 EX: ਤੁਹਾਨੂੰ ਆਰਾਮ ਕਰਨਾ ਚਾਹੀਦਾ ਹੈ। | tuhā̀nū̃ ārām karnā chā́hīdā hai. | You should rest.
 EX: ਤੁਹਾਨੂੰ ਘਰ ਜਾਣਾ ਚਾਹੀਦਾ ਹੈ। | tuhā̀nū̃ ghàr jāṇā chā́hīdā hai. | You should go home.
CARDS NOW: ਮੈਨੂੰ ਆਰਾਮ ਕਰਨਾ ਚਾਹੀਦਾ ਹੈ। = I should rest. [card] || ਤੁਹਾਨੂੰ ਆਰਾਮ ਕਰਨਾ ਚਾਹੀਦਾ ਹੈ। = You should rest. || ਤੁਹਾਨੂੰ ਘਰ ਜਾਣਾ ਚਾਹੀਦਾ ਹੈ। = You should go home.

### SECTION 2   (has 7)
Can: stem + ਸਕਦਾ ਹਾਂ 
 "Can" works differently. The person stays as he or she is ( ਮੈਂ , ਤੁਸੀਂ ), the main verb is only its stem ( ਜਾ , ਬੋਲ ), and ਸਕਦਾ sakdā agrees with the person like any habitual: ਸਕਦਾ for a man, ਸਕਦੀ for a woman, ਸਕਦੇ for ਤੁਸੀਂ and groups.
 EX: ਮੈਂ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਬੋਲ ਸਕਦੀ ਹਾਂ। | maĩ panjābī bol sakdī hā̃. | I can speak Punjabi. (a woman speaking)
 EX: ਕੀ ਤੁਸੀਂ ਮੇਰੀ ਮਦਦ ਕਰ ਸਕਦੇ ਹੋ? | kī tusī̃ merī madad kar sakde ho? | Can you help me?
 The same pattern asks for permission, with ਕੀ at the start:
 EX: ਕੀ ਮੈਂ ਅੰਦਰ ਆ ਸਕਦਾ ਹਾਂ? | kī maĩ andar ā sakdā hā̃? | May I come in? (a man speaking)
 A woman asks ਕੀ ਮੈਂ ਅੰਦਰ ਆ ਸਕਦੀ ਹਾਂ? At a doctor's office, the usual answer is ਹਾਂ ਜੀ, ਆਓ , "yes, come in", the polite command you already know, softened with ਜੀ .
CARDS NOW: ਮੈਂ = I [card] || ਤੁਸੀਂ = you (polite; or more than one) [card] || ਮੈਂ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਬੋਲ ਸਕਦੀ ਹਾਂ। = I can speak Punjabi. (a woman speaking) || ਕੀ ਤੁਸੀਂ ਮੇਰੀ ਮਦਦ ਕਰ ਸਕਦੇ ਹੋ? = Can you help me? || ਕੀ = kī (long ī) [card] || ਕੀ ਮੈਂ ਅੰਦਰ ਆ ਸਕਦਾ ਹਾਂ? = May I come in? (a man speaking) || ਜੀ = yes (polite); a word of respect [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Which person form?
 A quick test: "have to" and "should" take ਮੈਨੂੰ and an infinitive ending in - ਣਾ / - ਨਾ ; "can" takes ਮੈਂ and the bare stem. Mixing the two halves is the error to watch for, and it is the one English speakers make most, because English uses the same "I" for all three.
 The romanisation is a crutch: read the Gurmukhi first and glance down only to check.
 Most common mistake: keeping ਮੈਂ for "I have to", or using the infinitive with ਸਕਦਾ . ਮੈਂ ਜਾਣਾ ਪੈਂਦਾ ਹੈ → ਮੈਨੂੰ ਜਾਣਾ ਪੈਂਦਾ ਹੈ ; ਮੈਂ ਜਾਣਾ ਸਕਦਾ ਹਾਂ → ਮੈਂ ਜਾ ਸਕਦਾ ਹਾਂ .
CARDS NOW: ਨਾ = not (in commands); no [card]
