# Worksheet: en-th

## LESSON tones — 1 · The five tones — deck "Tones and vowel length"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
This course teaches standard Thai as spoken in Bangkok and understood everywhere in Thailand. The north, the northeast (Isan) and the south have their own dialects; you will hear them, and everyone there also understands standard Thai.
 Pitch is part of the word
 In English, pitch shows feeling: "Really?" goes up because you are surprised, but the word stays the same. In Thai, every syllable has its own pitch pattern, called a tone, and the tone is part of the word. Change the tone and you say a different word. Thai has five tones, and the same sounds maa with three of them give three words:
 EX: มา | maa | to come
 EX: ม้า | máa | horse
 EX: หมา | mǎa | dog
CARDS NOW: มา = to come [card] || ม้า = horse [card] || หมา = dog [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
The five tones and their marks
 Under every Thai word you will see a romanization: Thai sounds spelled in English letters. A small mark over the vowel tells you the tone. The mid tone has no mark.
 Tone ; Mark ; How it sounds ; Example ; 
 mid ; none: a ; level, in the middle of your normal voice ; ไกล ; 
 low ; à ; level, a little lower than normal ; ใหม่ ; 
 falling ; â ; starts high and drops, like a firm "No!" ; ไม่ ; 
 high ; á ; high and tense, rising a little at the end ; ม้า ; 
 rising ; ǎ ; dips low, then rises, like a doubtful "Hmm?" ; หมา ; 
 EX: ใหม่ | mài | new
 EX: ไม่ | mâi | not
 Mid and low are the pair English ears find hardest, because both are level. Low sits clearly lower, as if you were a little bored.
CARDS NOW: ไกล = far [card] || ใหม่ = new [card] || ไม่ = not [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 7)
Pairs to listen for
 ใกล้ glâi with a falling tone means near; ไกล glai with a mid tone means far. Ask for the wrong one and the driver goes the wrong way.
 EX: ใกล้มาก | glâi mâak | very near
 EX: ไกลมาก | glai mâak | very far
 Rice, white and he sound alike to a new ear: ข้าว khâao (falling), ขาว khǎao (rising) and เขา khǎo (rising, with a short vowel).
 EX: ข้าวขาว | khâao khǎao | white rice
CARDS NOW: ใกล้ = near [card] || ใกล้มาก = very near || ไกลมาก = very far [card] || ข้าว = rice [card] || ขาว = white [card] || เขา = he; she; they (one person) [card] || ข้าวขาว = white rice

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
Reading the romanization
 A doubled vowel is a long vowel: aa is held about twice as long as a . Length changes words too (the next lesson).
 Hyphens join the syllables of one word: sà-wàt-dii . A space separates words, and each word is one tile in practice.
 The romanization is a crutch. Listen first, copy the voice, and use the letters only to check.
CARDS NOW: (none)

### SECTION 4   (has 3)
Questions keep their tones
 English raises the voice at the end of a question. Thai does not need to: the small word ไหม mǎi at the end does the asking, and every word keeps its own tone.
 EX: เขามาไหม | khǎo maa mǎi | Is he coming?
 EX: ไกลไหม | glai mǎi | Is it far?
 Most common mistake: saying every word flat, the English way. glai said for near → glâi , with a falling tone. And don't push your voice up on mǎi as in English: it already has its rising tone.
CARDS NOW: ไหม = yes/no question particle [card] || เขามาไหม = Is he coming? || ไกลไหม = Is it far?

## LESSON sounds — 2 · Sounds: aspiration, long and short vowels — deck "Tones and vowel length"
### SECTION 0   (has 6)
Thai has a few sounds English does not have, and a few English habits that change Thai words. This lesson covers the four that matter most.
 A puff of air, or none
 Hold your hand in front of your mouth and say "pie": you feel a puff of air. English puts that puff on every p, t and k at the start of a word. Thai has both kinds, and the puff changes the word. The romanization writes the plain sounds with no puff as bp , dt , g and j , and the puffed ones as ph , th , kh and ch .
 No puff ; With a puff ; 
 ไก่ ; ไข่ ; 
 ปา ; พา ; 
 ตา ; ทา ; 
 EX: ไก่ | gài | chicken
 EX: ไข่ | khài | egg
 EX: ปลา | bplaa | fish
 bp sounds to English ears like something between b and p; say a b, but with tense lips. Two more traps: ph is never f ( ภูเก็ต phuu-gèt is not "Fuket"), and th is a t with a puff, never the th of "think".
 EX: ภูเก็ต | phuu-gèt | Phuket
CARDS NOW: ไก่ = chicken [card] || ไข่ = egg [card] || พา = to take (someone); to lead [card] || ตา = eye [card] || ปลา = fish [card] || ภูเก็ต = Phuket

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
ng at the start of a word
 English has ng only at the end ("sing"). Thai also starts words with it. Say "singing", then drop the "si".
 EX: งู | nguu | snake
CARDS NOW: งู = snake

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
Four new vowels
 ue is like "oo" with your lips spread in a smile: มือ muue . oe is close to the vowel in "her", with no r: เธอ thooe . ae is the a of "cat": แม่ mâae . aw is the vowel of "saw": ขอ khǎaw .
CARDS NOW: มือ = hand [card] || เธอ = she; you (casual) [card] || แม่ = mother; mom [card] || ขอ = to ask for [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 4)
Long and short vowels
 English stretches vowels for feeling. In Thai, vowel length is part of the word: a long vowel (written double) is held about twice as long as a short one.
 EX: ดู | duu | to look
 EX: ดุ | dù | fierce; to scold
 EX: ขาด | khàat | torn
 EX: ขัด | khàt | to scrub
CARDS NOW: ดู = to look; to watch [card] || ดุ = fierce; to scold [card] || ขาด = torn || ขัด = to scrub

### SECTION 4   (has 5)
Final sounds are held
 A Thai word can end in k, t or p, but the sound is not released: close your mouth or tongue and stop, with no little burst after it.
 EX: นก | nók | bird
 EX: บาท | bàat | baht
 In fast, casual speech you will hear r said as l ( รัก rák sounds like lák ) and the r dropped after another consonant ( ครับ khráp sounds like kháp ). This course writes and romanizes the careful form, which everyone understands.
 Most common mistake: an English puff on the plain sounds. ไป bpai (go) said with a puff, like phai → bpai , with tense lips and no air.
CARDS NOW: นก = bird [card] || บาท = baht [card] || รัก = to love [card] || ครับ = yes (male speaker) [card] || ไป = to go [card]

## LESSON greetings — 3 · Hello, thank you, sorry: ครับ and ค่ะ — deck "Greetings and politeness"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
A handful of phrases carries you through a day in Thailand. Each one gets a small polite word at the end, and that word depends on who is speaking.
 The polite particles
 English makes a sentence polite with "please" or a softer voice. Thai adds a polite particle at the end. A man says ครับ khráp . A woman says ค่ะ khâ , with a falling tone, in a statement, and คะ khá , with a high tone, in a question. Pick the set that matches how you want to be heard. Thai has no neutral polite particle; leaving it off sounds casual. In practice, prompts say "(male speaker)" or "(female speaker)".
CARDS NOW: ครับ = yes (male speaker) [card] || ค่ะ = yes (female speaker) [card] || คะ = polite particle in questions (female speaker) [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 6)
Hello and goodbye
 สวัสดี sà-wàt-dii is hello and goodbye, at any time of day.
 EX: สวัสดีครับ | sà-wàt-dii khráp | Hello. (male speaker)
 EX: สวัสดีค่ะ คุณสมชาย | sà-wàt-dii khâ khun sǒm-chaai | Hello, Somchai. (female speaker)
 คุณ khun before a first name is the polite title, for men and women: คุณสมชาย. Thais use first names, never the family name. When a host or anyone older greets you with a wai , palms together, return it. When staff in shops and hotels wai you, a smile and a nod is enough, and nobody expects you to start one. To leave, people say สวัสดี or ไปก่อนนะ bpai gàawn ná . ลาก่อน laa-gàawn means "farewell" and sounds like a book or a final goodbye.
 EX: ไปก่อนนะครับ | bpai gàawn ná khráp | I'm off now. (male speaker)
CARDS NOW: สวัสดีครับ = hello (male speaker) [card] || สวัสดีค่ะ คุณสมชาย = Hello, Somchai. (female speaker) || สวัสดีค่ะ = hello (female speaker) [card] || คุณ = you (polite) [card] || ไปก่อนนะ = I'm off now; bye [card] || ไปก่อนนะครับ = I'm off now. (male speaker)

### SECTION 2   (has 5)
Thank you, sorry, it's OK
 ขอบคุณ khàawp-khun is thank you; มาก mâak after it makes it "very much". ขอโทษ khǎaw-thôot is sorry, and also "excuse me" to get someone's attention. ไม่เป็นไร mâi-bpen-rai means it's OK, never mind, no problem.
 EX: ขอบคุณมากค่ะ | khàawp-khun mâak khâ | Thank you very much. (female speaker)
 EX: ขอโทษครับ | khǎaw-thôot khráp | Sorry. Excuse me. (male speaker)
 EX: ไม่เป็นไรค่ะ | mâi-bpen-rai khâ | It's OK. (female speaker)
 English answers "thank you" with "you're welcome". Thai has no single word for that: people say ไม่เป็นไร, or just smile.
CARDS NOW: มาก = very; a lot [card] || ไม่เป็นไร = it's OK; never mind [card] || ขอบคุณมากค่ะ = Thank you very much. (female speaker) || ขอโทษครับ = sorry; excuse me (male speaker) [card] || ไม่เป็นไรค่ะ = It's OK. (female speaker)

### SECTION 3   (has 7)
Meeting someone, and how are you
 EX: ยินดีที่ได้รู้จักครับ | yin-dii thîi dâai rúu-jàk khráp | Nice to meet you. (male speaker)
 สบายดีไหม sà-baai dii mǎi is a real question, for someone you haven't seen for a while, not a casual "hi". ไหม mǎi at the end makes it a question, so a woman ends it with คะ.
 EX: สบายดีไหมคะ | sà-baai dii mǎi khá | How are you? (female speaker)
 EX: สบายดีครับ แล้วคุณล่ะ | sà-baai dii khráp láaeo khun lâ | I'm fine. And you? (male speaker)
 Most common mistake: ค่ะ in a question. สบายดีไหมค่ะ → สบายดีไหมคะ: a woman's question ends in คะ, with a high tone. And a man copying a female teacher's ค่ะ → ครับ.
CARDS NOW: ยินดีที่ได้รู้จัก = nice to meet you [card] || ยินดีที่ได้รู้จักครับ = Nice to meet you. (male speaker) || ไหม = yes/no question particle [card] || สบายดีไหมคะ = How are you? (female speaker) [card] || สบายดีครับ แล้วคุณล่ะ = I'm fine. And you? (male speaker) || สบายดีครับ = I'm fine. (male speaker) [card] || แล้วคุณล่ะ = And you? [card]

DECK CARDS IN NO SECTION (add by front, as a string, only where a section teaches them): ขอบคุณครับ | khàawp-khun khráp | thank you (male speaker) || ขอบคุณค่ะ | khàawp-khun khâ | thank you (female speaker) || ขอบคุณมากครับ | khàawp-khun mâak khráp | thank you very much (male speaker) || ขอโทษค่ะ | khǎaw-thôot khâ | sorry; excuse me (female speaker) || โชคดีนะ | chôok-dii ná | good luck; take care || ยินดีต้อนรับ | yin-dii-dtâawn-ráp | welcome || ไหว้ | wâai | to wai (greet with palms together)

## LESSON numbers — 4 · Numbers and money — deck "Numbers and money"
### SECTION 0   (has 17)
Thai numbers are very regular: learn ten words and a few rules, and you can say any price. Watch the tones, because three of the numbers have a twin word.
 Zero to ten
 0 ; 1 ; 2 ; 3 ; 4 ; 5 ; 
 ศูนย์ ; หนึ่ง ; สอง ; สาม ; สี่ ; ห้า ; 
 sǔun ; nùeng ; sǎawng ; sǎam ; sìi ; hâa ;

 หก ; เจ็ด ; แปด ; เก้า ; สิบ ; ;

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
Eleven to ninety-nine
 Tens are a number plus สิบ: สามสิบ is thirty. Two exceptions. Twenty is ยี่สิบ yîi-sìp , never สองสิบ. And a 1 at the end of any bigger number is เอ็ด èt , not หนึ่ง (you may hear ยี่สิบหนึ่ง in casual speech, but ยี่สิบเอ็ด is the standard form).
 EX: สิบเอ็ด | sìp-èt | eleven
 EX: ยี่สิบ | yîi-sìp | twenty
 EX: ยี่สิบเอ็ด | yîi-sìp-èt | twenty-one
 EX: ยี่สิบห้า | yîi-sìp-hâa | twenty-five
CARDS NOW: ยี่สิบ = twenty [card] || ยี่สิบเอ็ด = twenty-one [card] || สิบเอ็ด = eleven [card] || ยี่สิบห้า = twenty-five

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
Hundreds and up
 ร้อย ráawi is a hundred and พัน phan a thousand. English stops there and counts thousands, but Thai has its own words for ten thousand, หมื่น mùuen , and a hundred thousand, แสน sǎaen . A million is ล้าน láan . Say the parts from big to small, as in English.
 EX: หนึ่งหมื่น | nùeng-mùuen | ten thousand
 EX: ห้าร้อยบาท | hâa-ráawi bàat | 500 baht
CARDS NOW: ร้อย = hundred [card] || พัน = thousand [card] || หนึ่งหมื่น = ten thousand [card] || ห้าร้อยบาท = 500 baht

### SECTION 3   (has 7)
Prices
 Prices are in baht, บาท bàat , which goes after the number. Price tags and menus use the same digits as English, but you read them in Thai.
 EX: 50 บาท | hâa-sìp bàat | 50 baht
 EX: 1,200 บาท | nùeng-phan-sǎawng-ráawi bàat | 1,200 baht
 To ask the price, say เท่าไร thâo-rai (how much). In speech it is often เท่าไหร่ thâo-rài ; both are right. It goes at the end of the sentence, with your polite particle after it.
 EX: เท่าไหร่คะ | thâo-rài khá | How much is it? (female speaker)
 EX: ทั้งหมดเท่าไรครับ | tháng-mòt thâo-rai khráp | How much is it altogether? (male speaker)
 Most common mistake: building 20 and 21 the English way. สองสิบ → ยี่สิบ, and ยี่สิบหนึ่ง → ยี่สิบเอ็ด.
CARDS NOW: บาท = baht [card] || 50 บาท = 50 baht || 1,200 บาท = 1,200 baht || เท่าไร = how much [card] || ทั้งหมดเท่าไร = How much altogether? [card] || เท่าไหร่คะ = How much is it? (female speaker) || ทั้งหมดเท่าไรครับ = How much is it altogether? (male speaker)

DECK CARDS IN NO SECTION (add by front, as a string, only where a section teaches them): หนึ่งร้อย | nùeng-ráawi | one hundred || หนึ่งพัน | nùeng-phan | one thousand || หนึ่งล้าน | nùeng-láan | one million || ห้าสิบบาท | hâa-sìp bàat | fifty baht || สองร้อยห้าสิบบาท | sǎawng-ráawi-hâa-sìp bàat | 250 baht

## LESSON consonants — 5 · Consonants and the three classes — deck "Consonants: mid and high class"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Thai script looks dense at first, but it is built simply. Each syllable is built on a consonant. The vowel is written around that consonant: before it, after it, above it or below it (the next lesson). Words run together with no spaces; a space marks the end of a phrase, where English would put a comma or a period.
 Letters have names
 The Thai alphabet has 44 consonants, but many share a sound, and about 30 cover almost everything you will read. Each letter has a name: its sound plus aaw , then a word that starts with it, the way English children say "A for apple".
 EX: ก ไก่ | gaaw gài | the letter g, as in chicken
 EX: ข ไข่ | khǎaw khài | the letter kh, as in egg
CARDS NOW: ก ไก่ = letter g, as in gài (chicken) [card] || ข ไข่ = letter kh, as in khài (egg) [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 16)
The three classes
 Every consonant belongs to one of three classes: mid, high or low. The class is not about the sound; it is part of how Thai spells tones, and you need it to read a word's tone.

 EX: จาน | jaan | plate
 EX: เด็ก | dèk | child
 EX: ตา | dtaa | eye
 EX: บ้าน | bâan | house
 EX: ปลา | bplaa | fish
 EX: อาหาร | aa-hǎan | food
 EX: ฉัน | chǎn | I (female speaker)
 EX: ถุง | thǔng | bag
 EX: ผม | phǒm | I (male speaker)
 EX: ฝน | fǒn | rain

### SECTION 2   (has 5)
Same sound, different class
 Several sounds have one high-class letter and one low-class letter. They sound the same; only the tone rules differ.
 Sound ; High class ; Low class ; 
 kh ; ข ; ค ; 
 ch ; ฉ ; ช ; 
 th ; ถ ; ท ;

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
Rare letters
 The other letters, such as ฆ ฌ ญ ฎ ฏ ฐ ฑ ฒ ณ ศ ษ ฬ, appear in a few formal or borrowed words; learn them when you meet them. ฃ and ฅ are no longer used.

## LESSON pronouns — 7 · Pronouns and polite particles — deck "People and family"
### SECTION 0   (has 10)
English needs a pronoun in every sentence: "I'm going." Thai has more pronouns to choose from, and then leaves them out most of the time.
 I and you
 "I" depends on who is speaking. A man says ผม phǒm . A woman says ฉัน chǎn in everyday speech and ดิฉัน dì-chǎn in formal situations. The polite "you" for adults is คุณ khun .
 EX: ผมชื่อไมค์ | phǒm chûue mái | My name is Mike. (male speaker)

 เขา khǎo means he or she, and เรา rao means we. พวก phûuak in front makes a group: พวกเขา phûuak-khǎo , they.
 EX: เขามา | khǎo maa | He's coming. She's coming.
 EX: พวกเขา | phûuak-khǎo | they

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
Leave it out
 Once it is clear who you mean, drop the pronoun. Saying ผม and คุณ in every sentence sounds stiff and foreign.

## LESSON pen — 9 · เป็น, คือ, อยู่: is, and is at — deck "About me"

## LESSON classifiers — 10 · Classifiers: counting things — deck "Counting with classifiers"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
English counts most things directly: two books, three cats. A few English words need a helper: two cups of coffee, three sheets of paper. Thai uses a helper for every noun. The helper is called a classifier , and it tells you what kind of thing you are counting.
 Noun, number, classifier
 English puts the number first. Thai puts the noun first, then the number, then the classifier: "book two volume". Nothing changes on the noun itself; Thai has no plural ending.
 EX: หนังสือสองเล่ม | nǎng-sǔue sǎawng lêm | two books
 EX: แมวสามตัว | maaeo sǎam dtuua | three cats
 EX: หนังสือเก้าเล่ม | nǎng-sǔue gâao lêm | nine books
CARDS NOW: หนังสือสองเล่ม = two books [card] || แมวสามตัว = three cats [card] || หนังสือเก้าเล่ม = nine books

 คัน ; cars, bikes, umbrellas ; รถสองคัน ; 
 แก้ว ; a glass of a drink ; กาแฟสองแก้ว ; 
 ขวด ; a bottle of a drink ; น้ำสามขวด ; 
 จาน ; a plate of food ; ข้าวผัดสองจาน ;

 EX: เพื่อนสี่คน | phûuean sìi khon | four friends
 EX: คนไทยห้าคน | khon thai hâa khon | five Thai people

 EX: กาแฟสองแก้ว | gaa-faae sǎawng gâaeo | two glasses of coffee

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
A describer goes before the number
 A describing word stays right after the noun, and the number and classifier follow it.
 EX: หนังสือเก่าสองเล่ม | nǎng-sǔue gào sǎawng lêm | two old books
CARDS NOW: หนังสือเก่าสองเล่ม = two old books

### SECTION 3   (has 4)
How many? กี่
 To ask "how many", put กี่ gìi where the number would go, before the classifier. มี mii means "to have" or "there is". The short answer is just the number and the classifier.
 EX: คุณมีแมวกี่ตัว | khun mii maaeo gìi dtuua | How many cats do you have?
 EX: สองตัว | sǎawng dtuua | Two.
CARDS NOW: กี่ = how many [card] || มี = to have; there is [card] || คุณมีแมวกี่ตัว = How many cats do you have? || สองตัว = Two.

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
This one
 นี้ níi ("this") also comes after the classifier: อันนี้ an níi is "this one", and the next lessons build on it. With the number one, you will also hear the classifier first ( หนังสือเล่มหนึ่ง , a book); both orders are normal.
 EX: อันนี้ | an níi | this one
 Most common mistake: counting in English order with no classifier. สองหนังสือ → หนังสือสองเล่ม . Say the noun first, then the number, then the classifier.
CARDS NOW: นี้ = this (after the noun) [card]

## LESSON tone-marks — 11 · Tone marks and the tone rules — deck "Vowels and tone marks"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
You can already work out the tone of a syllable with no tone mark: its consonant class and whether it is live or dead decide it. Thai adds four tone marks on top. A mark sits above the consonant that starts the syllable, and what it does depends on that consonant's class. This is the one big surprise for English readers: the same mark gives different tones on different letters.
 The four tone marks
 Mark ; Name ; Used on ; 
 ่ ; ไม้เอก ; all three classes ; 
 ้ ; ไม้โท ; all three classes ; 
 ๊ ; ไม้ตรี ; mid-class letters only ; 
 ๋ ; ไม้จัตวา ; mid-class letters only ;
CARDS NOW: (none)

### SECTION 1   (has 7)
Mid-class letters: four marks, four tones
 On a mid-class letter each mark gives its own tone: ่ low, ้ falling, ๊ high, ๋ rising. ป่า bpàa (forest) and ป้า bpâa (aunt) differ only in the mark.
 EX: ไก่ | gài | chicken
 EX: ป่า | bpàa | forest
 EX: ป้า | bpâa | aunt (a parent's older sister)
 EX: โต๊ะสองตัว | dtó sǎawng dtuua | two tables
 EX: ก๋วยเตี๋ยว | gǔuai-dtǐiao | noodle soup
CARDS NOW: ป่า = forest; the wild [card] || ป้า = aunt; you (to an older woman) [card] || โต๊ะ = table [card] || ตัว = body; classifier for animals and clothes [card] || ไก่ = chicken [card] || โต๊ะสองตัว = two tables || ก๋วยเตี๋ยว = noodle soup

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
High-class letters: low and falling
 High-class letters take only the first two marks, with the same tones as on a mid-class letter: ่ low, ้ falling.
 EX: ไข่ | khài | egg
 EX: ผมกินข้าว | phǒm gin khâao | I eat rice. (male speaker)
CARDS NOW: ไข่ = egg [card] || ผมกินข้าว = I eat rice. (male speaker)

### SECTION 3   (has 4)
Low-class letters: one step higher
 Here is the trap. On a low-class letter, ่ gives a falling tone and ้ gives a high tone. So ไม่ mâi (not) is falling, not low, and ม้า máa (horse) is high.
 EX: ไม่ใช่ม้า | mâi châi máa | It's not a horse.
 EX: น้อง | náawng | younger brother or sister
CARDS NOW: ไม่ = not [card] || ม้า = horse [card] || ไม่ใช่ม้า = It's not a horse. || น้อง = younger sibling; you (to someone younger) [card]

### SECTION 5   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Reading a new word
 Find the first consonant and its class, see whether the syllable has a mark, then read the tone from the chart. Try โต๊ะใหม่ dtó mài (a new table): ๊ on mid-class ต is high; ่ on ใหม่ gives a low tone, because the silent ห in front makes ม high-class (more on that in the lesson on clusters).
 EX: โต๊ะใหม่ | dtó mài | a new table
 Most common mistake: reading ่ as "low tone" everywhere. ไม่ read like ใหม่ (mài) → ไม่ (mâi), falling, because ม is a low-class letter.
CARDS NOW: โต๊ะใหม่ = a new table || ใหม่ = new [card]

## LESSON this-that — 12 · นี้, นั้น, ของ: this, that and whose — deck "Describing things"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
English puts "this", "that" and "my" in front of the noun: this shirt , my house . Thai puts all of them after it. Two pairs of words look almost the same, and only the tone tells them apart.
 Standing alone: นี่, นั่น, โน่น
 When "this" or "that" is the whole subject ("this is…", "that is…"), use the words with ่ and a falling tone: นี่ nîi (this), นั่น nân (that), โน่น nôon (that, over there).
 EX: นี่คือแมวของฉัน | nîi khuue maaeo khǎawng chǎn | This is my cat. (female speaker)
 EX: นั่นคือบ้านของผม | nân khuue bâan khǎawng phǒm | That is my house. (male speaker)
CARDS NOW: นี่ = this (standing alone) [card] || นั่น = that (standing alone) [card] || โน่น = that (over there, standing alone) [card] || นี่คือแมวของฉัน = This is my cat. (female speaker) || นั่นคือบ้านของผม = That is my house. (male speaker)

### SECTION 1   (has 10)
After a noun: noun + classifier + นี้, นั้น, โน้น
 To say "this shirt" or "that book", start with the noun, add its classifier, and end with the word with ้ and a high tone: นี้ níi , นั้น nán , โน้น nóon . With no noun at all, the classifier carries it: อันนี้ , this one.
 EX: เสื้อตัวนี้ | sûuea dtuua níi | this shirt
 EX: หนังสือเล่มนั้น | nǎng-sǔue lêm nán | that book
 EX: รถคันนั้น | rót khan nán | that car
 For people the classifier is คน khon , the same word as "person", so "that person" is just คนนั้น .
 EX: คนนั้นเป็นครู | khon nán bpen khruu | That person is a teacher.
 A few fixed words have นี้ built in with no classifier, such as วันนี้ wan-níi , "today" (literally "day this"). Learn those as single words; for things you can count, keep the classifier.
 A memory aid for the pair: the word that stands alone does the pointing, and its falling tone drops like a pointing finger. The word after a noun just closes the phrase, so it stays high at the end. Same consonant, same vowel: only the tone mark and the tone differ, so look at the mark every time you read one.

 this ; นี่ ; เสื้อตัวนี้ ;

 that, over there ; โน่น ; เสื้อตัวโน้น ;
CARDS NOW: นี้ = this (after the noun) [card] || นั้น = that (after the noun) [card] || โน้น = that over there (after the noun) [card] || อันนี้ = this one [card] || เสื้อตัวนี้ = this shirt [card] || หนังสือเล่มนั้น = that book [card] || รถคันนั้น = that car || คน = classifier for people [card] || คนนั้นเป็นครู = That person is a teacher. || วันนี้ = today [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 6)
Whose: ของ
 English says "my house". Thai says "house of me": บ้านของผม . In everyday speech ของ khǎawng is often dropped, so บ้านผม means the same. Either way, the owner comes after the thing.
 EX: บ้านของผม | bâan khǎawng phǒm | my house (male speaker)
 EX: บ้านผม | bâan phǒm | my house (male speaker, everyday)
 EX: หนังสือของฉัน | nǎng-sǔue khǎawng chǎn | my book (female speaker)
 To ask "whose?", use ของใคร khǎawng khrai , "of whom". To answer "it's mine", say ของ + the owner.
 EX: นี่ของใคร | nîi khǎawng khrai | Whose is this?
 EX: ของผมครับ | khǎawng phǒm khráp | It's mine. (male speaker)
 Most common mistake: English order. นี้เสื้อ → เสื้อตัวนี้ , and ผมของบ้าน → บ้านของผม . The thing comes first; this, that and the owner come after it.
CARDS NOW: ของ = of; belonging to [card] || บ้านของผม = my house (male speaker) || บ้านผม = my house (male speaker) [card] || หนังสือของฉัน = my book (female speaker) || นี่ของใคร = Whose is this? || ของผมครับ = It's mine. (male speaker)

## LESSON mai — 13 · ไม่ and ไหม: not, and yes/no questions — deck "Traps for English speakers"
### SECTION 0   (has 6)
English makes a negative with "do not" and a question by moving words around: "You like it" becomes "Do you like it?". Thai changes nothing in the sentence. It adds one small word: ไม่ in front for "not", ไหม at the end for a yes/no question.
 ไม่: not, before the verb
 Put ไม่ mâi right before the verb or the describing word. There is no "do": "not like" is simply ไม่ชอบ .
 EX: ผมไม่ชอบกาแฟ | phǒm mâi châawp gaa-faae | I don't like coffee. (male speaker)
 EX: อาหารไม่เผ็ด | aa-hǎan mâi phèt | The food isn't spicy.
 To say something is not a noun ("not a teacher"), keep using ไม่ใช่ from the lesson on เป็น.
CARDS NOW: ไม่ = not [card] || ไหม = yes/no question particle [card] || เผ็ด = spicy [card] || ผมไม่ชอบกาแฟ = I don't like coffee. (male speaker) || อาหารไม่เผ็ด = The food isn't spicy. || ไม่ใช่ = no; it isn't [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
ไหม: a yes/no question at the end
 Add ไหม mǎi to the end of a statement and it becomes a yes/no question; a polite particle can follow it. Your voice does not rise as in English: every word keeps its own tone, and ไหม does the asking. In speech you will often hear ไหม said with a high tone.
 EX: เผ็ดไหม | phèt mǎi | Is it spicy?
 EX: ชอบไหมคะ | châawp mǎi khá | Do you like it? (female speaker)
CARDS NOW: เผ็ดไหม = Is it spicy? || ชอบไหมคะ = Do you like it? (female speaker)

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
Answer with the verb
 Thai has no all-purpose "yes". To say yes, repeat the verb or describing word from the question. To say no, put ไม่ in front of it.
 Question ; Yes ; No ; 
 ชอบไหม ; ชอบ ; ไม่ชอบ ; 
 เผ็ดไหม ; เผ็ด ; ไม่เผ็ด ; 
 ไปไหม ; ไป ; ไม่ไป ; 
 EX: ไม่ชอบครับ | mâi châawp khráp | No, I don't (like it). (male speaker)
CARDS NOW: ชอบ = to like [card] || ไม่เผ็ด = not spicy [card] || ไป = to go [card] || ไม่ชอบครับ = No, I don't (like it). (male speaker)

### SECTION 3   (has 3)
ใช่: only for "is it…?"
 ใช่ châi means "that's right". Use it to confirm a fact, often after a question that ends in ใช่ไหม ("…, right?"). ไม่ใช่ means "no, that's not it".
 EX: คุณเป็นครูใช่ไหม | khun bpen khruu châi mǎi | You're a teacher, right?
 EX: ใช่ค่ะ | châi khâ | Yes, that's right. (female speaker)
CARDS NOW: ใช่ = yes; that's right [card] || คุณเป็นครูใช่ไหม = You're a teacher, right? || ใช่ค่ะ = Yes, that's right. (female speaker)

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
หรือเปล่า: "or not?"
 หรือเปล่า rǔue-bplàao at the end also asks a yes/no question, a bit like "…or not?". It is very common in speech, and you answer it the same way, with the verb.
 EX: ไปตลาดหรือเปล่า | bpai dtà-làat rǔue-bplàao | Are you going to the market?
CARDS NOW: หรือเปล่า = …or not? [card] || ไปตลาดหรือเปล่า = Are you going to the market?

## LESSON clusters — 14 · Clusters and leading consonants — deck "Directions and places"
### SECTION 0   (has 8)
Some Thai words start with two consonant letters. Sometimes both are said together, sometimes the first one is silent, and sometimes a vowel hides between them. This lesson shows how to tell which is which.
 True clusters: two letters, one syllable
 Like English cl , pr or tr , Thai can start a syllable with a consonant plus ร, ล or ว: ครับ khráp , ปลา bplaa (fish), ขวา khwǎa (right). The tone follows the class of the first letter, and a tone mark sits over the second one, as in ใกล้ glâi (near).
 EX: เลี้ยวขวา | líiao khwǎa | Turn right.
 EX: ตรงไป | dtrong bpai | Go straight.
 EX: ตลาดอยู่ใกล้ๆ | dtà-làat yùu glâi-glâi | The market is close by.
 EX: ไม่ไกล | mâi glai | It's not far.
 In casual speech many people drop the ร or ล of a cluster, so you will hear ครับ said without the r. The course writes and romanizes the full form.
CARDS NOW: ครับ = yes (male speaker) [card] || ปลา = fish [card] || ตลาด = market [card] || ใกล้ = near [card] || เลี้ยวขวา = turn right [card] || ตรงไป = go straight [card] || ตลาดอยู่ใกล้ๆ = The market is close by. || ไม่ไกล = It's not far.

### SECTION 1   (has 7)
Leading ห: silent, but it changes the tone
 A ห written before a low-class letter that has no high-class partner (ง ญ น ม ย ร ล ว) is not said. Its job is to make that letter high class, so the tone rules for high-class letters apply. หมา mǎa (dog) has a rising tone; มา (come) is mid.
 EX: หมาตัวใหญ่ | mǎa dtuua yài | a big dog
 EX: ห้องน้ำอยู่ที่ไหน | hâwng-náam yùu thîi-nǎi | Where is the bathroom?
 The same silent ห is in หนึ่ง (one), ไหน (where, which) and ใหญ่ (big).

### SECTION 3   (has 3)
Hidden vowels
 When two consonants cannot form a cluster, a short a is said between them but not written. The second syllable then takes the class of the first letter: ตลาด dtà-làat is read ตะ-หลาด, so ลาด gets the low tone of a mid-class dead syllable. The same happens in สบาย sà-baai , ขนม khà-nǒm and ถนน thà-nǒn (road), where นม and นน take the rising tone of high-class ข and ถ.
 EX: ถนนนี้ | thà-nǒn níi | this road

CARDS NOW: สบาย = comfortable; well [card] || ถนน = road; street [card] || ถนนนี้ = this road

## LESSON question-words — 15 · Question words stay in place — deck "Questions and requests"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
English moves the question word to the front: "You are eating rice" becomes " What are you eating?". Thai leaves everything where it was and puts the question word in the slot where the answer will go. Ask the question, and the answer simply replaces the question word.
 This makes Thai questions easy to build. Start from the statement you would expect as an answer, then swap the unknown part for a question word. "The bathroom is over there " becomes "The bathroom is where ?". You never need a helper like "do" or "does", and the verb never changes. Most questions end with the question word or with a polite particle after it, so listen for the end of the sentence.
 What: อะไร
 อะไร à-rai sits where the thing would be. The answer uses the same order.
 EX: คุณกินอะไร | khun gin à-rai | What are you eating?
 EX: ผมกินข้าวผัด | phǒm gin khâao-phàt | I'm eating fried rice. (male speaker)
 EX: คุณชื่ออะไร | khun chûue à-rai | What's your name?
CARDS NOW: อะไร = what; something [card] || คุณกินอะไร = What are you eating? || ผมกินข้าวผัด = I'm eating fried rice. (male speaker) || คุณชื่ออะไร = What's your name? [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
Who: ใคร
 ใคร khrai goes where the person would be: at the end for "who is that?", or after the verb for "who are you waiting for?".
 EX: คนนั้นคือใคร | khon nán khuue khrai | Who is that?
 EX: คุณรอใคร | khun raaw khrai | Who are you waiting for?
CARDS NOW: ใคร = who; someone [card] || คนนั้นคือใคร = Who is that? || คุณรอใคร = Who are you waiting for?

 Silent letters under ์

## LESSON food — 17 · Ordering food and drink — deck "Food and drink"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
Thai food is one of the best reasons to learn Thai, and ordering it takes only a few patterns you already know: the classifier, ไม่ and the polite particles.
 Asking for a dish: ขอ and เอา
 English says "Can I have…" or "I'll have…". Thai says ขอ khǎaw (may I have) + the dish, or the more casual เอา ao (I'll take) + the dish. Add the number and classifier after it, and ครับ or ค่ะ at the end. Dishes on a plate are counted with จาน jaan .
 EX: ขอผัดไทยสองจานครับ | khǎaw phàt-thai sǎawng jaan khráp | Two pad thai, please. (male speaker)
 EX: เอาส้มตำไม่เผ็ดค่ะ | ao sôm-dtam mâi phèt khâ | I'll have papaya salad, not spicy. (female speaker)
 Dish names follow the same order as the rest of Thai: the main word first, then what describes it. ข้าวผัดกุ้ง is "rice, fried, shrimp", shrimp fried rice. So when you read a menu, the first word tells you what kind of dish it is (rice, noodles, curry), and the words after it tell you how it is cooked and what is in it. You do not need "a" or "the", and there is no plural: the number and classifier do that job.
CARDS NOW: ขอ = to ask for [card] || เอา = to take; to want (a thing) [card] || ไม่เผ็ด = not spicy [card] || จาน = classifier for plates of food [card] || ขอผัดไทยสองจานครับ = Two pad thai, please. (male speaker) || เอาส้มตำไม่เผ็ดค่ะ = I'll have papaya salad, not spicy. (female speaker) || ข้าวผัดกุ้ง = shrimp fried rice [card]

## LESSON compare — 19 · Describing and comparing: กว่า, ที่สุด — deck "Describing things"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
You already know that Thai describing words work like verbs and need no เป็น bpen . This lesson puts them to work: where they sit, how to make them stronger, and how to compare two things.
 The describer comes after the noun
 English says "a big house", with the describer first. Thai says "house big": บ้านใหญ่ bâan yài . Colors work the same way, with สี sǐi (color) in front of the color word.
 EX: บ้านใหญ่ | bâan yài | a big house
 EX: รถสีแดง | rót sǐi daaeng | a red car
 With a classifier and นี้ níi , the order is noun, describer, classifier, นี้: เสื้อสีแดงตัวนี้ , this red shirt.
CARDS NOW: เป็น = to be (a job, role, nationality) [card] || บ้านใหญ่ = a big house [card] || สี = color [card] || รถสีแดง = a red car [card] || นี้ = this (after the noun) [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
มาก comes after, too
 English puts "very" before the word: "very beautiful". Thai puts มาก mâak after it: สวยมาก , "beautiful very".
 EX: อาหารร้านนี้อร่อยมาก | aa-hǎan ráan níi à-ràwi mâak | The food at this restaurant is very good.
CARDS NOW: มาก = very; a lot [card] || สวยมาก = very beautiful [card] || อาหารร้านนี้อร่อยมาก = The food at this restaurant is very good.

### SECTION 2   (has 5)
More than: กว่า
 English has two ways to compare: "-er" (cheaper) and "more" (more expensive). Thai has one: put กว่า gwàa right after the describing word. There is no word for "more" in front of it. To say what you compare with, add it after กว่า , the way English adds "than".
 EX: เสื้อตัวนี้แพงกว่าตัวนั้น | sûuea dtuua níi phaaeng gwàa dtuua nán | This shirt is more expensive than that one.
 EX: กรุงเทพฯร้อนกว่าเชียงใหม่ | grung-thêep ráawn gwàa chiiang-mài | Bangkok is hotter than Chiang Mai.
 Near and far are easy to swap: ใกล้กว่า glâi gwàa (falling tone) is closer, ไกลกว่า glai gwàa (mid tone) is farther.
CARDS NOW: กว่า = more (than) [card] || เสื้อตัวนี้ = this shirt [card] || แพงกว่า = more expensive [card] || เสื้อตัวนี้แพงกว่าตัวนั้น = This shirt is more expensive than that one. || กรุงเทพฯร้อนกว่าเชียงใหม่ = Bangkok is hotter than Chiang Mai.

### SECTION 3   (has 3)
The most: ที่สุด
 For "the cheapest" or "the most beautiful", put ที่สุด thîi-sùt after the describing word. Again, nothing goes in front.
 EX: ร้านนี้ถูกที่สุด | ráan níi thùuk thîi-sùt | This shop is the cheapest.
 EX: รถคันนี้แพงที่สุด | rót khan níi phaaeng thîi-sùt | This car is the most expensive.
CARDS NOW: ที่สุด = most [card] || ร้านนี้ถูกที่สุด = This shop is the cheapest. || รถคันนี้แพงที่สุด = This car is the most expensive.

### SECTION 4   (has 4)
The same: เท่ากัน and เหมือนกัน
 เท่ากัน thâo-gan after a describing word means "equally": the same price, the same size. เหมือนกัน mǔuean-gan means "alike, the same", and in conversation it is also a friendly "me too".
 EX: เสื้อสองตัวนี้ราคาเท่ากัน | sûuea sǎawng dtuua níi raa-khaa thâo-gan | These two shirts are the same price.
 EX: กระเป๋าสองใบนี้เหมือนกัน | grà-bpǎo sǎawng bai níi mǔuean-gan | These two bags are the same.
 Most common mistake: copying "more expensive" word for word. มากกว่าแพง → แพงกว่า . The describing word comes first, then กว่า , and Thai needs no "more".
CARDS NOW: เท่ากัน = equally [card] || เหมือนกัน = alike; the same [card] || เสื้อสองตัวนี้ราคาเท่ากัน = These two shirts are the same price. || กระเป๋าสองใบนี้เหมือนกัน = These two bags are the same.

## LESSON time-words — 21 · Time words: แล้ว, กำลัง, จะ — deck "Time and days"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
English changes the verb for time: go, went, gone, going. Thai verbs never change; time words do the work. ไป bpai is "go", "went" and "going", and the words around it say when.
 Past: just say when
 A time word such as เมื่อวาน mûuea-waan (yesterday) is enough. The verb stays as it is. If the time is already clear from the conversation, Thai often says nothing more at all, and the listener understands.
 EX: เมื่อวานฉันไปตลาด | mûuea-waan chǎn bpai dtà-làat | Yesterday I went to the market. (female speaker)
CARDS NOW: ไป = to go [card] || เมื่อวาน = yesterday [card] || เมื่อวานฉันไปตลาด = Yesterday I went to the market. (female speaker)

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
Later: จะ before the verb
 จะ jà goes right before the verb for something that will happen. With a time word like พรุ่งนี้ (tomorrow) it is often left out.
 EX: ผมจะไปภูเก็ต | phǒm jà bpai phuu-gèt | I'm going to go to Phuket. (male speaker)
CARDS NOW: จะ = will (future) [card] || พรุ่งนี้ = tomorrow [card] || ผมจะไปภูเก็ต = I'm going to go to Phuket. (male speaker)

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
Now: กำลัง … อยู่
 For something happening right now, put กำลัง gam-lang before the verb, อยู่ yùu after it, or both. All three are natural.
 EX: เขากำลังกินข้าวอยู่ | khǎo gam-lang gin khâao yùu | He's having a meal right now.
CARDS NOW: กำลัง = in the middle of (-ing) [card] || อยู่ = to be at; to live; to stay [card] || เขากำลังกินข้าวอยู่ = He's having a meal right now.

### SECTION 3   (has 3)
Done: แล้ว at the end
 แล้ว láaeo at the end means "already, done". Use it when the point is that something is finished, not on every past verb: "Yesterday I went to the market" needs no แล้ว. It is also the everyday "already" in short answers: อิ่มแล้ว , I'm full now.
 EX: กินแล้ว | gin láaeo | I've already eaten.
CARDS NOW: แล้ว = already; now (done) [card] || อิ่มแล้ว = I'm full [card] || กินแล้ว = I've already eaten. [card]

### SECTION 4   (has 3)
Ever: เคย
 เคย khooei before the verb means "have ever". Answer with เคย (yes, I have) or ไม่เคย (never).
 EX: เคยไปเชียงใหม่ไหม | khooei bpai chiiang-mài mǎi | Have you ever been to Chiang Mai?
 EX: ไม่เคย | mâi khooei | No, never.
CARDS NOW: เคย = ever; have done [card] || ไม่เคย = No, never. || เคยไปเชียงใหม่ไหม = Have you ever been to Chiang Mai?

### SECTION 5   (has 5)
Yet: ยัง and หรือยัง
 หรือยัง rǔue-yang at the end asks "… yet?". Answer with the verb and แล้ว for yes, or with ยัง yang (not yet). ยังไม่ before a verb means "not … yet". Thais often greet friends with กินข้าวหรือยัง ; it is a friendly hello, not an invitation.
 EX: กินข้าวหรือยัง | gin khâao rǔue-yang | Have you eaten yet?
 EX: เขายังไม่มา | khǎo yang mâi maa | He hasn't come yet.
 Most common mistake: answering a yes/no question with ใช่. กินข้าวหรือยัง , answered ใช่ → กินแล้ว or ยัง . ใช่ only confirms "is it …?".

## LESSON can-want — 22 · ได้ and อยาก: can and want — deck "Getting around"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
English puts "can", "want" and "must" before the verb. Thai does that with "want" and "must", but "can" goes at the end. That one difference causes most of the mistakes in this lesson.
 Want: อยาก + verb
 อยาก yàak goes before a verb: "want to go", "want to eat". The verb is needed: Thai does not say "want the beach". For "don't want", put ไม่ in front: ไม่อยากไป , I don't want to go. To turn down an offer of help, ไม่ต้อง with ครับ or ค่ะ does the job; it comes up below.
 EX: อยากไปทะเล | yàak bpai thá-lee | I want to go to the beach.
 To want a thing, use อยากได้ yàak dâai , literally "want to get".
 EX: ฉันอยากได้กระเป๋าใบนี้ | chǎn yàak dâai grà-bpǎo bai níi | I want this bag. (female speaker)
CARDS NOW: อยาก = to want to [card] || ไม่ = not [card] || อยากไปทะเล = I want to go to the beach. || ฉันอยากได้กระเป๋าใบนี้ = I want this bag. (female speaker)

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
Can: verb + ได้
 English says "I can speak Thai". Thai says "speak Thai can": ได้ dâai comes after the verb and what follows it. For "can't", put ไม่ before it: ไม่ได้ .
 EX: ผมพูดภาษาไทยได้นิดหน่อย | phǒm phûut phaa-sǎa thai dâai nít-nàwi | I can speak a little Thai. (male speaker)
 EX: ไปไม่ได้ | bpai mâi dâai | I can't go.
 For skills you learned, like driving or swimming, you will also hear เป็น in the same place: ขับรถไม่เป็น , "I don't know how to drive".
 Watch the position. ได้ before a verb means "got to": ได้ไป is "got to go, had the chance to go", not "can go".
CARDS NOW: ได้ = can; to get [card] || ผมพูดภาษาไทยได้นิดหน่อย = I can speak a little Thai. (male speaker) || ไปไม่ได้ = I can't go. || เป็น = to be (a job, role, nationality) [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Asking: ได้ไหม
 Add ได้ไหม dâai mǎi at the end to ask "can you …?" or "can I …?". Answer with ได้ (yes) or ไม่ได้ (no), never with ใช่.
 EX: ว่ายน้ำได้ไหม | wâai-náam dâai mǎi | Can you swim?
 EX: รอที่นี่ได้ไหม | raaw thîi-nîi dâai mǎi | Can you wait here?
CARDS NOW: ว่ายน้ำได้ไหม = Can you swim? || รอที่นี่ได้ไหม = Can you wait here? [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 3)
Must: ต้อง + verb
 ต้อง dtâwng goes before the verb, like "have to". ไม่ต้อง means "you don't have to", a handy "no need" when someone offers help.
 EX: ต้องไปแล้ว | dtâwng bpai láaeo | I have to go now.
 EX: ต้องเปลี่ยนรถไหม | dtâwng bplìian rót mǎi | Do I have to change?
 Most common mistake: putting "can" first, as in English. ได้พูดภาษาไทย for "I can speak Thai" → พูดภาษาไทยได้ . ได้ goes after the verb.
CARDS NOW: ต้อง = to have to; must [card] || ต้องไปแล้ว = I have to go now. || ต้องเปลี่ยนรถไหม = Do I have to change? [card]

## LESSON requests — 23 · Asking politely: ขอ, ช่วย, หน่อย — deck "Questions and requests"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
English makes requests polite with "please", "could you" and "may I". Thai has no single everyday word for "please". Instead it uses a few small words around the verb, and they matter: a bare verb sounds like an order.
 May I have: ขอ + thing
 ขอ khǎaw asks for something: "may I have". Add หน่อย nàwi (a little) at the end to make it softer, and finish with ครับ or ค่ะ.
 EX: ขอน้ำหน่อย | khǎaw náam nàwi | Could I have some water?
 EX: ขอเมนูหน่อยครับ | khǎaw mee-nuu nàwi khráp | Could I have the menu, please? (male speaker)
 ขอ + a verb asks permission to do something yourself.
 EX: ขอดูหน่อย | khǎaw duu nàwi | Could I have a look?
CARDS NOW: ขอ = to ask for [card] || หน่อย = a little; please (softens a request) [card] || ขอน้ำหน่อย = Could I have some water? [card] || ขอเมนูหน่อยครับ = Could I have the menu, please? (male speaker) || ขอดูหน่อย = Could I have a look?

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
Could you: ช่วย + verb + หน่อย
 To ask someone to do something, start with ช่วย chûuai (help) and end with หน่อย . Add ได้ไหม at the very end to make it a question, even more polite: "could you …?".
 EX: ช่วยพูดช้าๆหน่อย | chûuai phûut cháa-cháa nàwi | Could you speak slowly, please?
 EX: ช่วยเปิดแอร์หน่อยได้ไหม | chûuai bpòoet aae nàwi dâai mǎi | Could you turn on the air conditioning, please?
CARDS NOW: ช่วย = to help [card] || ช่วยพูดช้าๆหน่อย = Could you speak slowly, please? [card] || ช่วยเปิดแอร์หน่อยได้ไหม = Could you turn on the air conditioning, please?

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
Softeners: นะ and ด้วย
 นะ ná at the end makes a request friendly, like "OK?". ด้วย dûuai after a request is a polite "please" in fixed phrases, as when you ask for the bill.
 EX: รอก่อนนะ | raaw gàawn ná | Wait a moment, OK?
 EX: เช็คบิลด้วย | chék-bin dûuai | Check, please.
CARDS NOW: นะ = softener: …, OK? [card] || ด้วย = too; please (at the end of a request) [card] || รอก่อนนะ = Wait a moment, OK? || เช็คบิลด้วย = check, please [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 4)
Don't, and go ahead
 อย่า yàa before a verb means "don't". It is not the same as ไม่ , which only says "not". เชิญ chooen invites: "go ahead, after you, please sit".
 EX: อย่าลืมนะ | yàa luuem ná | Don't forget, OK?
 EX: เชิญครับ | chooen khráp | Go ahead. (male speaker)
CARDS NOW: ไม่ = not [card] || เชิญ = to invite; please, go ahead [card] || อย่าลืมนะ = Don't forget! [card] || เชิญครับ = Go ahead. (male speaker)

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
What about กรุณา?
 Dictionaries give กรุณา gà-rú-naa for "please". It is formal: you see it on signs and hear it in announcements, but people rarely say it to each other. In a shop or a taxi, ขอ and ช่วย are what you need.
 EX: กรุณาปิดประตู | gà-rú-naa bpìt bprà-dtuu | Please close the door. (on a sign)
 Most common mistake: giving a bare order. เปิดแอร์ for "Could you turn on the air conditioning?" → ช่วยเปิดแอร์หน่อย , with ครับ or ค่ะ. ช่วย and หน่อย turn an order into a request.
CARDS NOW: กรุณาปิดประตู = Please close the door. (on a sign)

## LESSON directions — 24 · Directions, places and transport — deck "Directions and places"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
Getting around in Thai needs only a handful of words, but two habits from English get in the way: putting "where" first, and adding "to" after "go".
 Where is it? Place + อยู่ที่ไหน
 English starts with "where". Thai starts with the place, then อยู่ yùu (is located), then ที่ไหน thîi-nǎi (where), at the end, in the slot where the answer goes.
 EX: ห้องน้ำอยู่ที่ไหน | hâwng-náam yùu thîi-nǎi | Where is the bathroom?
 EX: ตลาดอยู่ไกลไหม | dtà-làat yùu glai mǎi | Is the market far?
CARDS NOW: อยู่ = to be at; to live; to stay [card] || ที่ไหน = where [card] || ตลาด = market [card] || ห้องน้ำอยู่ที่ไหน = Where is the bathroom? [card] || ตลาดอยู่ไกลไหม = Is the market far?

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
Near and far: listen to the tone
 ใกล้ glâi with a falling tone is near; ไกล glai with a mid tone is far. They sound almost the same to English ears, so say the falling tone clearly. ใกล้ๆ means "close by, nearby".
 EX: ธนาคารอยู่ใกล้ๆ | thá-naa-khaan yùu glâi-glâi | The bank is close by.
CARDS NOW: ใกล้ = near [card] || ไกล = far [card] || ธนาคารอยู่ใกล้ๆ = The bank is close by. [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 12)
Left, right, straight
 ซ้าย sáai is left and ขวา khwǎa is right. เลี้ยว líiao means turn, and ตรงไป dtrong bpai means go straight. To say where to turn, add ที่ and the place. If you are lost, ไปทางไหน asks "which way?", and pointing helps too.
 EX: ตรงไป | dtrong bpai | go straight
 EX: เลี้ยวซ้ายที่ไฟแดง | líiao sáai thîi fai-daaeng | Turn left at the traffic light.
 ข้างหน้า khâang-nâa is "ahead, in front", and ตรงข้าม dtrong-khâam is "across from". Both go before the place, like English prepositions.
 EX: อยู่ตรงข้ามโรงแรม | yùu dtrong-khâam roong-raaem | It's across from the hotel.
CARDS NOW: เลี้ยว = to turn [card] || ตรงไป = go straight [card] || ที่ = at; in (a place) [card] || ไปทางไหน = Which way? [card] || เลี้ยวซ้าย = turn left [card] || ไฟแดง = traffic light [card] || เลี้ยวซ้ายที่ไฟแดง = Turn left at the traffic light. || ข้างหน้า = in front of; ahead [card] || ตรงข้าม = opposite; across from [card] || อยู่ตรงข้าม = It's across the street. [card] || โรงแรม = hotel [card] || อยู่ตรงข้ามโรงแรม = It's across from the hotel.

### SECTION 3   (has 4)
In a taxi or a tuk-tuk
 English says "go to the airport". Thai needs no word for "to": just ไป bpai + the place. In a taxi, ask for the meter; in a tuk-tuk, agree on the price before you get in. When you arrive, จอดตรงนี้ means "stop here".
 EX: ไปสนามบิน | bpai sà-nǎam-bin | to the airport
 EX: เปิดมิเตอร์ได้ไหม | bpòoet mí-dtôoe dâai mǎi | Can you use the meter?
 EX: จอดตรงนี้ | jàawt dtrong-níi | stop here
 Add ครับ or ค่ะ to each of these, and the driver will hear a polite passenger.
 Most common mistake: asking in English order. ที่ไหนห้องน้ำ → ห้องน้ำอยู่ที่ไหน . The place comes first, and ที่ไหน stays at the end.
CARDS NOW: ไป = to go [card] || จอดตรงนี้ = stop here [card] || ไปสนามบิน = to the airport [card] || เปิดมิเตอร์ได้ไหม = Can you use the meter? [card]

## LESSON shopping — 25 · Shopping: clothes and colors — deck "Shopping and colors"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
Shopping puts together three things you already know: question words, classifiers and describers after the noun. This lesson adds the words for clothes, colors and paying.
 How much?
 ราคาเท่าไร raa-khaa thâo-rai asks the price; you will often hear เท่าไหร่ . Point at the item with noun + classifier + นี้ .
 EX: เสื้อตัวนี้ราคาเท่าไร | sûuea dtuua níi raa-khaa thâo-rai | How much is this shirt?
 At markets, polite bargaining is normal; in malls and shops with price tags it is not. Ask with a smile:
 EX: ลดได้ไหม | lót dâai mǎi | Can you lower the price?
CARDS NOW: ราคาเท่าไร = How much is it? [card] || นี้ = this (after the noun) [card] || เสื้อตัวนี้ราคาเท่าไร = How much is this shirt? || ลดได้ไหม = Can you lower the price? [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 6)
Clothes and their classifiers
 Each kind of thing has its classifier. English counts pants as "a pair"; Thai counts them like shirts, with ตัว dtuua . Shoes really come in pairs: คู่ khûu .
 Thing ; Thai ; Classifier ; 
 shirt ; เสื้อ ; ตัว ; 
 pants ; กางเกง ; ตัว ; 
 skirt ; กระโปรง ; ตัว ; 
 shoes ; รองเท้า ; คู่ ; 
 hat, bag ; หมวก, กระเป๋า ; ใบ ; 
 EX: รองเท้าคู่นี้ | raawng-tháao khûu níi | this pair of shoes
CARDS NOW: ตัว = body; classifier for animals and clothes [card] || คู่ = classifier for pairs [card] || เสื้อ = shirt; top [card] || รองเท้า = shoes [card] || ใบ = classifier for bags, tickets, leaves [card] || รองเท้าคู่นี้ = this pair of shoes [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 5)
Colors after the noun
 A color is a describer, so it comes after the noun, with สี sǐi (color) in front. With a classifier, the order is noun, color, classifier, นี้.
 EX: เสื้อสีแดง | sûuea sǐi daaeng | a red shirt
 EX: กางเกงสีดำตัวนี้ | gaang-geeng sǐi dam dtuua níi | these black pants
CARDS NOW: สี = color [card] || สีแดง = red [card] || สีดำ = black [card] || เสื้อสีแดง = a red shirt || กางเกงสีดำตัวนี้ = these black pants

### SECTION 3   (has 6)
Trying on: too big, too small
 ลองได้ไหม asks "can I try it on?". After a describing word, ไป bpai means "too": ใหญ่ไป is too big, แพงไป too expensive.
 EX: ลองได้ไหม | laawng dâai mǎi | Can I try it on?
 EX: แพงไป | phaaeng bpai | too expensive
 Careful with ฟิต fít : it does not mean "fits well". It means tight.
 EX: กางเกงตัวนี้ฟิต | gaang-geeng dtuua níi fít | These pants are too tight.
CARDS NOW: ลองได้ไหม = Can I try it on? [card] || ไป = to go [card] || ใหญ่ไป = too big [card] || แพงไป = too expensive [card] || ฟิต = tight (clothes) [card] || กางเกงตัวนี้ฟิต = These pants are too tight.

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Paying
 Most shops and many stalls take QR payment from a banking app; you can also pay in cash, เงินสด ngoen-sòt . If you are only browsing, ดูก่อน ("look first") with ครับ or ค่ะ tells the seller you are just looking.
 EX: สแกนได้ไหม | sà-gaaen dâai mǎi | Can I pay by QR code?
 Most common mistake: the color in front, as in English. สีแดงเสื้อ → เสื้อสีแดง . The noun comes first, then สี and the color.
CARDS NOW: ดูก่อน = I'm just looking. [card] || สแกนได้ไหม = Can I pay by QR code? [card]

## LESSON feelings — 26 · Feelings and health — deck "Health and feelings"
### SECTION 0   (has 10)
English says "I am tired", "I have a headache", "I have a cold". Thai sorts these differently: feelings need no verb at all, pains need no "have", and only illnesses take เป็น bpen .
 Feelings: no เป็น
 Feelings are describing verbs, like หิว (hungry). Put them straight after the person.
 EX: ผมเหนื่อย | phǒm nùueai | I'm tired. (male speaker)
 EX: ฉันดีใจมาก | chǎn dii-jai mâak | I'm very glad. (female speaker)
 Others to know: เสียใจ sǐia-jai (sad, sorry about something), เครียด khrîiat (stressed), ง่วงนอน ngûuang-naawn (sleepy). ไม่สบาย mâi-sà-baai , literally "not comfortable", is the everyday word for "sick".
CARDS NOW: เป็น = to be (a job, role, nationality) [card] || เหนื่อย = tired [card] || หิว = hungry [card] || ดีใจ = glad, happy [card] || ผมเหนื่อย = I'm tired. (male speaker) || ฉันดีใจมาก = I'm very glad. (female speaker) || เสียใจ = sad; sorry about something [card] || เครียด = stressed [card] || ง่วงนอน = sleepy [card] || ไม่สบาย = sick, unwell [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
What hurts: ปวด and เจ็บ
 English says "I have a headache". Thai says "ache head": ปวด bpùuat + the body part, with no "have". ปวด is a dull ache inside; เจ็บ jèp is a sharp or sore pain, like a sore throat or a cut.
 EX: ปวดหัว | bpùuat-hǔua | headache; my head hurts
 EX: ปวดท้อง | bpùuat-tháawng | stomachache
 EX: เจ็บคอ | jèp khaaw | sore throat
CARDS NOW: เจ็บ = to hurt (sharp pain) [card] || ปวดหัว = headache; my head hurts [card] || ปวดท้อง = stomachache [card] || เจ็บคอ = sore throat [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 6)
Illnesses: เป็น + illness
 Of feelings, pains and illnesses, only illnesses take เป็น : you "are" a cold, you "are" a fever. A fever is ไข้ khâi , with a falling tone; with a low tone, ไข่ is an egg. You will also hear มีไข้ . ร้อน (hot) alone means hot weather or a hot stove, so for a fever say เป็นไข้ or มีไข้.
 EX: ผมเป็นหวัด | phǒm bpen wàt | I have a cold. (male speaker)
 EX: ฉันเป็นไข้ | chǎn bpen khâi | I have a fever. (female speaker)
CARDS NOW: ไข่ = egg [card] || ร้อน = hot (weather, to the touch) [card] || เป็นหวัด = to have a cold [card] || เป็นไข้ = to have a fever [card] || ผมเป็นหวัด = I have a cold. (male speaker) || ฉันเป็นไข้ = I have a fever. (female speaker)

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Allergies: แพ้
 แพ้ pháae + the thing means "allergic to". It is worth knowing before you order food.
 EX: แพ้กุ้ง | pháae gûng | I'm allergic to shrimp.
CARDS NOW: แพ้ = to lose; to be allergic to [card] || แพ้กุ้ง = I'm allergic to shrimp.

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
At the pharmacy
 Pharmacies, ร้านขายยา ráan-khǎai-yaa ("shop selling medicine"), are everywhere, and pharmacists help with small problems. Say what is wrong, then ask มียาไหม : "do you have medicine?".
 EX: ร้านขายยาอยู่ใกล้ไหม | ráan-khǎai-yaa yùu glâi mǎi | Is the pharmacy near?
