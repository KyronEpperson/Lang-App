# Worksheet: en-pt

## LESSON sounds — 1 · Sounds, spelling and stress — deck "Sounds and spelling"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
Many Portuguese letters do not sound the way an English reader expects. Under every Portuguese phrase there is a pronunciation line in English letters; the syllable in CAPITALS is stressed.
 Vowels, and the end of a word
 The line spells vowels ah, ee, oo , plus two pairs: eh (open, as in bet ) and ay (closed, as in bait without the y), aw (open, as in law ) and oh (closed, as in go without the w). At the end of a word, an unstressed e sounds ee , o sounds oo , and a is a weak uh . So de , te and que sound jee , chee and kee .
 EX: a casa | ah KAH-zuh | the house
 EX: o carro | oo KAH-hoo | the car
CARDS NOW: de = of; from [card] || te = you; to you (object) [card] || que = that; than [card] || a casa = the house; home [card] || o carro = the car [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
t and d before an "ee" sound
 In most of Brazil, t and d before an ee sound turn into ch and j . That includes a final te or de , because that e sounds ee .
 EX: a cidade | ah see-DAH-jee | the city
 EX: Boa noite. | BOH-uh NOY-chee | Good evening.
CARDS NOW: a cidade = the city [card] || Boa noite. = Good evening; good night. [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
Nasal vowels
 English has no real nasal vowels. A tilde ( ã, õ ) or a final m sends the vowel through the nose. The line writes ng after it: hum it through your nose and do not finish with a hard g. Pão (bread) is nasal; pau (stick) is not.
 EX: o pão | oo powng | the bread
 EX: a mãe | ah maing | the mother
 EX: Bom dia. | bohng JEE-uh | Good morning.
CARDS NOW: o pão = the bread [card] || a mãe = the mother; mom [card] || Bom dia. = Good morning. [card]

### SECTION 4   (has 5)
Where the stress goes
 A written accent shows the stress: café , você . Without one, words ending in a, e, o (with or without s ), am , em or ens stress the next-to-last syllable; all other words stress the last. The marks also show the vowel: avó (grandmother) has an open aw , avô (grandfather) a closed oh .
 EX: o café | oo kah-FEH | the coffee
 EX: a avó | ah ah-VAW | the grandmother
 EX: o avô | oo ah-VOH | the grandfather
CARDS NOW: você = you (one person) [card] || em = in; on; at [card] || o café = the coffee [card] || a avó = the grandmother || o avô = the grandfather

### SECTION 5   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
o and a
 Nouns here come with o or a in front. Both mean "the": every noun is masculine ( o carro ) or feminine ( a casa ), as lesson 3 explains. For more than one, both add s : os carros , as casas .
 The line is a crutch: listen to the audio first, and hide it once the spelling feels natural.
 Most common mistake: reading with English letter values. cidade said "see-DAH-day" → cidade said "see-DAH-jee", with the final de sounding jee .

## LESSON greetings — 2 · Greetings: você and o senhor — deck "Greetings and courtesy"
### SECTION 0   (has 9)
Oi is the everyday "hi" in Brazil, and it works with anyone. Olá is a little more formal. To sound warmer, add the greeting for the time of day.
 Morning, afternoon, evening
 English says "good morning". Portuguese says "good day": bom dia . After about noon it is boa tarde , and after dark boa noite , which means both "good evening" and "good night".
 EX: Oi, bom dia! | oy bohng JEE-uh | Hi, good morning!
 EX: Boa tarde, tudo bem? | BOH-uh TAHR-jee TOO-doo bayng | Good afternoon, how are you?
 Why bom in one and boa in the others? Dia is a masculine word, while tarde and noite are feminine, and the word for "good" changes to agree. This is your first taste of agreement, which runs through all of Portuguese.
CARDS NOW: Oi! = Hi! [card] || Bom dia. = Good morning. [card] || bom = good [card] || Boa tarde. = Good afternoon. [card] || tarde = late [card] || Boa noite. = Good evening; good night. [card] || Oi, bom dia! = Hi, good morning! || Boa tarde, tudo bem? = Good afternoon, how are you? || Tudo bem? = How are you? [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
How are you?
 Tudo bem? literally means "all good?", and the usual answer is the same words: Tudo bem! Then pass the question back with E você? 
 EX: Tudo bem, e você? | TOO-doo bayng ee voh-SAY | I'm good, and you?
CARDS NOW: Tudo bem, e você? = Fine, and you? [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Names
 To give your name, say Eu me chamo (literally "I call myself") or Meu nome é . Answer an introduction with Prazer or Muito prazer : nice to meet you.
 EX: Eu me chamo Ana. Muito prazer! | ayw mee SHAH-moo AH-nuh MOOYN-too prah-ZAYR | My name is Ana. Nice to meet you!
CARDS NOW: Prazer. = Nice to meet you. [card] || Eu me chamo Ana. Muito prazer! = My name is Ana. Nice to meet you!

### SECTION 3   (has 5)
Thank you agrees with you
 English "thank you" never changes. Obrigado literally means "obliged", so it agrees with the person speaking: a man says obrigado , a woman says obrigada , whoever she is thanking. The answer is de nada : you're welcome.
 EX: Muito obrigada! | MOOYN-too oh-bree-GAH-duh | Thank you so much! (said by a woman)
CARDS NOW: Obrigado. = Thank you. [card] || Muito obrigado. = Thank you very much. [card] || Obrigada. = Thank you (said by a woman). [card] || De nada. = You're welcome. [card] || Muito obrigada! = Thank you so much! (said by a woman)

### SECTION 4   (has 5)
Polite words
 por favor : please
 com licença : excuse me, to get past someone or to interrupt
 desculpa : sorry
 tchau : bye
 EX: Com licença, por favor. | kohng lee-SAYN-suh pohr fah-VOHR | Excuse me, please.
CARDS NOW: Por favor. = Please. [card] || Com licença. = Excuse me. [card] || Desculpa. = Sorry. [card] || Tchau! = Bye! [card] || Com licença, por favor. = Excuse me, please.

### SECTION 5   (has 4)
você and o senhor
 English has one "you". In Brazil, você is the "you" for almost everyone: friends, coworkers, shop staff, people your age. For older people, a friend's parents or a formal setting, say o senhor to a man and a senhora to a woman. They take the same verb as você , so only the word changes.
 EX: Tudo bem, e a senhora? | TOO-doo bayng ee ah seh-NYAW-ruh | I'm fine, and you? (to an older woman)
 In Rio, the South and parts of the North and Northeast you will hear tu , usually with the você verb form ( tu vai , tu quer ); in Portugal tu has its own form ( tu vais ). This course uses você .
 Most common mistake: a woman saying Obrigado → Obrigada . It agrees with the person speaking, not with the person being thanked.
CARDS NOW: você = you (one person) [card] || o senhor = you (to an older man) [card] || a senhora = you (to an older woman) [card] || Tudo bem, e a senhora? = I'm fine, and you? (to an older woman)

## LESSON articles — 3 · Gender and o / a, um / uma — deck "Food and drink"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
English has one "the" (the definite article) and one "a" (the indefinite article). Portuguese has a set of each, because every noun is masculine or feminine, even a coffee or a glass of water. The article agrees with its noun.
 Masculine and feminine
 Most nouns ending in -o are masculine and most ending in -a are feminine. Nouns ending in -e or a consonant can be either, so learn every noun with its article. Say new words out loud with it: a água , o leite , a carne .
 ; Masculine ; Feminine ; 
 the (one) ; o suco ; a água ; 
 the (more) ; os sucos ; as águas ; 
 a, an ; um suco ; uma água ; 
 some ; uns sucos ; umas águas ; 
 EX: o suco | oo SOO-koo | the juice
 EX: a água | ah AH-gwuh | the water
CARDS NOW: a água = the water [card] || o leite = the milk [card] || a carne = the meat [card] || o suco = the juice

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
Endings that mislead
 A few common nouns break the pattern. O dia (the day), o problema and o mapa are masculine; a mão (the hand) and a foto are feminine. And an -e tells you nothing: o leite (milk), but a carne (meat).
 EX: o problema | oo proh-BLAY-muh | the problem
 EX: a carne | ah KAHR-nee | the meat
CARDS NOW: o dia = the day [card] || o problema = the problem [card] || a mão = the hand [card] || a foto = the photo [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Plurals
 Most nouns add -s , and the article goes plural with them: o ovo, os ovos . Many nouns in -ão change to -ões ( o limão, os limões ), but not all: o pão, os pães . Nouns in -al change to -ais : one real , Brazil's money, but two reais .
 EX: os ovos | oos AW-voos | the eggs
 EX: os limões | oos lee-MOYNGS | the limes
CARDS NOW: os ovos = the eggs || os limões = the limes

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Things in general
 English drops "the" when it talks about a thing in general: "Cheese bread is good." Portuguese usually keeps the article. ( é means "is"; lesson 4 covers it.)
 EX: O pão de queijo é bom. | oo powng jee KAY-zhoo eh bohng | Cheese bread is good.
CARDS NOW: O pão de queijo é bom. = Cheese bread is good. || um pão de queijo = a cheese bread [card]

### SECTION 4   (has 3)
Ordering with um and uma
 When you order, "a" agrees with the food or drink: um suco , uma água , um pão de queijo . Add por favor and you have a full order.
 EX: Um pão de queijo, por favor. | oong powng jee KAY-zhoo pohr fah-VOHR | A cheese bread, please.
 EX: Uma água, por favor. | OO-muh AH-gwuh pohr fah-VOHR | A water, please.
 Most common mistake: making every noun masculine. o água → a água . And watch the reverse trap: a problema → o problema .
CARDS NOW: Por favor. = Please. [card] || Um pão de queijo, por favor. = A cheese bread, please. || Uma água, por favor. = A water, please.

## LESSON ser — 4 · Subject pronouns and ser — deck "About me"
### SECTION 0   (has 8)
English "be" has three forms: am, is, are. Portuguese has two whole verbs for "be". This lesson is about ser , for who you are: your name, where you are from, your job and your nationality.
 Who's who
 English ; Portuguese ; 
 I ; eu ; 
 you (one person) ; você ; 
 he, she ; ele, ela ; 
 we ; a gente, nós ; 
 you (more than one) ; vocês ; 
 they ; eles, elas ; 
 Eles is for men or a mixed group, elas only for women. A gente (literally "the people") is the everyday spoken "we"; nós is a little more careful. You will hear both, and both are right.
CARDS NOW: ser = to be (what someone or something is) [card] || eu = I [card] || você = you (one person) [card] || a gente = we [card] || nós = we; us (formal) [card] || vocês = you (more than one) [card] || eles = they; them (men or mixed) [card] || elas = they; them (women only) [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 5)
The forms of ser
 Subject ; ser ; 
 eu ; sou ; 
 você, ele, ela, a gente ; é ; 
 nós ; somos ; 
 vocês, eles, elas ; são ; 
 The verb ending matches the subject. Notice that você and a gente use the same form as ele : você é , a gente é . Say nós somos or a gente é , never a mix of the two.
 EX: Eu sou americano. | ayw soh ah-meh-ree-KAH-noo | I'm American. (a man speaking)
 EX: Você é brasileira? | voh-SAY eh brah-zee-LAY-ruh | Are you Brazilian? (to a woman)
 EX: A gente é de Boston. | ah ZHAYN-chee eh jee BAWS-tohng | We're from Boston.
CARDS NOW: ele = he; him (in speech) [card] || Eu sou americano. = I'm American. (a man speaking) || Sou americano. = I'm American. [card] || Você é brasileira? = Are you Brazilian? (to a woman) || A gente é de Boston. = We're from Boston.

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
Where you are from
 Use ser de with a city: Sou de Miami. Most country names take an article that joins with de , which lesson 8 covers, so stick to cities for now. To ask, English puts "from" at the end; Portuguese puts de first: "from where are you?"
 EX: De onde você é? | jee OHN-jee voh-SAY eh | Where are you from?
 EX: Nós somos de São Paulo. | naws SOH-moos jee sowng POW-loo | We're from São Paulo.
CARDS NOW: de = of; from [card] || De onde você é? = Where are you from? [card] || Nós somos de São Paulo. = We're from São Paulo.

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Jobs and nationalities
 English says "I'm a teacher". Portuguese drops the article: Sou professor. Jobs and nationalities agree with the person: a woman is professora , americana , brasileira . Nationalities are written in lowercase.
 EX: Ela é professora. | EH-luh eh proh-feh-SOH-ruh | She's a teacher.
 EX: Eles são brasileiros. | AY-lees sowng brah-zee-LAY-roos | They're Brazilian.
CARDS NOW: Ela é professora. = She's a teacher. || Eles são brasileiros. = They're Brazilian.

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Keep the pronoun, mostly
 You can leave out eu and nós , because sou and somos already say who. Brazilians usually keep você , ele and ela , because é alone could mean any of them.
 EX: Sou enfermeira. | soh ayn-fehr-MAY-ruh | I'm a nurse.
 Most common mistake: copying the English "a" before a job. Eu sou um professor. → Eu sou professor.
CARDS NOW: ela = she; her [card] || Sou enfermeira. = I'm a nurse. [card]

## LESSON adjectives — 5 · Adjectives: agreement and position — deck "People and family"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
In English an adjective never changes: a tall man, tall women. In Portuguese it agrees with its noun in gender (masculine or feminine) and number (one or more), so most adjectives have four forms.
 Four forms
 ; One ; More than one ; 
 Masculine ; o menino alto ; os meninos altos ; 
 Feminine ; a menina alta ; as meninas altas ; 
 EX: O pai é alto. | oo pai eh OW-too | The father is tall.
 EX: A irmã é alta. | ah eer-MAHNG eh OW-tuh | The sister is tall.
 EX: Os amigos são simpáticos. | oos ah-MEE-goos sowng seem-PAH-chee-koos | The friends are nice.
 The adjective agrees with its noun even after é or são , far from the noun: a Ana é alta , os meninos são altos . Colors and nationalities work the same way: preto, preta, pretos, pretas ; americano, americana, americanos, americanas .
 A group of men and women takes the masculine plural: O irmão e a irmã são altos.
CARDS NOW: O pai é alto. = The father is tall. || A irmã é alta. = The sister is tall. || Os amigos são simpáticos. = The friends are nice.

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Adjectives in -e or a consonant
 These have no separate feminine form, only one and more than one: grande, grandes ; feliz, felizes ; jovem, jovens . Adjectives in -l change to -is : azul, azuis .
 EX: A família é grande. | ah fah-MEE-lyuh eh GRAHN-jee | The family is big.
 EX: os olhos azuis | oos AW-lyoos ah-ZOOYS | the blue eyes
CARDS NOW: A família é grande. = The family is big. || os olhos azuis = the blue eyes

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
After the noun
 English puts the adjective first: "a black car". Portuguese usually puts it after: um carro preto . Colors and nationalities always go after. A few common adjectives, such as bom , often come first, but after the noun is the safe default.
 EX: uma camisa branca | OO-muh kah-MEE-zuh BRAHN-kuh | a white shirt
 EX: um amigo brasileiro | oong ah-MEE-goo brah-zee-LAY-roo | a Brazilian friend
CARDS NOW: bom = good [card] || uma camisa branca = a white shirt || um amigo brasileiro = a Brazilian friend

### SECTION 3   (has 3)
Very: muito
 Muito before an adjective means "very", and in that job it never changes. Only the adjective agrees: muito alta , muito simpáticos .
 EX: Ela é muito simpática. | EH-luh eh MOOYN-too seem-PAH-chee-kuh | She's very nice.
 Watch one false friend: simpático means nice or friendly, not "sympathetic".
CARDS NOW: muito = very; a lot [card] || Ela é muito simpática. = She's very nice. [card] || simpático = friendly; easy to like [card]

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
Learn adjectives in pairs
 When you meet a new adjective, learn both forms at once: bonito, bonita ; simpático, simpática ; brasileiro, brasileira . Then look at the noun before you speak: if it takes a , the adjective ends in -a too.
 Most common mistake: leaving the adjective in its masculine form. a casa bonito → a casa bonita : the adjective agrees with the noun.

## LESSON present — 6 · Present tense: -ar, -er, -ir — deck "Work and study"
### SECTION 0   (has 9)
English verbs barely change: I work, you work, we work; only "he works" adds an s. Portuguese changes the verb ending to match the subject. The good news: everyday Brazilian Portuguese uses only four forms of most verbs.
 Three families
 Every infinitive (the "to" form) ends in -ar , -er or -ir . Take off those two letters to get the stem, then add the ending. Other regular verbs that work the same way: falar (speak), morar (live), estudar (study), comer (eat).
 Subject ; trabalhar (work) ; aprender (learn) ; abrir (open) ; 
 eu ; trabalho ; aprendo ; abro ; 
 você, ele, ela, a gente ; trabalha ; aprende ; abre ; 
 nós ; trabalhamos ; aprendemos ; abrimos ; 
 vocês, eles, elas ; trabalham ; aprendem ; abrem ; 
 EX: Eu trabalho em Miami. | ayw trah-BAH-lyoo ayng mee-AH-mee | I work in Miami.
 EX: Ela aprende português. | EH-luh ah-PRAYN-jee pohr-too-GAYS | She's learning Portuguese.
CARDS NOW: ir = to go [card] || falar = to speak; to talk [card] || morar = to live (somewhere) [card] || estudar = to study [card] || comer = to eat [card] || eu = I [card] || nós = we; us (formal) [card] || Eu trabalho em Miami. = I work in Miami. || Ela aprende português. = She's learning Portuguese.

### SECTION 1   (has 7)
The ele form does a lot
 The ele form also goes with você and a gente . So "we work" is nós trabalhamos or, in everyday speech, a gente trabalha . Both are correct; just do not mix them, as in a gente trabalhamos .
 EX: Você fala inglês? | voh-SAY FAH-luh een-GLAYS | Do you speak English?
 EX: Nós moramos em Orlando. | naws moh-RAH-moos ayng ohr-LAHN-doo | We live in Orlando.
 EX: A gente estuda de manhã. | ah ZHAYN-chee ees-TOO-duh jee mah-NYAHNG | We study in the morning.
 The plural "you", vocês , takes the eles form. And the eu form always ends in -o , in all three families: falo, como, abro .
