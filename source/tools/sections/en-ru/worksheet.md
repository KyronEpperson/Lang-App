# Worksheet: en-ru

## LESSON alphabet — 1 · Letters 1: familiar shapes and false friends — deck "Letters 1: friends and false friends"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Russian is written in Cyrillic: 33 letters, read left to right, with spaces between words, just like English. This course uses printed letters only. Handwriting and italics use different shapes (an italic т looks like an m), so Russian is never shown in italics here. Twelve letters are enough to read real words today.
 Five true friends
 А К М О Т look like English letters and sound like them: А is "ah" as in father , К is k, М is m, О is "oh", Т is t.
 EX: мама | MAH-mah | mom
 EX: кот | koht | cat
CARDS NOW: мама = mom (А = ah) [card] || кот = cat (К = k) [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 8)
Seven false friends
 These seven look English but say something else. This is the part to drill.
 Letter ; Looks like ; Says ; Example ; 
 В ; B ; v ; вот ; 
 Е ; E ; "ye" as in yes ; нет ; 
 Н ; H ; n ; он ; 
 Р ; P ; r, tapped or rolled ; ресторан ; 
 С ; C ; s ; сок ; 
 У ; y ; "oo" as in food ; утро ; 
 Х ; X ; kh, the breathy sound in Bach ; сахар ; 
 EX: нет | nyeht | no
 EX: ресторан | rihs-tah-RAHN | restaurant
 EX: сахар | SAH-khahr | sugar
 EX: Москва | mahs-KVAH | Moscow
CARDS NOW: вот = here is; there is (В = v) [card] || нет = no (Е = ye) [card] || он = he; it (О = oh) [card] || ресторан = restaurant (Р = r) [card] || сок = juice (С = s) [card] || утро = morning (У = oo) [card] || сахар = sugar (Х = kh) [card] || Москва = Moscow [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Reading the respelling
 Under every Russian word is a line in English letters. Its vowels are always ah (father), eh (bed), ee (see), oh (more, with no w at the end) and oo (food). The syllable in CAPITALS is the stressed one: мама is MAH-mah. Russian stress can fall on any syllable, and an о without stress sounds like "ah". That is why окно reads ahk-NOH. Lesson 4 explains it.
 EX: окно | ahk-NOH | window
CARDS NOW: окно = window [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 6)
He, she and it for things
 English calls a cat or a window "it". Russian has three words: он (he), она (she) and оно (it), and things get them too. For now, look at the last letter: a consonant ( кот ) takes он , -а ( мама , Москва ) takes она , and -о ( окно , метро ) takes оно . Вот means "here is" or "there is".
 EX: Кот? Вот он. | koht voht ohn | The cat? There it is.
 EX: Мама? Вот она. | MAH-mah voht ah-NAH | Mom? There she is.
 EX: Метро? Вот оно. | mih-TROH voht ah-NOH | The subway? There it is.
 Most common mistake: reading Р as p and Н as h, so ресторан comes out as "pectopah". Wrong: "pectopah" → right: ресторан , rihs-tah-RAHN. Р is always r, Н is always n.
CARDS NOW: она = she; it (Н = n) [card] || оно = it (neuter) [card] || метро = subway (М = m) [card] || Кот? Вот он. = The cat? There it is. || Мама? Вот она. = Mom? There she is. || Метро? Вот оно. = The subway? There it is.

DECK CARDS IN NO SECTION (add by front, as a string, only where a section teaches them): там | tahm | there (Т = t) || кто | ktoh | who || тут | toot | here || торт | tohrt | cake

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
Ы: a vowel English doesn't have
 Ы is one letter, not two. Say "ee", then pull your tongue back without moving your lips: that is "ih". Compare мы (mih) with такси (tahk-SEE).
 EX: мы | mih | we
 EX: такси | tahk-SEE | taxi
CARDS NOW: (none)

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Й after a vowel
 Й almost always follows a vowel and glides off it: ой is "oy", ай is "ai", ей is "ey". мой rhymes with boy .
 EX: трамвай | trahm-VAI | streetcar
 EX: музей | moo-ZYEY | museum
CARDS NOW: трамвай = streetcar || музей = museum [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Э and your first sentences
 Это means "this" or "that". English says "This is a house". Russian says just "this house": no word for "is" and none for "a". Lesson 6 explains why.
 EX: Это мой дом. | EH-tah moy dohm | This is my house.
CARDS NOW: Это мой дом. = This is my house.

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
мой and мои
 "My" changes with the thing you own. мой goes with one thing like дом , мои with more than one. It matches the thing, not you. Lesson 3 adds the forms for feminine and neuter words.
 EX: Это мои билеты. | EH-tah mah-EE bee-LYEH-tih | These are my tickets.
 Most common mistake: reading И as an English N, so такси comes out as "taksn". Wrong: "taksn" → right: такси , tahk-SEE. И always says "ee".
CARDS NOW: Это мои билеты. = These are my tickets.

## LESSON sounds — 3 · Soft and hard, voiced and voiceless — deck "Letters 3: soft vowels and hushing sounds"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
With this lesson you have all 33 letters. The new ones are three vowels with a y in them, five hushing sounds, and two signs with no sound of their own.
 Я Ё Ю, and Е again
 Я is "ya", Ё is "yo", Ю is "yu", and Е , as you know, is "ye". At the start of a word you hear the y. Ё is always stressed.
 EX: я | yah | I
 EX: меню | mih-NYOO | menu
 EX: ещё | yih-SH'OH | more; still
CARDS NOW: я = I (Я = ya) [card] || меню = menu (Ю = yu) [card] || ещё = more; still; yet (Ё = yo) [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Hard and soft consonants
 Most Russian consonants come in two versions. A soft consonant is said with the middle of the tongue raised toward the roof of the mouth, as in the "ny" of canyon . (Textbooks call this "palatalized".) After a consonant, я ё ю е и make it soft instead of adding a y. The respelling shows a soft consonant with a y after it ( нет , nyeht) or an apostrophe ( мать , maht').
CARDS NOW: нет = no (Е = ye) [card] || мать = mother (ь = soft sign) [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
The soft sign ь and the hard sign ъ
 ь has no sound. It makes the consonant before it soft, and that changes the word.
 EX: мать | maht' | mother
 EX: брат | braht | brother
 EX: брать | braht' | to take
 Before a vowel, ь also adds a y: семья is sihm'-YAH. The rare ъ keeps the consonant hard and adds a y.
 EX: семья | sihm'-YAH | family
 EX: подъезд | pahd-YEHST | building entrance
CARDS NOW: брат = brother [card] || брать = to take [card] || семья = family [card] || подъезд = building entrance (ъ = hard sign) [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 5)
Five hushing sounds

 Щ ; a long, soft sh, as in fresh sheets ; борщ ; 
 Ч ; ch, as in cheap ; always soft ; чай ; 
 Ц ; ts, as in cats ; центр ; 
 EX: школа | SHKOH-lah | school
 EX: борщ | bohrsh' | borscht
CARDS NOW: жена = wife (Ж = zh) [card] || школа = school (Ш = sh) [card] || борщ = borscht (Щ = soft long sh) [card] || чай = tea (Ч = ch) [card] || центр = center; downtown (Ц = ts) [card]

### SECTION 4   (has 4)
Voiced and voiceless
 At the end of a word, б д г в з ж lose their voice and sound like п т к ф с ш. Inside a word, a consonant takes the voice of the one after it, and в before a voiceless consonant sounds like f.
 EX: хлеб | khlyehp | bread
 EX: друг | drook | friend
 EX: водка | VOHT-kah | vodka
 EX: в парк | f pahrk | to the park
CARDS NOW: хлеб = bread [card] || друг = friend (a man) [card] || водка = vodka || в парк = to the park

### SECTION 5   (has 4)
моя and моё
 Now you can read all of "my": мой for masculine words, моя for feminine words in -а, моё for neuter words in -о.
 EX: Это моя мама. | EH-tah mah-YAH MAH-mah | This is my mom.
 EX: Это моё письмо. | EH-tah mah-YOH pees'-MOH | This is my letter.
 Most common mistake: ignoring ь. Wrong: мат for "mother" (it means "checkmate") → right: мать , maht', with a soft т.
CARDS NOW: мой = my (Й = y) [card] || Это моя мама. = This is my mom. || Это моё письмо. = This is my letter. || мат = checkmate [card]

## LESSON stress — 4 · Stress and vowel reduction — deck "Greetings and politeness"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
Every Russian word of two or more syllables has one stressed syllable, said louder and longer than the rest. It can fall on any syllable, and ordinary Russian writing never shows it, so learn each word together with its stress. In this course the CAPITALS of the respelling show it, and the voice is your model.
 Stress changes the vowels
 English does this a little: the three a's in banana are not all the same. Russian does it by rule, and the rule is called vowel reduction. An о you can't see in capitals sounds like "ah". An е or я you can't see in capitals sounds like "ih".
 EX: молоко | mah-lah-KOH | milk
 EX: хорошо | khah-rah-SHOH | good; well
 Молоко has three о's, and only the last one, the stressed one, sounds like "oh". In хорошо it is the same.
 EX: сестра | sihs-TRAH | sister
 EX: неделя | nih-DYEH-lyah | week
 У , ы and ю keep their sound with or without stress. Ё is always stressed, so a word with ё needs nothing else: ещё .
CARDS NOW: молоко = milk [card] || хорошо = good; well; OK [card] || сестра = sister [card] || неделя = week [card] || ещё = more; still; yet (Ё = yo) [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Words that look English
 Many words look like English ones, but the stress often does not sit where English puts it. Ресторан and университет are stressed on the last syllable.
 EX: университет | oo-nee-vihr-see-TYEHT | university
CARDS NOW: ресторан = restaurant (Р = r) [card] || университет = university

### SECTION 2   (has 6)
First polite words
 Спасибо is "thank you". Пожалуйста is "please" and also the answer to thanks, "you're welcome". It is said shorter than it is written: pah-ZHAHL-stah. Извините is "excuse me" or "sorry" to a stranger. Можно? means "May I?": point at a free seat or a menu and ask.
 EX: Спасибо! | spah-SEE-bah | Thank you!
 EX: Пожалуйста. | pah-ZHAHL-stah | Please. / You're welcome.
 EX: Извините, можно? | eez-vee-NEE-tih MOHZH-nah | Excuse me, may I?
 EX: Можно меню? | MOHZH-nah mih-NYOO | Can I have the menu?
CARDS NOW: Спасибо! = Thank you! [card] || Пожалуйста. = Please. / You're welcome. [card] || Извините! = Excuse me! / Sorry! (formal) [card] || Можно? = May I? [card] || Извините, можно? = Excuse me, may I? || Можно меню? = Can I have the menu? [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
"My" keeps matching
 Whatever the stress, "my" still matches the word it goes with: мой телефон , моя сумка , моё молоко .
 EX: Это моя сумка. | EH-tah mah-YAH SOOM-kah | This is my bag.
 Most common mistake: reading every о as "oh". Wrong: молоко as "mo-lo-ko" → right: mah-lah-KOH. Only the о in capitals sounds like "oh".
CARDS NOW: Это моя сумка. = This is my bag.

## LESSON greetings — 5 · Greetings: ты and вы — deck "Formal and informal"
### SECTION 0   (has 21)
English has one "you". Russian has two. ты is for one person you know well: friends, family, children, classmates. вы is for anyone you don't know, anyone older or above you at work, and for more than one person. When in doubt, use вы . The greetings change with it.
 ; Formal (вы) ; Informal (ты) ; 
 Hello ; Здравствуйте! ; Привет! ; 
 What's your name? ; Как вас зовут? ; Как тебя зовут? ; 
 How are you? ; Как у вас дела? ; Как дела? ; 
 And you? ; А у вас? ; А у тебя? ; 
 Goodbye ; До свидания! ; Пока! ; 
 EX: Здравствуйте! | ZDRAHS-tvooy-tih | Hello! (formal)
 EX: Привет! | pree-VYEHT | Hi! (informal)
 The first в of здравствуйте is silent. You can also greet anyone by the time of day: Доброе утро , Добрый день , Добрый вечер .
 Names
 English says "My name is Anna". Russian says "they call me Anna": Меня зовут Анна . Then Очень приятно , "nice to meet you", works with anyone. "Glad to meet you" depends on who speaks: a man says рад , a woman says рада .
 EX: Как вас зовут? | kahk vahs zah-VOOT | What's your name? (formal)
 EX: Меня зовут Анна. | mih-NYAH zah-VOOT AHN-nah | My name is Anna.
 EX: Рада познакомиться. | RAH-dah pahz-nah-KOH-mee-tsah | Glad to meet you. (a woman)
 A friend who is a man is друг ; a friend who is a woman is подруга , so "my" changes too: мой друг , моя подруга .

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Teachers, bosses and friends
 Russians address a teacher, a boss or an older person by first name plus patronymic, a middle name made from the father's name: Анна Сергеевна is Anna, daughter of Sergey. Men's patronymics end in -ович or -евич, women's in -овна or -евна. You will hear them; you don't need to make them. Friends use short names: Маша for Мария , Саша for Александр or Александра .
 EX: Анна Сергеевна, здравствуйте! | AHN-nah sihr-GYEH-yihv-nah ZDRAHS-tvooy-tih | Hello, Anna Sergeyevna!
CARDS NOW: Анна Сергеевна, здравствуйте! = Hello, Anna Sergeyevna!

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Switching to ты
 Start with вы . The older or more senior person usually offers to switch:

 Most common mistake: greeting a teacher like a friend. Wrong: Привет! to your professor → right: Здравствуйте! From now on, read the Cyrillic first and use the respelling only to check the stress.
CARDS NOW: Давай на ты? = Shall we say ты? (informal) [card]

## LESSON eto — 6 · Это: this is, with no 'is' — deck "People and family"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
English needs "is", "am" and "are". In the present tense Russian simply leaves them out. And Russian has no words for "a" or "the" at all. Two short words do the work of a whole English sentence, which makes Russian sentences feel compact at first.
 Это: this is, that is, these are
 Это plus a noun is a whole sentence. Это дом is "this is a house" or "this is the house": the situation tells which. Это points at people and things, near or far, one or many.
 EX: Это дом. | EH-tah dohm | This is a house.
 EX: Это мой брат. | EH-tah moy braht | This is my brother.
 EX: Это мои родители. | EH-tah mah-EE rah-DEE-tih-lee | These are my parents.
CARDS NOW: это = this; that (Э = eh) [card] || Это дом. = This is a house. || Это мой брат. = This is my brother. [card] || Это мои родители. = These are my parents. || родители = parents [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Это never changes
 In "this is" sentences, это stays the same whatever follows: a masculine, feminine or neuter word, or more than one. What changes is "my", which matches the noun.
 EX: Это моя сестра. | EH-tah mah-YAH sihs-TRAH | This is my sister.
CARDS NOW: Это моя сестра. = This is my sister. [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
X is Y: two words side by side
 To say who someone is, put the two words next to each other: Я студент , literally "I student". Many words for people have a separate form for a woman: a man is a студент , a woman a студентка . Врач (doctor) is used for both.
 EX: Я студент. | yah stoo-DYEHNT | I'm a student. (a man)
 EX: Я студентка. | yah stoo-DYEHNT-kah | I'm a student. (a woman)
 EX: Анна врач. | AHN-nah vrahch | Anna is a doctor.
CARDS NOW: Я студент. = I'm a student. (a man) [card] || врач = doctor [card] || Я студентка. = I'm a student. (a woman) [card] || Анна врач. = Anna is a doctor.

### SECTION 3   (has 7)
Кто это? Что это?
 Кто is "who" and что is "what". Russian uses кто for people and also for animals, and что for things. The answer starts with это again.
 EX: Кто это? | ktoh EH-tah | Who is that?
 EX: Что это? | shtoh EH-tah | What is this?
 EX: Кто это? Это моя бабушка. | ktoh EH-tah EH-tah mah-YAH BAH-boosh-kah | Who is that? That's my grandmother.
 Later you will meet a word есть , "there is". It is not the "is" of these sentences, so leave it out here.
 Most common mistake: copying the English "am" or "is". Wrong: Я есть студент → right: Я студент . Read the Cyrillic first; check the stress in the respelling only if you need to.

 A statement: the voice falls

## LESSON time — 28 · Time, days and dates — deck "Time and days"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
English says " at two", " on Monday", " in May": three different words. Russian uses в for all three, but the case after it changes.
 What time is it?
 Ask Который час? or Сколько времени? The answer is a number plus час , which changes like рубль : час alone for one, два, три, четыре часа , and пять часов up to twelve.
 EX: Который час? | kah-TOH-rihy chahs | What time is it?
 EX: Сейчас три часа. | sihy-CHAHS tree chih-SAH | It's three o'clock now.
CARDS NOW: Который час? = What time is it? [card] || час = hour [card] || рубль = ruble [card] || в два часа = at two o'clock [card] || в пять часов = at five o'clock [card] || Сейчас три часа. = It's three o'clock now. || сейчас = now [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
At what time: в + the time
 Put в before the time, and keep the same час, часа, часов : в час , в два часа , в пять часов .
 EX: Урок в пять часов. | oo-ROHK f pyaht' chih-SOHF | The class is at five o'clock.
CARDS NOW: Урок в пять часов. = The class is at five o'clock.

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
On which day: в + accusative
 Days take в and the accusative case. Feminine days change -а to -у : в среду , в пятницу , в субботу . Masculine days look the same as always: в понедельник . Before вторник , в becomes во : во вторник . Days and months are not capitalized.
 EX: В субботу я буду дома. | f soo-BOH-too yah BOO-doo DOH-mah | On Saturday I'll be at home.
CARDS NOW: в пятницу = on Friday [card] || в понедельник = on Monday [card] || вторник = Tuesday [card] || В субботу я буду дома. = On Saturday I'll be at home.

### SECTION 3   (has 5)
Parts of the day: one word
 Утром , днём , вечером , ночью mean "in the morning", "in the afternoon", "in the evening", "at night". They are single words, with no в .
 EX: Вечером я читаю. | VYEH-chih-rahm yah chee-TAH-yoo | In the evening I read.
CARDS NOW: утром = in the morning [card] || днём = in the afternoon [card] || вечером = in the evening [card] || ночью = at night [card] || Вечером я читаю. = In the evening I read.

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
In which month: в + prepositional
 Months take в and the prepositional case: в январе , в марте , в мае .
 EX: Мой день рождения в мае. | moy dyehn' rahzh-DYEH-nee-yah v MAH-yih | My birthday is in May.
CARDS NOW: в мае = in May [card] || Мой день рождения в мае. = My birthday is in May.

### SECTION 5   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Dates, to recognize
 A date is an ordinal number ("first", "second") in the neuter form plus the month with a new ending: первое мая , "the first of May". Just recognize dates for now.
 EX: Сегодня первое мая. | sih-VOHD-nyah PYEHR-vah-yih MAH-yah | Today is the first of May.
 Most common mistake: using the "where" ending for days. в понедельнике → в понедельник , and два часы → два часа . Read the Cyrillic first, and use the respelling only to check the stress.
CARDS NOW: Сегодня первое мая. = Today is the first of May. || сегодня = today [card]
