## LESSON alphabet — 1 · The Polish letters — deck "Sounds and spelling"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Polish uses the Latin alphabet you know, plus nine letters: ą ć ę ł ń ó ś ź ż . Under every Polish phrase in this course there is a pronunciation line in English letters. The syllable in CAPITALS is the stressed one. A word of one syllable stays in small letters. A capital N inside ohN is not stress, and it is not its own syllable. It is a nasal tail on the vowel.
 Vowels that stay put
 English vowels slide. Say no slowly and the end is a w . Polish vowels hold one sound. The line spells them the same way every time.

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
ł is w, and w is v
 ł and l are different letters. ł is the English w sound. Plain l stays a clear l. The letter w is an English v . At the end of a word, w sounds like f . The r is a tap, one quick flick of the tongue. The line cannot show the tap, so it writes r.
 EX: łódka | WOOT-kah | a boat
 EX: Kraków | KRAH-koof | Kraków
 Kraków is two beats, KRAH-koof . The k and the r stay together. A cluster takes no extra vowel, and ó is oo .
CARDS NOW: łódka = a boat [card] || Kraków = Kraków [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
The nasal tail and the soft mark
 Final ą is a nasal o, ohN . Final ę is an ordinary eh . It is not nasal. Hard sz is sh . ś , and si before a vowel, are shy . At the end of a word the soft sound is sh' . The apostrophe adds no vowel. It only marks softness. cz and ć , and ż and ź , follow that same three-way split: hard, soft with a y, soft with an apostrophe. ch and h are both kh , the sound in Scottish "loch". The next lesson drills the pairs.
 EX: są | sohN | they are
 EX: proszę | PROH-sheh | Please. You're welcome.
 EX: prosię | PROH-shyeh | a piglet
 EX: cześć | chehsh'ch' | Hi. Bye. To a friend.
CARDS NOW: są = they are [card] || proszę = Please. You're welcome. [card] || prosię = a piglet [card] || cześć = Hi. Bye. To a friend. [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 7)
A bare w, and three small words
 The word w has no vowel of its own. Before a vowel it is a bare v . w domu is a set phrase, "at home". Keep it as a piece. The little word z can be a bare consonant too. You will meet it with place names.
 EX: w domu | v DOH-moo | at home
 on is he, the masculine form. ona is she, the feminine form. ono is it, the neuter form. jest means "is". The full set of "to be" comes later. For now the pronoun changes, and jest stays with it.
 EX: On jest. | ohn yehst | He is.
 EX: Ona jest. | OH-nah yehst | She is.
 EX: Ono jest. | OH-noh yehst | It is.
 Most common mistake: reading ł as l, and ó as o. lódka → łódka , and Krakow → Kraków .

## LESSON spelling — 2 · sz, cz, ś and the pairs — deck "Sounds and spelling"
### SECTION 0   (has 6)
English has one "sh" and one "ch". Polish splits each of them. If you spell both with the English letter, proszę and prosię collapse into one word. They are not one word.
 Hard, soft with a y, soft with an apostrophe
 Hard sz is sh , as in "show". ś , and si before a vowel, are softer: shy before a vowel, and sh' at the end or before a consonant. The apostrophe adds no vowel. The same three-way split works for the other pairs. Hard cz is ch as in "chop", and soft ć or ci is chy before a vowel and ch' otherwise. Hard ż is zh as in "measure", and soft ź or zi is zhy or zh' .

rz is ż, and ch is h

### SECTION 2   (has 6)
dz, dż, and ń
 dż is the hard "j" of "jam", as in dżem , jam. dz is the voiced partner of that pair, a voiced "ts". Before a vowel, dzi softens. That is why dzień , day, starts with jy on the line, not with a separate d. ń is ny , as in "canyon", including at the end of a syllable. ni before a vowel is the same softness. You will hear it in pani a few lessons from now.
 EX: dżem | jehm | jam
 EX: dzień | jyehny | day
 You already have three pronouns. on is the masculine form, ona is the feminine form, and ono is the neuter form. They stay in the practice so the letters do not push them out.
 Most common mistake: one English "sh" for both courtesy and the piglet. prosię for please → proszę . The piglet stays prosię .

## LESSON stress — 3 · Stress and the hard clusters — deck "Sounds and spelling"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
English often stresses the first syllable of a long word. Polish does not. The ordinary rule is the next-to-last syllable. Say the city aloud and let the capitals show you: Warszawa is vahr-SHAH-vah . The stress sits on sza , not on the first syllable.
 EX: Warszawa | vahr-SHAH-vah | Warsaw
 Each word keeps its own stress. Dzień dobry is two words, so the line has two stresses: jyehny DOH-brih . dzień is one syllable. dobry is stressed on its first syllable, which is the next to last. The next lesson teaches when you say this hello. Here, hear the two beats, and notice the agreement: dzień is masculine, so "good" is the masculine form dobry .
 EX: Dzień dobry. | jyehny DOH-brih | Good morning. Good afternoon.
 One careful exception
 muzyka means music. Its careful stress is the third syllable from the end: MOO-zih-kah . The voice in this course may put the stress on the next-to-last syllable instead. When the voice and the capitals disagree, read the capitals for this word. This is the careful stress, and it is the exception you learn here. Ordinary words stay on the next-to-last syllable.
 EX: muzyka | MOO-zih-kah | music
CARDS NOW: Warszawa = Warsaw [card] || Dzień dobry. = Good morning. Good afternoon. [card] || dzień = day [card] || dobry = good [card] || muzyka = music [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 8)
Clusters stay tight
 A cluster is a run of consonants. Polish does not insert a vowel to make it easier. Kraków is KRAH-koof , two syllables. An English reading that starts "kuh-RAH" has added a vowel the word does not have. barszcz , beet soup, is one syllable: bahrshch . The r is still a tap. The line writes r and then the consonants. ó in Kraków is still oo , and the final w is still f .
 EX: Kraków | KRAH-koof | Kraków
 EX: barszcz | bahrshch | barszcz, beet soup
 Two syllables you already know follow the same rule. proszę is stressed on the first syllable, because that syllable is the next to last. on is the masculine form, ona is the feminine form, and ono is the neuter form. jest still means "is".

 EX: On jest. | ohn yehst | He is.
 EX: Ona jest. | OH-nah yehst | She is.
 Most common mistake: a vowel stuffed into the cluster, and the stress pulled onto the first syllable. kuh-RAH-koof → Kraków , KRAH-koof .

## LESSON greetings — 4 · Hello, please and thank you — deck "Greetings and courtesy"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
English changes the hello with the clock: good morning, good afternoon, good evening, good night. Polish uses fewer hellos, and one of them does two jobs.
 Morning, afternoon, and when it is dark
 Dzień dobry is good morning and good afternoon. The words do not change at noon. There is no separate afternoon hello, and Dobrego popołudnia is not the greeting. Dobry wieczór is good evening, the hello for when it is dark. It is at home in private company. With a stranger, Dzień dobry is also a safe hello, including after dark, because you do not have to decide whether evening has started. Late at night, if the person is still up, the hello is still Dobry wieczór . There is no separate late-night hello.
 EX: Dzień dobry. | jyehny DOH-brih | Good morning. Good afternoon.
 EX: Dobry wieczór. | DOH-brih VYEH-choor | Good evening.
 The adjective agrees. dzień , day, is masculine, so "good" is the masculine form dobry . wieczór , evening, is masculine too, so the hello is Dobry wieczór . Night, noc , is feminine. Good night is one word, Dobranoc , built from the feminine form dobra plus noc .
CARDS NOW: Dzień dobry. = Good morning. Good afternoon. [card] || dzień = day [card] || dobry = good [card] || Dobry wieczór. = Good evening. [card] || wieczór = evening [card] || noc = night [card] || Dobranoc. = Good night. [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Good night is not goodbye
 Dobranoc is what you say when you have reason to think the person will soon go to sleep. It is a private good night. It is not the goodbye in a shop, and it is not the banner hello for late night. Said to a shopkeeper hours before closing, it can sound like a joke at their expense. To a stranger, goodbye is Do widzenia .
 EX: Dobranoc. | doh-BRAH-nohts | Good night.
 EX: Do widzenia. | doh vyee-DZEH-nyah | Goodbye.
CARDS NOW: Do widzenia. = Goodbye. [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
A friend, and the small polite words
 Cześć is hello and goodbye to a friend. You recognize it. When a stranger says it, you still answer with Dzień dobry , and you leave with Do widzenia . Dziękuję is thank you. It is also the safe reply when someone pays you a compliment. Proszę is please, and it is also you're welcome. Przepraszam is excuse me and I'm sorry.
 EX: Cześć. | chehsh'ch' | Hi. Bye. To a friend.
 EX: Dziękuję. | jyehng-KOO-yeh | Thank you.

 EX: Przepraszam. | psheh-PRAH-shahm | Excuse me. I'm sorry.
 Most common mistake: Dobranoc as a shop goodbye, and an afternoon hello that Polish does not use. Dobranoc on the way out of a shop → Do widzenia , and Dobrego popołudnia → Dzień dobry .

## LESSON pan-pani — 5 · pan and pani — deck "Formal and informal"
### SECTION 0   (has 12)
English has one word, "you". Every "you" you produce in this course is pan or pani plus a third-person verb. pan is for a man. pani is for a woman. The verb is the same form you already use with on and ona . It agrees with pan , and it agrees with pani .
 EX: Pan jest. | pahn yehst | You are. (to a man)
 EX: Pani jest. | PAH-nyee yehst | You are. (to a woman)
 Pan jest is the line. Pan jesteś puts the friend's verb on pan . That mix is the mistake. Write pan and pani in lowercase, except when they start the sentence. A capital Pan turns up in letters, as a courtesy. Letters are not the drill. A capital answer still counts, because the course ignores capitals.
 Questions you can say today
 Czy is the little word that makes a yes-no question. Czy pani mówi po angielsku? is "Do you speak English?" to a woman. mówi agrees with pani . po angielsku is a chunk, "in English". po polsku is "in Polish". Pan jest z Krakowa is "You are from Kraków", to a man. Pani jest z Warszawy is the same sentence to a woman. z Krakowa and z Warszawy are chunks. The next lesson lines up the rest of "to be".
 EX: Czy pani mówi po angielsku? | chih PAH-nyee MOO-vyee poh ahny-GYEHL-skoo | Do you speak English?
