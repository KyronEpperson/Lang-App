# Worksheet: en-hmn

## LESSON tones — 1 · Tones: the silent last letter — deck "Tones"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
This course teaches White Hmong ( Hmoob Dawb high · high ) as written by Hmong Americans, in the Hmong Latin alphabet (RPA). Green Hmong (Mong Leeg) speakers will understand you, and the notes show where Green Hmong differs.
 The last letter is not a sound
 English says every letter it writes. Hmong does not: no consonant is ever said at the end of a syllable. When a syllable is written with b , j , v , s , g , m or d at the end, that letter is silent. It is the tone letter: it tells you the tone, the pitch and voice of the syllable. So Hmoob high is "Hmong" said with a high tone, never "h-moo-b".
 EX: Hmoob | high | Hmong
 A syllable with no tone letter has the mid tone: zoo mid (good). So Nyob zoo is high, then mid.
 EX: Nyob zoo. | high · mid | Hello.
CARDS NOW: Hmoob = Hmong [card] || zoo = good [card] || Nyob zoo. = Hello. [card] || nyob = to live; to be (somewhere) [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 5)
One letter, a new word
 English can say "dog" high or low and it is still a dog. In Hmong the tone letter changes the word: dej is water but dev rising is dog; kuv is I but kub high is hot. When you type an answer, the tone letter must be right: there is no "almost".
 EX: Kuv hlub koj. | rising · high · falling | I love you.
 EX: Ua tsaug. | mid · falling-breathy | Thank you.
CARDS NOW: dev = dog [card] || kub = hot [card] || Kuv hlub koj. = I love you. || koj = you (one person) [card] || Ua tsaug. = Thank you. [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
w is a vowel
 English w is a consonant. In Hmong, w is always a vowel: the i of "roses", said with your lips spread. tw mid means tail and pw mid means to lie down. Written after a, it is a glide: sawv rising (to get up).
 EX: pw | mid | to lie down; to sleep
CARDS NOW: tw = tail [card] || pw = to lie down; to sleep

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
An h after a consonant is a puff of air
 Hold your hand in front of your mouth and say "pin", then "spin". "pin" has a puff of air; "spin" does not. English never uses that puff to tell words apart. Hmong does, and writes it with an h. Plain p, t, k and q have no puff, like the p of "spin". With an h (ph, th, kh, qh, txh, tsh, ch) they are aspirated: a puff follows. So ph is never an f, and th is never the th of "think".
 No puff ; Meaning ; Aspirated ; Meaning ; 
 pom ; to see ; phom ; gun ; 
 kaus ; umbrella ; khaus ; itchy ; 
 tsis ; not ; tshis ; goat ; 
 tiab ; skirt ; thiab ; and ; 
 EX: Thov txim. | rising · creaky | Sorry.
 EX: phooj ywg | falling · falling-breathy | friend
 Most common mistake: reading x as English "ks" and ph as f. xav said "ksav" → xav , with a plain s; phooj ywg said "fong" → a p with a puff of air.
CARDS NOW: pom = to see [card] || Thov txim. = Sorry. [card] || phooj ywg = friend

## LESSON consonants-2 — 3 · n- and h- sounds: npua, ntawv, hmo, hnub — deck "Consonants and vowels"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
Two more patterns: an n in front of a consonant, and an h in front of m, n or l. Then the vowels.
 n in front: a nasal start
 English has "mb" in "number" and "nd" in "under", but never at the start of a word. Hmong starts words this way. These sounds are called pre-nasalised: hum briefly through your nose, then let the consonant go.
 Spelling ; Sounds like ; Example ; Meaning ; 
 np ; mb ; npua ; pig ; 
 nt ; nd ; ntawv ; paper; book ; 
 nk ; ngg ; nkauj ; song ; 
 nq ; ng + a far-back g ; nqaij ; meat ; 
 ntx ; ndz ; ntxuav ; to wash ; 
 nts ; ndj, tongue curled back ; ntses ; fish ; 
 EX: npua | mid | pig
 EX: ntawv | rising | paper; book; letter
 EX: ntses | low | fish
 The n makes a new word: pua mid is hundred but npua mid is pig; tsev is house but ntsev rising is salt. With an h after the consonant you get a nasal start plus a puff of air: ntxhais low (daughter), ntshai mid (afraid).
CARDS NOW: npua = pig [card] || ntawv = paper; writing; book [card] || nkauj = song [card] || ntses = fish [card] || ntsev = salt [card] || ntxhais = daughter [card] || ntshai = afraid; scared [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
h in front: breathy sounds
 hm, hn and hny are breathy: let air out through your nose before the m or n. hl is a breathy l, like the Welsh ll: tongue in place for l, then blow air past its sides. These breathy sounds belong to White Hmong; Green Hmong often drops the h (nub for day).
 EX: hmo | mid | night
 EX: hnub | high | day; sun
 EX: hlub | high | to love
 Again one letter makes a new word: nab high is snake, hnab high is bag.
CARDS NOW: hmo = night; evening meal [card] || hnub = day; sun [card] || hlub = to love [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 5)
Vowels
 A doubled vowel is a nasal vowel. "ee" is close to "eng" ( neeg , person) and "oo" is close to "ong" ( zoo , good; Hmoob ). No g is said: the vowel simply goes through your nose. Vowel pairs glide: "ia" like "ee-uh" ( niam , mother), "ua" like "oo-uh" ( ua , to do), "ai" like "eye" ( hais , to say), "au" like "ow" ( nkauj , song), and "aw" is "a" sliding into the Hmong w ( sawv , to get up).
 EX: neeg | falling-breathy | person; people
 EX: Peb hais lus Hmoob. | high · low · low · high | We speak Hmong.
 Most common mistake: dropping the n and saying np as a plain b. npua said "bua" → npua , starting with a short hum: "mbua".
CARDS NOW: neeg = person; people [card] || zoo = good [card] || Hmoob = Hmong [card] || niam = mother [card] || Peb hais lus Hmoob. = We speak Hmong. [card]

## LESSON greetings — 4 · Nyob zoo, ua tsaug, sib ntsib dua — deck "Greetings and courtesy"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Nyob zoo high · mid is hello, to anyone, at any hour: morning, noon and night.
 EX: Nyob zoo. | high · mid | Hello.
 No "good morning"
 English changes its greeting with the clock. Hmong does not. Omniglot's phrase list marks Nyob zoo sawv ntxov ("hello, morning") as not common, so do not build "good morning" word by word. Just say Nyob zoo .
CARDS NOW: Nyob zoo. = Hello. [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 5)
How are you?
 Two common ways to ask, and the usual answer:
 EX: Koj nyob li cas? | falling · high · mid · low | How are you?
 EX: Koj puas nyob zoo? | falling · low · high · mid | Are you well?
 EX: Kuv nyob zoo. | rising · high · mid | I'm fine.
 koj falling is you and kuv rising is I. They look alike, so read the vowel and the tone letter with care.
CARDS NOW: Koj nyob li cas? = How are you? [card] || koj = you (one person) [card] || Koj puas nyob zoo? = Are you doing well? [card] || Kuv nyob zoo. = I'm fine. [card] || kuv = I; me; my [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 6)
Thanks, sorry, excuse me
 Hmong ; English ; When ; 
 Ua tsaug ; Thank you ; any thanks ; 
 Tsis ua li cas ; You're welcome; it's nothing ; the reply to thanks ; 
 Thov txim ; Sorry ; an apology ; 
 Thov zam kev ; Excuse me ; to get past someone ; 
 Thov hais dua ; Please say it again ; when you did not catch it ; 
 EX: Ua tsaug. | mid · falling-breathy | Thank you.
 EX: Tsis ua li cas. | low · mid · mid · low | You're welcome.
 thov rising means please, or to ask. Put it in front of a request and the request becomes polite. Two requests every learner needs:
 EX: Thov hais dua. | rising · low · mid | Please say it again.
 EX: Thov hais maj mam. | rising · low · falling · creaky | Please speak slowly.
 Tsis ua li cas means "it's nothing", the reply when someone thanks you.
CARDS NOW: Ua tsaug. = Thank you. [card] || Tsis ua li cas. = You're welcome. [card] || Thov txim. = Sorry. [card] || Thov zam kev. = Excuse me (let me pass). [card] || Thov hais dua. = Please say it again. [card] || Thov hais maj mam. = Please speak slowly. [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 6)
Goodbye, and the soft os
 Sib ntsib dua high · high · mid means "see you again". To the person who is leaving you can say Mus zoo low · mid , "go well". At night: Pw zoo os , "sleep well". The little word os at the end softens a sentence and sounds warm to Hmong ears.
 EX: Sib ntsib dua. | high · high · mid | Goodbye. (See you again.)
 EX: Pw zoo os. | mid · mid · low | Good night.
 Meeting someone for the first time, say Zoo siab tau ntsib koj mid · high · mid · high · falling , "happy to meet you". Omniglot gives Kuv zoo siab ntsib koj , with the same meaning.
 EX: Zoo siab tau ntsib koj. | mid · high · mid · high · falling | Nice to meet you.
 When you arrive at a Hmong home, older relatives may greet you differently, for example Koj tuaj los? , "You've come!". The lesson on talking with elders covers those.
 Most common mistake: translating "good morning" word for word. Nyob zoo sawv ntxov → Nyob zoo , at any time of day.
CARDS NOW: Sib ntsib dua. = See you again. [card] || Mus zoo. = Go well (goodbye to someone leaving). [card] || Pw zoo os. = Good night. [card] || Zoo siab tau ntsib koj. = Nice to meet you. [card] || Kuv zoo siab ntsib koj. = I'm pleased to meet you. [card] || Koj tuaj los? = You've come! (greeting a guest) [card]

DECK CARDS IN NO SECTION (add by front, as a string, only where a section teaches them): Kuv thov txim. | rising · rising · creaky | I'm sorry. || Hais dua puas tau? | low · mid · low · mid | Could you say that again? || Kuv tsis nkag siab. | rising · low · falling-breathy · high | I don't understand.

## LESSON pronouns — 5 · kuv, koj, nws, and "the two of us" — deck "About me"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
English has I, you, he, she, it, we and they. Hmong has nine pronouns, because it counts: one person, two people, or three or more.
 ; One ; Two ; Three or more ; 
 I, we ; kuv ; wb ; peb ; 
 you ; koj ; neb ; nej ; 
 he, she, it, they ; nws ; nkawd ; lawv ; 
 One word for he, she and it
 nws low means he, she or it. Hmong does not mark the person's sex here; the situation tells you. English speakers sometimes hunt for a separate word for she: there is none.
 EX: Nws nyob zoo. | low · high · mid | He's fine. (or She's fine.)
CARDS NOW: kuv = I; me; my [card] || peb = three [card] || koj = you (one person) [card] || nws = he; she; it [card] || Nws nyob zoo. = He's fine. (or She's fine.)

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
The two of us
 English "we" can be two people or twenty. Hmong splits it. wb high is exactly two (you and I, or she and I), and peb high is three or more. The same split holds for you ( neb high two, nej falling three or more) and for they ( nkawd rising-final two, lawv rising three or more). Some books write nkawv for nkawd .
 EX: Wb nyob zoo. | high · high · mid | We're fine. (the two of us)
 EX: Peb hais lus Hmoob. | high · low · low · high | We speak Hmong.
 EX: Lawv nyob zoo. | rising · high · mid | They're fine.
CARDS NOW: Wb nyob zoo. = We're fine. (the two of us) || Peb hais lus Hmoob. = We speak Hmong. [card] || Lawv nyob zoo. = They're fine.

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
I, me and my are one word
 Hmong pronouns never change form. kuv is I, me and my; koj is you and your. Where the word sits tells you which.
 EX: Kuv hlub koj. | rising · high · falling | I love you.
 EX: Koj hlub kuv. | falling · high · rising | You love me.
 EX: Koj yog kuv tus phooj ywg. | falling · falling-breathy · rising · low · falling · falling-breathy | You are my friend.
CARDS NOW: Kuv hlub koj. = I love you. || Koj hlub kuv. = You love me. || Koj yog kuv tus phooj ywg. = You're my friend. [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
One "you" for everyone
 English has one "you", and so does Hmong for one person: koj . Phrase books use it with strangers as well as friends. Politeness comes from other words, such as thov (please) and the soft os , and, with older relatives, from family words that a later lesson teaches.
CARDS NOW: (none)

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Watch the tone letters
 neb and nej differ only in the tone letter, and so do kuv and kub (hot). peb is also the number three. Green Hmong uses other forms for some of these (mej for nej, nwg for nws, puab for lawv); this course writes the White ones.
 Most common mistake: using peb for just two people. "The two of us are fine" as Peb nyob zoo → Wb nyob zoo .
CARDS NOW: kub = hot [card]

## LESSON yog-nyob — 6 · Two ways to say "is": yog and nyob — deck "About me"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
English uses "is" for three jobs: "This is the house", "I am in the house", "I am happy". Hmong has a different tool for each.
 yog: X is a Y
 Use yog falling-breathy to say what or who something is. Nov rising means this (here).
 EX: Nov yog lub tsev. | rising · falling-breathy · high · rising | This is the house.
 EX: Koj yog kuv tus phooj ywg. | falling · falling-breathy · rising · low · falling · falling-breathy | You are my friend.
 EX: Kuv yog Hmoob. | rising · falling-breathy · high | I'm Hmong.
 The small words lub high and tus low in front of a noun are classifiers, counting words: lub tsev (house), tus phooj ywg (friend). The classifiers lesson explains them; for now, copy each one with its noun.
CARDS NOW: Nov yog lub tsev. = This is the house. || lub tsev = a house; the house [card] || Koj yog kuv tus phooj ywg. = You're my friend. [card] || Kuv yog Hmoob. = I'm Hmong. [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
nyob: to be somewhere
 nyob high means to be in a place, to live, to stay. Use it for where.
 EX: Kuv nyob hauv lub tsev. | rising · high · rising · high · rising | I'm in the house.
 EX: Kuv nyob Minneapolis. | rising · high · — | I live in Minneapolis.
 It is also the verb in Kuv nyob zoo , "I'm fine": literally, I stay well. In Kuv nyob hauv lub tsev , the word hauv rising means in, inside.
 So ask yourself one question: am I saying what something is, or where it is? What it is takes yog ; where it is takes nyob . A third kind of "is", hu ua (is called), comes in the lesson on introducing yourself.
CARDS NOW: nyob = to live; to be (somewhere) [card] || Kuv nyob hauv lub tsev. = I'm in the house. || Kuv nyob Minneapolis. = I live in Minneapolis. [card] || Kuv nyob zoo. = I'm fine. [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
Describing words need no "is"
 In Hmong a describing word works like a verb by itself. Nothing goes between the person and the description: "I happy", "she kind".
 EX: Kuv zoo siab. | rising · mid · high | I'm happy.
 EX: Nws zoo siab heev. | low · mid · high · rising | She's very happy.
 EX: Nws siab zoo. | low · high · mid | He's kind.
 Notice zoo siab (happy) and siab zoo (kind): the same two words in the other order mean something else. heev (very) comes after the describing word.
 Most common mistake: adding yog before a describing word. Kuv yog zoo siab → Kuv zoo siab .
CARDS NOW: Kuv zoo siab. = I'm happy. [card] || zoo siab = happy; glad [card] || Nws zoo siab heev. = She's very happy. || Nws siab zoo. = She's kind. [card]

## LESSON questions — 7 · puas, los, dab tsi, leej twg, qhov twg — deck "Questions and connectors"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
English questions move words around: "You speak English" becomes "Do you speak English?". Hmong keeps the sentence in its order and adds one small word.
 Yes or no: puas before the verb
 Put puas low right before the verb.
 EX: Koj puas hais lus Askiv? | falling · low · low · low · low · rising | Do you speak English?
 EX: Koj puas nyob zoo? | falling · low · high · mid | Are you well?
 Word for word, the first one is "you puas speak language English?": lus is language, and lus Askiv is English. Yes is yog ("it is"); for no, the next lesson puts tsis before the verb.
CARDS NOW: Koj puas hais lus Askiv? = Do you speak English? [card] || Koj puas nyob zoo? = Are you doing well? [card] || Tsis yog. = No, it isn't. [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
"... or not?" and the friendly los
 Another way to ask is to put los tsis yog low · low · falling-breathy , "or not?", at the end.
 EX: Koj noj mov lawm los tsis yog? | falling · falling · rising · creaky · low · low · falling-breathy | Have you eaten yet?
 A short los at the end asks a question that expects yes, as when a visitor arrives.
 EX: Koj tuaj los? | falling · falling · low | You've come!
CARDS NOW: Koj noj mov lawm los tsis yog? = Have you eaten yet? [card] || Koj tuaj los? = You've come! (greeting a guest) [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 9)
Question words stay put
 English moves "what", "who" and "where" to the front. Hmong leaves the question word where the answer will go.

 EX: Koj xav noj dab tsi? | falling · rising · falling · high · mid | What do you want to eat?

## LESSON classifiers — 11 · tus, lub, daim, rab: counting words — deck "Things and classifiers"

## LESSON time — 14 · Days, times and "when" — deck "Time and days"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
English changes the verb for the past: go , went . Hmong verbs never change. A time word tells you when, and it usually comes at the start of the sentence.
 Today, yesterday, tomorrow
 hnub no ; today ; 
 nag hmo ; yesterday ; 
 tag kis ; tomorrow (also written taskis ) ; 
 EX: Nag hmo kuv mus tom khw. | falling-breathy · mid · rising · low · creaky · mid | Yesterday I went to the store.
 EX: Hnub no kuv mus tom khw. | high · mid · rising · low · creaky · mid | Today I'm going to the store.
 The verb mus is the same both times. Only the time word changed.
CARDS NOW: hnub no = today [card] || nag hmo = yesterday [card] || tag kis = tomorrow [card] || Nag hmo kuv mus tom khw. = Yesterday I went to the store. [card] || Hnub no kuv mus tom khw. = Today I'm going to the store.

### SECTION 1   (has 7)
Parts of the day
 sawv ntxov rising · rising is morning, tav su rising · mid noon and early afternoon, tsaus ntuj low · falling evening and hmo ntuj mid · falling night.
 EX: Sawv ntxov kuv haus kav fes. | rising · rising · rising · low · rising · low | In the morning I drink coffee.
 For clock times, US Hmong texts write the digit followed by the part of the day. teev rising means "hour" or "o'clock".
 EX: 7 sawv ntxov txog 6 tsaus ntuj | — · rising · rising · falling-breathy · — · low · falling | 7 a.m. to 6 p.m.
CARDS NOW: sawv ntxov = morning [card] || tav su = noon; midday [card] || tsaus ntuj = evening [card] || hmo ntuj = night [card] || Sawv ntxov kuv haus kav fes. = In the morning I drink coffee. || 7 sawv ntxov txog 6 tsaus ntuj = 7 a.m. to 6 p.m. || 7 sawv ntxov = 7 a.m. [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 7)
Days and weeks
 Day is hnub high . US Hmong agencies name the weekdays with the English name after hnub : hnub Monday , hnub Friday . Some families and books number the days ( hnub ib , hnub ob …), but the books disagree on whether hnub ib is Sunday or Monday, so ask your family. hnub so high · mid is a day off (older texts use it for Sunday). A week is lim tiam creaky · creaky (also as thiv ), and every day is txhua hnub mid · high .

CARDS NOW: hnub = day; sun [card] || hnub Monday = Monday [card] || hnub so = day off; rest day [card] || lim tiam = week [card] || txhua hnub = every day [card] || hnub Monday txog hnub Friday = Monday to Friday [card] || Kuv ua hauj lwm txhua hnub. = I work every day.

### SECTION 3   (has 5)
Months and years
 Months are numbered: ib hlis ntuj high · low · falling is January, ob hlis ntuj February, and so on up to kaum ob hlis ntuj , December. Copy each name as written, because a few have hli instead of hlis : rau hli ntuj (June), yim hli ntuj (August), kaum hli ntuj (October). A year is xyoo mid . Next year is lwm xyoo , and last year is tsaib no .
 EX: kaum ob hlis ntuj | creaky · high · low · falling | December
 To ask "when?", use thaum twg creaky · falling-breathy .
 Most common mistake: hunting for a past form of the verb. For "Yesterday I went to the store", Kuv mus tom khw on its own → Nag hmo kuv mus tom khw . The time word does the work.
CARDS NOW: ib hlis ntuj = January [card] || kaum ob hlis ntuj = December || xyoo = year [card] || lwm xyoo = next year [card] || Kuv mus tom khw. = I'm going to the market. [card]

## LESSON aspect — 15 · Done, doing, going to: lawm, tab tom, yuav — deck "Time and days"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
Lesson 14 showed that a time word says when. Hmong also has small words that show the stage of an action: done, going on, or still to come. They are optional, so use one when it adds something. When a time word already makes the meaning clear, Hmong often leaves them out:
 EX: Nag hmo kuv mus tom khw. | falling-breathy · mid · rising · low · creaky · mid | Yesterday I went to the store.
 English, by contrast, has to mark the verb every time: go , went , have gone , am going . In Hmong the verb stays the same and these helpers sit around it.
 Done: lawm
 lawm creaky goes after the verb and its object. Its tone letter is m, the creaky tone. lawv , with v, means "they".
 EX: Kuv noj mov lawm. | rising · falling · rising · creaky | I've eaten.
 EX: Koj noj mov lawm los tsis yog? | falling · falling · rising · creaky · low · low · falling-breathy | Have you eaten yet?
 twb high before the verb, with lawm after the verb and its object, means "already": the two words wrap around the action. tau mid before a verb also points to a finished action.
 EX: Kuv twb noj mov lawm. | rising · high · falling · rising · creaky | I've already eaten.
CARDS NOW: Nag hmo kuv mus tom khw. = Yesterday I went to the store. [card] || nag hmo = yesterday [card] || Kuv noj mov lawm. = I've eaten. [card] || Koj noj mov lawm los tsis yog? = Have you eaten yet? [card] || Kuv twb noj mov lawm. = I've already eaten.

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
Going on: tab tom
 tab tom high · creaky goes before the verb and means "in the middle of". It is the closest thing to English "-ing". Like all these words, it never changes with the person: kuv , koj and lawv all use the same tab tom .
 EX: Lawv tab tom haus dej. | rising · high · creaky · low · falling | They're drinking water.
CARDS NOW: kuv = I; me; my [card] || koj = you (one person) [card] || Lawv tab tom haus dej. = They're drinking water. [card]

 EX: Kuv yuav mus tsev. | rising · rising · low · rising | I'm going home.
 EX: Kuv mam li mus. | rising · creaky · mid · low | I'll go later.

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)

## LESSON places — 16 · pem, nram, tom, tim: up, down, over there — deck "Getting around"
### SECTION 0   (has 6)
English says "there" for any place away from you. Hmong picks a word by height and distance.
 Up, down, over there
 pem ; up there (higher than you) ; 
 nram ; down there (lower than you) ; 
 tom ; over there, at (about the same level) ; 
 tim ; over there, across (farther than tom ) ; 
 EX: Nws nyob pem roob. | low · high · creaky · high | She's up on the mountain.
 EX: Lawv nyob nram lub zos. | rising · high · creaky · high · low | They're down in the village.
 Because tom also means "at", Kuv mus tom khw is simply "I'm going to the market". And since these words describe a place from where you stand, the same house can be pem for one person and nram for another.
 EX: Kuv mus tom khw. | rising · low · creaky · mid | I'm going to the market.

## LESSON serial-verbs — 18 · Verbs in a row: mus yuav, los noj — deck "Food and drink"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
English joins two actions with "and" or "to": go and buy , come to eat , want to go . Hmong simply puts the verbs side by side, with one subject in front.
 Go and do: mus + verb
 "I'm going out to buy vegetables" is Kuv mus yuav zaub rising · low · rising · high : I, go, buy, vegetables. There is no word for "and" or "to", and the subject is said only once, at the front. The verbs come in the order the actions happen: first you go, then you buy. The same goes for "go to work" and "go to school": mus ua hauj lwm , mus kawm ntawv .
 EX: Kuv mus yuav zaub. | rising · low · rising · high | I'm going out to buy vegetables.
 EX: Peb mus kav khw. | high · low · rising · mid | We're going shopping.

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
Come and do: los + verb

## LESSON requests — 19 · Please and may I: thov, puas tau — deck "Health and help"
### SECTION 0   (has 6)
English makes a request polite with "please" or "could you". Hmong has two tools for the same job: a word before the verb, and a short tag at the end of the sentence.
 thov before the verb
 You already know thov rising from thov txim (sorry) and thov hais dua (please say it again). It means "to ask, to request", and as "please" it goes right before the verb. English can put "please" at the start or the end; Hmong keeps thov in front of the action.
 EX: Thov zaum. | rising · creaky | Please sit down.
 EX: Thov los noj mov. | rising · low · falling · rising | Please come and eat.
 EX: Thov pab kuv. | rising · high · rising | Please help me.
CARDS NOW: Thov txim. = Sorry. [card] || Thov hais dua. = Please say it again. [card] || Thov zaum. = Please sit down. || Thov los noj mov. = Please come and eat. || Thov pab kuv. = Please help me. [card] || Pab! = Help! [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
puas tau? at the end
 English asks "May I…?" or "Could you…?" at the start. Hmong says the action first and adds puas tau? low · mid at the end, roughly "is that OK?". The same tag works for asking permission ( Kuv nug puas tau? , May I ask?) and for asking a favor ( Pab kuv puas tau? , Could you help me?).
 EX: Hais dua puas tau? | low · mid · low · mid | Could you say it again?
 EX: Pab kuv puas tau? | high · rising · low · mid | Could you help me?

 pab high is "to help". On its own, Pab! , it is the word to shout in an emergency: "Help!" (Omniglot gives Pab! ; a Wikivoyage phrasebook writes Pab os! .)

## LESSON family-events — 26 · Family events and blessings — deck "Family events"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
Family events bring the whole extended family together. This lesson gives you the words for the people, the gatherings and some of the traditions. Families of different faiths, traditional and Christian, mark births, weddings and New Year in their own ways.
 Clan and family
 Every Hmong family belongs to a clan, xeem creaky , which is also the surname: Vang, Yang, Xiong, Lee, Thao, Vue, Moua, Her, Lor and others. Members of the same clan often think of each other as related. The family is tsev neeg rising · falling-breathy , and sawvdaws rising · low is "everyone".

Two traditional blessings
 In many families, a traditional ceremony called hu plig mid · falling-breathy , soul calling, calls a person's soul home to the body: for example for a new baby, at New Year, or after an illness or a fright. In another blessing, khi tes mid · low ("tie hand"), elders tie strings around a person's wrist and wish them health. Families name it in slightly different ways; ask yours what they call it.
