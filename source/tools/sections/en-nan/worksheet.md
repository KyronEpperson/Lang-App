# Worksheet: en-nan

## LESSON initials — 1 · Tâi-lô consonants: p and ph, ts and tsh, and the voiced b, g, j — deck "Tâi-lô sounds"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
This course teaches Taiwanese Hokkien as spoken in Taiwan. From here on we call it Taigi, or Taiwanese. Taigi is written in characters, and under every word you will see a line of Tâi-lô , the Ministry of Education's romanization. The course has no audio, so Tâi-lô is your guide to the sound. Learn to read it first.
 One character, one syllable
 Every character is one syllable. In Tâi-lô a hyphen joins the syllables of one word, and a space separates words. 食飯 tsia̍h-pn̄g is one word of two syllables; 毋是 m̄ sī is two words. The marks over the vowels are tones (lesson 3). For now, copy them exactly: a different mark makes a different word.
 EX: 食飯 | tsia̍h-pn̄g | eat a meal
 EX: 毋是 | m̄ sī | isn't; is not
CARDS NOW: 食飯 = eat a meal; have dinner [card] || 毋是 = isn't; not (to be) [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 6)
With or without a puff of air
 Hold a hand in front of your mouth and say English pin , then spin . The p of pin comes with a puff of air; the p of spin does not. English never uses that difference to tell words apart. Taigi does, and Tâi-lô writes the puff with an h .
 No puff (like spin) ; With a puff (like pin) ; 
 p: 飽 pá ; ph: 歹勢 pháinn-sè ; 
 t: 茶 tê ; th: 天 thinn ; 
 k: 狗 káu ; kh: 去 khì ; 
 ts: 早 tsá ; tsh: 厝 tshù ; 
 So ph is never an f, and th is never the th of think . Plain ts is like the ts in cats .
 EX: 飽 | pá | full (after eating)
 EX: 歹勢 | pháinn-sè | sorry; excuse me
 EX: 厝 | tshù | house; home
CARDS NOW: 飽 = full (after eating) [card] || 歹勢。 = Sorry. / Excuse me. [card] || 茶 = tea [card] || 天 = sky [card] || 早 = early [card] || 厝 = house; home [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
Voiced b, g and j
 b and g are real voiced sounds, as in bee and go : your throat buzzes. j is voiced too. Bite-size Taiwanese, a podcast that teaches Taigi pronunciation, writes it [dʑ] in phonetic notation. Copy the Tâi-lô j and let the voice buzz.
 EX: 肉 | bah | meat
 EX: 牛 | gû | cow; ox
 EX: 熱 | jua̍h | hot
CARDS NOW: 肉 = meat [card] || 牛 = cow; ox [card] || 熱 = hot (weather) [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
The easy ones
 m, n, l, s and h are close to English: 麵 mī (noodles), 貓 niau (cat), 老師 lāu-su (teacher), 三 sann (three), 好 hó (good). ng can also start a syllable, like the ng of singer without the "si".
CARDS NOW: 老師 = teacher [card]

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
If you learned POJ
 Older church books use POJ. Read ch as ts, chh as tsh, o͘ as oo, ⁿ as nn, oa and oe as ua and ue. When you type, POJ spellings are accepted.
CARDS NOW: (none)

### SECTION 5   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
Typing tones
 No tone marks on your keyboard? Type the tone number after the syllable: pa2 for pá , tshu3 for tshù .
 Most common mistake: reading ph as an English f. 歹勢 said "fai-seh" → pháinn-sè , a p with a puff of air.
CARDS NOW: (none)

## LESSON finals — 2 · Vowels and endings: o and oo, nasal nn, and the stops -p -t -k -h — deck "Tâi-lô sounds"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
A Taigi syllable is an initial (lesson 1) plus a final: the vowel and whatever comes after it. Several finals look English and are not.
 o and oo
 Tâi-lô has two o's. oo is open, like the aw of law . Single o is a different vowel: Bite-size Taiwanese writes 好 hó as [hə] and 虎 hóo as [hɔ]. Copy the spelling: o and oo are never interchangeable. They make different words: 好 hó is good, 虎 hóo is tiger.
 EX: 好 | hó | good
 EX: 虎 | hóo | tiger
CARDS NOW: 好 = good; fine; OK [card] || 虎 = tiger [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Nasal vowels: nn
 A double nn after the vowel is not a letter n. It means the vowel itself goes through the nose, as in French vin . Say "ah" and let air out through your nose: that is 三 sann . Do not close your tongue for an n.
 EX: 三 | sann | three
 EX: 天 | thinn | sky
CARDS NOW: 三 = three [card] || 天 = sky [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
-m, -n, -ng
 These are ordinary closing nasals, as in English hum , sun , sung . Keep them apart: 鹹 kiâm (salty) closes the lips, 真 tsin (very) touches the tongue behind the teeth, 人 lâng (person) closes at the back.
 EX: 鹹 | kiâm | salty
CARDS NOW: 鹹 = salty [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 4)
Short stops: -p, -t, -k, -h
 In English, a final p is often released with a small pop. In Taigi, -p, -t and -k are never released: close the lips or tongue and stop, with no pop and no extra vowel. 十 tsa̍p ends with closed lips; 七 tshit ends with the tongue held up; 鹿 lo̍k ends at the back of the mouth.
 A final -h is not a breath. It is a glottal stop, a catch in the throat that cuts the vowel short, like the break in uh-oh . These short syllables are called checked syllables, and they have only two tones (lesson 3).
 EX: 十 | tsa̍p | ten
 EX: 鹿 | lo̍k | deer
 EX: 肉 | bah | meat
CARDS NOW: 十 = ten [card] || 七 = seven [card] || 鹿 = deer [card] || 肉 = meat [card]

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Syllables with no vowel
 m and ng can be whole syllables. 毋 m̄ (not) is a hummed m, and 卵 nn̄g (egg) is a hummed ng. The tone mark sits on the m or the n. 卵 sounds exactly like 兩 nn̄g (two).
 EX: 毋 | m̄ | not (won't, isn't)
 EX: 卵 | nn̄g | egg
 Most common mistake: saying nn as a real n. 三 said "san" → sann , an open nasal "ah" with no n at the end.
CARDS NOW: 毋 = not (won't, isn't) [card] || 卵 = egg [card]

## LESSON tones — 3 · The seven tones and their marks — deck "Seven tones"
### SECTION 0   (has 6)
In English, pitch shows feeling: a rise for a question, a fall for a statement. In Taigi, pitch is part of the word. 獅 sai is a lion; 豹 pà is a leopard; 飽 pá is full. Change the tone and you change the word.
 Seven tones, numbered 1 to 8
 Tones are named by number. The general accent this course teaches has no tone 6, so you meet tones 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 7 and 8. The pitch numbers run from 1 (low) to 5 (high): 51 means start high and fall low. Sources differ slightly: the numbers below are from taioaan.org, while Bite-size Taiwanese writes tone 2 as 53, tone 3 as 21 and tone 4 as 2.
 Tone ; Mark ; Pitch ; Example ; 
 1 ; none ; 55: high, level ; 獅 sai (lion) ; 
 2 ; acute á ; 51: high, falling ; 虎 hóo (tiger) ; 
 3 ; grave à ; 31: low, falling ; 豹 pà (leopard) ; 
 4 ; none, ends in -p -t -k -h ; 3: short, mid ; 鱉 pih (soft-shelled turtle) ; 
 5 ; circumflex â ; 24: rising ; 猴 kâu (monkey) ; 
 7 ; macron ā ; 33: middle, level ; 象 tshiūnn (elephant) ; 
 8 ; short vertical line a̍ ; 5: short, high ; 鹿 lo̍k (deer) ;
CARDS NOW: 獅 = lion [card] || 豹 = leopard [card] || 鱉 = soft-shelled turtle [card] || 猴 = monkey [card] || 象 = elephant [card] || 鹿 = deer [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
The eight animals
 Learners in Taiwan remember the tones with eight animals in tone order: 獅虎豹鱉猴狗象鹿 . The sixth animal, 狗 káu (dog), stands in for the missing tone 6 and is tone 2 again.
 EX: 獅 | sai | lion
 EX: 虎 | hóo | tiger
 EX: 豹 | pà | leopard
 EX: 鱉 | pih | soft-shelled turtle
 EX: 猴 | kâu | monkey
 EX: 象 | tshiūnn | elephant
 EX: 鹿 | lo̍k | deer
CARDS NOW: 獅虎豹鱉猴狗象鹿 = lion, tiger, leopard, turtle, monkey, dog, elephant, deer [card] || 狗 = dog [card] || 虎 = tiger [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
Two tones without a mark
 Tones 1 and 4 have no mark. You tell them apart by the ending: a syllable ending in -p, -t, -k or -h is a checked syllable, and an unmarked checked syllable is tone 4. Checked syllables take only tone 4 or tone 8.
CARDS NOW: (none)

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
Watch tone 2
 English speakers hear high tones as "the same". Tone 1 stays high and flat; tone 2 starts high and drops fast, like a firm "No!". It never rises like an English question. Tone 5 is the rising one.
 Most common mistake: saying tone 2 with a question rise. 虎 rising like "hoo?" → hóo , a sharp fall from high to low.
CARDS NOW: (none)

## LESSON sandhi — 4 · Tone sandhi 1: every syllable but the last changes — deck "Tone sandhi in action"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
English words keep their sounds when you put them together. Taigi syllables do not: they change their tone depending on what follows. This is called tone sandhi, and it is the biggest surprise for new learners.
 The rule
 The tone mark shows each syllable's own tone, its citation tone. In speech, every syllable except the last one of a group changes to its sandhi tone. The last one keeps the tone you see. A group is a phrase, and where it ends depends on the structure of the sentence (Wikipedia, Tone sandhi; Bite-size Taiwanese). A word said on its own is a group by itself.
 So in a two-syllable word, the first syllable changes and the second keeps its mark. 火車 hué-tshia (train): hué has tone 2, but before tshia it is said in tone 1.
 EX: 火車 | hué-tshia | train
CARDS NOW: 火車 = train [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
The circle
 For syllables that do not end in -p, -t, -k or -h, the changes go in a circle:
 Citation tone ; Said before another syllable ; Example ; 
 1 ; 7 ; 公園 kong-hn̂g ; 
 7 ; 3 ; 大人 tuā-lâng ; 
 3 ; 2 ; 菜單 tshài-tuann ; 
 2 ; 1 ; 火車 hué-tshia ; 
 5 ; 7 (some northern speakers: 3) ; 牛肉 gû-bah ; 
 EX: 公園 | kong-hn̂g | park
 EX: 大人 | tuā-lâng | adult
 EX: 菜單 | tshài-tuann | menu
 EX: 牛肉 | gû-bah | beef
 Read the circle as a chain: 1 becomes 7, 7 becomes 3, 3 becomes 2, 2 becomes 1. Tone 5 becomes 7 in the general accent this course follows; some northern speakers make it 3. Both are normal (Wikipedia: "Tone 5 becomes 7 or 3, depending on dialect").
CARDS NOW: 公園 = park [card] || 大人 = adult [card] || 菜單 = menu [card] || 牛肉 = beef [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
The spelling does not change
 Tâi-lô always prints the citation tone, as the Ministry of Education's dictionary does. You see hué and say it in tone 1; you see kong and say it in tone 7. When you type or spell a word, write the citation tone.
 EX: 好食 | hó-tsia̍h | tasty; delicious
 EX: 老師 | lāu-su | teacher
CARDS NOW: 好食 = tasty; delicious [card] || 老師 = teacher [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
Practise on two-syllable words
 For now, apply the rule inside one word: change the first syllable, keep the last. Checked syllables and the light syllables after -- come in the next lesson.
 Most common mistake: reading every mark literally. 公園 with kong in high tone 1 → kong said in middle tone 7 before hn̂g, while the spelling stays kong-hn̂g .
CARDS NOW: (none)

## LESSON sandhi-2 — 5 · Tone sandhi 2: checked syllables, 仔, and the neutral tone after -- — deck "Tone sandhi in action"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
Lesson 4 covered syllables that end in a vowel or a nasal. This lesson adds the short checked syllables, the light syllable after -- , and a word about 仔 .
 Checked syllables in -p, -t, -k
 Checked syllables have only two tones, 4 and 8. When they change in sandhi, they swap: high becomes low and low becomes high (Wikipedia, Tone sandhi; Bite-size Taiwanese, "4 and 8 swap"). So tone 4 becomes tone 8, and tone 8 becomes tone 4. 失禮 sit-lé (I apologize): sit is tone 4, said in tone 8 before lé.
 EX: 失禮 | sit-lé | I apologize; excuse me
 EX: 讀冊 | tha̍k-tsheh | study; go to school
 EX: 捷運 | tsia̍t-ūn | MRT; metro
CARDS NOW: 失禮。 = I apologize. [card] || 讀冊 = study; go to school [card] || 捷運 = MRT; metro [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Checked syllables in -h
 A syllable ending in -h loses its catch in sandhi: the -h disappears and the syllable takes a full tone. Tone 4 becomes tone 2 (Bite-size Taiwanese; taioaan.org: -h syllables are "usually pronounced in shouting tone", its name for tone 2). Tone 8 becomes tone 3: only Bite-size Taiwanese gives this pair; Wikipedia says such syllables "drop their final consonant to become tone 2 or 3". 肉圓 bah-uân (meat dumpling): bah is said without its catch.
 EX: 肉圓 | bah-uân | Taiwanese meat dumpling
 As always, the spelling keeps the citation tone: you still write sit , tha̍k , bah .
CARDS NOW: 肉圓 = Taiwanese meat dumpling [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
The neutral tone after --
 Two hyphens mark a neutral tone. The syllable after -- is said light and short, without its own tone, and the syllable before it keeps its citation tone instead of changing (the MOE romanization manual: the syllable before -- "唸本調", keeps its own tone). After tones 1, 5 and 7 the light syllable continues the pitch of the one before it. Tâi-lô still prints the light syllable's own mark.
 EX: 多謝你！ | To-siā--lí! | Thank you!
 EX: 你食飽未？ | Lí tsia̍h-pá--buē? | Have you eaten? (a friendly hello)
 EX: 厝裡 | tshù--lí | at home; in the house
 When you read a line with --, give the syllable before it its full written tone, then say the light one short and soft. In 多謝你 , siā keeps tone 7 and lí is said light. Particles at the end of a question, like 未 in 你食飽未？, are light too.
CARDS NOW: 多謝你！ = Thank you! [card] || 你食飽未？ = Have you eaten? (a friendly hello) [card] || 厝裡 = at home; in the house [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
仔
 Many nouns end in 仔 á : 桌仔 toh-á (table). The syllable before 仔 changes in its own way, which this course does not cover. Copy the word and its Tâi-lô as you meet it.
 Most common mistake: giving the light syllable a full tone. 多謝你 with a full, falling lí → To-siā--lí , with lí light and short.
CARDS NOW: 桌仔 = table; desk [card]

## LESSON greetings — 6 · Greetings: 𠢕早, 食飽未？, 多謝, 歹勢, 再會 — deck "Greetings and courtesy"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
English has "hello" for everyone. Taigi greetings are more about the moment: the time of day, a meal, a favor. Here are the ones you will hear from grandparents and neighbors.
 Good morning
 𠢕早 gâu-tsá is "good morning". Put a person's title in front to be warm and polite: an older man is 阿伯 a-peh .
 EX: 𠢕早！ | Gâu-tsá! | Good morning!
 EX: 阿伯𠢕早。 | A-peh gâu-tsá. | Good morning, sir.
 𠢕 is a rare character. If your phone shows an empty box, the Tâi-lô still tells you the word.
 Titles make any greeting warmer: 阿伯 for an older man, 阿姨 a-î for an older woman, 阿公 and 阿媽 for grandpa and grandma. Students greet a teacher as 老師 .
 You may also see 你好 lí hó , built from 你 (you) and 好 (good). This course leads with 𠢕早 and the next greeting.
CARDS NOW: 𠢕早！ = Good morning! [card] || 阿伯 = uncle (father's older brother); sir [card] || 阿伯𠢕早。 = Good morning, sir. [card] || 阿姨 = aunt (mother's sister); ma'am [card] || 阿公 = grandpa [card] || 阿媽 = grandma [card] || 老師 = teacher [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Have you eaten?
 你食飽未？ literally asks "Have you eaten yet?". It is a friendly hello, not an invitation to lunch. Answer 食飽矣 (I have) or smile and ask back. The 未 at the end is light: --buē .
 EX: 你食飽未？ | Lí tsia̍h-pá--buē? | Have you eaten? (a friendly hello)
 EX: 食飽矣。 | Tsia̍h-pá--ah. | I have eaten.
CARDS NOW: 你食飽未？ = Have you eaten? (a friendly hello) [card] || 食飽矣。 = I've eaten. [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
Thank you
 多謝 to-siā is "thank you". To one person, add 你 , said light. 勞力 lóo-la̍t also thanks someone: the MOE lists it, with 感謝 kám-siā , as a near-synonym of 多謝.
 EX: 多謝你！ | To-siā--lí! | Thank you!
CARDS NOW: 多謝 = Thanks! [card] || 勞力。 = Thanks. [card] || 感謝 = Thank you so much! [card] || 多謝你！ = Thank you! [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 3)
Sorry: small or serious
 Taigi has two everyday words for "sorry". 歹勢 pháinn-sè covers "excuse me" and "sorry": the MOE glosses it 不好意思、對不起、抱歉. 失禮 sit-lé is "I apologize"; the MOE gives it with a full apology, the example below.
 EX: 歹勢。 | Pháinn-sè. | Sorry. / Excuse me.
 EX: 真失禮，這攏是我的毋著。 | Tsin sit-lé, tse lóng sī guá ê m̄-tio̍h. | I'm very sorry, this is all my fault.
CARDS NOW: 歹勢。 = Sorry. / Excuse me. [card] || 失禮。 = I apologize. [card] || 真失禮，這攏是我的毋著。 = I'm so sorry, this is all my fault. [card]

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Meeting and leaving
 When you meet someone for the first time, say 請你多多指教 , literally "please teach me a lot": the set phrase for "nice to meet you". To leave, say 再會 tsài-huē , "meet again".
 EX: 請你多多指教。 | Tshiánn lí to-to tsí-kàu. | Nice to meet you.
 EX: 再會！ | Tsài-huē! | Goodbye!
 Most common mistake: taking 你食飽未？ as an invitation. Answering "Thanks, I'd love to eat!" → 食飽矣 , or simply greeting back.
CARDS NOW: 請你多多指教。 = Nice to meet you. (please teach me well) [card] || 再會！ = Goodbye! [card]

## LESSON characters — 7 · Reading Taigi characters: 毋, 佇, 遮, 欲, 予 — deck "Taigi characters"
### SECTION 0   (has 18)
Taigi is written in traditional characters, like Taiwan Mandarin, but many everyday words have their own characters. This course uses the Ministry of Education's recommended characters. If you read Mandarin, this lesson is about unlearning a few habits. Taigi and Mandarin are different languages with different words, so a Mandarin character is not just another spelling: 不 is a Mandarin word, while 毋 is the Taigi word m̄ , and they are not used the same way.
 Taigi words, Taigi characters
 Taigi ; Meaning ; Not (Mandarin) ; 
 毋 m̄ ; not (won't, isn't) ; 不 ; 
 佇 tī ; at, in ; 在 ; 
 欲 beh ; want to, about to ; 要 ; 
 食 tsia̍h ; eat ; 吃 ; 
 啉 lim ; drink ; 喝 ; 
 媠 suí ; beautiful ; 漂亮 ; 
 Others to know: 予 hōo (give, let), 囝 kiánn (child, son), 睏 khùn (sleep), 蹛 tuà (live), 遮 tsia (here) and 遐 hia (there), 冊 tsheh (book). Never write the Mandarin character for a Taigi word, even if a keyboard offers it first.
 EX: 我毋知。 | Guá m̄ tsai. | I don't know.
 EX: 伊佇厝裡。 | I tī tshù--lí. | She's at home.
 EX: 我欲啉茶。 | Guá beh lim tê. | I'm going to drink tea.
 EX: 伊真媠。 | I tsin suí. | She's very pretty.
 EX: 伊這馬佇厝裡咧睏。 | I tsit-má tī tshù--lí teh khùn. | He's sleeping at home now.
 Watch your reading too. A Mandarin reader's eye gives each character its Mandarin sound, but 毋 is read m̄ and 佇 is read tī . Always read the Tâi-lô line under a Taigi word, not the sound you already know.

## LESSON classroom — 8 · Classroom phrases: 請閣講一擺, 慢慢仔講 — deck "In class"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
A few phrases keep a conversation going when you are lost. Learn them early and use them often.
 Teacher and student
 A teacher is 老師 lāu-su , and a student is 學生 ha̍k-sing . English says "Ms. Lin" or "Teacher Lin" with the title first. Taigi puts the surname first: 林老師 . Surnames have their own readings: 林 Lîm , 王 Ông , 陳 Tân .
 EX: 林老師，𠢕早！ | Lîm lāu-su, gâu-tsá! | Good morning, Teacher Lîm!
CARDS NOW: 老師 = teacher [card] || 學生 = student [card] || 林老師 = Teacher Lîm [card] || 林老師，𠢕早！ = Good morning, Teacher Lîm!

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
Once more, please

 EX: 請閣講一擺。 | Tshiánn koh kóng tsi̍t pái. | Please say it again.
 EX: 請閣講兩擺。 | Tshiánn koh kóng nn̄g pái. | Please say it two more times.
 請 is not an all-purpose "please". It invites, treats and asks politely, and it comes at the start of the request. You already met it in 請你多多指教. Do not tack it on at the end the way English adds "please".
CARDS NOW: 請閣講一擺。 = Please say it again. [card] || 請閣講兩擺。 = Please say it two more times. || 請你多多指教。 = Nice to meet you. (please teach me well) [card]

Slowly

CARDS NOW: 慢慢仔講。 = Speak slowly, please. [card] || 請慢慢仔講。 = Please speak slowly.

CARDS NOW: 我毋知。 = I don't know. [card] || 我毋知影。 = I don't know. (with 知影) [card]

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)

## LESSON si — 9 · 是 and 毋是; 我, 你, 伊, and two words for we: 阮 and 咱 — deck "People and family"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
This lesson gives you your first full sentences: "I'm a student", "she isn't a teacher", and the pronouns to say them about anyone.
 是 joins two nouns
 是 sī works like English "am, is, are" between two nouns. Taigi verbs never change: 是 is the same for I, you, she and they, past or present. No word for "a" is needed either: 我是學生 is literally "I am student".
 EX: 我是學生。 | Guá sī ha̍k-sing. | I'm a student.

## LESSON intro — 10 · Introducing yourself: 你號做啥物名？ 我是學生 — deck "About me"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
This lesson gives you the sentences for meeting someone: your name, your surname, what you do and where you live. They are built from words you know from lesson 9: 我 , 你 and 是 . Learn them as whole sentences first; the grammar notes explain why each word sits where it does, so that you can swap in your own name and city.
 Your name

 EX: 你號做啥物名？ | Lí hō-tsò siánn-mih miâ? | What's your name?

 EX: 我是學生。 | Guá sī ha̍k-sing. | I'm a student.

Where you live, and where you study

## LESSON u-bo — 14 · 有 and 無: have, there is, and questions with 無 and 敢 — deck "Questions and particles"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
English uses "have" for owning and "there is" for what exists. Taigi uses one word for both: 有 ū . Its opposite is 無 bô , "not have" or "there isn't". Who has it, or where it is, comes first; the thing comes after 有 or 無. This lesson also gives you the two everyday ways to ask a yes/no question.
 Have and there is
 EX: 我有一个小弟。 | Guá ū tsi̍t ê sió-tī. | I have a younger brother.
 EX: 遮無茶。 | Tsia bô tê. | There's no tea here.
 In "there is", the place comes first: 遮 (here) sits before 有 or 無, just as places go before the verb everywhere in Taigi.
CARDS NOW: 我有一个小弟。 = I have a younger brother. || 遮無茶。 = There's no tea here.

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Not having: 無, never 毋
 English has one "not". Taigi has several, and the negative of 有 is always 無. You met 毋 in 毋是; it does not go with 有.
 EX: 我無錢。 | Guá bô tsînn. | I don't have any money.
CARDS NOW: 我無錢。 = I don't have any money.

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Yes/no questions with 無
 English turns a statement into a question with "do" or with a rising voice. Taigi adds 無 bô at the end, said light, in the neutral tone: "you have a book, or not?". With 有 the question is "have or not?"; with other verbs it can be "want to or not?".
 EX: 你有冊無？ | Lí ū tsheh--bô? | Do you have a book?
 EX: 你欲去無？ | Lí beh khì--bô? | Do you want to go?
 To answer yes, repeat the verb: 有. To answer no: 無.
CARDS NOW: 你有冊無？ = Do you have a book? || 你欲去無？ = Do you want to go? [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 4)
Yes/no questions with 敢
 The other way to ask is 敢 kám . 敢 goes after the subject, right before the verb or 是, and nothing is added at the end. 敢有 is "is there? do you have?".
 EX: 這敢是你的？ | Tse kám-sī lí ê? | Is this yours?
 EX: 你敢有錢？ | Lí kám ū tsînn? | Do you have money?
 EX: 你敢無欲去？ | Lí kám bô beh khì? | Don't you want to go?
 Both kinds of question are everyday Taigi. 無 at the end is the easier one to start with. 敢 can also go before 無, as in the last example.
CARDS NOW: 敢有 = is there…?; did you…? [card] || 這敢是你的？ = Is this yours? [card] || 你敢有錢？ = Do you have money? || 你敢無欲去？ = You're not going? [card]

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
有 before a verb
 Before another verb, 有 says that something did happen, and 無 that it did not. 無 + verb is "didn't". With 過 at the end, 有 + verb means "have ever done it" (more in lesson 27).
 EX: 我無食早起。 | Guá bô tsia̍h tsá-khí. | I didn't eat breakfast.
 EX: 我有去過。 | Guá ū khì--kuè. | I've been there.
 Most common mistake: using 毋 with 有. 我毋有錢 → 我無錢 .
CARDS NOW: 我無食早起。 = I didn't eat breakfast. [card] || 我有去過。 = I've been there. [card]

 Thing + 佇 + place

## LESSON directions — 19 · Directions: 正手, 倒手, 直直行, 斡 — deck "Places and directions"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
Directions in Taigi use a handful of words, and one habit you already know from the lesson on places: where comes before the action.
 Right and left
 Taigi names the sides after your hands. 正手 tsiànn-tshiú is your right hand and 倒手 tò-tshiú your left hand. For the side of something, the words are 正爿 tsiànn-pîng (the right side) and 倒爿 tò-pîng (the left side).
 EX: 伊寫字、食飯攏用倒手。 | I siá-jī, tsia̍h-pn̄g lóng īng tò-tshiú. | He writes and eats with his left hand.
CARDS NOW: 正手 = right hand; right [card] || 倒手 = left hand; left [card] || 正爿 = the right side [card] || 倒爿 = the left side; on the left [card] || 伊寫字、食飯攏用倒手。 = He writes and eats with his left hand.

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
Go straight
 直直 ti̍t-ti̍t means "straight on" and 行 kiânn means "walk, go". Put them together, in that order, and you have the most useful direction of all.
 EX: 直直行。 | Ti̍t-ti̍t kiânn. | Go straight ahead.
 EX: 直直行就到矣。 | Ti̍t-ti̍t kiânn tō kàu--ah. | Go straight and you're there.
 就到矣 tō kàu--ah at the end means "and then you're there": 到 kàu is "arrive", and 矣 says the change has happened.
CARDS NOW: 直直 = straight on; all the time [card] || 直直行 = go straight ahead [card] || 直直行就到矣。 = Go straight and you're there.

### SECTION 2   (has 5)
Turn at the intersection
 The verb for turning is 斡 uat . The side comes right after it: 斡正爿 (turn right), 斡倒爿 (turn left). An intersection is 路口 lōo-kháu .
 English says "turn right at the intersection ", with the place at the end. Taigi says the place first, then the turn: 路口斡正爿 , literally "intersection, turn right". This is the same rule as with 佇: the place goes before the verb.
 EX: 頭前路口斡正爿就到矣。 | Thâu-tsîng lōo-kháu uat tsiànn-pîng tō kàu--ah. | Turn right at the intersection ahead and you're there.
 EX: 頭前路口斡倒爿。 | Thâu-tsîng lōo-kháu uat tò-pîng. | Turn left at the intersection ahead.
 頭前 thâu-tsîng here means "ahead, in front", so 頭前路口 is "the intersection up ahead". Its opposite is 後壁 āu-piah , "behind, at the back".
CARDS NOW: 路口 = intersection; street corner [card] || 頭前路口斡正爿就到矣。 = Turn right at the intersection ahead and you're there. [card] || 頭前路口斡倒爿。 = Turn left at the intersection ahead. || 頭前 = in front; ahead [card] || 後壁 = behind; at the back [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Asking the way
 To ask where something is, use the pattern from the places lesson, thing + 佇 + place, and put 佗位 tó-uī (where) in the place slot. The question word stays where the answer will go.
 EX: 便所佇佗位？ | Piān-sóo tī tó-uī? | Where is the restroom?
 Put together, a full set of directions is a chain of short steps, each with the place first: go straight, then at the intersection ahead turn right, and you are there. Read the example sentences again with that in mind.
 Most common mistake: putting the place at the end, as in English. 斡正爿路口 → 路口斡正爿 . Say where first, then what to do there.
CARDS NOW: 佗位 = where [card] || 便所佇佗位？ = Where's the restroom? [card]

## LESSON go-come — 20 · Going and coming: 去, 來, 坐車, 捷運 — deck "Getting around"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
Getting around Taiwan takes two verbs, a few vehicles, and one word-order habit that English does the other way round.
 去 and 來
 去 khì is "go", away from where you are; 來 lâi is "come", toward you. As in English, the place you go to follows the verb: 去臺南 , go to Tainan. Neither verb ever changes its form: there is no "went" or "came". Words around the verb do that job instead. 會 (will) in front says it is going to happen, and 欲 (want to, about to) says you plan to or are about to. So 伊會來 is "he'll come" and 你欲去無？ asks whether you want to go, with 無 at the end turning it into a yes/no question.
 EX: 伊會來。 | I ē lâi. | He'll come.
 EX: 你欲去無？ | Lí beh khì--bô? | Do you want to go?
CARDS NOW: 來去 = let's go; go (and do something) [card] || 伊會來。 = He'll come. [card] || 你欲去無？ = Do you want to go? [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
來去: let's go
 來去 lâi-khì is "go (now)", and with 咱 lán (we, you included) it becomes an invitation: let's go.
 EX: 咱來去踅夜市。 | Lán lâi-khì se̍h iā-tshī. | Let's go and stroll around the night market.
CARDS NOW: 咱來去踅夜市。 = Let's go stroll around the night market. [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 10)
How you travel comes first
 English puts the means at the end: "I go to Tainan by train ." Taigi puts it before the verb, with 坐 tsē (sit, ride): "I ride-train go Tainan".
 EX: 我坐火車去臺南。 | Guá tsē hué-tshia khì Tâi-lâm. | I'm going to Tainan by train.
 EX: 我坐捷運去公園。 | Guá tsē tsia̍t-ūn khì kong-hn̂g. | I go to the park by MRT.
 EX: 伊坐高鐵來臺北。 | I tsē Ko-thih lâi Tâi-pak. | He comes to Taipei by high-speed rail.
 The vehicles: 捷運 tsia̍t-ūn (MRT), 火車 hué-tshia (train), 臺鐵 Tâi-thih (Taiwan Railway), 高鐵 Ko-thih (high-speed rail) and 計程車 kè-thîng-tshia (taxi). On foot is 行路 kiânn-lōo : 我行路去 , I walk there.
 Order matters here. 我去臺南坐火車 is a real sentence, but it says something else: "I'm going to Tainan to take the train."

## LESSON time — 21 · Time and days: 幾點？, 今仔日, 禮拜日 — deck "Time and days"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
Time words are easy to learn and easy to misplace. This lesson gives you the words and the one rule for where they go.
 The clock
 點 tiám after a number is "o'clock": 十二點 tsa̍p-jī tiám is twelve o'clock. To ask the time, put 幾 kuí (how many) in front: 幾點 kuí tiám . 這馬 tsit-má means "now".
 EX: 這馬幾點？ | Tsit-má kuí tiám? | What time is it now?
 EX: 十二點欲食中晝矣！ | Tsa̍p-jī tiám beh tsia̍h tiong-tàu--ah! | It's twelve: time for lunch!
 Notice that the time question has no special word order: 幾點 sits exactly where the answer will sit, so "What time is it now?" and "It's twelve now" have the same shape. Numbers here are the counting numbers you already know, and 十二 joins into one word in the Tâi-lô line. For how long something lasts, Taigi has 點鐘 tiám-tsing (hour) and 分鐘 hun-tsing (minute).
CARDS NOW: 中晝 = noon; lunch [card] || 幾點？ = What time is it? [card] || 這馬 = now [card] || 這馬幾點？ = What time is it now? || 十二點欲食中晝矣！ = It's twelve: time for lunch! [card] || 點鐘 = hour (amount of time) [card] || 分鐘 = minute [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 5)
Today, tomorrow, yesterday
 今仔日 kin-á-ji̍t is today, 明仔載 bîn-á-tsài is tomorrow and 昨昏 tsa-hng is yesterday. Taigi verbs never change for past or future, so these words, together with 矣 (a change has happened) and 欲 (about to, going to), carry the time of the whole sentence.
 EX: 今仔日伊閣來矣。 | Kin-á-ji̍t i koh lâi--ah. | He came again today.
 EX: 伊講明仔載欲閣來。 | I kóng bîn-á-tsài beh koh lâi. | He says he'll come again tomorrow.
CARDS NOW: 今仔日 = today [card] || 明仔載 = tomorrow [card] || 昨昏 = yesterday [card] || 今仔日伊閣來矣。 = He came again today. [card] || 伊講明仔載欲閣來。 = He says he'll come again tomorrow.

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
Parts of the day
 早起 tsá-khí is morning (it also means breakfast), 中晝 tiong-tàu is noon or lunch, 下晡 ē-poo is afternoon and 暗時 àm-sî is evening or night.
 EX: 下晡我欲招阿美仔去𨑨迌。 | Ē-poo guá beh tsio A-bí--á khì tshit-thô. | This afternoon I'm going to ask A-bí to go out and have fun.

 English usually puts time at the end: "He exercises in the park every morning ." Taigi puts it before the verb, either at the very start or right after the person, as in every MOE example in this lesson.
 EX: 伊逐工早起攏會去公園運動。 | I ta̍k-kang tsá-khí lóng ē khì kong-hn̂g ūn-tōng. | He goes to the park to exercise every morning.
 逐工 ta̍k-kang is every day, so 逐工早起 is every morning. Both 下晡我欲… and 伊明仔載欲… follow the rule: the time comes before the action.
CARDS NOW: 伊逐工早起攏會去公園運動。 = Every morning she goes to the park to exercise. [card] || 逐工 = every day [card]

The week

## LESSON food — 22 · At the night market: 頭家, 一碗麵, 好食 — deck "Night market food"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
The night market, 夜市 iā-tshī , is where Taigi is heard at its liveliest. Ordering takes three things: what to call the cook, how to count food, and how to say it tastes good.
 Call the owner 頭家
 The person running a stall is 頭家 thâu-ke , "boss". You call out 頭家 and then say what you want, the way an English speaker might say "Excuse me, ...". The same word also means "husband" in some contexts, so the context matters.
 EX: 頭家，一碗麵！ | Thâu-ke, tsi̍t uánn mī! | Boss, a bowl of noodles!
CARDS NOW: 夜市 = night market [card] || 頭家 = boss; owner (of a stall) [card] || 一碗麵 = a bowl of noodles [card] || 頭家，一碗麵！ = Boss, a bowl of noodles!

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
Count food with a classifier
 English can say "two noodles" or "a tea". Taigi always counts with a classifier between the number and the food: 碗 uánn for a bowl, 杯 pue for a cup or glass. And two is 兩, never 二, before a classifier.
 EX: 頭家，兩碗滷肉飯！ | Thâu-ke, nn̄g uánn lóo-bah-pn̄g! | Boss, two bowls of braised pork rice!
 EX: 我欲啉一杯烏龍茶。 | Guá beh lim tsi̍t pue oo-liông-tê. | I'd like to drink a glass of oolong tea.
 Eat is 食 tsia̍h and drink is 啉 lim . Tea is 茶 tê , water 水 tsuí , and oolong tea 烏龍茶 oo-liông-tê .
CARDS NOW: 滷肉飯 = braised pork rice [card] || 頭家，兩碗滷肉飯！ = Boss, two bowls of braised pork rice! || 我欲啉一杯烏龍茶。 = I'd like to drink a glass of oolong tea. || 烏龍茶 = oolong tea [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 5)
The classics
 滷肉飯 lóo-bah-pn̄g is braised pork rice. In the south the same name can mean rice topped with a whole slab of braised pork, and the minced kind is called 肉燥飯 bah-sò-pn̄g . 肉圓 bah-uân is the Taiwanese meat dumpling, 炒飯 tshá-pn̄g fried rice and 麵 mī noodles. If the stall has a menu, it is a 菜單 tshài-tuann .
 EX: 我欲食炒飯，你咧？ | Guá beh tsia̍h tshá-pn̄g, lí--leh? | I'll have fried rice, and you?
CARDS NOW: 肉燥飯 = minced pork rice [card] || 肉圓 = Taiwanese meat dumpling [card] || 炒飯 = fried rice [card] || 菜單 = menu [card] || 我欲食炒飯，你咧？ = I'll have fried rice. And you? [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 4)
Tasty or not: no 是
 好食 hó-tsia̍h is "good to eat", tasty; 歹食 pháinn-tsia̍h is the opposite. They are adjectives, so as with every adjective there is no 是 in front. Use 真 (very) instead, or another word for "very" if you know one.
 EX: 這碗麵真好食。 | Tsit uánn mī tsin hó-tsia̍h. | This bowl of noodles is really good.
 EX: 伊煮的物件真歹食。 | I tsú ê mi̍h-kiānn tsin pháinn-tsia̍h. | What he cooks tastes awful.
 Notice 這碗麵 : "this" also needs the classifier, 這 + 碗 + 麵, exactly like a number does.
 Most common mistake: leaving out the classifier. 頭家，一麵！ → 頭家，一碗麵！ A number, or 這, always takes a classifier before the food.

## LESSON market — 23 · At the market: 菜, 肉, 卵, 魚, and asking how much — deck "At the market"
### SECTION 0   (has 6)
The traditional morning market, 菜市仔 tshài-tshī-á , sells food by the piece and by weight, and the stall owners speak Taigi. This lesson gives you the food words and the price question.
 What is on the stalls
 菜 tshài is vegetables, 肉 bah is meat, 魚 hî is fish and 水果 tsuí-kó is fruit; 弓蕉 king-tsio is a banana. An egg is 卵 nn̄g , and a chicken egg 雞卵 ke-nn̄g .
 A sound trap: 卵 (egg) and 兩 (two) are both nn̄g, with the same tone. Only the context tells them apart.
 EX: 我欲買兩粒雞卵。 | Guá beh bé nn̄g lia̍p ke-nn̄g. | I want to buy two eggs.
 Eggs are counted with 粒 lia̍p , a classifier for round things (Bite-size Taiwanese: "measure word for round objects"). Buy is 買 bé . Like every counted noun, eggs need the number, then the classifier, then the noun, and two is 兩, never 二. So when you hear nn̄g lia̍p ke-nn̄g, the first nn̄g is the number and the last one is the egg.
CARDS NOW: 菜市仔 = traditional market [card] || 魚 = fish [card] || 水果 = fruit [card] || 弓蕉 = banana [card] || 雞卵 = chicken egg [card] || 我欲買兩粒雞卵。 = I want to buy two eggs.

By the catty

CARDS NOW: 一斤 = one catty (600 grams) [card] || 我欲買一斤肉。 = I want to buy a catty of meat.

Cheap and expensive
 俗 sio̍k is cheap and 貴 kuì is expensive. Both are adjectives, so there is no 是 in front. 較 khah in front means "more": 較俗, cheaper. If it is clear what you compare with, you do not need a word for "than".
 EX: 彼擔賣的水果較俗。 | Hit tànn bē ê tsuí-kó khah sio̍k. | The fruit at that stall is cheaper.

 彼擔 hit tànn is "that stall": 彼 (that) needs its classifier, like 這 and like numbers. And 莫 tells someone not to do something.
 EX: 雞卵真俗。 | Ke-nn̄g tsin sio̍k. | Eggs are really cheap.
 Most common mistake: putting 是 before cheap or expensive. 雞卵是俗 → 雞卵真俗 . Use a word for "very", or 較, never 是.

## LESSON describing — 24 · Describing and comparing: 真, 誠, 足; 比…較, 上 — deck "Describing and comparing"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
English describes with "be": "it is hot". Taigi adjectives need no "be" at all. This lesson shows what goes in its place, and how to compare.
 No 是: use a word for "very"
 Before an adjective you put 真 tsin , 誠 tsiânn or 足 tsiok . All three mean "very", and a plain statement usually has one of them even when English has no "very".
 EX: 今仔日真熱。 | Kin-á-ji̍t tsin jua̍h. | It's hot today.
 EX: 𪜶某真媠。 | In bóo tsin suí. | His wife is beautiful.
 So where English has "is", Taigi simply has the adverb, and nothing else is needed. 熱 jua̍h is hot, 寒 kuânn cold, 媠 suí pretty, 濟 tsē many or much, 低 kē low. 是 joins two nouns (我是學生); it never stands before these words.
CARDS NOW: 誠 = really; very [card] || 足 = very; extremely [card] || 今仔日真熱。 = It's hot today. [card] || 𪜶某真媠。 = His wife is beautiful. [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
Comparing: A 比 B 較 + adjective
 English says "A is colder than B". Taigi says "A 比 pí B 較 khah cold": 比 introduces what you compare with, and 較 (more) goes right before the adjective.
 EX: 伊的糖仔比我較濟。 | I ê thn̂g-á pí guá khah tsē. | He has more candy than me.
 EX: 這塊桌仔比彼塊較低。 | Tsit tè toh-á pí hit tè khah kē. | This table is lower than that one.
 EX: 氣象報告講明仔載會比今仔日較寒。 | Khì-siōng pò-kò kóng bîn-á-tsài ē pí kin-á-ji̍t khah kuânn. | The weather report says tomorrow will be colder than today.
 Notice 這塊桌仔 and 彼塊 : "this" and "that" keep their classifier, 塊, even when the noun is left out the second time. And if it is clear what you compare with, 較 alone is enough: 較寒 , colder.
CARDS NOW: 伊的糖仔比我較濟。 = He has more candy than me. [card] || 這塊桌仔比彼塊較低。 = This table is lower than that one. [card] || 氣象報告講明仔載會比今仔日較寒。 = The weather report says tomorrow will be colder than today. [card] || 這塊桌仔 = this table [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
The most: 上
 上 before an adjective means "most": 上好 siōng hó is best. Careful with the reading: before an adjective 上 is read as in 上好 (siōng hó); in other words, such as 上車 (tsiūnn-tshia, get on), it has another reading. Like 較, it sits right before the adjective, with no 是 and no "the". A stronger form is 上蓋 siōng-kài , "the very most": the dictionary of the Ministry of Education gives 上蓋媠 (the most beautiful) and 上蓋好 (the best).
 EX: 臺北上熱。 | Tâi-pak siōng jua̍h. | Taipei is the hottest.
CARDS NOW: 上好 = best [card] || 上蓋媠 = the most beautiful [card] || 臺北上熱。 = Taipei is the hottest.

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Too much: 傷
 傷 siunn before an adjective means "too": 傷鹹 siunn kiâm is too salty. You met it with prices: 傷貴, too expensive. For something that is simply very good, use 真.
 EX: 你講話傷過份。 | Lí kóng-uē siunn kuè-hūn. | You're going too far with what you say.
 Most common mistake: putting "is" in front of the adjective. 今仔日是熱 → 今仔日真熱 . And for "than": 伊的糖仔較濟比我 → 伊的糖仔比我較濟 .
CARDS NOW: 傷鹹 = too salty [card] || 你講話傷過份。 = What you said went too far. [card]

## LESSON can — 25 · Can and may: 會 and 袂, 會使, 會曉 — deck "Traps for English speakers"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
English "can" covers several ideas: possibility, a learned skill, permission, being able to. Taigi gives each its own word, and each has a matching negative that starts with 袂.
 會 and 袂: will, it happens
 會 ē before a verb says something will happen or tends to happen. Its negative is 袂 bē : won't, can't happen. It also covers being able to: the MOE glosses 伊會食會睏 as "he can eat and sleep" (能吃能睡). The examples: a friend turns up, a cold spreads.
 EX: 伊會來。 | I ē lâi. | He'll come.
 EX: 感冒會過人。 | Kám-mōo ē kuè--lâng. | Colds are catching.
 EX: 無穿衫會感冒。 | Bô tshīng sann ē kám-mōo. | If you don't wear clothes, you'll catch a cold.
CARDS NOW: 伊會來。 = He'll come. [card] || 感冒會過人。 = Colds are contagious. [card] || 無穿衫會感冒。 = If you don't wear clothes, you'll catch a cold.

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
會曉 and 袂曉: know how
 For a skill you have learned, such as a language or a song, use 會曉 ē-hiáu ; the negative is 袂曉 bē-hiáu . "I can't (I never learned)" is 我袂曉 : the MOE gives 袂曉 as the opposite of 會曉.
 EX: 我會曉講臺語。 | Guá ē-hiáu kóng Tâi-gí. | I can speak Taigi.
CARDS NOW: 會曉 = know how to; can [card] || 我袂曉。 = I don't know how. [card] || 我會曉講臺語。 = I can speak Taigi.

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
會使 and 袂使: may
 For permission, use 會使 ē-sái (may, it's OK) and 袂使 bē-sái (may not, not allowed). This is the word a parent, a teacher or a sign uses: you are able to do it, but are you allowed to?
 EX: 你袂使去。 | Lí bē-sái khì. | You may not go.
CARDS NOW: 會使 = may; be allowed to [card] || 袂使 = may not; not allowed [card] || 你袂使去。 = You may not go.

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
會當 and 袂當: be able
 會當 ē-tàng and 袂當 bē-tàng are a third pair: be able to, not able to. As always, a time word like 明仔載 goes before the verb.
 EX: 我明仔載袂當來。 | Guá bîn-á-tsài bē-tàng lâi. | I can't come tomorrow.
CARDS NOW: 會當 = be able to [card] || 我明仔載袂當來。 = I can't come tomorrow.

### SECTION 4   (has 3)
Asking: 會…袂？

 EX: 這條歌你會曉唱袂？ | Tsit tiâu kua lí ē-hiáu tshiùnn--bē? | Can you sing this song?
 EX: 柑仔會酸袂？ | Kam-á ē sng--bē? | Are the tangerines sour?
 The song has its classifier, 這條歌 , and stands at the front. The final 袂 is a question particle, not a "can't": the whole sentence asks "can you or can't you?"
 So before you say "can", ask yourself which kind you mean: will it happen, do you know how, are you allowed, or are you able. Each answer has its own pair.
 Most common mistake: reaching for the Mandarin "can't". 我不會 → 我袂曉 . Every 會 word in this lesson has its own negative with 袂.

## LESSON negation — 26 · Four words for not: 毋, 無, 袂, 莫 — deck "Traps for English speakers"

English has one "not" and puts it anywhere. Taigi has four everyday negatives, plus a fifth for "no need", and each one carries its own meaning. You have met them one by one; here they are together.

 毋: isn't, won't

## LESSON aspect — 27 · Actions in time: 咧 (in progress), 矣 (done), 有…過 (ever) — deck "Describing and comparing"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
Taigi verbs never change: there is no -ing, no -ed. Instead, small words around the verb say how the action sits in time. This lesson covers four of them.
 咧: going on now
 咧 teh right before a verb says the action is in progress, like English "-ing". It works with actions you can be in the middle of: studying, sleeping, eating.
 EX: 伊咧讀冊。 | I teh tha̍k-tsheh. | He's studying.
 EX: 伊這馬佇厝裡咧睏。 | I tsit-má tī tshù--lí teh khùn. | He's sleeping at home now.
 The order is time, then place, then 咧 and the verb: 這馬 (now), 佇厝裡 (at home), 咧睏 (is sleeping). Time and place both come before the verb. 拄咧 tú-teh is "right in the middle of".
 EX: 伊拄咧食飯。 | I tú-teh tsia̍h-pn̄g. | He's eating right now.
CARDS NOW: 伊咧讀冊。 = He's studying. [card] || 伊這馬佇厝裡咧睏。 = He's sleeping at home now. [card] || 這馬 = now [card] || 伊拄咧食飯。 = She's eating right now. [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
矣: something has changed
 矣 --ah at the end of a sentence says a change has happened, or is just about to: things are now different. It is not a past tense.
 EX: 今仔日伊閣來矣。 | Kin-á-ji̍t i koh lâi--ah. | He came again today.
 EX: 欲落雨矣。 | Beh lo̍h-hōo--ah. | It's about to rain.
 In the second sentence nothing has happened yet: 欲 says it is about to, and 矣 says the situation has changed.
CARDS NOW: 今仔日伊閣來矣。 = He came again today. [card] || 欲落雨矣。 = It's going to rain. [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
有 + verb + 過: ever
 For "have you ever", Taigi puts 有 ū before the verb and 過 kuè after it, at the end of the sentence. 過 is said light, in the neutral tone.
 EX: 我有去過。 | Guá ū khì--kuè. | I've been there.
 EX: 這條歌有聽過無？ | Tsit tiâu kua ū thiann--kuè--bô? | Have you heard this song?
 The song is 這條歌 , with its classifier, at the front; the question ends in 無. Keep 過 at the very end in your own sentences, right after the verb, and put the thing you have done at the front, as here.
CARDS NOW: 我有去過。 = I've been there. [card] || 這條歌有聽過無？ = Have you heard this song? [card] || 這條歌 = this song [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
猶未: not yet
 猶未 iáu-buē before a verb means "not yet": it has not happened, but you expect it to. Compare 無, which simply says it did not happen.
 EX: 我猶未食飯。 | Guá iáu-buē tsia̍h-pn̄g. | I haven't eaten yet.
 Most common mistake: reading 矣 as a past tense. 欲落雨矣 taken as "it rained" → 欲落雨矣 is "it's about to rain". 矣 marks a change, past or just starting.
