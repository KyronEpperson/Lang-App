# Worksheet: en-it

## LESSON sounds — 1 · Sounds, spelling and stress — deck "Sounds and spelling"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Italian spelling is regular: once you know a handful of rules, you can read almost any word. Under each Italian phrase in this course there is a pronunciation line in English letters. The syllable in CAPITALS is the stressed one.
 Five short, pure vowels
 English vowels slide: say no slowly and your lips end on a w . Italian vowels stay put. The pronunciation line writes them ah, eh, ee, oh, oo , one sound each, with no glide.
 EX: la casa | lah KAH-sah | the house
 EX: il vino | eel VEE-noh | the wine
CARDS NOW: la casa = house; home [card] || il vino = wine [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
c and g: soft or hard
 Before e or i , c sounds like the ch in church and g like the j in jam . Before a, o, u they are hard, as in cat and go . To keep them hard before e or i , Italian adds an h : ch is always a k sound, gh always a hard g . In cia, cio, gia, gio the i is not said; it only softens the letter before it.
 EX: Ciao! | CHAH-oh | Hi! Bye!
 EX: il gelato | eel jeh-LAH-toh | the ice cream
 EX: la chiave | lah KYAH-veh | the key
CARDS NOW: Ciao! = Hi! / Bye! [card] || il gelato = ice cream [card] || la chiave = key [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 5)

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Double consonants
 A double letter is held a little longer, and it changes the word. La palla is a ball; la pala is a shovel. The pronunciation line writes the letter on both sides of the hyphen.
 EX: la palla | lah PAHL-lah | the ball
 EX: la notte | lah NOHT-teh | the night
CARDS NOW: la palla = ball [card] || la notte = night [card]

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Stress
 Most Italian words are stressed on the next-to-last syllable. A written accent appears only on a stressed final vowel, as in città . Many other words put the stress earlier, with nothing in the spelling to warn you, so learn the stress with the word. Words that look English often surprise: telefono is stressed on its second syllable.
 EX: la città | lah cheet-TAH | the city
 EX: il telefono | eel teh-LEH-foh-noh | the phone
CARDS NOW: la città = city; town [card] || il telefono = phone [card]

### SECTION 5   (has 3)
il and la, and their plurals
 Nouns here come with a little word for "the". Every noun is masculine or feminine: masculine nouns take il , feminine nouns take la . For more than one, il becomes i and la becomes le , and the noun changes its last letter: -o becomes -i , -a becomes -e , -e becomes -i . So it's le notti and le chiavi . Some words take other forms: gli gnocchi , gli spaghetti . Lesson 4 explains them all.
 Finally, a single r is a quick tap of the tongue and rr is rolled, and p, t, k have no puff of air. Listen first; the pronunciation line is a crutch to drop later.
 Most common mistake: reading sch the English way. bruschetta with an English sh sound → bruschetta with a hard sk sound, as the line shows.
CARDS NOW: il = the (masculine) [card] || la = the (feminine) [card] || le = the (feminine plural) [card]

## LESSON greetings — 2 · Greetings: tu and Lei — deck "Greetings and courtesy"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
English has one "you" and one "hello" for everybody. Italian picks its words by the time of day and by who you are talking to.
 Hello and goodbye
 Ciao means both "hi" and "bye", but only with friends, family and kids. With anyone else, greet by the time of day: buongiorno from morning to early afternoon, buonasera from mid-afternoon on. Buonanotte is only for late at night or going to bed. To leave, say arrivederci ; the extra-polite version for one stranger is ArrivederLa .
 EX: Buongiorno. | bwohn-JOHR-noh | Good morning. Hello.
 EX: Buonasera, signora. | bwoh-nah-SEH-rah see-NYOH-rah | Good evening, ma'am.
 Why buon in one and buona in the others? Giorno (day) is masculine, while sera (evening) and notte (night) are feminine, and the word for "good" changes to match. The same goes for buona serata , "have a good evening". This is your first taste of agreement, which runs through all of Italian. You will also see buona sera written as two words; both are fine.
CARDS NOW: Ciao! = Hi! / Bye! [card] || Buongiorno. = Good morning. / Hello. [card] || Buonasera. = Good evening. / Hello. [card] || Buonanotte. = Good night. [card] || Arrivederci. = Goodbye. [card] || ArrivederLa. = Goodbye. (very formal) [card] || Buonasera, signora. = Good evening, ma'am.

### SECTION 1   (has 10)
Two ways to say "you"
 Use tu with friends, family, children and people your age in relaxed settings. Use Lei with strangers, older people, shop and hotel staff and officials. Lei is written with a capital L, so it isn't confused with lei (she). The verb changes too: come stai? goes with tu , come sta? with Lei . The tu form ends in -i .

 How are you? ; Come stai? ; Come sta? ; 
 And you? ; E tu? ; E Lei? ; 
 Excuse me. ; Scusa. ; Mi scusi. ; 
 EX: Ciao, Luca! Come stai? | CHAH-oh LOO-kah KOH-meh stai | Hi, Luca! How are you?
 EX: Buongiorno. Come sta? | bwohn-JOHR-noh KOH-meh stah | Good morning. How are you?
 EX: Sto bene, grazie. E tu? | stoh BEH-neh GRAH-tsyeh eh too | I'm fine, thanks. And you?
 If you're unsure, use Lei . With a stranger, tu can sound rude; Lei at worst sounds a little formal.

How are you? || Sto bene, grazie. E tu? = I'm fine, thanks. And you? || Bene, grazie, e tu? = Fine, thanks, and you? [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 5)
Names and small polite words
 To give your name, say mi chiamo , literally "I call myself". When you meet someone, say piacere , "nice to meet you". Per favore is "please", grazie is "thank you", and the answer is prego , "you're welcome".
 EX: Mi chiamo Emma. Piacere. | mee KYAH-moh EHM-mah pyah-CHEH-reh | My name is Emma. Nice to meet you.
 EX: Grazie. Prego. | GRAH-tsyeh PREH-goh | Thank you. You're welcome.
 Most common mistake: using the friend form with a stranger. Buongiorno, come stai? to a shop assistant → Buongiorno, come sta?
CARDS NOW: Piacere. = Nice to meet you. [card] || Per favore. = Please. [card] || Prego. = You're welcome. [card] || Mi chiamo Emma. Piacere. = My name is Emma. Nice to meet you. || Grazie. Prego. = Thank you. You're welcome.

## LESSON gender — 3 · Gender and number of nouns — deck "People and family"
### SECTION 0   (has 9)
In English, only people and animals are "he" or "she". In Italian every noun has a gender, masculine or feminine, and that includes things. The gender is part of the word, so always learn a noun with its il (masculine) or la (feminine).
 -o and -a: the usual pattern
 Most nouns ending in -o are masculine, and most nouns ending in -a are feminine. For many family words you just swap the ending.
 Masculine ; Feminine ; English ; 
 il nonno ; la nonna ; grandfather, grandmother ; 
 il figlio ; la figlia ; son, daughter ; 
 il ragazzo ; la ragazza ; boy, girl ; 
 EX: il fratello e la sorella | eel frah-TEHL-loh eh lah soh-REHL-lah | the brother and the sister
CARDS NOW: il = the (masculine) [card] || la = the (feminine) [card] || la nonna = grandmother [card] || la figlia = daughter [card] || il ragazzo = boy; guy; young man [card] || la ragazza = girl; young woman [card] || il fratello e la sorella = the brother and the sister || il fratello = brother [card] || la sorella = sister [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 5)
Nouns in -e: learn them with the article
 Nouns ending in -e can be either gender, and the ending gives no clue: il padre (father) but la madre (mother), il pane (bread) but la chiave (key). Here the article is the only way to know.
 EX: il padre e la madre | eel PAH-dreh eh lah MAH-dreh | the father and the mother
CARDS NOW: il padre = father [card] || la madre = mother [card] || il pane = bread [card] || la chiave = key [card] || il padre e la madre = the father and the mother

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Exceptions worth memorizing
 A few very common nouns break the pattern. Problema ends in -a but is masculine. Mano (hand) ends in -o but is feminine, and so is foto , short for fotografia .
 EX: il problema | eel proh-BLEH-mah | the problem
 EX: la mano | lah MAH-noh | the hand
CARDS NOW: il problema = problem [card] || la mano = hand [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
More than one
 English adds -s . Italian changes the last vowel, and the article changes too: il becomes i , la becomes le .
 Ending ; One ; More than one ; 
 -o becomes -i ; il nonno ; i nonni ; 
 -a becomes -e ; la sorella ; le sorelle ; 
 -e becomes -i ; la chiave ; le chiavi ; 
 masculine -a becomes -i ; il problema ; i problemi ; 
 EX: i ragazzi e le ragazze | ee rah-GAHT-tsee eh leh rah-GAHT-tseh | the boys and the girls
 The exceptions keep their gender in the plural: le mani (the hands), i problemi .
CARDS NOW: le = the (feminine plural) [card] || i ragazzi e le ragazze = the boys and the girls

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Words that never change
 Nouns ending in an accented vowel, and short forms like foto , stay the same in the plural. Only the article tells you there is more than one.
 EX: la città, le città | lah cheet-TAH leh cheet-TAH | the city, the cities
CARDS NOW: la città, le città = the city, the cities

### SECTION 5   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Mixed groups
 For a group of men and women, Italian uses the masculine plural. I nonni means "the grandparents", and i figli means "the children", sons and daughters together.
 A last note: nouns that start with a vowel, z or s plus a consonant take other forms of "the". You'll meet them in the next lesson.
 Most common mistake: trusting the ending of an exception. la problema → il problema .
CARDS NOW: i figli = children (sons and daughters) [card]

## LESSON articles — 4 · Articles: il, lo, la, l', i, gli, le — deck "Food and drink"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
English has one "the" and two forms of "a". Italian has seven forms of "the" and four of "a". You choose by three things: the gender of the noun, singular or plural, and the sound the next word starts with.
 Masculine: il, lo, l'
 Il is the usual masculine "the". Lo comes before z , gn , ps and s + consonant. Before a vowel, the vowel drops and an apostrophe takes its place: l' . This is called elision.
 EX: il vino e lo zucchero | eel VEE-noh eh loh DZOOK-keh-roh | the wine and the sugar
 EX: l'olio | LOH-lyoh | the oil
CARDS NOW: il = the (masculine) [card] || lo = the (masculine, before s + consonant, z) [card] || il vino e lo zucchero = the wine and the sugar || l'olio = the oil

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Feminine: la, l'
 Feminine nouns take la , or l' before a vowel.
 EX: la pizza e l'acqua | lah PEET-tsah eh LAHK-kwah | the pizza and the water
CARDS NOW: la = the (feminine) [card] || la pizza e l'acqua = the pizza and the water

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
More than one: i, gli, le
 Masculine il becomes i . Both lo and masculine l' become gli . All feminine forms become le , even before a vowel. So the masculine plural depends on the first sound of the noun, but the feminine plural is always le .
 Noun starts with ; Masculine ; Feminine ; 
 most consonants ; il panino, i panini ; la pizza, le pizze ; 
 z, gn, ps, s + consonant ; lo zucchero, gli spaghetti ; la spremuta, le spremute ; 
 a vowel ; l'amico, gli amici ; l'amica, le amiche ; 
 EX: gli spaghetti e gli gnocchi | lyee spah-GHEHT-tee eh lyee NYOHK-kee | the spaghetti and the gnocchi
CARDS NOW: gli = the (masculine plural, before a vowel, s + consonant, z) [card] || le = the (feminine plural) [card] || gli spaghetti e gli gnocchi = the spaghetti and the gnocchi

### SECTION 3   (has 8)
The word for "a"
 The same sound rules apply. Masculine: un , or uno before z, gn, ps and s + consonant. Feminine: una , or un' before a vowel. Masculine un never takes an apostrophe: un amico is a friend who is a man, un'amica a friend who is a woman.

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
"The" where English has none
 English says "I like coffee" for coffee in general. Italian uses the article: il caffè . Take mi piace ("I like") as a set phrase for now.
 EX: Mi piace il caffè. | mee PYAH-cheh eel kahf-FEH | I like coffee.
 Most common mistake: using i before a vowel. i amici → gli amici .
CARDS NOW: Mi piace il caffè. = I like coffee.

## LESSON pronouns — 5 · Subject pronouns: io, tu, Lei, voi — deck "About me"
### SECTION 0   (has 9)
The subject is who the sentence is about. English has I, you, he, she, we, they . Italian has a few more, because it splits "you" three ways.

 io ; I ; 
 tu ; you (a friend) ; 
 lui, lei ; he, she ; 
 Lei ; you (a stranger) ;

 The verb changes with the subject

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Lei: the formal "you"
 Lei with a capital L is how you say "you" to a stranger, and it takes the same verb as lei , "she". So Lei come sta? is "How are you?" to a stranger, and E lei come sta? with a small l is "And how is she?". In speech, the situation tells you which.
 EX: Signora, Lei come sta? | see-NYOH-rah lay KOH-meh stah | Ma'am, how are you?
 EX: E Lei? | eh lay | And you? (to a stranger)
CARDS NOW: E Lei? = And you? (to a stranger) || Signora, Lei come sta? = Ma'am, how are you?

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Voi: more than one "you"
 English uses "you" for one person or many. Italian uses voi for any group, friends or strangers. In very formal service you may hear Loro for a group of guests; you don't need to say it yourself.
 EX: Ragazzi, come state? | rah-GAHT-tsee KOH-meh STAH-teh | Hey guys, how are you all?
 EX: Marco e Chiara stanno bene. | MAHR-koh eh KYAH-rah STAHN-noh BEH-neh | Marco and Chiara are fine.
CARDS NOW: Ragazzi, come state? = Hey guys, how are you all? || Marco e Chiara stanno bene. = Marco and Chiara are fine.

### SECTION 3   (has 3)
Leave the pronoun out
 English always needs "I" or "we". In Italian the verb ending already tells you who, so sto alone means "I am". Italians usually drop io , tu and noi , and keep a pronoun for contrast or to make clear who is meant.
 EX: Sto bene, grazie. | stoh BEH-neh GRAH-tsyeh | I'm fine, thanks.
 EX: Stiamo bene. E voi? | STYAH-moh BEH-neh eh voy | We're fine. And you all?
 That is also why Mi chiamo Emma needs no io in front.
 Most common mistake: keeping io in every sentence, as English does. Io sto bene, io mi chiamo Emma → Sto bene, mi chiamo Emma .
CARDS NOW: Sto bene, grazie. = I'm fine, thanks. || Stiamo bene. E voi? = We're fine. And you all? || Mi chiamo Emma. = My name is Emma. [card]

## LESSON essere-avere — 6 · essere and avere: I am, I have — deck "Feelings and states"

## LESSON adverbs — 22 · Adverbs: time, place and quantity — deck "Days, dates and weather"
### SECTION 0   (has 11)
Adverbs are the small words that say when, where and how much. Most of them never change. The one to watch is molto , which sometimes agrees and sometimes doesn't.
 When: sempre, mai, già, ancora
 Sempre means "always". For "never", English needs one word; Italian needs two: non before the verb and mai after it.
 EX: Non bevo mai il caffè la sera. | nohn BEH-voh mai eel kahf-FEH lah SEH-rah | I never drink coffee in the evening.
 Già means "already" or "yet" in a question, and non… ancora means "not yet". In the passato prossimo they sit between the auxiliary and the participle.
 EX: Hai già mangiato? No, non ancora. | ai jah mahn-JAH-toh noh nohn ahn-KOH-rah | Have you eaten yet? No, not yet.
 Ora and adesso both mean "now". Presto means "early" or "soon", and tardi means "late".
 In a question, mai without non means "ever": it asks whether something has happened at any time. Word order differs from English here: "I always drink" puts "always" before the verb, but Italian puts sempre and mai right after it ( bevo sempre ). Ora and adesso can also start the sentence.
 EX: Adesso sono a casa. | ah-DEHS-soh SOH-noh ah KAH-sah | I'm at home now.

 Qui (or qua ) means "here". Lì (or là ) means "there". The accent mark matters: la is "the" and li is "them", but là and lì mean "there". Qui vicino is "near here", and È lontano? asks "Is it far?".

### SECTION 2   (has 5)
How much: molto, poco, troppo
 English uses different words: "very tall", "a lot of friends", "too much traffic". Italian uses molto (very, a lot), poco (not very, little) and troppo (too, too much) for all of them, with one rule:
 Before an adjective, or after a verb, they never change: molto bella , troppo caro , lavoro molto .
 Before a noun, they agree with it like an adjective: molti amici , molta acqua , troppe persone .
 So ask yourself one question: is the next word a noun? If it is, make the ending agree with it. If it is an adjective, or nothing follows, leave the word as it is.
 EX: Firenze è molto bella. | fee-REHN-tseh eh MOHL-toh BEHL-lah | Florence is very beautiful.
 EX: Ho molti amici a Roma. | oh MOHL-tee ah-MEE-chee ah ROH-mah | I have a lot of friends in Rome.
 EX: C'è troppa gente. | cheh TROHP-pah JEHN-teh | There are too many people.
 In the last one, gente (people) is a feminine singular noun in Italian, so troppa and c'è .
 Most common mistakes: leaving molto unchanged before a noun: Ho molto amici → Ho molti amici . Making it agree before an adjective: È molta bella → È molto bella . And forgetting non : Bevo mai il latte → Non bevo mai il latte .
