# Worksheet: en-ptpt

## LESSON vowels — 1 · Sounds of Portugal: reduced vowels and final s — deck "Sounds and spelling"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
Lisbon Portuguese does not sound like the spelling. Under each new phrase there is a pronunciation line in English letters. CAPITALS mark the stressed syllable. A one-syllable word stays in small letters. Trust a Portugal voice. A Brazilian clip teaches the wrong t , the wrong final l , and the wrong final s .
 Vowels that shrink
 An unstressed vowel gets small. Unstressed a is uh . Unstressed e is ih , and fast speech often drops it. The line keeps ih . Unstressed o is oo . Stressed a is ah , except before m , n , or nh , where it is uh . Between vowels, b , d , and g are softer than in English. The line does not mark that.
 EX: o telefone | oo tih-lih-FAW-nih | the telephone
 EX: pequeno | pih-KAY-noo | small
 EX: a casa | uh KAH-zuh | the house
 EX: a cama | uh KUH-muh | the bed
CARDS NOW: o telefone = the telephone || telefone = telephone [card] || pequeno = small [card] || a casa = the house [card] || a cama = the bed [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
Final s, clean t and d
 Before a pause or a voiceless consonant, syllable-final s sounds like sh . Before a voiced consonant it sounds like zh , the s in measure . Before a vowel it often links on as z . The line keeps the pause form of each word.
 EX: dois | doysh | two
 EX: Lisboa | lihzh-BOH-uh | Lisbon
 t and d stay t and d , including before i . dia has a clean d . noite has a clean t .
 EX: o dia | oo DEE-uh | the day
 EX: a noite | uh NOY-tih | the night
CARDS NOW: dois = two [card] || Lisboa = Lisbon [card] || o dia = the day [card] || a noite = the night [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
Two r sounds, and l
 The strong r , written kh , is a word-initial r , rr , and r after l , n , or s . A single r elsewhere, including on falar , is a tap. l stays l , as on Brasil .
 EX: o carro | oo KAH-khoo | the car
 EX: caro | KAH-roo | expensive
 EX: Brasil | bruh-ZEEL | Brazil
 EX: falar | fuh-LAHR | to speak
CARDS NOW: falar = to speak [card] || Brasil = Brazil || o carro = the car [card] || caro = expensive [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 5)
A few labels the line uses
 ou is a closed oh , with no w . ei is uh plus a short y . The line writes uhy . ng after a vowel means the vowel is nasal: air goes through the nose. lh is one sound, close to the lli in million : the line writes ly . nh is one nasal sound, close to the ny in canyon : the line writes ny .
 EX: o leite | oo LUHY-tih | the milk
 EX: não | nowng | no; not
CARDS NOW: ou = or [card] || Ei = Hey! [card] || o leite = the milk [card] || leite = milk [card] || não = not [card]

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
The small words o and a
 English has one word for the . Every Portuguese noun is masculine or feminine, and the word for the agrees with that group. o is masculine, and on the line it is oo . The article a is feminine, and it is uh . Learn each noun with that small word. Two agrees as well: dois with a masculine noun, duas with a feminine noun. The euro is masculine.
 EX: dois euros | doysh AYW-rawsh | two euros
 Most common mistake: saying dia with an English or Brazilian j → dia with a clean d.
CARDS NOW: dois euros = two euros [card]

## LESSON nasals — 2 · Nasal sounds, lh, nh and the written accents — deck "Sounds and spelling"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
This lesson is the nasal spellings, plus lh , nh , ç , and the accent marks. An accent tells you where the stress sits, and whether a stressed e or o is open or closed.
 Nasal vowels
 English sends almost every vowel out through the mouth. A nasal vowel sends air through the nose at the same time. You do not finish it with a consonant n . ão and a final -am are owng . ãe and a final -em are the same ending in this Lisbon standard, uhyng . So bem rhymes with mãe . ã alone is uhng . When m or n stands before another consonant, the letter stays on the line and the vowel is still nasal.
 EX: não | nowng | no; not
 EX: o pão | oo powng | the bread
 EX: a mãe | uh muhyng | the mother
 EX: bem | buhyng | well
 EX: também | tuhm-BUHYNG | also
 EX: cantar | kuhn-TAHR | to sing
CARDS NOW: em = in; on; at [card] || bem = well [card] || não = not [card] || o pão = the bread [card] || a mãe = the mother [card] || também = also [card] || cantar = to sing [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
lh and nh, each one sound
 lh is one palatal sound, not l plus y . Think of the lli in million , said as one piece, with the tongue tip down. nh is a different single sound, close to the ny in canyon . The line writes them ly and ny : read each as one quick sound, not two. In vinho the i stays a clear ee . Before nh , a stressed a is uh , as in banho .
 EX: o filho | oo FEE-lyoo | the son
 EX: a filha | uh FEE-lyuh | the daughter
 EX: o vinho | oo VEE-nyoo | the wine
 EX: o banho | oo BUH-nyoo | the bath
CARDS NOW: o filho = the son || a filha = the daughter || o vinho = the wine [card] || o banho = the bath [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
The c with a tail
 ç is an s sound. Portuguese writes it when the next vowel is a , o , or u , because a plain c in that spot would sound like k . Before e or i , a plain c is already s , so you do not write ç there.
 EX: a praça | uh PRAH-suh | the square
 EX: o açúcar | oo uh-SOO-kahr | the sugar
 EX: a maçã | uh muh-SUHNG | the apple
CARDS NOW: a praça = the square (in a town) [card] || o açúcar = the sugar [card] || a maçã = the apple [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 5)
Accents: stress, open, closed
 A word that ends in a , e , o , in those plus s , or in am or em , is stressed on the next-to-last syllable. Any other ending is stressed on the last syllable. An accent overrides that. á é í ó ú and â ê ô show the stressed syllable. Acute é and ó are open. Circumflex ê and ô are closed. Open o is aw , as in law . Closed o is oh . The grave accent à is not a stress mark. It means to the , and a later lesson handles it.
 EX: café | kuh-FEH | coffee
 EX: a avó | uh uh-VAW | the grandmother
 EX: o avô | oo uh-VOH | the grandfather
 Some common words do not write the open or closed difference. porta has an open aw . Porto , the city, has a closed oh . They are different vowels. The line shows which one you have.
 EX: a porta | uh PAWR-tuh | the door
 EX: Porto | POHR-too | Porto
 Most common mistake: saying filha as feel-ya , two separate sounds → one lh , a single sound (the line's ly ).
CARDS NOW: café = coffee || a avó = the grandmother || o avô = the grandfather || Porto = Porto || a porta = the door [card]

## LESSON greetings — 3 · Greetings and courtesy — deck "Greetings and courtesy"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
Olá works with anyone, at any hour. To sound placed in the day, add the greeting for the time. English says good morning as two general words. Portuguese picks bom or boa to agree with the noun, and the noun is singular.
 Morning, afternoon, after dark
 From dawn until noon, say bom dia . Many people switch at lunch instead of at noon. From that point until sunset, say boa tarde . After sunset, say boa noite . That one phrase covers the evening and good night. There is no separate greeting to learn for the small hours. dia is masculine, so the word for good is bom . tarde and noite are feminine, so the word is boa . Answer a greeting with the same greeting.
 EX: Olá | aw-LAH | Hello
 EX: Bom dia | bohng DEE-uh | Good morning
 EX: Boa tarde | BOH-uh TAHR-dih | Good afternoon
 EX: Boa noite | BOH-uh NOY-tih | Good evening. Good night.
CARDS NOW: Olá = Hi [card] || bom = good [card] || Boa = Nice one! Great! [card] || Bom dia = Good morning [card] || Boa tarde = Good afternoon [card] || tarde = late [card] || Boa noite = Good evening, or good night [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
Thank you matches the speaker
 English thank you never changes. Portuguese obrigado agrees with the person who is speaking, not with the person who helped. A man says obrigado , even to a woman. A woman says obrigada , even to a man. Some women do say obrigado . The form this course asks you to produce still agrees: obrigada from a woman. The answer to thanks is de nada , you're welcome .
 EX: Obrigado | aw-bree-GAH-doo | Thank you (said by a man)
 EX: Obrigada | aw-bree-GAH-duh | Thank you (said by a woman)
 EX: De nada | dih NAH-duh | You're welcome
CARDS NOW: Obrigado = Thank you (said by a man) [card] || Obrigada = Thank you (said by a woman) [card] || De nada = You're welcome [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Excuse me, and please
 To get a stranger's attention, in the street or in a shop, say desculpe . The ending is the one for a person you do not know. A friend hears a different ending, and the next lesson shows why. Se faz favor is please, especially when you ask for something in a shop. Por favor means the same and is also normal. When an exercise asks for please, it asks for se faz favor .
 EX: Desculpe | dihsh-KOOL-pih | Excuse me (to a stranger)
 EX: Se faz favor | sih fahsh fuh-VOHR | Please
 Most common mistake: a woman saying obrigado → obrigada , agreeing with the woman who is speaking.
CARDS NOW: Desculpe = Excuse me (to a stranger) [card] || Se faz favor = Please [card]

## LESSON address — 4 · Saying you: tu, o senhor, a senhora — deck "Greetings and courtesy"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
English has one word, you . Portuguese picks the word, and then the verb ending, from the relationship.
 tu for people close to you
 Use tu with friends, family, people your own age, and children. The verb ending that goes with tu is the tu form, and it has an s . Tu falas and Falas are both normal. The ending already says who it is, so tu can drop. Tu és is you are with a friend. With a friend, excuse me is desculpa .
 EX: Tu falas | too FAH-luhsh | You speak (to a friend)
 EX: Tu és | too ehsh | You are (to a friend)
 EX: Desculpa | dihsh-KOOL-puh | Excuse me (to a friend)
CARDS NOW: tu = you (to a friend) [card] || tu falas = you speak (tu) [card] || Tu és = you are (to a friend) [card] || Desculpa = Excuse me (to a friend) [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 13)
o senhor and a senhora
 With an older stranger, a shop, a doctor, or anyone you want to treat with distance, use o senhor for a man and a senhora for a woman. The verb is the ele form, the same ending as ele (he) and ela (she), with no s . O senhor fala , a senhora fala , o senhor é . You can also drop the noun and keep that verb: Fala inglês? is a normal question to a stranger in a shop. Desculpe , from the last lesson, is the polite command that goes with o senhor and a senhora . Desculpa is the friendly one, for tu .
 EX: O senhor fala | oo sih-NYOHR FAH-luh | You speak (to an older man)
 EX: A senhora fala | uh sih-NYOH-ruh FAH-luh | You speak (to an older woman)
 EX: O senhor é | oo sih-NYOHR eh | You are (to an older man)
 EX: Fala inglês? | FAH-luh een-GLAYSH | Do you speak English? (to a stranger)
 The other subject words: eu I, nós we, eles they (men, or a mixed group), elas they (women). The ending still has to match the subject.
CARDS NOW: o senhor = you (to an older man) [card] || a senhora = you (to an older woman) [card] || ele = he; him (after a preposition) [card] || ela = she; her (after a preposition) [card] || O senhor fala = you speak (to an older man) [card] || A senhora fala = you speak (to an older woman) [card] || O senhor é = you are (to an older man) [card] || Fala inglês? = Do you speak English? (to a stranger) || Desculpe = Excuse me (to a stranger) [card] || eu = I [card] || nós = we; us (after a preposition) [card] || eles = they (men or mixed); them [card] || elas = they (women only); them [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
você, once
 Textbooks, and Portuguese from Brazil, often use você for you . In Portugal it sits in an uncomfortable middle: less close than tu , less respectful than o senhor . To an older stranger it can sound rude. This course names it so you recognize it, and does not drill it. If you hear it, the verb is the ele form, the same shape as o senhor . Your own sentence stays with tu , or with o senhor and a senhora , or with the bare ele form.
CARDS NOW: (none)

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
More than one person
 Vocês is the everyday plural you , whether the group is friends or strangers. The verb is the eles form: vocês falam . Vós is an old plural you that you will see in solemn language, and it is still ordinary in parts of the north. You do not need its endings. Use vocês .
 EX: Vocês falam | vaw-SAYSH FAH-lowng | You all speak
 EX: Desculpe | dihsh-KOOL-pih | Excuse me (to a stranger)
 Most common mistake: você for an older stranger → o senhor or a senhora , with the ele form of the verb.
CARDS NOW: vocês = you (more than one) [card] || Vocês falam = you speak (vocês) [card]

## LESSON intro — 5 · Introducing yourself — deck "About me"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
Two sentences carry a first meeting. One gives your name. One says where you are from. Both are short, and both have a shape English does not use.
 Your name
 English says my name is or I am . For the name itself, Portuguese says chamo-me , literally I call myself . The little word me sits after the verb, joined with a hyphen: Chamo-me Ana . Nothing goes between the verb and the name. You do not add a or o there, and you do not put me in front. Me chamo Ana is the order you will hear from Brazil. In Portugal the affirmative sentence keeps me after the verb. The spelling with a space, Chamo me Ana , is the same sentence if the hyphen key fails you. The hyphen is the spelling to aim at.
 EX: Chamo-me Ana | SHUH-moo mih UH-nuh | My name is Ana
 EX: Chamo-me João | SHUH-moo mih zhwowng | My name is João
 EX: Chamo-me Maria | SHUH-moo mih muh-REE-uh | My name is Maria
CARDS NOW: me = me; to me; myself [card] || Chamo-me Ana = My name is Ana [card] || Chamo-me João = My name is João || Chamo-me Maria = My name is Maria [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 6)
I am Ana, I am Pedro
 There is a second way to say who you are, and this one does take the article. In Portugal a person's name usually keeps o or a . The article agrees with the person. A woman says Sou a Ana . A man says Sou o Pedro . Sou is I am . Eu sou a Ana is the same sentence with the word eu put back. Use this frame when you mean I am Ana , and keep chamo-me for my name is Ana . Do not mix them into Chamo-me a Ana .
 EX: Sou a Ana | soh uh UH-nuh | I am Ana
 EX: Sou o Pedro | soh oo PAY-droo | I am Pedro
 EX: Sou o João | soh oo zhwowng | I am João
CARDS NOW: Chamo-me Pedro = My name is Pedro [card] || Sou a Ana = I am Ana || Sou o Pedro = I am Pedro || Eu sou = I am [card] || eu = I [card] || Sou o João = I am João

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
Where you are from
 Sou de plus a place is where you are from. De is from or of . Lisbon, Portugal, and Coimbra take no extra article in this frame. Where you are standing right now uses a different verb, in a later lesson. Do not build that sentence yet.
 EX: Sou de Lisboa | soh dih lihzh-BOH-uh | I am from Lisbon
 EX: Eu sou de Portugal | ayw soh dih poor-too-GAHL | I am from Portugal
 EX: Sou de Coimbra | soh dih KWEEN-bruh | I am from Coimbra
CARDS NOW: de = of; from [card] || Sou de Lisboa = I am from Lisbon [card] || Eu sou de Portugal = I am from Portugal || Sou de Coimbra = I am from Coimbra

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Pleased to meet you
 When someone has given you a name, answer muito prazer . It is the set phrase, pleased to meet you . Muito here means very or much . You can add your own name after it, or stay with the phrase alone. Then give your name with chamo-me if you have not said it yet.
 EX: Muito prazer | MOOYNG-too pruh-ZAYR | Pleased to meet you
 Most common mistake: Me chamo Ana → Chamo-me Ana , with me after the verb.
CARDS NOW: Muito prazer = Pleased to meet you [card] || muito = very; a lot [card]

## LESSON articles — 6 · Gender, plural and articles — deck "Food and drink"
### SECTION 0   (has 10)
You already use o and a for the . This lesson adds a and an , the plural, and the food words you will actually order. The article agrees with the noun in gender and in number. Learn the noun and the article as one piece.
 o, a, um, uma
 Most nouns ending in -o are masculine and take o or um . Most nouns ending in -a are feminine and take a or uma . um is one syllable, the nasal oong . uma is two syllables, OO-muh . Nouns ending in -e can go either way, so the article is part of the word you memorize: o leite , o peixe , a tarde , a noite .

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
In the café
 In Lisbon and the south, a small espresso is uma bica . In Porto the same drink is um cimbalino . Um galão is a milky coffee in a tall glass. O pastel de nata is a custard tart. Bica is feminine. Galão , café , pastel , and cimbalino are masculine. Order them with um or uma when you mean one or a .
 EX: uma bica | OO-muh BEE-kuh | an espresso (Lisbon and the south)
 EX: um cimbalino | oong seem-buh-LEE-noo | an espresso (in Porto)

CARDS NOW: uma bica = an espresso (Lisbon and the south) [card] || um cimbalino = an espresso (in Porto) [card] || um galão = a milky coffee in a glass [card] || o pastel de nata = a custard tart [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
More than one
 For more than one, o becomes os and a becomes as . A noun that ends in a vowel adds -s : a casa , as casas . The article and the noun agree together. A group of men, or a mixed group, takes the masculine plural: os amigos . A group of women is as amigas . On the line, os is oosh . In real speech that s may sound like z before a vowel. The line keeps the pause form.
 EX: as casas | uhsh KAH-zuhsh | the houses
 EX: os amigos | oosh uh-MEE-goosh | the friends (men, or men and women)
 EX: as amigas | uhsh uh-MEE-guhsh | the friends (women)
CARDS NOW: as casas = the houses || os amigos = the friends (men, or men and women) || as amigas = the friends (women)

### SECTION 3   (has 4)
Endings that lie, and dois or duas
 Do not trust the ending every time. Dia ends in -a and is masculine: o dia . Mão is feminine: a mão . Problema , a problem, ends in -a and is masculine too: o problema . Two also agrees. Dois with a masculine noun, duas with a feminine noun, and the noun itself goes to the plural. From three to twenty, the number word does not change for gender. That pattern comes back with prices.
 EX: o dia | oo DEE-uh | the day
 EX: a mão | uh mowng | the hand
 EX: duas bicas | DOO-uhsh BEE-kuhsh | two espressos
 Most common mistake: a queijo → o queijo . The article agrees with the masculine noun.
CARDS NOW: dois = two [card] || o dia = the day [card] || a mão = the hand [card] || duas bicas = two espressos

## LESSON ser — 7 · ser for who you are — deck "About me"
### SECTION 0   (has 12)
Ser is the verb for who a person is: a name you already know how to frame, a job, a nationality, and the place you are from. English uses am , are , and is for all of that and also for how you feel and where you are standing. Those last two are a different Portuguese verb, in a later lesson. Do not sort the world into permanent and temporary. Sort it into the sentences this lesson actually builds.
 The forms
 The ending matches the subject. The tu form is és , with an s and an accent. O senhor and a senhora take é , the ele form, the same as ele and ela . The word e , with no accent, is a different word: it means and . Eu can drop, because sou already says I .

## LESSON questions — 9 · Questions with é que, and não — deck "Questions and small words"
### SECTION 0   (has 16)
A Portuguese question does not need an extra helper verb the way English needs do or does . You either keep the statement and let your voice rise, or you drop in two small words, é que , after the question word. Não is both the answer no and the word not in front of a verb.
 é que
 Onde is where . O que is what . Como is how . This como is the question word, not I eat . É que sits immediately after the question word, and the rest of the sentence stays in statement order. Onde é que está o café? is the everyday Lisbon shape of where is the café? Onde está o café? is also normal. Onde é que o café está? is normal too: the café can stand before the verb. When you practice, the prompt tells you which shape to build. Está and estás are the is and are inside where is and how are you . The full pattern of that verb comes in a later lesson. You can use the chunks now.

English uses one word, am, for all of these: I am from Lisbon, I am in Lisbon, I am tired. Portuguese splits that job. You already use ser for who you are and where you are from. Sou de Lisboa means I am from Lisbon. Sou português means I am Portuguese, said by a man. A woman says Sou portuguesa, with an a on the end. Estar is the other am. ## LESSON ter-haver — 12 · ter and há — deck "Traps for English speakers"
### SECTION 0   (has 10)
English says I have a sister, and there is a pharmacy, with two different patterns. Portuguese does the same split, and English speakers mix the verbs. Ter is for possession: people in the family, and things you have. Há is for there is and there are. Há does not change when the thing is plural.
 ter: what you have
