# Worksheet: en-vi

## LESSON alphabet — 1 · Letters and sounds: reading Vietnamese — deck "Letters and sounds"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Vietnamese is written in the same Latin letters as English, so you can start reading on day one. The catch: many letters stand for different sounds than in English. Under every Vietnamese phrase in this course there is a pronunciation line in plain English letters. This lesson teaches you to read both.
 Seven extra letters
 The alphabet has 29 letters. f, j, w and z appear only in foreign words. Seven letters carry a vowel mark or a stroke: ă â ê ô ơ ư and đ , the crossed d. Each one is a separate letter, not a decoration, so leaving the mark off writes a different letter.
 EX: đi | dee | to go
 EX: ăn | an | to eat
CARDS NOW: đi = to go [card] || ăn = to eat [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 8)
Letters that fool English readers
 This course follows Hanoi speech. These are the letters to watch:

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Reading the pronunciation line
 Vietnamese puts a space after every syllable, and the line has one chunk for each written syllable. A hyphen inside a chunk is still one syllable: Việt is vee-uht. , one quick glide. There are no capitals in the line, because no syllable is stressed more than another.
 EX: Việt Nam | vee-uht. nahm | Vietnam
 The symbol at the end of a chunk names the tone, which the next lesson teaches: nothing for the level tone, then / rising, _ falling, ? dipping, ~ broken and . heavy.
CARDS NOW: Việt Nam = Vietnam

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Vowel spellings in the line
 The line uses fixed vowel spellings: ah (father), a (a short ah), uh (cup), eh (bed), ey (hey), ee (see), aw (law), oh (go), er (her), oo (food) and uu (oo said with a smile). er never has an r sound: it is the vowel of her with the r left off.
 EX: cơm | kerm | cooked rice
 The line is a crutch. Listen to the audio first, read the line second, and hide it once the letters feel natural.
 Most common mistake: reading d as an English d. da said "dah" → da said "zah". The English d sound belongs to the crossed d: đi .
CARDS NOW: cơm = cooked rice; meal [card]

## LESSON tones — 2 · The six tones and their marks — deck "Tone pairs"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
English uses pitch for feeling: your voice goes up on a question and down when you are sure. Vietnamese uses pitch to tell words apart. Every syllable has one of six tones, and changing the tone changes the word, just as changing a letter would.
 Six tones, five marks
 The level tone has no mark. Each of the other five has a mark on the main vowel, and the pronunciation line ends each chunk with a matching symbol.
 Tone ; Mark ; Line ; How it sounds ; Example ; 
 level (ngang) ; none ; none ; flat, in the middle of your voice ; ma (ghost) ; 
 falling (huyền) ; à ; _ ; starts low and falls gently ; mà (but) ; 
 rising (sắc) ; á ; / ; starts in the middle and rises ; má (mom, in the South) ; 
 dipping (hỏi) ; ả ; ? ; falls low, then may rise a little ; mả (grave) ; 
 broken (ngã) ; ã ; ~ ; rises, with a catch in the middle ; mã (horse) ; 
 heavy (nặng) ; ạ ; . ; low and short, ending with a catch ; mạ (rice seedling) ; 
 EX: ma | mah | ghost
 EX: mà | mah_ | but
 The dipping and broken tones are the hardest for English speakers. In Hanoi they sound clearly different. In the South they merge into one tone, so if you grew up hearing Southern Vietnamese, they may sound the same to you at first.
CARDS NOW: ma = ghost [card] || mà = but [card] || má = mom (in the South); cheek [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 7)
One set to learn by heart
 bàn , bán and bạn share every letter. Only the tone tells them apart.
 EX: bàn | bahn_ | table
 EX: bán | bahn/ | to sell
 EX: bạn | bahn. | friend
 EX: Mẹ bán bàn. | meh. bahn/ bahn_ | Mom sells tables.
 Mẹ means "mom". Notice also bạn tôi , "my friend": Vietnamese says "friend me", with the owner after the noun.
 EX: Bạn tôi bán cá. | bahn. dtohy bahn/ kah/ | My friend sells fish.
CARDS NOW: bàn = table [card] || bán = to sell [card] || bạn = friend; you (to a friend) [card] || Mẹ bán bàn. = Mom sells tables. || mẹ = mother [card] || Bạn tôi bán cá. = My friend sells fish. || cá = fish [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Questions keep their tones
 In English, "You're tired?" rises at the end. Vietnamese asks with a word instead, and every syllable keeps its own tone. In Anh khỏe không? ("How are you?" to a man a little older), không stays level. Push it up the English way and it starts to sound like a rising tone.
 EX: Anh khỏe không? | aing khweh? khohng | How are you? (to a man a little older)
CARDS NOW: Anh khỏe không? = How are you? (to a man a little older) [card] || không = no; not; zero [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
Marks are part of the spelling
 Vietnamese readers can often guess a word typed with no marks, so in this course a missing mark counts as "almost". A wrong mark, though, is a different word, and counts as wrong.
 Most common mistake: treating the marks as decoration. Bạn tôi bán bán. → Bạn tôi bán bàn. (My friend sells tables.)
CARDS NOW: (none)

## LESSON syllables — 3 · Syllables: first and last sounds — deck "Letters and sounds"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
An English word can pile up consonants: strengths has three at the start and four at the end. A Vietnamese syllable is much tidier. It has at most one first consonant, one vowel or vowel pair, one last consonant, and always a tone.
 Four parts
 Syllable ; First consonant ; Vowel ; Last consonant ; Tone ; 
 bánh ; b ; a ; nh ; rising ; 
 ngủ ; ng ; u ; none ; dipping ; 
 ăn ; none ; ă ; n ; level ; 
 Every syllable needs a vowel and a tone. The consonants at either end are optional, and there is never a cluster like the str of street .
CARDS NOW: bánh = cake; pastry; snack [card] || ngủ = to sleep [card] || ăn = to eat [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
One sound, two spellings
 Before i , e and ê , three sounds change their spelling: the k sound is written k , g becomes gh , and ng becomes ngh . Everywhere else they are c , g and ng . The k sound before a w glide is written qu .
 EX: kem | kehm | ice cream
 EX: ghế | gey/ | chair
 EX: nghe | ngeh | to listen
 EX: quà | kwah_ | gift
CARDS NOW: kem = ice cream || ghế = chair || nghe = listen; hear [card] || quà = gift; present [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
ng at the start
 English has the ng sound only at the end of a syllable, as in sing . Vietnamese also starts syllables with it. Say singer , then drop the si : what is left starts like ngủ . The family name Nguyễn is a single syllable that starts the same way.
 EX: ngủ | ngoo? | to sleep
 EX: Nguyễn | ngwee-uhn~ | Nguyen (family name)
CARDS NOW: Nguyễn = Nguyen (family name)

### SECTION 3   (has 3)
Last consonants
 A syllable can end in m, n, ng, nh, p, t, c or ch . The last p , t and c are not released: your mouth gets into position and stops, with no puff. In Hanoi, -anh sounds like "aing" and -ach like "aik", so bánh is never the English word bank .
 EX: bánh mì | baing/ mee_ | bread; banh mi sandwich
 EX: sách | saik/ | book
CARDS NOW: anh = you (to a man a little older); I (a man to someone younger) [card] || bánh mì = bread; banh mi sandwich [card] || sách = book

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Stop endings
 Syllables that end in p, t, c or ch are cut short, so they can carry only two tones: rising or heavy. That makes them easier to spell, since only two marks are possible. In the pronunciation line, these chunks always end in / or a dot, never in any other tone symbol.
 EX: một | moht. | one
 EX: Tôi học tiếng Việt. | dtohy hawk. dtee-uhng/ vee-uht. | I'm learning Vietnamese.
 Most common mistake: reading -anh the English way. bánh mì said "bank me" → bánh mì said as its line shows, baing/ mee_ , with no k sound.
CARDS NOW: một = one [card] || Tôi học tiếng Việt. = I'm learning Vietnamese. [card]

## LESSON vowels — 4 · Vowels: ă, â, ơ, ư and vowel pairs — deck "Letters and sounds"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
English spells about fifteen vowel sounds with five letters, so a in cat , car and cake are three sounds. Vietnamese gives each vowel sound its own letter, and the vowel marks tell you which one. Once you know the twelve vowel letters (i and y share one sound), a word always sounds the way it is spelled.
 Three families
 Letter ; Line ; Sound ; 
 a / ă / â ; ah / a / uh ; father ; a short ah; cup ; 
 o / ô / ơ ; aw / oh / er ; law ; go , with no glide; her , with no r ; 
 e / ê ; eh / ey ; bed ; hey , with no glide ; 
 u / ư ; oo / uu ; food ; oo said with spread lips, as in a smile ; 
 ă and â are short, so they never end a syllable: something always follows them. ơ is the vowel you may have heard in pho ("fuh"): the line writes it er , but there is no r anywhere.
 EX: cơm gà | kerm gah_ | rice with chicken
 EX: Tôi uống sữa. | dtohy oo-uhng/ suu-uh~ | I drink milk.
 For ư , say oo , then pull your lips into a smile without moving your tongue.
CARDS NOW: cơm gà = chicken and rice [card] || Tôi uống sữa. = I drink milk.

### SECTION 1   (has 5)
Long and short
 Some vowels change length before a glide, and the length changes the word. ai has a long ah, ay a short one; ao is long, au short.
 EX: tai | dtahy | ear
 EX: tay | dtai | hand; arm
 EX: sao | sahw | star
 EX: sau | sow | after; behind
CARDS NOW: Ai? = Who? [card] || tai = ear [card] || tay = hand; arm [card] || sao = why; how come [card] || sau = after; behind [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 4)
Vowel pairs
 Three pairs glide from one vowel into a soft uh, inside one syllable: iê (written ia at the end), uô ( ua at the end) and ươ ( ưa at the end). The line shows them with a hyphen.
 EX: người Việt | nguu-uhy_ vee-uht. | a Vietnamese person
 EX: Tôi muốn ăn cơm gà. | dtohy moo-uhn/ an kerm gah_ | I want to eat rice with chicken.
CARDS NOW: người Việt = a Vietnamese person || người = person [card] || Tôi muốn ăn cơm gà. = I want to eat rice with chicken. || ăn = to eat [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
o and u as a glide
 Before another vowel, o and u often turn into a quick w: hoa sounds like hwah , thuê like twey . The line writes the glide as a w, so you never read it as an extra syllable.
 EX: hoa | hwah | flower
 Most common mistake: saying o , ô and ơ the same way, or adding an r. cơm said "kohm" or "kurm" → cơm said "kerm", with no r.
CARDS NOW: hoa = flower || cơm = cooked rice; meal [card]

## LESSON pronouns — 5 · Greetings and kinship pronouns: anh, chị, em — deck "Greetings and courtesy"
### SECTION 0   (has 9)
English has one "you" for everyone, from a child to the president. Vietnamese has no neutral "you". Instead it borrows family words: you call a man a little older than you anh ("older brother"), even if you just met him.
 Hello and thank you, with a person
 Xin chào is a safe hello with anyone. People more often say Chào plus the word for the listener. Cảm ơn (also spelled cám ơn ) is "thank you", and it sounds warmer with the person added too.
 EX: Xin chào. | seen chahw_ | Hello.
 EX: Chào chị. | chahw_ chee. | Hello. (to a woman a little older)
 EX: Cảm ơn anh. | kahm? ern aing | Thank you. (to a man a little older)
 Xin lỗi is "sorry" and "excuse me". Tạm biệt is "goodbye", a little formal; friends just say Chào again.
 EX: Xin lỗi. | seen lohy~ | Sorry. / Excuse me.
CARDS NOW: Chào anh. = Hello. (to a man a little older) [card] || anh = you (to a man a little older); I (a man to someone younger) [card] || Xin chào. = Hello. [card] || Em chào chị ạ. = Hello. (politely, to a woman a little older) [card] || Cảm ơn. = Thank you. [card] || Chào chị. = Hello. (to a woman a little older) [card] || Cảm ơn anh. = Thank you. (to a man a little older) [card] || Xin lỗi. = Sorry. / Excuse me. [card] || Tạm biệt. = Goodbye. [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 9)
Choosing the word for "you"
 Say ; To ; And call yourself ; 
 bạn ; someone about your age ; tôi ; 
 anh ; a man a little older than you ; em ; 
 chị ; a woman a little older than you ; em ; 
 em ; anyone younger than you ; anh or chị ; 
 If you are not sure of someone's age, it is safer to treat them as a little older than you. Between friends of the same age, bạn and tôi are fine.
 The words go both ways. Talking to chị , you are em ; she calls you em and herself chị . tôi ("I") is never wrong, just more distant. For people your parents' or grandparents' age you will learn cô, chú, ông and bà later: never call them bạn .

 EX: Em khỏe, cảm ơn chị. | ehm khweh? kahm? ern chee. | I'm fine, thank you. (to a woman a little older)

(to a woman a little older) || Em khỏe, cảm ơn chị. = I'm fine, thank you. (to a woman a little older)

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
Calling someone
 To get a waiter's or a driver's attention, add ơi : Anh ơi! for a man older than you, Em ơi! for anyone younger.
 EX: Em ơi! | ehm uhy | Excuse me! (calling someone younger)
CARDS NOW: ơi = (calling someone) [card] || Anh ơi! = Excuse me! (calling a man) [card] || Em ơi! = Excuse me! (calling someone younger) [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Two small polite words

 Most common mistake: one English "you" for everyone. Chào bạn to a woman ten years older → Chào chị .

## LESSON la — 6 · là: A is B, and không phải là — deck "Traps for English speakers"
### SECTION 0   (has 6)
English uses "is", "am" and "are" for almost everything: "I am a student", "I am tired", "I am in Hanoi". Vietnamese là has one job only. It joins two nouns: A là B, "A is B".
 A là B
 Put the person first, then là , then what they are. The word for "a" is not needed. You may see một ("one") used for "a" in writing, but in everyday speech it is simply left out.
 EX: Tôi là sinh viên. | dtohy lah_ sing vee-uhn | I'm a college student.
 EX: Chị Lan là giáo viên. | chee. lahn lah_ zahw/ vee-uhn | Lan is a teacher.
 Notice chị Lan : the family word goes before a first name, a bit like "Ms. Lan" but friendlier. Vietnamese names are written family name first, and people are called by the last part, so Nguyễn Văn Minh is anh Minh .
 Your name works the same way: tên means "name", and Tôi tên là… is "My name is…".
 EX: Tôi tên là Emma. | dtohy dteyn lah_ ehm mah | My name is Emma.
CARDS NOW: là = be (A is B) [card] || một = one [card] || Tôi là sinh viên. = I'm a college student. || Chị Lan là giáo viên. = Lan is a teacher. || tên = name [card] || Tôi tên là Emma. = My name is Emma. [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 8)
He, she and we
 Add ấy ("that") to a family word and you get "he" or "she": anh ấy for a man, chị ấy for a woman, and often cô ấy for a young woman. Bạn ấy is a friend of about your age. Chúng tôi is "we", leaving out the person you are talking to, whether the group is two people or two hundred. Never call an adult nó : it is for animals and small children.
 EX: Anh ấy là bác sĩ. | aing ay/ lah_ bahk/ see~ | He's a doctor.
 EX: Chúng tôi là sinh viên. | choong/ dtohy lah_ sing vee-uhn | We're students.
CARDS NOW: Anh ấy là bạn tôi. = He's my friend. [card] || anh ấy = he; him (a young or middle-aged man) [card] || chị ấy = she; her (a woman a little older than you) [card] || cô ấy = she; her (a young woman) [card] || chúng tôi = we (not including you) [card] || nó = it; he or she (a child or an animal) [card] || Anh ấy là bác sĩ. = He's a doctor. || Chúng tôi là sinh viên. = We're students.

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
Not: không phải là
 English just adds "not". Vietnamese does not say không là . The negative of là is không phải là . In speech the là is often dropped: không phải alone also works.
 EX: Tôi không phải là bác sĩ. | dtohy khohng fahy? lah_ bahk/ see~ | I'm not a doctor.
 EX: Chị ấy không phải là giáo viên. | chee. ay/ khohng fahy? lah_ zahw/ vee-uhn | She's not a teacher.
CARDS NOW: Tôi không phải là giáo viên. = I'm not a teacher. [card] || Tôi không phải là bác sĩ. = I'm not a doctor. || Chị ấy không phải là giáo viên. = She's not a teacher.

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Where là does not go
 Words like "tired", "hot" or "happy" are not nouns, and they never take là . Vietnamese simply says "I tired". The lesson on adjectives comes back to this. Places are different too: "I am in Hanoi" uses another verb, which comes later.
 EX: Tôi mệt. | dtohy meyt. | I'm tired.
 Most common mistake: making the negative the English way. Tôi không là giáo viên. → Tôi không phải là giáo viên.
CARDS NOW: Tôi mệt. = I'm tired. [card]

## LESSON yes-no — 7 · Yes/no questions: có … không, phải không — deck "Questions and connectors"
### SECTION 0   (has 8)
English turns a sentence into a question by moving words around ("You are…" becomes "Are you…?") or just by raising the voice. Vietnamese does neither. It wraps the sentence in question words, and every syllable keeps its tone.
 có … không
 Put có before the verb and không at the very end. The rest stays in normal order. có is often dropped in speech, but không never is.
 EX: Anh có uống cà phê không? | aing kaw/ oo-uhng/ kah_ fey khohng | Do you drink coffee? (to a man a little older)
 EX: Em có thích phở không? | ehm kaw/ tik/ fer? khohng | Do you like pho? (to someone younger)
 It works the same with words like "busy" or "well": bận means "busy".
 EX: Chị có bận không? | chee. kaw/ buhn. khohng | Are you busy? (to a woman a little older)
 You already know one question like this: Anh khỏe không? is a có … không question with có dropped. The usual answer repeats the key word, then thanks the person.
 EX: Khỏe, cảm ơn anh. | khweh? kahm? ern aing | Fine, thanks. (to a man a little older)

## LESSON co — 14 · có: have, and there is — deck "Shopping and the market"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
English uses "have" for what you own and "there is / there are" for what exists somewhere. Vietnamese uses one word for both: có .
 có: have
 Person, then có , then the thing. Counted things keep their classifier.
 EX: Tôi có hai anh trai. | dtohy kaw/ hahy aing chahy | I have two older brothers.
 EX: Chị Lan có một con mèo. | chee. lahn kaw/ moht. kawn mehw_ | Lan has a cat.
CARDS NOW: có = have; there is [card] || Tôi có hai anh trai. = I have two older brothers. || Chị Lan có một con mèo. = Lan has a cat.

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
có: there is, there are
 Say the place first, then có , then what is there. ở đây means "here".
 EX: Ở đây có wifi. | er? day kaw/ wai fai | There's wifi here.
 EX: Ở Hà Nội có nhiều quán phở. | er? hah_ nohy. kaw/ nyee-uhw_ kwahn/ fer? | There are lots of pho places in Hanoi.
 When the place is clear from the situation, leave it out and start with có : Có wifi không? is a quick "Is there wifi?" at a café.
 English "there is" never becomes là . là joins two nouns ("A is B"); it does not say that something exists.
CARDS NOW: Ở đây có wifi. = There's wifi here. || Ở Hà Nội có nhiều quán phở. = There are lots of pho places in Hanoi. || là = be (A is B) [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
không có: don't have, there isn't
 Put không before có , as before any verb.
 EX: Tôi không có xe máy. | dtohy khohng kaw/ seh mai/ | I don't have a motorbike.
CARDS NOW: không = no; not; zero [card] || Tôi không có xe máy. = I don't have a motorbike.

### SECTION 3   (has 4)
Asking: có … không?
 You already ask yes/no questions with có … không . With "have", the có is the verb itself, so the question is simply có + thing + không . This is how you ask in a shop or at a stall.
 EX: Chị có áo màu đỏ không? | chee. kaw/ ahw/ mow_ daw? khohng | Do you have a red shirt? (to a saleswoman a little older)
 EX: Ở đây có nhà vệ sinh không? | er? day kaw/ nyah_ vey. sing khohng | Is there a restroom here?
 You can ask about one particular thing too. Keep its classifier and này : Chị có quyển sách này không? (Do you have this book?)
 Answer yes with Có . Answer no with Không or Không có . A seller may soften it: Dạ, không có ạ. 
 EX: Không có. | khohng kaw/ | No, we don't. / No, there isn't.
CARDS NOW: Chị có áo màu đỏ không? = Do you have a red shirt? (to a saleswoman) [card] || Ở đây có nhà vệ sinh không? = Is there a restroom here? || này = this (after a noun) [card] || Không có. = No, we don't. / No, there isn't.

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 0)
Not for age
 Don't stretch có to ages. "I'm twenty" is Tôi hai mươi tuổi , with no có and no là .
 Most common mistake: using là for "there is". Ở đây là nhà vệ sinh không? → Ở đây có nhà vệ sinh không?

## LESSON cua — 15 · của: possession, and nhà tôi — deck "Family and kinship"
### SECTION 0   (has 3)
English puts the owner first: Lan's book, my house. Vietnamese puts the thing first and the owner after it, joined by của (of): sách của chị Lan , "book of Lan".
 Thing + của + owner
 EX: sách của chị Lan | saik/ koo-uh? chee. lahn | Lan's book
 EX: Đây là xe máy của anh Minh. | day lah_ seh mai/ koo-uh? aing ming | This is Minh's motorbike.
 There is nothing like English 's. The order alone shows who owns what.
CARDS NOW: của = of; belonging to [card] || sách của chị Lan = Lan's book || Đây là xe máy của anh Minh. = This is Minh's motorbike.

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
my, your: của + pronoun
 "My" is của tôi , "your" is của bạn , của anh , của chị . They go after the thing, like any owner. "Mine" is the same: của tôi .
 EX: Cái áo này là của tôi. | kahy/ ahw/ nai_ lah_ koo-uh? dtohy | This shirt is mine.
CARDS NOW: của tôi = mine; my [card] || của bạn = yours (to a friend) [card] || Cái áo này là của tôi. = This shirt is mine.

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
Family and home: drop của
 With family, friends and home, people usually leave của out: mẹ tôi (my mother), bạn tôi (my friend), nhà tôi (my house). The owner still comes after the noun. mẹ của tôi is not wrong, but it sounds as if you are stressing "mine, not yours".
 EX: Mẹ tôi là giáo viên. | meh. dtohy lah_ zahw/ vee-uhn | My mother is a teacher.
 EX: Nhà tôi rất nhỏ. | nyah_ dtohy zuht/ nyaw? | My house is very small.
 With objects, của is usual, though people often drop it in quick speech.
CARDS NOW: mẹ = mother [card] || Mẹ tôi là giáo viên. = My mother is a teacher. || Nhà tôi rất nhỏ. = My house is very small.

### SECTION 3   (has 3)
With a classifier and này
 The classifier still goes in front, and the owner after the noun: quyển sách của tôi (my book). Any describing word comes before the owner, right after the noun.
 EX: chiếc xe máy mới của tôi | chee-uhk/ seh mai/ muhy/ koo-uh? dtohy | my new motorbike
 Watch the difference between "my shirt" and "mine". Đây là áo của tôi. means "This is my shirt". With này , the noun group closes with này , and là của says who it belongs to: Cái áo này là của tôi. (This shirt is mine.)
CARDS NOW: này = this (after a noun) [card] || Đây là mẹ tôi. = This is my mother. [card] || chiếc xe máy mới của tôi = my new motorbike

### SECTION 4   (has 3)
Whose? của ai
 Put ai where the owner would go. The answer can be just của + owner.
 EX: Quyển sách này là của ai? | kwee-uhn? saik/ nai_ lah_ koo-uh? ahy | Whose book is this?
 EX: Của chị Lan. | koo-uh? chee. lahn | It's Lan's.
 Most common mistake: putting the owner first, as in English. chị Lan sách → sách của chị Lan ; tôi nhà → nhà tôi .
CARDS NOW: Ai? = Who? [card] || Quyển sách này là của ai? = Whose book is this? || Của chị Lan. = It's Lan's.

## LESSON compare — 17 · hơn, bằng, nhất: comparing — deck "Describing things"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
English compares in two ways: add -er (colder) or put "more" in front (more expensive). Vietnamese has one way for every adjective: put hơn after it.
 hơn: more … than
 Adjective, then hơn , then the other thing. As with every adjective, there is no là .
 EX: Hà Nội lạnh hơn Sài Gòn. | hah_ nohy. laing. hern sahy_ gawn_ | Hanoi is colder than Saigon.
 EX: Cái áo này đắt hơn cái áo kia. | kahy/ ahw/ nai_ dat/ hern kahy/ ahw/ kee-uh | This shirt is more expensive than that one.
 If the other thing is clear, stop after hơn . Nothing else changes: the same word works for short adjectives and long ones, where English would switch from -er to "more".
 EX: Phở này ngon hơn. | fer? nai_ ngawn hern | This pho tastes better.
CARDS NOW: Cái này to hơn cái kia. = This one is bigger than that one. [card] || hơn = more (in comparisons) [card] || là = be (A is B) [card] || Hà Nội lạnh hơn Sài Gòn. = Hanoi is colder than Saigon. [card] || Cái áo này đắt hơn cái áo kia. = This shirt is more expensive than that one. || to = big [card] || Phở này ngon hơn. = This pho tastes better.

## LESSON verbs — 18 · Verbs and time words: đã, đang, sẽ — deck "Time, days and dates"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
English changes the verb for time: go, went, going, will go. Vietnamese verbs never change. đi is "go", "went" and "going". Time comes from small time words before the verb, or from a word like hôm qua (yesterday).
 đã: it happened
 đã before the verb shows the action is done. All three time words in this lesson sit in the same slot, between the person and the verb: tôi đã đi , tôi đang đi , tôi sẽ đi .
 EX: Anh ấy đã đi Huế. | aing ay/ dah~ dee hwey/ | He went to Hue.
CARDS NOW: đi = to go [card] || hôm qua = yesterday [card] || đã = already happened (past marker) [card] || Anh ấy đã đi Huế. = He went to Hue.

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
đang: it is happening

 EX: Tôi đang học tiếng Việt. | dtohy dahng hawk. dtee-uhng/ vee-uht. | I'm learning Vietnamese.
 EX: Bây giờ chị Lan đang ăn cơm. | bay zer_ chee. lahn dahng an kerm | Lan is eating right now.

## LESSON location — 20 · ở, trên, dưới: where things are — deck "Around town and directions"
### SECTION 0   (has 6)
English needs the verb "to be" to say where something is: "I am in Hanoi", "The book is on the table". Vietnamese uses one small word instead: ở .
 ở means "be at" and "live at"
 ở is a verb of its own. It already means "is at", so it never takes là . With a person, ở can mean both "am in" and "live in". Like every Vietnamese verb, it never changes: the same ở works for I, you, she and they.
 EX: Tôi ở Hà Nội. | dtohy er? hah_ nohy. | I'm in Hanoi. / I live in Hanoi.
 EX: Chị Lan sống ở Huế. | chee. lahn sohng/ er? hwey/ | Lan lives in Hue.
 In the second sentence ở follows another verb, sống (live), and works like English "in" or "at". It does the same after làm việc (work) and học (study): you work ở a place, you study ở a place.

## LESSON biet — 21 · biết, có thể, được: can and know how — deck "Work and study"

English uses one word, "can", for three different ideas: a skill you learned, something that is possible, and something you are able or allowed to do. Vietnamese has a word for each.
 biết + verb: know how to

### SECTION 3   (has 6)
Dates: day, then month
 Months are numbered too: tháng một is January, tháng chín is September (April is tháng tư ). A date goes from small to big: ngày (day) and its number, then tháng (month) and its number. ## LESSON because — 27 · vì … nên: because, so — deck "Health and help"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
English joins a reason and a result with one word: "because" or "so", never both. Vietnamese likes to mark both halves.
 vì … nên: because … so
 vì introduces the reason and nên introduces the result. When the reason comes first, Vietnamese often uses both, which sounds odd in English ("because it rains, so I stay home") but is normal here.
 EX: Vì trời mưa nên tôi ở nhà. | vee_ chuhy_ muu-uh neyn dtohy er? nyah_ | Because it's raining, I'm staying home.
 You can also drop vì and keep nên , like English "so". When the result comes first, use vì alone, like English "because".
 EX: Trời mưa nên tôi ở nhà. | chuhy_ muu-uh neyn dtohy er? nyah_ | It's raining, so I'm staying home.
 EX: Tôi ở nhà vì trời mưa. | dtohy er? nyah_ vee_ chuhy_ muu-uh | I'm staying home because it's raining.
 In speech you will also hear tại vì and bởi vì : both mean "because".

### SECTION 2   (has 12)
bị: when something bad happens to you
 English says "I have a headache" or "I got lost". Vietnamese often puts bị before the problem. It marks something unpleasant that happens to you, so it goes with illness, pain, losing things and getting lost, not with good news. For something good that comes your way, Vietnamese uses được instead: Tôi được nghỉ is "I got the day off". Losing works the same way as illness: Tôi bị mất ví (I lost my wallet). ## LESSON requests — 28 · Polite requests: xin, mời, cho, nhé, ạ — deck "Polite Vietnamese"
### SECTION 0   (has 11)
English makes a request polite with "please" and "could you". Vietnamese does it mostly with the right pronouns and a few small words at the start and the end of the sentence.
 Cho …: give me, let me
 cho means "give" or "let", and Cho tôi… is the basic way to order. To someone older than you, such as a stall-holder, call yourself em and end with ạ , which shows respect. Adding xin (ask for) makes it softer still. Choose the self-word by the listener: em to someone a little older, cháu to someone your parents' or grandparents' age. A grandchild thanks a grandmother with Cháu cảm ơn bà ạ .
 EX: Cho em một ly cà phê ạ. | chaw ehm moht. lee kah_ fey ah. | One coffee, please. (to someone a little older)
 EX: Cho em xin một cốc nước ạ. | chaw ehm seen moht. kohk/ nuu-uhk/ ah. | Could I have a glass of water, please? (to someone a little older)
 The thing you order keeps its classifier and describing words in place: number, then cốc or ly (glass) or bát (bowl), then the noun, then what kind: hai cốc trà đá , một bát phở bò .
 Cho em hỏi (let me ask) is the polite way to start a question to a stranger.
 EX: Chị ơi, cho em hỏi. | chee. uhy chaw ehm hawy? | Excuse me, can I ask you something? (to a woman a little older)
