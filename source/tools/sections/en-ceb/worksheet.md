# Worksheet: en-ceb

## LESSON sounds — 1 · Sounds, spelling and stress — deck "Sounds and spelling"
### SECTION 0   (has 4)
Cebuano is written in plain English letters, and most of them sound the way you expect. Two things are never written: which syllable is stressed, and a short stop in the throat called the catch. That is why every Cebuano phrase in this course has a pronunciation line under it. This course has no audio, so keep that line switched on.
 Three vowel sounds, five letters
 English has about a dozen vowel sounds and blurs the unstressed ones (the a in "about"). Cebuano has three, and every one stays full.
 Letter ; Line ; Sounds like ; Example ; 
 a ; ah ; father ; balay ; 
 i, e ; ee, eh ; see ; e is mostly in borrowed words ; tubig, kape ; 
 u, o ; oo, oh ; food ; go with no w at the end ; buntag, maayo ; 
 Native words write u early in the word and o in the last syllable. Online you will see other spellings of the same words; this course always uses one.
 EX: tubig | TOO-beeg | water
CARDS NOW: balay = house [card] || tubig = water [card] || kape = coffee [card] || maayo = good; well [card]

### SECTION 1   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Reading the line
 A syllable in CAPITALS is stressed: balay is bah-LAI. Moving the stress can turn one word into another, so do not stress Cebuano the English way. A word printed all in lowercase, such as dee-lee for dili , has a stress the course has not yet checked in a dictionary: give its syllables equal weight rather than guess.
 EX: balay | bah-LAI | house
CARDS NOW: Dili. = No. [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
The catch
 The catch is the stop in the middle of "uh-oh". Cebuano has it between two vowels: maayo is three syllables, ma-a-yo, not "my-yo". It comes after a hyphen: bag-o (new). And some words end in it; the line writes it as q . Stop the breath sharply and add no k.
 EX: maayo | mah-AH-yoh | good; well
 EX: bata | BAH-tahq | child
CARDS NOW: bag-o = new [card] || bata = child [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 3)
ng and mga
 ng is the sound in "singer", never "finger", and Cebuano can start a word with it. Say "singing", then drop the "si". The plural word mga is said "manga".
 EX: ngalan | NGAH-lahn | name
 EX: mga bata | mah-ngah BAH-tahq | children
CARDS NOW: mga = plural word [card] || ngalan = name [card] || mga bata = children [card]

### SECTION 4   (has 4)
Other letters
 ay sounds like "Thai" and aw like "how"; uy , oy and iw glide the same way (ooy, oy, eew in the line). g is always hard, as in "go"; before ee or eh the line writes gh, so you do not read it as in "gem". ts is said ch and dy is said j. r is a quick tap, like the tt in American "butter". p, t, k have no puff of air: say them as in "spin", not "pin".
 EX: otso | oh-choh | eight
 EX: dyip | jeep | jeepney
CARDS NOW: Ay! = Oh! [card] || Uy! = Hey! (friendly) [card] || otso = eight [card] || dyip = jeepney [card]

### SECTION 5   (has 3)
My: akong
 The deck for this lesson pairs words with akong , "my". Like English "my", it goes before the noun, but it always ends in -ng , which joins it to the noun.
 EX: akong balay | ah-kohng bah-LAI | my house
 EX: akong ngalan | ah-kohng NGAH-lahn | my name
 Most common mistake: reading mga letter by letter. m-ga → manga . A close second: ako balay → akong balay .
CARDS NOW: akong = my (before the noun) [card] || akong balay = my house [card] || akong ngalan = my name

## LESSON greetings — 2 · Greetings and polite words — deck "Greetings and courtesy"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
Cebuanos greet by the time of day, and politeness comes from small words and titles. There is no po : that is Tagalog, and in Cebu it sounds out of place.
 Good morning, good afternoon
 English says "good morning"; Cebuano says Maayong buntag . It is maayo (good) plus -ng , which joins it to the next word. Only the second word changes through the day.
 Greeting ; When ; 
 Maayong buntag. ; morning, until about 11 ; 
 Maayong udto. ; around noon ; 
 Maayong hapon. ; afternoon, until dark ; 
 Maayong gabii. ; evening, after dark ; 
 EX: Maayong buntag. | mah-ah-yohng boon-tahg | Good morning.
 EX: Maayong gabii. | mah-ah-yohng gah-BEE-ee | Good evening.
CARDS NOW: Maayong buntag. = Good morning. [card] || maayo = good; well [card] || Maayong udto. = Good day. (around noon) [card] || Maayong hapon. = Good afternoon. [card] || Maayong gabii. = Good evening. [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 7)
How are you?
 Kumusta comes from Spanish. Add ka , the short word for "you": Kumusta ka? The usual answer is Maayo man , "I'm fine". The small word man makes it sound relaxed. Add ko , the short word for "I", to say it in full. Short words like ka and ko never start a sentence: they follow the first word.
 EX: Kumusta ka? | koo-MOOS-tah kah | How are you?
 EX: Maayo man ko. Salamat. | mah-AH-yoh mahn koh sah-LAH-maht | I'm fine. Thanks.
CARDS NOW: ka = you (one person, short form) [card] || Kumusta ka? = How are you? [card] || Maayo man. = I'm fine. [card] || man = after all; softener [card] || ko = I; me; my (short form) [card] || Maayo man ko. Salamat. = I'm fine. Thanks. || Salamat. = Thank you. [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
Thanks and please
 Salamat is "thank you", and Daghang salamat is "thank you very much" (literally "many thanks"). The reply is Walay sapayan , "you're welcome". Palihug means "please".
 EX: Daghang salamat. | dahg-hahng sah-LAH-maht | Thank you very much.
 EX: Walay sapayan. | wah-lai sah-pah-yahn | You're welcome.
CARDS NOW: Daghang salamat. = Thank you very much. [card] || Walay sapayan. = You're welcome. [card] || Palihug. = Please. [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 3)
Titles instead of "sir" and "ma'am"
 Cebuano uses the same "you" with everyone, older people included. Respect comes from a title. Call an older man, a driver or a guard Nong (short for Manong ), and an older woman or a vendor Nang (short for Manang ). Put the title after your thanks or greeting.
 EX: Salamat, Nang. | sah-LAH-maht nahng | Thank you, ma'am.
 EX: Maayong hapon, Nong. | mah-ah-yohng HAH-pohn nohng | Good afternoon, sir.
CARDS NOW: Nong = sir (to an older man) [card] || Salamat, Nang. = Thank you, ma'am. [card] || Maayong hapon, Nong. = Good afternoon, sir.

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
At the gate, and goodbye
 When you visit a house, call Maayo! at the gate; the host answers Dayon! , "come in". When someone leaves, say Amping , "take care".
 EX: Amping, Nong. | AHM-peeng nohng | Take care, sir.
 Most common mistake: keeping the morning greeting all day. At two in the afternoon, Maayong buntag → Maayong hapon .
CARDS NOW: dayon = right away; then [card] || Amping, Nong. = Take care, sir.

(please forgive me) || Pasensya na. | pah-sehn-syah nah | Sorry about that. || Nang, pangutana lang. | nahng pah-ngoo-tah-nah lahng | Excuse me, ma'am, just a question. || Una na ko. | oo-nah nah koh | I'll go ahead now. || Amping ka. | AHM-peeng kah | Take care.

## LESSON particles — 3 · Small words: man, lang, sab, diay — deck "Short forms and Cebu talk"
### SECTION 0   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Cebuano is full of one-syllable words that English handles with tone of voice: "just", "too", "oh, really?". Without them Cebuano can sound blunt, even cross. This lesson gives you the seven you will hear most. Do not worry about using them perfectly yet: just start noticing them, and add one or two to your own sentences.
 Where they go: second place
 English often puts such words first: "Just one." Cebuano never starts a sentence with them. They go in second place, right after the first word or phrase.
 EX: Usa lang. | oo-SAH lahng | Just one.
CARDS NOW: Usa lang. = Just one.

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
lang and ra: just, only
 lang means "just, only", and it also softens a request or an answer, a bit like "just" in "I just wanted to ask". ra means much the same.
 EX: Gamay ra. | gah-MAI rah | Just a little.
CARDS NOW: lang = just; only [card] || ra = only; just (merely) [card] || Gamay ra. = Just a little. [card]

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
man: softening
 man has no English twin. It softens an answer or a question, often with a feeling of "well" or "after all". You already know Maayo man , "I'm fine". In a question it sounds friendly rather than demanding.
 EX: Unsa man? | oon-sah mahn | What is it?
CARDS NOW: man = after all; softener [card] || Maayo man. = I'm fine. [card] || Unsa man? = What is it? [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 3)
sab: too, also
 sab means "too, also". To return a greeting, add it at the end.
 EX: Maayong buntag sab. | mah-ah-yohng boon-tahg sahb | Good morning to you too.
 In Cebu City you will also hear sad and pod , used the same way. All three are fine; this course writes sab .
CARDS NOW: sab = also; too [card] || Maayong buntag sab. = Good morning to you too. || pod = also; too (everyday) [card]

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
diay: oh, so…
 diay shows that you have just learned something: "oh, so it is", "oh, really". Alone, Diay? means "Really?"
 EX: Maayo diay. | mah-AH-yoh dee-ai | Oh, so it's good.
CARDS NOW: Diay? = Really? [card] || Maayo diay. = Oh, so it's good.

### SECTION 5   (has 4)
ba and gyud
 ba turns a statement into a yes-or-no question; English does it by raising the voice or adding "is it". gyud means "really, truly". You will also see it spelled gyod or jud .
 EX: Maayo ba? | mah-AH-yoh bah | Is it good?
 EX: Lami gyud. | lah-mee gyood | It's really tasty.
 Most common mistake: putting the small word first, as in English. Lang usa. → Usa lang.
CARDS NOW: ba = question particle [card] || gyud = really; truly [card] || Maayo ba? = Is it good? || Lami gyud. = It's really tasty.

(short) || Kinsa to? | keen-SAHQ toh | Who was that? || Unsay balita? | oon-sai bah-lee-tah | What's new? || Naay saging? | nah-ai SAH-gheeng | Any bananas? || Walay problema. | wah-lai proh-bleh-mah | No problem. || Day | dai | miss (to a girl or young woman) || Sige. | SEE-gheh | OK. / Go ahead. || Bitaw. | BEE-tow | That's true. || Hala! | hah-lah | Oh no!

## LESSON this-that — 4 · kini, kana, kadto: this and that — deck "Shopping and prices"
### SECTION 0   (has 6)
English has two pointing words, "this" and "that". Cebuano has three, and the choice depends on where the thing is: near you, near the person you are talking to, or away from you both. Think of a market stall: the mango in your hand, the one the vendor is holding, and the basket at the back.
 Word ; Where the thing is ; English ; 
 kini ; near me ; this ; 
 kana ; near you, the listener ; that (by you) ; 
 kadto ; away from both of us ; that (over there) ; 
 EX: Kini. | kee-NEE | This one.
 EX: Kana. | kah-NAHQ | That one, by you.
 EX: Kadto. | kahd-TOH | That one over there.
 What's this?
 The question word comes first: Unsa kini? , "What is this?". At a market stall that is all you need: point and ask. The answer will usually start with the name of the thing.
 EX: Unsa kini? | oon-sah kee-NEE | What's this?
 EX: Unsa kana? | oon-sah kah-NAHQ | What's that?
CARDS NOW: kini = this [card] || Kini o kana? = This one or that one? [card] || kana = that (near you) [card] || kadto = that (far from us both) [card] || Unsa kini? = What's this? [card] || Unsa kana? = What's that? (near you) [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 3)
Short forms
 After another word, people often shorten the three to ni , na and to . You will hear Unsa ni? at least as often as Unsa kini? . Learn the full forms first; just recognize the short ones.
 EX: Unsa ni? | oon-sah nee | What's this?
CARDS NOW: ni = of; by (before a name) [card] || na = already; now; anymore [card] || Unsa ni? = What's this? (short) [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
This is…: the main word first
 English says "This is a book". Cebuano has no word for "is", and it puts the main word first: Libro kini , literally "book this".
 EX: Libro kini. | lee-broh kee-NEE | This is a book.
 EX: Balay kadto. | bah-LAI kahd-TOH | That's a house over there.
CARDS NOW: Libro kini. = This is a book. || Balay kadto. = That's a house over there.

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Just this one
 Add lang from the last lesson: Kini lang , "Just this one". It is what you say once you have chosen at a stall.
 EX: Kini lang. | kee-NEE lahng | Just this one.
CARDS NOW: lang = just; only [card] || Kini lang. = Just this one. [card]

### SECTION 4   (has 3)
This is my…
 To show something of yours, put kini first and ang before the thing: Kini ang akong balay , "This is my house". Here kini is the main word, and ang akong balay is what the sentence is about. akong stays before the noun.
 EX: Kini ang akong balay. | kee-NEE ahng ah-kohng bah-LAI | This is my house.
CARDS NOW: ang = the (marks the focus) [card] || Kini ang akong balay. = This is my house. || akong = my (before the noun) [card]

### SECTION 5   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
this house
 Right before a noun, the pointing word takes -ng , just like akong : kining balay , "this house". For now you mostly need the words on their own.
 EX: kining balay | kee-neeng bah-LAI | this house
 Most common mistake: one word for every "that". For a thing in the vendor's hand, Unsa kadto? → Unsa kana? Keep kadto for things far from you both.
CARDS NOW: kining balay = this house

## LESSON ako — 5 · I, you, he, she: ako, ikaw, siya and ang / si — deck "About me"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
English says "I am a teacher": the person, then "am", then the main word. Cebuano has no word for "am, is, are", and it turns the order round: Maestro ko , literally "teacher I".
 I, you, he and she
 English ; Full form ; Short form ; 
 I ; ako ; ko ; 
 you (one person) ; ikaw ; ka ; 
 he, she ; siya ; none ; 
 siya means both "he" and "she": Cebuano does not show which. Context tells you, so a sentence about your sister and one about your brother look exactly the same.
CARDS NOW: ko = I; me; my (short form) [card] || ako = I; me (full form) [card] || ikaw = you (one person, full form) [card] || ka = you (one person, short form) [card] || siya = he; she [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
The main word comes first
 Start with what you are saying about the person (a job, a nationality, a describing word), then say who.
 EX: Maestro ko. | mah-ehs-troh koh | I'm a teacher.
 EX: Amerikana siya. | ah-meh-ree-kah-nah see-YAH | She's American.
 EX: Estudyante ka. | ehs-too-jahn-teh kah | You're a student.
 Words for jobs and nationalities from Spanish change for a woman: maestro for a man, maestra for a woman; Amerikano , Amerikana .
CARDS NOW: Maestro ko. = I'm a teacher. || maestro = teacher (man) [card] || Amerikana siya. = She's American. || Estudyante ka. = You're a student.

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 1)
Short or full?
 After the main word you can use either form: Maestro ko and Maestro ako both mean "I'm a teacher"; the short one is more common. But ko and ka can never start a sentence. To start with "I" or "you", use the full form, as you will see next lesson in Ako si Emma .
CARDS NOW: Ako si Emma. = I'm Emma. [card]

### SECTION 3   (has 6)
si before names, ang before other nouns
 The person or thing the sentence is about needs a small marker in front. Names take si . Other nouns take ang , which is close to "the". English has nothing like si , so it is easy to forget.
 EX: Maestra si Ana. | mah-ehs-trah see ah-nah | Ana is a teacher.
 EX: Amerikano si Ben. | ah-meh-ree-kah-noh see behn | Ben is American.
 EX: Estudyante ang bata. | ehs-too-jahn-teh ahng BAH-tahq | The child is a student.
 The word with si or ang is the focus of the sentence, and it comes after the main word, just like ko , ka and siya .
 Most common mistake: English order with a name and no marker. Ana maestra. → Maestra si Ana.
CARDS NOW: si = marks a name as the focus [card] || ang = the (marks the focus) [card] || Maestra si Ana. = Ana is a teacher. || Siya si Ana. = This is Ana. [card] || Amerikano si Ben. = Ben is American. || Estudyante ang bata. = The child is a student.

DECK CARDS IN NO SECTION (add by front, as a string, only where a section teaches them): Pedro ang akong ngalan. | peh-droh ahng ah-kohng NGAH-lahn | My name is Pedro. || Unsay imong ngalan? | oon-sai ee-mohng NGAH-lahn | What's your name? || Taga-Amerika ko. | tah-gah-ah-meh-ree-kah koh | I'm from the US. || Taga-asa ka? | tah-gah-ah-sah kah | Where are you from? || Taga-Cebu ka? | tah-gah-seh-boo kah | Are you from Cebu? || Nagpuyo ko sa Cebu. | nahg-poo-yoh koh sah seh-BOO | I live in Cebu. || Asa ka nagpuyo? | ah-sah kah nahg-poo-yoh | Where do you live? || Nalipay ko nga nakaila nimo. | nah-lee-pai koh ngah nah-kah-ee-lah nee-moh | Nice to meet you. || Traynta anyos ko. | train-tah ahn-yohs koh | I'm thirty. || Pila na ang imong edad? | pee-lah nah ahng ee-mohng eh-dahd | How old are you? || Maestra ko. | mah-ehs-trah koh | I'm a teacher (woman). || Estudyante ko. | ehs-too-jahn-teh koh | I'm a student. || Unsay imong trabaho? | oon-sai ee-mohng trah-BAH-hoh | What's your job? || Nagtrabaho ko sa ospital. | nahg-trah-bah-hoh koh sah ohs-pee-tahl | I work at a hospital. || Minyo na ko. | meen-yoh nah koh | I'm married. || Nagtuon ko og Bisaya. | nahg-too-ohn koh ohg bee-sah-yah | I'm studying Cebuano.

## LESSON intro — 6 · Introducing yourself — deck "About me"
### SECTION 0   (has 7)
Meeting people in Cebu, you will be asked the same few things: your name, where you are from, and often your age. This lesson gives you all of them, in the order people usually ask. Questions about age are common and not meant to be rude, so it helps to have the answer ready.
 My name is…
 English says "I'm Emma". Cebuano says Ako si Emma : the full form ako , then the name with its marker si . The short ko cannot start a sentence, so here it has to be ako .
 EX: Ako si Emma. | ah-KOH see ehm-mah | I'm Emma.
 To ask a name: Unsa ang imong ngalan? , "What is your name?" In speech, unsa ang shrinks to unsay . imong means "your"; like akong , it goes before the noun and ends in -ng.
 EX: Unsay imong ngalan? | oon-sai ee-mohng NGAH-lahn | What's your name?
CARDS NOW: Ako si Emma. = I'm Emma. [card] || ako = I; me (full form) [card] || si = marks a name as the focus [card] || ko = I; me; my (short form) [card] || imong = your (before the noun) [card] || akong = my (before the noun) [card] || Unsay imong ngalan? = What's your name? [card]

### SECTION 1   (has 4)
Where are you from?
 Taga- means "from", and it joins the place with a hyphen: Taga-Amerika ko , "I'm from the US". To ask, join it to asa (where). The place comes first and the short pronoun second.
 EX: Taga-Amerika ko. | tah-gah-ah-meh-ree-kah koh | I'm from the US.
 EX: Taga-asa ka? | tah-gah-ah-sah kah | Where are you from?
 Do not translate "Where are you from?" word for word: Asa ka gikan? means "Where have you just been?"
CARDS NOW: Taga-Amerika ko. = I'm from the US. [card] || asa = where [card] || Taga-asa ka? = Where are you from? [card] || Asa ka gikan? = Where have you just been? [card]

### SECTION 2   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Where I live
 Learn this sentence whole for now; its verb comes in a later lesson: Nagpuyo ko sa Cebu , "I live in Cebu". Swap in your own town after sa .
 EX: Nagpuyo ko sa Cebu. | nahg-poo-yoh koh sah seh-BOO | I live in Cebu.
CARDS NOW: Nagpuyo ko sa Cebu. = I live in Cebu. [card] || sa = at; in; to; of (place and owner marker) [card]

### SECTION 3   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Age
 English "is" thirty; some languages "have" thirty years. Cebuano gives the age in Spanish numbers with anyos (years), main word first: Traynta anyos ko . The numbers come later; for now, learn this one pattern.
 EX: Traynta anyos ko. | train-tah ahn-yohs koh | I'm thirty.
CARDS NOW: anyos = years old [card] || Traynta anyos ko. = I'm thirty. [card]

### SECTION 4   <-- NEEDS CARDS (has 2)
Nice to meet you
 Also learned whole: Nalipay ko nga nakaila nimo , "I'm glad to have met you". A smile and Salamat work too.
 EX: Nalipay ko nga nakaila nimo. | nah-lee-pai koh ngah nah-kah-ee-lah nee-moh | Nice to meet you.

## LESSON mo — 16 · Doer-focus verbs 1: mo- (will) and ni- (did) — deck "Everyday verbs"
### SECTION 0   (has 5)
English verbs change with the person and the time: "I eat", "she eats", "I ate", "I will eat". A Cebuano verb never changes for the person. Instead, a prefix on the root (the bare verb, such as kaon , eat) tells you whether the action has happened yet.
 mo-: not done yet (the future form)
 Put mo- on the root for something that has not happened yet, where English says "will". The same form is used for habits, things you usually do.
 EX: Mokaon ko. | moh-kah-ohn koh | I'll eat.
 EX: Moadto ko sa merkado. | moh-ahd-toh koh sah mehr-kah-doh | I'll go to the market.
 EX: Moinom ko og kape matag buntag. | moh-ee-nohm koh ohg kah-peh mah-tahg boon-tahg | I drink coffee every morning.
CARDS NOW: kaon = eat [card] || mo = you (more than one, short form); by you [card] || Mokaon ko. = I'll eat. [card] || Moadto ko sa merkado. = I'll go to the market. [card] || Moinom ko og kape matag buntag. = I drink coffee every morning.

### SECTION 1   (has 7)
ni- or mi-: done (the past form)
 For something that has happened, change mo- to ni- . You will also see mi- : both are right, and this course writes ni- .
 EX: Nikaon ko. | nee-kah-ohn koh | I ate.
 Time words help you choose: ugma (tomorrow) goes with mo- , and gahapon (yesterday) with ni- .
 EX: Moadto ko ugma. | moh-ahd-toh koh oog-MAHQ | I'll go tomorrow.
 EX: Niadto ko gahapon. | nee-ahd-toh koh gah-hah-pohn | I went yesterday.
CARDS NOW: ni = of; by (before a name) [card] || mi = we (not you, short form) [card] || Nikaon ko. = I ate. || ugma = tomorrow [card] || gahapon = yesterday [card] || Moadto ko ugma. = I'll go tomorrow. || Niadto ko gahapon. = I went yesterday.

### SECTION 2   (has 3)
The doer is the focus
 With these verbs, the person who does the action is the focus of the sentence, the ang word. So it takes the forms you already know: ko, ka, siya ; si before a name; ang before other nouns. The verb comes first, and a short pronoun follows it. With a question word, the question word comes first and the short pronoun moves up behind it.
 EX: Asa ka moadto? | ah-sah kah moh-ahd-toh | Where are you going?
CARDS NOW: ang = the (marks the focus) [card] || si = marks a name as the focus [card] || Asa ka moadto? = Where are you going?

### SECTION 3   (has 3)
The thing: og
 English says "I'll buy water". After a mo- or ni- verb, a thing that is not a specific one ("water", "some bananas", "a coffee") takes og . Do not use ang for it: a later lesson shows the kind of verb Cebuano prefers for "the water".
 EX: Mopalit ko og tubig. | moh-pah-leet koh ohg TOO-beeg | I'll buy water.
 EX: Nipalit ko og saging. | nee-pah-leet koh ohg SAH-gheeng | I bought some bananas.
 Most common mistake: the future form for the past. Mokaon ko gahapon. → Nikaon ko gahapon. If it already happened, use ni- .
CARDS NOW: og = a; some (marks the thing done) [card] || Mopalit ko og tubig. = I'll buy water. [card] || Nipalit ko og saging. = I bought some bananas. [card]

### SECTION 0   (has 4)
Some verbs take mag- instead of mo- . They work the same way: the prefix shows whether the action has happened, and the person doing it is the ang word. Which prefix a root takes is something you learn with each verb, the way English learners learn "went" with "go". Common mag- verbs: magtrabaho (work), magluto (cook), magtuon (study), magdula (play).
 mag-: not done yet
