# pa-en `pron` tool (rules: docs/profiles/pa.md §6). **If the string is already in the course,** look up the exact English text (same punctuation and capitals) in `tools/content/pa/pron_lookup.json` and paste the value verbatim. It covers 766 strings: every zh deck card (the alphabet deck too), `{{ex}}` row and idiom. Never hand-edit a lookup value.
**For a new string,** first write the IPA in zh conventions (`tools/content/zh/PRON-RULES.md`: American values, ASCII g, ˈ on content words, weak forms, ` ‖ ` between sentences, two slashed groups for alternatives, numbers as words). Copy word IPA from zh rows wherever the same words occur (grep `tools/content/zh/`).
Then run `node tools/content/pa/pron_pa.js "/aɪ ˈlaɪk ˈmæŋgoʊz/"`, which prints ਆਈ ਲਾਈਕ ˈਮੈਂਗੋਜ਼. Read the result aloud and compare it with the audio. **Copy the output, never retype it:** it is NFC, dotted letters are base + U+0A3C, ˈ is U+02C8. A phonetic keyboard gives precomposed dotted letters, an apostrophe or an addak, and all of those fail.
**The tool refuses** (exit 1, nothing printed): any symbol outside the §6 tables (ɡ U+0261, ɒ, ɚ, a flap, ɑ / ɔ / ɜ without ː, a or o outside a diphthong, capitals, digits, punctuation, an ASCII colon or apostrophe); text outside the slashes; an empty group or sentence; a dangling or doubled ˈ; ŋ with no vowel straight before it; j or w with no vowel after it; and any output that fails `pronPattern` or holds a banned character. **It warns** (stderr, the row is still printed) for a consonant directly followed by h (*childhood*): the subjoined ha reads as a tone mark, so fix that row by hand with the lead (§6 rule 4).
`node tools/content/pa/pron_pa.js --selftest` checks the 240 example pairs printed in §6 (the 70 worked rows, then the key tables and rules 1–14) and 28 bad inputs. Run it after any change to the converter. `--lookup` rebuilds `pron_lookup.json` from `tools/content/zh/`.
`node tools/content/pa/pron_check.js [all] [strict]` checks the course files: KOREAN (scaffold row not written yet), PATTERN, ENCODING (not NFC, tone letter, addak, precomposed dotted letter, ZWNJ / ZWJ), DIFF (a lookup string with another aid; the lookup wins), CONFLICT (one string, two aids), WORD (a new row that spells a word unlike every lookup row), PROSE (a quoted respelling with ˈ that matches no row).
**ˈ appears only in words of two or more syllables** and ˌ is dropped (rule 2), so a one-syllable row has no mark. Do not add marks. A word zh leaves unstressed stays unmarked (*about* as a preposition: ਅਬਾਊਟ; the citation form is ਅˈਬਾਊਟ).
**Hand case 1, weak or strong forms** come from the IPA you write. Use the strong form when the word is stranded, contrastive or alone (*Where are you from?* = frʌm, *you* = ˈjuː), then convert. *you, me, he, she, we*, *to* before a vowel and *from* look the same both ways (§6 collapses).
**Hand case 2, strings that are not in zh:** 53 English strings of the ko scaffold have no lookup row (the Korea / Seoul / Busan / Minji / kimchi rows that are localised anyway, plus about 25 others such as *Good luck!*, *No, thanks.*, *I like movies.*). `pron_check.js all` lists them. Write IPA from zh word forms and convert.
**Hand case 3, names and places** (rule 13, §9): the lookup still has zh's 23 localised rows (Li Wei, China, Beijing, Shanghai, Chengdu). Do not use them. The pa strings need new IPA from the en-US voice: listen first, then convert. **Starting points, none verified against the en-US voice** (the first-syllable / second-syllable stress and the a-vowels are guesses; only Simran, Punjab, Ludhiana and rupees are printed in §6). ## Decisions
All 70 worked rows and every key and rule example but one come out of the converter byte for byte. Where §6 is silent or two rules meet, the tool takes the reading that keeps one IPA form = one spelling:
1. **W is ˈਡਬਲਿਊ, not the ˈਡਬ੍ਲਿਊ that rules 8 and 11 print.** zh writes W as /ˈdʌbəljuː/, and rule 5 makes ə after a consonant the bare letter. The printed form is what /ˈdʌbljuː/ would give. The selftest reports this row as ERRATUM. It affects two lookup rows (*W*, *BTW*). The lead decides whether to correct §6 or to add a word exception.
2. **Syllabic n before tʃ / dʒ takes the tippi.** Rule 6 names only t and d, and rule 7 lists all four stops. No zh row has the case.
3. **Syllabic l / n** is hi's test: l or n after a consonant other than r, with only consonants up to the word end.
4. **Rule N needs the nasal straight after a vowel (or a syllabic n) and only its own stops.** So n + g and n + k keep letter and halant: *Congratulations* /kənˌgrætʃəˈleɪʃnz/ → ਕਨ੍ਗ੍ਰੈਚˈਲੇਸ਼ਨ੍ਜ਼, as zh writes n there, not ŋ. ŋ after a consonant or at the word start is refused.
5. **Tippi or bindi follows the last character written** (rule 7's two lists): after rule J's vowel letter (… + n + t) it is the bindi.
6. **Rule J applies only when the consonant stands directly before j.** With ˈ between them the consonant takes the halant and j is the letter, as at a word start.
7. **Consonant + ˈ + vowel** is halant, ˈ, vowel letter (rule 3 and rule 4 together; without the halant the consonant would read with the inherent vowel).
8. **ɑ, ɔ and ɜ without ː are refused** (hi accepts them). The §6 table lists only ɑː, ɔː, ɜːr and ɔɪ, and zh has none.
9. **j or w with no vowel after it is refused:** the tables give no spelling for it.
10. **Consonant + h warns and still prints** (rule 4 says "must warn"). No zh row has it.
