DECIDE AND JUSTIFY, section by section:
1. Summary (8 lines).
2. Variant and register: the roadmap's variant (`docs/roadmaps/cs.json` → `variant`). Which form of "you" (or politeness level) the course teaches first and when the other appears; how examples address people; spelling policy where the language has more than one (state ONE house rule and the alternates learners will see); how regional or colloquial forms are handled. The queue asks for formal vy. State one house rule for what learners produce, and what they only recognize. Spoken Czech (obecná čeština) is real and is not the written standard: say once what learners will hear, and do not drill it.
3. Glossary: the English grammar terms the course uses, each once, consistently. Name the cases in English. Do not make learners memorize Latin names as the main label.
4. Error zones of English speakers, ranked, each with a wrong → right pair.
5. Learners: who, why, what they need first.
6. Pronunciation aid (`pron` on every card and example row): what in the spelling misleads English speakers. The queue names ř and vowel length. Decide between a generated English-letter respelling, IPA, or none; build `respell.js` if it is reliable enough and say how reliable, with a list of the words it gets wrong (6.5). Give the full key, the exact format, a `pronPattern` regex (printed by `pronPattern()`), and 20 worked examples that pass it. Say what the first lesson must teach so the aid can be read. ř has to be audible in the aid if you ship one. Long and short vowels have to look different (`byt` / `být`). Say whether stress is marked. Czech stress is usually on the first syllable.
7. Standing practice rule: the ONE or two things every practice set must keep re-testing because English speakers get them wrong everywhere. Give English keywords (a regex alternation) that a `why` line uses when an item tests it, tested against a table of sample `why` lines that should and should not match. Seven cases cannot all be the standing rule. Pick the one or two that show up in almost every sentence after the early lessons.
8. Lessons: FINAL ordered list. Start from the roadmap's items (keep their ids without the `cs-` prefix as lesson ids; the `profile.json` roadmap field is the FULL item id, `cs-alphabet`, not `alphabet`). Decide how many cases reach this course, and say which cases are named once and not drilled.
9. Decks: FINAL list of 16–18 decks in order, English deck names, about 16–18 cards each, in 4–5 groups (English group names), each with a one-line description and the lesson(s) that link to it. Include a "Traps for English speakers" deck (false friends, calques) and a "Compliments" deck (kind things to say about food, work, skills and people, and how to answer a compliment). Give 5 sample cards per deck so writers know the level and tone. Localisation: names, cities, food and money used in examples. Koruna, not a dollar sign.
10. Typing and script: the letters beyond plain a–z and how they are typed (`keys` for the accent bar), whether a missing accent counts as "almost" (`accents`), apostrophes and hyphens, capitalisation, punctuation, what a typed answer must and need not include, and the `targetScript` regex. Check what the app already supports (`docs/MULTILANG-SPEC.md`). Vowel length changes meaning. If `accents: true` scores a missing length mark as "almost", set `accents` false and say which pairs need `!strict`. Do not edit the app. Check the fold for á, é, í, ó, ú, ů, ý, and for č, ď, ě, ň, ř, š, ť, ž.
