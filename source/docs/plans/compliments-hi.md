# Hindi — compliments plan
**How compliments work here:** Indians compliment food, cooking, effort and children readily; hosts expect guests to praise the meal (and to accept a second helping). Praise of children is often softened ("may no evil eye fall on them"). The normal answer is to play it down — "it's nothing", "I only know a little", "that's your generosity" — rather than a plain "thank you", which can sound a little cool. Foreigners get told their Hindi is excellent after two words; the expected reply is modest.
**Who you are talking to:** polite आप (āp) throughout Deck 1, as the course does everywhere. Hindi verbs and adjectives change with the listener's and the speaker's gender, so each card carries a cue "(to a woman)" / "(a man speaking)" and the note gives the other form, exactly as the existing decks do.

**How forward is this deck:** mild to warm, with three bold cards for people already dating (14–16). Left out on purpose: eyes, hair and body comments, filmi pick-up lines, and anything said to strangers in public, where it reads as harassment ("eve-teasing").

## Left out, and why
- तुम मेरी टाइप की हो ("you're my type"): a word-for-word copy; card 15 is what people really say.
- नज़र न लगे ("touch wood, no evil eye"): parents say it about children; good, but needs a cultural note. It could be added as a 24th card.
- पेट भर गया, पर मन नहीं भरा ("I'm full, but I want more"): charming, but I'm not sure how common it is.
- Film-style lines (चाँद सा चेहरा "a face like the moon"): sound like song lyrics or a joke.
- तू forms: too intimate, or rude.
