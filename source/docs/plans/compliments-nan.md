# Taiwanese Hokkien (Taigi) — compliments plan
**How compliments work here:** Taiwanese people compliment warmly and often, above all food and cooking, cleverness and ability, children (古錐 "cute"), and anyone who speaks Taigi: a foreigner who tries gets big smiles and lots of praise. The usual answer is a modest brush-off ("no, no", "I still have to learn more"), much as in Mandarin. Older people especially expect that, though a warm "thank you" is fine too.
**Who you are talking to:** 你 (lí) is the only "you". There is no polite pronoun and no gender endings. Politeness comes from tone of voice and the reply you choose.

**Important for this course:** the course rule is that no Taigi word may come from a writer's memory. Each word must already be in the course word list or be checked in the Ministry of Education dictionary first. Each note starts with **[list]** (every word is already in the course word list; the Tâi-lô shown is the course tool's output) or **[MOE: word]** (that word still has to be looked up; the Tâi-lô shown is provisional). Characters are kept to ones already in the course or ordinary Traditional characters, with no rare Taigi-only ones.

**How forward is this deck:** gentle dating talk up to "I like you". Left out on purpose: "I love you" (heavy, and its usual Taigi form is not in the course sources), pickup lines, anything about bodies, and nicknames.

## Left out, and why
- Mandarin words used inside Taigi (厲害, 帥, 可愛): many speakers mix them in, but the course teaches only Taigi words.
- "Great idea": no Taigi phrase in the course sources. Dropped rather than translated from Mandarin.
- "Your pronunciation is good" and "nice photo": the words are not verified.
- "Beautiful eyes": the usual word for "eye" uses a rare character, which the course avoids.
- Words written with rare Taigi-only characters, even common ones.
