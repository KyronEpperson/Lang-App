# Arabic — compliments plan
**How compliments work here:** Arabs are generous with praise: food and hospitality above all (a guest praises the meal and the cook), effort, children and a nice home. Praise of a child or anything precious comes with a God-phrase (مَا شَاءَ اللّٰه, already a card in the course, or "God protect them") against the evil eye. Answers are modest or return the blessing: the cook answers "enjoy it, to your health", and a learner answers "I'm still learning". Every learner hears "your Arabic is excellent" after a few words.
**Who you are talking to:** the course teaches Modern Standard Arabic (MSA) with full vowel marks. "You" and its endings change for a man or a woman, so each card says "(to a man)" or "(to a woman)" and the note gives the other form, as the course already does. One honest caveat: people chat and flirt in their dialect, so MSA compliments are understood everywhere but sound a little careful. The notes say so where it matters.

**How forward is this deck:** mild to warm; three bold cards (13–15). In much of the Arab world open flirting with strangers is frowned on, so the cards suit people already getting to know each other. Left out on purpose: comments on eyes, hair or body, poetic lines, and anything for a stranger in public.

## Left out, and why
- مَا شَاءَ اللّٰه: already a card in the course (Traps deck).
- Dialect favourites (Egyptian *ʾamar* "you're a moon", Levantine *ḥilw/ḥilwa*, *taʾburnī*): very common, but the course keeps dialect words out of cards. They could go into notes, for recognition.
- يَا قَمَر / يَا حَبِيبِي ("moon", "my love"): too intimate or too poetic for a learner.
- صِحَّة وَعَافِيَة: often said around meals, but closer to "bon appétit" than a compliment.
