# Punjabi — compliments plan
**How compliments work here:** Punjabis praise generously and loudly. Food matters most: guests praise the meal, and the host keeps pressing more food on them. People also praise effort, children, a nicely tied turban or a new suit, and a foreigner's Punjabi (even a few words get a big reaction). The usual answer is a modest one: thank them and shrug it off ("it's just home cooking", "I'm still learning"), or give the credit to God ("it's God's grace"). Accepting with a plain "thank you" is fine too. Foreigners' usual mistakes: saying "well done" (ਸ਼ਾਬਾਸ਼) to an older person, which sounds like talking down to them, and praising a baby too much without the customary blessing, which some older people worry draws the evil eye.
**Who you are talking to:** the polite ਤੁਸੀਂ ("you") with plural verbs throughout, as the course does. Punjabi verbs show the speaker's gender: "I'm still learning" is ਸਿੱਖ ਰਿਹਾ for a man and ਸਿੱਖ ਰਹੀ for a woman. Cards in the "I" form are written for a man speaking, with the woman's form in the note. Cards that say "you" (ਤੁਸੀਂ … ਦੇ ਹੋ) use the plural, which is fine for a man or a woman. Where a separate to-a-woman form is also heard (ਬਣਾਉਂਦੀਆਂ ਹੋ, ਲੱਗ ਰਹੀਆਂ ਹੋ), the note gives it.

**How forward is this deck:** mild to warm, polite ਤੁਸੀਂ except card 16. Left out: lines from Punjabi pop songs, ਤੂੰ-only flirting with strangers, and anything about the body beyond eyes and smile. In India, open flirting with a stranger can be badly received, especially by a man toward a woman in public. Notes will say this deck is for people who already know each other or are on a date.

## Left out, and why
- ਮਜ਼ਾ ਆ ਗਿਆ ("that was fun / great"): it's common, but it is shared with Hindi. ਸੁਆਦ ਆ ਗਿਆ is the more Punjabi way to say it.
- ਬੱਲੇ ਬੱਲੇ / ਚੱਕ ਦੇ: cheers and slang, not really compliments.
- ਸੋਹਣੀ ਕੁੜੀ / ਗੱਭਰੂ jawan-style song words ("pretty girl", "strapping lad"): they sound like catcalling when said to a stranger.
- ਤੁਹਾਡੀ ਪੰਜਾਬੀ ਬਹੁਤ ਵਧੀਆ ਹੈ ("your Punjabi is great"): it already appears in a course dialogue, so #18 says it a different way.

## Open questions for Kii
1. Card 23 (ਰੱਬ ਦੀ ਮਿਹਰ ਹੈ, "it's God's grace") is a religious reply. It is very common, but keep it or swap it for a non-religious one?
2. Flirty card 16 uses the intimate ਤੂੰ ("you"). Keep one ਤੂੰ card, or keep the whole deck on the polite ਤੁਸੀਂ?
3. Cards in the "I" form (#21, flirty #14) are planned for a man speaking, with the woman's form in the note. Would you rather have the woman's form first, or both as separate cards?

## Needs a native check first
- #4 ਤੁਹਾਡੇ ਹੱਥਾਂ ਵਿੱਚ ਸੁਆਦ ਹੈ and #12 ਤੁਹਾਡਾ ਦਿਲ ਬਹੁਤ ਵੱਡਾ ਹੈ: are these the common forms of the idioms?
- #8 ਸਲਾਹ for "idea", and #13 ਜਚਦਾ with ਨੂੰ (versus ਤੁਹਾਡੇ ਉੱਤੇ).
- Flirty #8 ਸਮੇਂ ਦਾ ਪਤਾ ਹੀ ਨਹੀਂ ਲੱਗਦਾ and flirty #23 ਆਪਾਂ ਦੋਸਤ ਹੀ ਚੰਗੇ ਹਾਂ: do they sound natural?
- Pron: the course's pron tool gives ਮਿਹਨਤੀ as *míhantī*. It should probably be *míhnatī*, which needs a lexicon entry before the deck is built.
