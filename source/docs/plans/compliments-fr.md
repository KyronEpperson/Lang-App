# French — compliments plan
**How compliments work here:** French people compliment less freely than Americans, and big praise ("amazing!") can sound insincere. Praise is usually short and specific: the food, the wine, someone's taste, a nice home. Answering with a plain "merci" is fine; a light doubt ("Tu trouves?" = "You think so?") or a small modest line is very common. Locals often praise a foreigner's French once you try. Common mistakes: using *tu* with a stranger, and over-praising.
**Who you are talking to:** *tu* (friends, peers), as the course teaches first; notes give the *vous* form. The learner cards use *vous*, because strangers will say them to you. Adjectives that change for a woman (gentil → gentille) show both, with a note on whether they sound different.

**How forward is this deck:** first-date level: warm, clear, nothing physical beyond eyes and smile. Left out on purpose: body comments, pick-up lines and slang terms of endearment.

## Left out, and why
- *Je t'aime*: means "I love you", far too strong here.
- *Canon, bombe* (= hot): slang about looks, can sound crude.
- *Ma belle, chérie* to a stranger: overfamiliar, can feel like street harassment.
- *Tu es trop drôle* (trop = "so"): very common but informal; kept the neutral *tellement*.
