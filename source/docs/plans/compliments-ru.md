# Russian — compliments plan
**How compliments work here:** Russians compliment less casually than Americans: a "great job!" for small things can sound insincere, so praise is saved for things that matter — the host's cooking (eat well and ask for more), a cozy home, real skill, and a woman looking good on a special day. The normal answer is "thank you" (*Спасибо, мне очень приятно*, Spasibo, mne ochen priyatno) or a modest "Oh, not at all!" (*Ну что вы!*, Nu chto vy). Foreigners get wrong: *ты* (ty, informal you) vs *вы* (vy, polite you) (*ты* to a stranger or an older person is rude), and the endings that change with the listener's gender (*умный* umny to a man, *умная* umnaya to a woman).
**Who you are talking to:** Deck 1 uses *вы* (vy, polite) with hosts, older people and colleagues and *ты* (ty) with friends, as the course teaches; each note gives the other form and the feminine ending. Deck 2 uses *ты* (ty), as two adults use once they are on friendly terms (asking for a number still uses *вы*, vy). The Latin letters in brackets are a letter-for-letter spelling, not the pronunciation; the course's pronunciation tool will give the real sound and stress.

**How forward is this deck:** mild to warm; Russian dating still expects the man to make the first move and flowers on a first date, but the lines stay respectful; nothing about bodies, no *Я тебя люблю* (Ya tebya lyublyu, I love you).

## Left out, and why
- *Я тебя люблю* (Ya tebya lyublyu, I love you): far too strong for this stage.
- *Я скучаю по тебе* (Ya skuchayu po tebe, I miss you): already a card in the course.
- Diminutive pet names (*зайка, солнышко*: zayka, solnyshko, "bunny, sunshine"): for couples, sound odd from a new acquaintance.
- *Девушка, можно познакомиться?* (Devushka, mozhno poznakomitsya?, "Miss, may I meet you?") (street pick-up opener): common but can feel pushy from a stranger.
