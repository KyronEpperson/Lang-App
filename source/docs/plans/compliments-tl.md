# Tagalog — compliments plan
**How compliments work here:** Filipinos compliment often and warmly: food (a guest should praise the cooking and accept seconds), children, looks ("blooming!") and skill. The usual answer is modest — "Hindi naman" (not really) with a laugh, then thanks — or returning the compliment. Locals are delighted by any Tagalog from a foreigner and say so loudly. Foreigners get two things wrong: forgetting *po* with older people and strangers, and repeating the very direct remarks Filipinos make to each other about weight ("Tumaba ka!" you got fat) — normal among Filipinos, rude from an outsider.
**Who you are talking to:** as the course teaches: *po* + *kayo/ninyo* to older people, hosts and strangers; plain *ikaw/ka/mo* to friends. Each note gives the other form. Tagalog words do not change for gender (except *maganda* for women, *gwapo* for men, and the Spanish-style *bolero/bolera*). Deck 2 drops *po*: two adults getting to know each other.

**How forward is this deck:** mild to warm; Filipino flirting is playful and teasing (*bolero*, *crush*), so the deck uses that tone; nothing about bodies, and *Mahal kita* (I love you, already a card in the course) is not repeated.

## Left out, and why
- *Ang sexy mo*, body comments: crude.
- *Mahal kita* (I love you): already in the course, and too strong here.
- Corny pick-up lines ("banat" jokes like "Google ka ba?"): fun but jokes, not real flirting.
- *Tumaba / Pumayat ka* (you got fat / thin): common among Filipinos, risky from a foreigner.
