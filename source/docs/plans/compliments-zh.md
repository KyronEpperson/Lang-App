# Mandarin Chinese — compliments plan
**How compliments work here:** People compliment often, especially food, a host's cooking, effort and hard work, children, and a foreigner's Chinese (even a single 你好 gets "your Chinese is so good!"). The traditional answer is to brush praise off modestly: 哪里哪里 ("where? where?"), 过奖了 ("you praise me too much"). Younger city people now often just say 谢谢. A foreigner's usual mistake is to accept praise with a flat "yes, I know", or to praise looks too directly in a friendly setting.
**Who you are talking to:** 你 (plain "you") throughout, as the course does for friends and people your age. Notes give the polite 您 version where you would praise an older host or a teacher. Chinese has no gender endings, so every phrase works for anyone.

**How forward is this deck:** warm, polite dating talk up to "I like you". Left out on purpose: "I love you" (我爱你, far too heavy early on), pet names, body compliments and pickup lines.

## Left out, and why
- 美女 / 帅哥 ("beautiful / handsome" as a form of address): service-sector slang, and the course profile avoids it.
- 你真好笑: can mean "you're ridiculous".
- 你好可爱 to an adult: can sound like talking to a child. Kept only for children (card 16).
- 你的中文比我还好 ("your Chinese is better than mine"): a joke, easily misread.
- Internet slang (yyds, 绝绝子): goes out of date fast.

## Open questions for Kii
1. Teach 哪里哪里 as the main reply (the traditional custom, which the brief asks for) or 谢谢夸奖 (what many young people say now)? The plan teaches both, with the custom explained.
2. Flirty deck: WeChat is mainland-specific. Keep it (it is the course's variety) or use a neutral "can I have your number?"

## Needs a native check first
- Deck 1 #1 真香！: it is also an internet meme for eating your own words. Does it still read as plain "smells great"?
- Deck 1 #13 你今天真精神！ as a looks compliment for women as well as men.
- Deck 2 #14 我一直在想你: is it too intense for an early date?
- Deck 2 #22: is this no too blunt? The softer alternative is 不好意思，我不太方便.
