# Indonesian — compliments plan
**How compliments work here:** Indonesians compliment freely and warmly, above all food (a guest should praise the cooking), children, the home and effort. The expected answer is modest: people play praise down ("Ah, it's nothing special") rather than simply say "thank you", and a foreigner who just accepts can sound a little proud. Locals praise a foreigner's Indonesian after only a few words; the normal reply is "Not fluent yet, I'm still learning". Common mistakes: using *Anda* ("you") with a host (cold and formal; use *Ibu* / *Bapak*), and remarks on weight, which Indonesians make to each other but a foreigner should not.
**Who you are talking to:** Deck 1 uses *Ibu* / *Bapak* (ma'am / sir) for hosts and older people and *kamu* for friends, as the course teaches; each note gives the other form. Indonesian words do not change for gender, except *cantik* (women) / *ganteng* (men). Deck 2 switches to *aku* / *kamu*, what two adults getting to know each other use.

**How forward is this deck:** mild to warm; Indonesia is fairly conservative, so the deck stays on smiles, talk, coffee and "I like you" — nothing about bodies, touching, or religion-sensitive topics.

## Left out, and why
- *Kamu seksi*, body comments: crude in Indonesia.
- *Aku cinta kamu* / *Aku sayang kamu*: "I love you" is far too strong for this stage.
- Jakarta slang *gue / lo*, gombal pick-up lines ("Bapak kamu pencuri, ya?"): jokes, not real flirting.
- Remarks on weight (*Kamu gemukan*): common between Indonesians, risky from a foreigner.
