# Korean — compliments plan
**How compliments work here:** Koreans compliment often. They praise food, effort (수고하셨어요, "thanks for your hard work", is said every day), skin and looking young, children, and a foreigner's Korean, which gets praised after one sentence. The expected answer is modest: 아니에요, 아직 멀었어요 ("oh no, I still have a long way to go") or 과찬이세요 ("you're praising me too much"). A warm "thank you for the compliment" is more and more common, especially among younger people. Foreigners' usual mistakes: answering "yes, I'm good at it", and praising a senior's skill as if grading them. "You did well" (잘했어요) is said by a senior to a junior, not the other way.
**Who you are talking to:** the polite 해요 style throughout, as the existing decks do, with the respectful -시- ending (좋으시네요, 하시네요) for compliments about the other person. Korean has no gender forms. The romanisation follows the existing decks: Revised Romanization, spelled as it sounds (맛있네요 = masinneyo).

**Already in the course (not repeated here):** Sweet talk, Cute things to say and Flirting cover dating (pretty, handsome, cute, I like you, I miss you, asking for a number or a date). The existing `compliment` set covers 멋져요, 최고예요, 대단해요, 잘했어요, 센스 있어요, 친절하시네요, 똑똑해요, 재미있어요, 웃는 게 예뻐요, 목소리 좋아요, 잘 어울려요, 멋있어요, 성격 좋아요, 한국어 잘하시네요, 열심히 하시네요, 생각이 깊어요 and others. Elsewhere the course also has 이거 진짜 맛있어요, 잘 먹었습니다, 수고하셨습니다, 고생하셨어요, 아니에요, 별말씀을요, 언니 옷이 예뻐요 and 덕분에요. None of these are reused.

## Left out, and why
- 한국어 잘하시네요 and 잘 어울려요: already in the course.
- 얼굴이 작으시네요 ("your face is so small"): a real Korean compliment, but it confuses English speakers and needs a long note. Can be added if Kii wants a "surprising compliments" card.
- 살 빠지셨어요? ("did you lose weight?"): common in Korea, but it touches on weight, which many learners would find rude.
- 대박 / 쩐다: slang, and 대박 is already in the course.
