# Latin American Spanish — compliments plan
**How compliments work here:** People compliment often and warmly, especially food, the home and children. A guest is expected to praise the meal, and saying nothing can seem cold. The usual answer is a warm thank-you ("Gracias, qué amable"), often with a little modesty ("No es para tanto"), or returning the compliment. Locals praise a foreigner's Spanish generously, even after a few words; the normal reply is thanks plus "I'm still learning". A common mistake is saying *me gustas* ("I like you", romantic) when you mean *me caes bien* ("I like you" as a person): see deck 2.
**Who you are talking to:** *tú* (friends, peers, a host your age), as the course teaches first; each note gives the *usted* form for older people and strangers. Adjectives that change for a woman (chistoso → chistosa) show both forms in the note.

**How forward is this deck:** like a first date: warm and clear, nothing physical beyond eyes and smile. Left out on purpose: street catcalls (*piropos*), body comments and anything with a double meaning.

## Left out, and why
- *Piropos* (street compliments to strangers): many women find them harassment.
- *Está padre / chido* (Mexican "cool"): slang, and it describes things, not people.
- *Te quiero / te amo*: too strong for this deck (they mean "I love you").
- *Mamacita, mi reina*: crude or overfamiliar in many places.
- *Eres muy bueno para esto*: real, but its meaning is less clear than *¡Qué bien lo haces!*

## Open questions for Kii

2. Keep the Mexican slang card *Eres muy buena onda* (very common in Mexico, less so elsewhere)?
3. Deck 2 assumes a man speaking to a woman in the looks cards and a woman speaking in the "no" cards (the other forms are in the notes). Keep that, or flip some cards?
