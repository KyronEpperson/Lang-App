# Italian — compliments plan
**How compliments work here:** Italians compliment freely and with feeling, especially food (the cook is always praised), style and clothes, the home and children. "Complimenti!" is used for almost any achievement, even a good meal. People usually accept with a warm thanks ("Grazie, troppo gentile!") or a playful brush-off ("Ma dai!"); returning the compliment is common. Your Italian will be praised after very little. A common mistake is ignoring the food, or using *tu* with an older stranger.
**Who you are talking to:** *tu* (friends, peers), as the course teaches first; notes give the *Lei* form. The learner card uses *Lei*, because strangers will say it to you. Words that change for a woman (bravissimo → bravissima) show both.

**How forward is this deck:** first-date level: warm and clear, nothing physical beyond eyes and smile. Left out on purpose: body comments, catcalls and pet names for strangers.

## Left out, and why
- *Ti amo / Ti voglio bene*: "I love you" (romantic / family); too strong here.
- *Bella!* / *Ciao bella* to a stranger: reads as a catcall.
- *Sei figo / figa* (= hot, cool): slang, and *figa* is vulgar in many regions.
- *Che bono!* (= hot guy, Roman slang): regional and easy to get wrong.
