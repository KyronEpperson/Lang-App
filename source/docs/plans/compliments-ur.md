# Urdu — compliments plan
**How compliments work here:** Pakistanis praise food, hospitality, effort and children warmly, and guests are expected to praise the meal. Praise of a child, a new house or anything precious is usually wrapped in ماشاء اللہ ("God has willed it"), which also wards off the evil eye; praising a child without it can make parents uneasy. Replies are modest and flowery: "that's just your kindness", "I only know a little". Foreigners who say two words of Urdu get lavish praise; a modest reply is expected.
**Who you are talking to:** polite آپ (āp) throughout Deck 1, as the course does everywhere. Verbs and adjectives change with the listener's and the speaker's gender, so each card carries a cue "(to a woman)" / "(a man speaking)" and the note gives the other form, as the existing decks do.

**How forward is this deck:** mostly mild and indirect. In Pakistan open flirting is uncommon and can be offensive in public or with someone's family present, so most cards praise someone's company or words rather than their looks, and the notes say so. Bold cards (14–16) are for people already seeing each other. Left out on purpose: looks and body comments to strangers, poetry-style lines, and anything said in front of family.

## Left out, and why
- شعر (couplets) and filmi lines: much loved, but they sound like a performance or a joke in a beginner's mouth.
- جان / جانو ("darling"): too intimate for a learner's deck.
- لذیذ ("delicious"): correct but bookish in speech; card 1 is what people say.
- جزاک اللہ ("may God reward you") as a thank-you: common, but religious. It could be added to Deck 1 with a note if Kii wants it.
