# Japanese — compliments plan
**How compliments work here:** Japanese people compliment a lot, especially food, effort (お疲れさま "thanks for your hard work"), skill, children, pets and a foreigner's Japanese. A foreigner hears "your Japanese is so good!" after saying very little. The expected answer is to deflect, not accept: いえいえ、まだまだです ("oh no, I still have a long way to go") or そんなことないです ("that's not true"). Saying a plain "thank you" is getting more common, but a modest reply is still the safe default. Foreigners' usual mistakes: accepting praise with "yes, I'm good at it", and praising a superior's skill. Saying "good job" to your boss sounds like you are grading them.
**Who you are talking to:** the polite です/ます style throughout, as the course does, which is right for new friends, hosts and colleagues. Targets are written in kana, as in the rest of the course. Polite Japanese has no gender endings. The few phrases only one gender uses (きれい for a woman, かっこいい for a man) are marked.

**How forward is this deck:** polite, gentle dating talk up to すきです. Left out on purpose: あいしてる ("I love you", very heavy and rarely said), casual-speech pickup lines, anything about bodies.

## Left out, and why
- よく できました ("well done"): said by teachers to children. Condescending to an adult.
- がんばりましたね ("you tried hard"): praises from above, so it can sound patronizing.
- あたまが いいですね ("you're smart"): fine, but can sound like grading someone. すごい / さすが cover it.
- かわいい to an adult: it can mean "cute" in a childish way. Kept for kids and pets only.
- まじめですね ("so serious and diligent"): can hint at "boring".
