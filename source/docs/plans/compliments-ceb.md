# Cebuano — compliments plan
**How compliments work here:** Cebuanos compliment easily and with humor: food first (*Lami kaayo!*), then children, looks (*gwapa*, *gwapo* are said freely, even to strangers in a friendly way) and skill. The usual answer is a laughing, modest denial ("Not that much"), a pleased "Really? Thanks!", or returning it ("You too!"). People are thrilled when a foreigner speaks Bisaya instead of Tagalog or English, and say so. Foreigners get wrong: using Tagalog (*po*, *ang galing*) — in Cebu it lands badly — and copying the blunt weight remarks locals make to each other.
**Who you are talking to:** Cebuano has no *po* and one "you" (*ka / ikaw / nimo*) for everyone; respect comes from a title (*Nong*, *Nang*) and a warm tone, as the course teaches. Words do not change for gender except *gwapa* (woman) / *gwapo* (man) and *bolera / bolero*. Note: this course has no audio, so the pronunciation line carries the sound.

**How forward is this deck:** mild to warm, with the teasing humor Cebuanos use (*bolero*, *crush*); nothing about bodies; no "I love you" (*Gihigugma tika*), which is far too strong here.

## Left out, and why
- Street slang and reversed words (*lodi*, *petmalu*-style): slangy and short-lived.
- *Gihigugma tika* (I love you): too strong for dating talk.
- Body comments, *sexy*: crude.
- Tagalog lines (*Ang ganda mo*): many people in Cebu dislike Tagalog used as if it were Cebuano.
