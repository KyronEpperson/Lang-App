# Cantonese (Hong Kong) — compliments plan
**How compliments work here:** Hong Kong people compliment easily and casually, most often about food, cleverness and skill (叻 "clever, capable" is the big word), kids, clothes and a foreigner's Cantonese. The polite reply is a light deflection: 邊有呀 ("no way!") or 過獎喇 ("you flatter me"). Accepting with 多謝 is also normal, more so than in Mandarin-speaking places. Foreigners often thank with 唔該, but praise is answered with 多謝 (thanks for kind words), never 唔該 (thanks for a service).
**Romanisation:** the Jyutping shown is the course tool's output where the tool knows every word. Where it does not, the Jyutping is my own and gets checked first.
**Who you are talking to:** 你 is the only "you" in Cantonese. Politeness comes from friendly ending sounds (呀, 喎) and the reply you choose, not from a polite pronoun. No gender endings. Where a word differs for a man or a woman (靚仔 / 靚女), both are given.

**How forward is this deck:** friendly dating talk up to "I like you" and "I miss you". Left out on purpose: 我愛你 (too heavy early), slang pickup lines, anything about bodies.

## Left out, and why
- English code-mixing (你好 cute, 好 nice): very common in Hong Kong, but the course allows no Latin letters in Cantonese strings. It could go in a note.
- 好型 / 有型 as a card: kept as a note only, since "stylish" overlaps with 好靚.
- 冇啦冇啦 as a reply: common, but its meaning is fuzzy without context.
- 靚女 / 靚仔 used to call strangers ("hey, pretty girl"): shop-talk, not a compliment.
