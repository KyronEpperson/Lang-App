# Vietnamese — compliments plan
**How compliments work here:** Vietnamese compliment food, hard work, children's good behavior and looking young most of all; a guest is expected to praise the meal. The normal answer is modest: deny it a little ("Not at all, I still have a lot to learn") or say "you praise me too much", and thank the person with the right kinship word. Locals praise a foreigner's Vietnamese warmly after a few words. Foreigners get two things wrong: the pronoun (every sentence says who is talking to whom: *em* to an older *anh/chị*, *cháu* to someone your parents' age), and over-praising a baby — some older people avoid praising babies directly, out of old superstition.
**Who you are talking to:** Hanoi Vietnamese, as in the course. Each card is written for one pair (learner as *em* to a slightly older *anh* (man) or *chị* (woman); guest as *cháu* to a host *cô* (woman, parents' age)); the note names the pair and gives the other forms. Deck 2 uses *anh* (the man) and *em* (the woman), the normal pair for a couple whatever their exact ages; each note says who is speaking to whom, and a woman speaking to a man swaps them (she says *em* for "I" and *anh* for "you").

**How forward is this deck:** mild to warm; dating talk in Vietnam is fairly indirect, so the deck leans on charm, coffee and "I like you"; no body comments, no "I love you".

## Left out, and why
- *Anh yêu em* (I love you): too strong for early dating.
- Southern words (*dễ thương ghê, xạo, nha*): the course is Hanoi; can go in notes.
- *Em gầy/béo* (you're thin/fat): Vietnamese comment on weight freely; a foreigner should not.
- Pick-up line jokes ("thả thính" one-liners): slang, short-lived.
