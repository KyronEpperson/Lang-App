# German — compliments plan
**How compliments work here:** Germans compliment less often than Americans and value praise that is specific and sincere; gushing ("amazing!") can sound fake. Food, a nice apartment, reliability and good work are typical things to praise ("Auf dich kann man sich verlassen" is high praise). A simple "Danke" or "Danke, das freut mich" is the normal reply; Germans rarely brush praise off, though a light "Findest du?" or, about your German, "Ich lerne noch" is fine. Strangers praise a learner's German, but less lavishly than in southern Europe. A common mistake is using *du* with a stranger or an older host.
**Who you are talking to:** *du* (friends, peers, a host your age); notes give the *Sie* form. The learner cards use *Sie*, because strangers will say them to you. German adjectives here do not change for men and women; nouns like *Freund / Freundin* do.

**How forward is this deck:** first-date level, with one bolder card (*verknallt*). Left out on purpose: body comments, slang like *heiß / sexy*, and pick-up lines.

## Left out, and why
- *Ich liebe dich* / *Ich hab dich lieb*: "I love you"; too strong.
- *Du bist heiß*: means "you're hot" (sexual); and the course already warns that *Ich bin heiß* means something else.
- *Das steht dir gut*: too close to the existing card *Das steht Ihnen gut*.
- Austrian and Swiss forms (*fesch, herzig*): the course is German of Germany.
