# English speakers learning Kannada: course profile

Course `en-kn`: Kannada for English speakers. Every explanation, prompt, `why`, `hint`, meaning and card note is in English. Answers, examples and card targets are in Kannada, written in Kannada script. Machine-readable twin: `tools/target/kn/profile.json`, written from the same data as §8 and §9, so the two files agree. Pronunciation generator: `tools/target/kn/respell.js` (§6). The course folder has a `HOLD` file until the lead releases it.

Models used: the finished Telugu profile (`docs/profiles/en-te.md`, whole), `tools/target/te/profile.json` and `tools/target/te/respell.js` for structure only (no Telugu fact is carried over: Kannada has no person endings on nouns, no masculine / non-masculine split, no inclusive / exclusive "we" taught at A1, no past / negative length twin and no ts / dz); the Hindi generator's key (shared by en-hi, en-pa and en-te). Planned lesson list: `docs/roadmaps/kn.json` (the `kn` block in `packs/roadmaps.js` carries the same variant and basis); its 27 ids are all kept; three moves are explained in §8.

**Evidence tags.**
- **[research-backed]**: an empirical study or a data source opened for this profile, named where it is used.
- **[curriculum convention]**: what syllabuses, textbooks, grammars, Unicode data and vendor documentation do or say.
- **[practitioner opinion]**: a teacher's, school's, blog's or guide's stated view, with no data behind it.
- **[inference]**: this profile's own reasoning, **including the author's own knowledge of Kannada**. Every claim of that kind that writers will copy into the course is in the native-check list (§11).

Untagged rules in §6 to §10 are design decisions, not findings. Every Kannada string that carries a pron in this file was generated by `respell.js` when the file was rendered (render script in the author's scratch folder); every other Kannada string was run through it in a final sweep (counts at the end of §11).

## 1. Summary

- **Which Kannada?** The everyday spoken standard of educated speakers in Bengaluru and Mysuru, as the roadmap says (ಮಾಡ್ತೀನಿ, ಮಾಡಲ್ಲ, ಇದೆ / ಇಲ್ಲ), written in Kannada script; written forms (ಮಾಡುತ್ತೇನೆ) are taught for recognition and accepted in typed answers. One spelling is fixed for every spoken form (§2.2). Voice `kn-IN`: Azure **kn-IN-SapnaNeural** and **kn-IN-GaganNeural**, both confirmed on a Microsoft page; Android and iOS both list a Kannada voice, but iOS reports are poor, so every card and example gets an Azure clip (§2.1).
- **Register:** ನೀವು (with -ಈರಿ verbs and -ಇ commands) is the default "you"; ನೀನು for friends and children; ಅವರು for any adult third person you respect; ಸರ್ / ಮೇಡಂ in offices; ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ (§2.3–2.4).
- **Pronunciation aid (§6):** the en-hi / en-te learner romanisation (ā ī ū ē ō, ṭ ḍ ṇ ḷ, kh gh ch chh, anusvara resolved to n / ṇ / m by rule: ಬೆಂಗಳೂರು *bengaḷūru*, ಗಂಟೆ *gaṇṭe*, ತುಂಬಾ *tumbā*). **Generated** by `respell.js`: Kannada writes every vowel it says (no silent a), and spoken forms are spelled with the virama where a vowel drops, so the pron follows the spelling. It throws on non-Kannada and warns where it guesses. Test set: 74 items, 0 mismatches.
- **Biggest traps (§4):** verb agreement (person endings; he / she / it; the respect plural; things take the neuter); no word for "is", with ಇದೆ for place and having and ಅಲ್ಲ / ಇಲ್ಲ for "not"; case endings after the noun with linking sounds (ಬೆಂಗಳೂರಿಗೆ, ಮರಕ್ಕೆ, ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ); the ನನಗೆ pattern (ನನಗೆ … ಬೇಕು / ಇಷ್ಟ / ಗೊತ್ತು); three words for "no" (ಇಲ್ಲ, ಅಲ್ಲ, ಬೇಡ); human counting forms (ಇಬ್ಬರು); length, retroflex and doubled consonants (ಕತೆ / ಕತ್ತೆ).
- **Standing rule (§7):** two strands: **(a) agreement and person endings**, **(b) case endings after the noun** (including the ನನಗೆ pattern); in the script lessons a sound strand. Keywords tested on 47 lines.
- **Lessons and decks (§8, §9):** 27 lessons (the roadmap's 27 ids). The roadmap already puts the present before the past; three moves: vowel signs before the consonants, the locative before the present, the continuous straight after the present. 18 decks in 5 groups, including five ordinary script decks and "Traps for English speakers".
- **Typing (§10):** word tiles by default; typing optional in Kannada script or romanisation. Measured in a copy of the matcher: today Kannada input has no "almost" path and no nukta fold; romanised typing already folds macrons and dots, which makes dot-only and length-only pairs "almost" for each other (ಅಣ್ಣ / ಅನ್ನ, ಹುಳಿ / ಹುಲಿ, ಕಾರು / ಕರು, ಮಾಡ್ತೀಯಾ / ಮಾಡ್ತೀಯ). Kannada must **not** get Telugu's doubled-consonant fold (it would make ಬೇಕು "want" and ಬೆಕ್ಕು "cat" the same). Four engine items before writers start (§10.8).

## 2. Variant, register and voice

### 2.1 Variant: the spoken standard, in Kannada script

- **The roadmap names** "Modern standard Kannada of Bengaluru and Mysuru, everyday spoken standard (spoken verb forms such as ಮಾಡ್ತೀನಿ, not the written ಮಾಡುತ್ತೇನೆ)"; this profile keeps it. Kannada has a wide gap between the written standard (newspapers, signs, schoolbooks, formal speech: ಮಾಡುತ್ತೇನೆ, ಮಾಡುವುದಿಲ್ಲ) and everyday speech (ಮಾಡ್ತೀನಿ, ಮಾಡಲ್ಲ) [curriculum convention: the roadmap note; Penn's *Preliminary Lessons: Spoken Kannada* and its *A Grammar of Spoken Kannada* exist as a separate tradition from written grammars]. Penn's lessons write the spoken forms *iddiini*, *iddiiri*, *iddaane*, *iddaaLe*, *ide*, *hoogtiini*, *bartiiraa?*, *gottilla*, *saaklilla* ([Penn lessons 1–6](https://ccat.sas.upenn.edu/plc/kannada/)) [curriculum convention].
- **Why spoken, not written:** learners want to talk to family, colleagues, drivers and shopkeepers; a learner who says ಮಾಡುತ್ತೇನೆ in a shop sounds like a news reader [inference]. The written forms still matter for signs, menus and messages, so each verb lesson shows them in one recognition table, and typed answers accept them (§2.2).
- **Regional speech** (north Karnataka, coastal Mangaluru, Dharwad) differs in endings and words [inference]. The course uses the Bengaluru–Mysuru forms that media and the roadmap treat as standard; regional forms appear only in notes.
- **Say it plainly to users** (course blurb and lesson 1): "This course teaches everyday Kannada as it is spoken in Bengaluru and Mysuru, in the Kannada script. Signs, newspapers and formal letters use fuller written forms; the course shows you those too, so you can read them."
- **Voice: `ttsLang: "kn-IN"`.** Microsoft's February 2022 post on new languages lists locale kn-IN with two neural voices, **Gagan** (male) and **Sapna** (female), each with a Kannada sample sentence ([Microsoft Tech Community, "Azure Cognitive Services releases new languages and voices"](https://techcommunity.microsoft.com/t5/azure-ai-blog/azure-cognitive-services-releases-new-languages-and-voices-for/ba-p/3177458)) [curriculum convention]. A third-party catalogue ranks kn-IN-GaganNeural "#2 of 2 Kannada Azure voices" in its gallery ([json2video](https://json2video.com/ai-voices/azure/voices/kn-in-gaganneural/)), which suggests these two are all there is; Microsoft's current voice list was not opened (§11).
- **Android:** Google's TalkBack help lists "Kannada (India)" among Google Text-to-Speech languages, with "Some devices may not support all languages in all regions" ([Google, *Languages supported by TalkBack*](https://support.google.com/accessibility/android/answer/11101402?hl=en)).
- **iOS:** Apple's iOS feature-availability page lists "Kannada" under "Accessibility: VoiceOver, Live Speech, Read & Speak", and lists Kannada (Alphabetic), (InScript) and (Transliteration) keyboards ([Apple, iOS feature availability](https://www.apple.com/ios/feature-availability/)). So a device voice exists; but an AppleVis thread (November 2025) has a blind user saying "VoiceOver is not reading Telugu, Tamil, Kannada, or Malayalam correctly" and that the voice must be switched by hand ([AppleVis](https://applevis.com/comment/199202)). Whether the Web Speech API in Safari exposes the Kannada voice was not tested (§11).
- **What happens with no device voice:** the app shows "No voice for this language on this device" once when `voiceMatch` finds no voice (`src/app.html`, `speak`), and plays a recorded clip whenever one exists (`audio/<target>/index.json`). Decision, as for en-pa, en-ur and en-te: **record Azure clips (Sapna; Gagan for male lines if the generator supports a second voice) for every card target and example row** (request 4, §10.8). `profile.json` sets `voiceMatch: "^kn(?:[-_]|$)"`.
- **How text-to-speech reads spoken spellings** [inference; unverified, request 9]: Kannada spelling is phonemic and the spoken spellings write the dropped vowel as a virama (ಮಾಡ್ತೀನಿ = ಮಾ + ಡ್ + ತೀ + ನಿ), so a letter-to-sound voice should say exactly what is written, *māḍtīni*. The risks: Microsoft's samples are formal written Kannada, so the voice's lexicon and prosody were trained on written forms; spoken clusters such as ಡ್ತ, ಗ್ತ, ದ್ಲು and fused forms (ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೀನಿ) may get odd stress or an inserted vowel. Every spoken form in §2.2 goes into the voice check list; if Sapna misreads one, the clip is re-recorded with SSML phonemes or the form is dropped from card targets (it can still be taught in the lesson body).

**Fixed spelling rules beyond the table** [inference, §11]:
- **Long vowels are written where they are said in the spoken endings:** -ತೀನಿ, -ತೀರಿ, -ತೀವಿ (never ತಿನಿ, ತಿರಿ); -ತಾನೆ, -ತಾಳೆ, -ತಾರೆ.
- **2nd person singular statement -ಈಯ, question -ಈಯಾ** (ಮಾಡ್ತೀಯ "you do", ಮಾಡ್ತೀಯಾ? "do you?"): a length-only pair, kept apart by request 3 (§10.5). The polite statement is -ಈರಿ (Penn), so ಬರ್ತೀರಿ / ಬರ್ತೀರಾ? are not a length pair.
- **The neuter present** is ಮಾಡುತ್ತೆ, ಆಗುತ್ತೆ, ಬರುತ್ತೆ (with ು), not ಮಾಡ್ತೆ.
- **Fused greeting forms** are stored fused, as one word (ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೀನಿ, ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಾ?, ಎಲ್ಲಿದೆ?); the separate forms are accepted.
- **English loans:** as modern Kannada writes them, with the virama at the end (ಬಸ್, ಬ್ಯಾಂಕ್, ಆಫೀಸ್, ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್, ಫೋನ್, ಟಿಕೆಟ್); before a case ending they take -ಇ / -ಇನ (ಆಫೀಸಿಗೆ, ಬ್ಯಾಂಕಿಗೆ, ಬಸ್ಸಿನಲ್ಲಿ, ಫೋನಿನಲ್ಲಿ). This keeps every course string free of ZWNJ (the common web spelling, ಬಸ್ + ZWNJ + ನಲ್ಲಿ, needs one; §10.3). ಬಸ್ಸು is accepted for ಬಸ್.
- **Postpositions** ಜೊತೆ, ಹತ್ತಿರ, ಮೇಲೆ, ಕೆಳಗೆ, ಒಳಗೆ, ಮುಂದೆ, ಹಿಂದೆ, ಆಗಿ are separate words after the genitive (ಅಮ್ಮನ ಜೊತೆ, ಮನೆಯ ಹತ್ತಿರ, ಮೇಜಿನ ಮೇಲೆ); case endings are attached (ಮನೆಗೆ, ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ, ಮನೆಯಿಂದ, ಮನೆಯನ್ನು).
- **One spelling per word:** ಧನ್ಯವಾದಗಳು; ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ; ಹೌದು; ಏನು (ಏನ್ accepted); ಯಾಕೆ (ಏಕೆ accepted); ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ; ತುಂಬಾ; ಐದು; ಪರವಾಗಿಲ್ಲ; ಗೊತ್ತಿಲ್ಲ.

|---|---|---|---|
| ನೀವು *nīvu* | -ಈರಿ: ನೀವು ಎಲ್ಲಿ ಇದ್ದೀರಿ? Commands in -ಇ: ಬನ್ನಿ, ಕೊಡಿ, ಕೂತ್ಕೊಳ್ಳಿ | strangers, elders, shopkeepers, teachers, in-laws, colleagues; also "you all" | **default** in every prompt, example and dialogue from `greetings` |
| ನೀನು *nīnu* | -ಈಯ: ನೀನು ಎಲ್ಲಿ ಇದ್ದೀಯ? Command = the bare stem: ಬಾ, ಕೊಡು, ಕೂತ್ಕೋ | close friends of your age, children, younger siblings; rude to a stranger or an elder | taught in `greetings` and `imperative`; used only when the prompt says "(to a friend)" or "(to a child)" |

- **Third person** [inference]: ಅವರು *avaru* (he / she, respectful; also "they", with -ಆರೆ / -ರು verbs) is the default for any adult the speaker respects, including parents and teachers; ಅವನು *avanu* (he, familiar: boys, friends, people you would call ನೀನು) and ಅವಳು *avaḷu* (she, familiar) with -ಆನೆ / -ಆಳೆ; ಅದು *adu* (it) for animals and things, with the neuter. English speakers use "he / she" for everyone; Kannada picks by respect (§4.1).
- **Respect markers:** -ಅವರು after a name or title is the polite way to refer to an adult (ರಾಮು ಅವರು, ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ ಅವರು; written as a separate word in the course); ಸರ್ and ಮೇಡಂ in offices, shops and to teachers; ಅಣ್ಣ / ಅಕ್ಕ ("older brother / sister") to a man or woman a bit older, also to drivers and shopkeepers; ಅಮ್ಮ to an older woman [inference]. **Never -ಅವರು after your own name.**
- **Rules for writers.** A prompt whose answer contains "you" says who is addressed when it matters: "(to a friend)" → ನೀನು; no cue or "(politely)" → ನೀವು. A bare "you" accepts only ನೀವು forms. Kannada verbs do not show the speaker's gender in the first and second person (ನಾನು ಹೋದೆ for a man or a woman), so **no speaker cue is needed**, with one exception: "where from" nouns show the speaker's gender (ನಾನು ಅಮೆರಿಕದವನು, a man; ನಾನು ಅಮೆರಿಕದವಳು, a woman), so prompts that need one say "(a woman speaking)" or "(a man speaking)", and `ty` items accept both when the prompt gives no cue. The third person shows gender and respect, so every prompt about "he / she" or a named person says which (and "(respectfully)" when the answer is ಅವರು).

### 2.5 Everyday Kannada, not bookish and not Hindi

**Rule:** use the word an educated Bengaluru speaker uses in neutral, polite speech.
- **Spoken verb forms** (§2.2); written forms only in recognition tables.
- **English loans in Kannada script where they are the normal word:** ಬಸ್, ಆಟೋ, ಮೆಟ್ರೋ, ಆಫೀಸ್, ಬ್ಯಾಂಕ್, ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್, ಫೋನ್, ಟಿಕೆಟ್, ಕಾಫಿ, ಟೀ, ಸ್ಕೂಲ್ (ಶಾಲೆ is also everyday). Code-mixing with English is very heavy in Bengaluru [inference]; the course uses a loan where a speaker would, but teaches the Kannada word for core vocabulary (ನೀರು, not ವಾಟರ್; ಊಟ, not ಲಂಚ್; ಮನೆ, not ಹೌಸ್).
- **Sanskrit words that are everyday Kannada stay** (ಧನ್ಯವಾದಗಳು, ಸಮಯ, ಸಹಾಯ, ಪ್ರೀತಿ).
- **No Hindi.** Learners who know Hindi may reach for हाँ, अच्छा, ठीक है, कल; Kannada has ಹೌದು, ಸರಿ, ಆಯ್ತು, and two words for कल (ನಿನ್ನೆ yesterday, ನಾಳೆ tomorrow). Hindi forms are never accepted answers.
- **Never Latin letters in Kannada strings.**

### 2.6 Politeness basics an A1 learner needs

1. ನೀವು + -ಈರಿ verbs with anyone you do not know (§2.3).
2. -ಇ for requests (ಕೊಡಿ, ಹೇಳಿ, ಬನ್ನಿ); ದಯವಿಟ್ಟು ("please") is used, but less than English "please": the -ಇ form is already polite [inference].
3. ಅವರು for adults you respect, with plural verbs: ಅಪ್ಪ ಬಂದ್ರು ("Dad came").
4. Address terms: ಸರ್ / ಮೇಡಂ, ಅಣ್ಣ / ಅಕ್ಕ, ಅಮ್ಮ, ಅಂಕಲ್ / ಆಂಟಿ (a friend's parents) [inference].
5. Offer and take things with the right hand [inference; cultural note only].

### 2.7 Voice of the English text

- Plain, friendly US English, US spelling. A `why` is one sentence.
- Kannada inside English text: in lesson bodies `<b>` around the Kannada, then the romanisation in `<i>` once at first mention in that lesson: `<b>ಮನೆ</b> <i>mane</i>`. In plain-text fields (`why`, `hint`, notes) write the Kannada bare; add the romanisation in brackets only when the point is the sound ("ಟ (ṭa, tongue curled back)").
- Never add English endings to Kannada words ("two case endings", not "two -ಗೆs").
- Sample voice lines: "ಅವಳು takes the feminine ending, so it's ಅವಳು ಬರ್ತಾಳೆ, not ಅವಳು ಬರ್ತಾನೆ." · "In Kannada the ending goes on the noun: ಮನೆಗೆ, 'to the house'." · "Nice work! Review these cards tomorrow so they stick."

### 2.8 Kannada text conventions (every file)

- **Kannada script only**, Unicode NFC, spaces between words, **no ZWNJ / ZWJ** (the validator rejects them; §10.3).
- **Spellings from §2.2**, case endings attached, postpositions separate.
- **Punctuation:** ASCII . ? ! , as in English. No danda (modern Kannada prose uses Western punctuation [inference]).
- **Numbers as words** in Kannada strings (ಐವತ್ತು ರೂಪಾಯಿ, ಏಳು ಗಂಟೆಗೆ); the generator throws on any digit. Real prices are written in Western digits (₹50); lessons say so in English and show ₹50 only in English text.
- **No Latin letters, no Kannada digits, no archaic letters** (ೞ, ಱ; the generator throws), no nukta (§10.3).

## 4. Error zones of English speakers, ranked

**How the list is ranked** [inference]: (1) points that occur in nearly every sentence and can be checked in tiled or typed practice; (2) points where English gives a learner a confident wrong model (no "is", the ನನಗೆ pattern, "no"); (3) lesson-local, sound and vocabulary traps.

**Evidence is thin.** No study of errors by English-speaking learners of Kannada was found. The grammar facts below are standard in descriptions of Kannada: the *Kannada grammar* article gives the case endings (tammanannu, tammanige, marakke, tammanalli, tammaninda), the person endings (present-future -ēne, -īri, -āne, -āḷe, -ade, -āre), that human beings are grammatically masculine or feminine while animals and things are neuter, and that the spoken negative is periphrastic (*māḍuvudillā*) [curriculum convention]; Penn's spoken lessons give the spoken forms; Kochetov (2015) the single / doubled pairs. Which points English speakers get wrong, and how often, is [inference] and on the native-check list (§11).

1. **Verb agreement: person endings; he / she / it; respect; things are neuter**
   - Wrong → right: ಅವಳು ಬರ್ತಾನೆ → ಅವಳು ಬರ್ತಾಳೆ · ನೀವು ಎಲ್ಲಿ ಇದ್ದೀಯ? → ನೀವು ಎಲ್ಲಿ ಇದ್ದೀರಿ? · ನಾನು ಹೋಗ್ತಾರೆ → ನಾನು ಹೋಗ್ತೀನಿ · ಬಸ್ ಬಂದ (as if the bus were "he") → ಬಸ್ ಬಂತು · ಅಪ್ಪ ಬಂದ (to a stranger, about your father) → ಅಪ್ಪ ಬಂದ್ರು · ನಾಯಿ ಬರ್ತಾನೆ → ನಾಯಿ ಬರುತ್ತೆ.
   - [curriculum convention] the person endings and the gender / animacy rule of the *Kannada grammar* article; the roadmap lists "verb endings for person and gender at the end of the sentence" among its four hardest points.
   - Design consequence: strand (a) of the standing rule (§7); every lesson with a third-person verb contrasts ಅವನು / ಅವಳು / ಅದು / ಅವರು.

2. **No word for "is" in "X is Y"; ಇದೆ for place and having; ಅಲ್ಲ and ಇಲ್ಲ for "not"**
   - Wrong → right: ನಾನು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ ಇದ್ದೀನಿ → ನಾನು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ · ಇದು ಪುಸ್ತಕ ಇದೆ → ಇದು ಪುಸ್ತಕ · ನನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು ಸಾರಾ ಇದೆ → ನನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು ಸಾರಾ · ನಾನು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ ಇಲ್ಲ → ನಾನು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ ಅಲ್ಲ · but place does take ಇರು: ರವಿ ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ → ರವಿ ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ ಇದ್ದಾನೆ; ಕಾಫಿ ಅಲ್ಲ ("there's no coffee") → ಕಾಫಿ ಇಲ್ಲ.
   - [inference; Penn lessons 1–2 use ಇರು only for location: *naan ruumnalli iddiini*, *pustaka yell-ide?*] English "is" splits three ways: nothing (identity), ಇರು (place, existence, having), and in the negative ಅಲ್ಲ (identity) or ಇಲ್ಲ (place, existence). Unlike Telugu, spoken Kannada puts **no person ending on the noun** (ನಾನು ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್, with no ending on ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್).
   - Design consequence: `no-is` and `ide`; the Traps deck.

3. **Case endings after the noun, with linking sounds**
   - Wrong → right: ಗೆ ಮನೆ → ಮನೆಗೆ · ಬೆಂಗಳೂರುಗೆ → ಬೆಂಗಳೂರಿಗೆ · ಮರಗೆ → ಮರಕ್ಕೆ · ನಾನುಗೆ → ನನಗೆ · ಮನೆಅಲ್ಲಿ → ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ · ಜೊತೆ ಅಮ್ಮ → ಅಮ್ಮನ ಜೊತೆ · ಕಾಫಿಯನ್ನು ಕುಡಿದೆ (marked: an indefinite object takes no ending) → ಕಾಫಿ ಕುಡಿದೆ.
   - [curriculum convention] the article's declension (dative "-(i)ge, which becomes -kke after some stems": tammanige, marakke); Penn *ruum-ige*, *kelsa-kke*, *maneg-banni*.
   - Design consequence: strand (b); the `dative`, `locative` and `directions` lessons; deck cards show nouns with their endings (ಮನೆಗೆ, ಬಸ್ಸಿನಲ್ಲಿ).

4. **The ನನಗೆ pattern: want, like, know, have relatives, feel**
   - Wrong → right: ನಾನು ಕಾಫಿ ಬೇಕು → ನನಗೆ ಕಾಫಿ ಬೇಕು · ನಾನು ದೋಸೆ ಇಷ್ಟ → ನನಗೆ ದೋಸೆ ಇಷ್ಟ · ನಾನು ಗೊತ್ತು → ನನಗೆ ಗೊತ್ತು · ನನಗೆ ಗೊತ್ತು ಇಲ್ಲ → ನನಗೆ ಗೊತ್ತಿಲ್ಲ · ನಾನು ಒಬ್ಬ ಅಣ್ಣ ಇದ್ದಾನೆ → ನನಗೆ ಒಬ್ಬ ಅಣ್ಣ ಇದ್ದಾನೆ · ನಾನು ಹಸಿವು → ನನಗೆ ಹಸಿವಾಗಿದೆ · but "I have to go" is ನಾನು ಹೋಗಬೇಕು, while ನನಗೆ ಹೋಗಬೇಕು means "I want to go" [inference, §11].
   - [curriculum convention] Penn lessons 4–5 ("dative-stative": *nimage kaafi iSTavaa?*, *nanage haalu tumba iSTa*, *gottilla*); the roadmap's `like-want` lesson.
   - Design consequence: strand (b) counts these items; lessons `like-want`, `food`, `must-can`.

5. **Three words for "no", and the spoken negative**
   - Wrong → right: ಇದು ಕಾಫಿ ಇಲ್ಲ → ಇದು ಕಾಫಿ ಅಲ್ಲ · (offered more rice) ಇಲ್ಲ → ಬೇಡ · ನಾನು ಹೋಗ್ತೀನಿ ಇಲ್ಲ → ನಾನು ಹೋಗಲ್ಲ · ನಾನು ಹೋದೆ ಇಲ್ಲ → ನಾನು ಹೋಗಲಿಲ್ಲ · ಹೋಗಲ್ಲ ("I won't go") said for ಹೋಗಲಿಲ್ಲ ("I didn't go").
   - [curriculum convention] Penn *beeDa, idu saaku* ("no thanks, this is plenty"), *illa, saaklilla*; the roadmap's ಮಾಡಲ್ಲ / ಹೋಗಲಿಲ್ಲ.
   - Design consequence: `greetings` (ಹೌದು / ಇಲ್ಲ), `no-is` (ಅಲ್ಲ), `like-want` (ಬೇಡ), `present` (ಮಾಡಲ್ಲ), `past-neg` (ಮಾಡಲಿಲ್ಲ); the Traps deck.

6. **The verb at the end; question words in place; -ಆ for yes-no**
   - Wrong → right: ನಾನು ತಿಂತೀನಿ ದೋಸೆ → ನಾನು ದೋಸೆ ತಿಂತೀನಿ · ನೀವು ಹೋಗ್ತೀರಿ ಎಲ್ಲಿಗೆ? → ನೀವು ಎಲ್ಲಿಗೆ ಹೋಗ್ತೀರಿ? · ನೀವು ಬರ್ತೀರಿ? (rising voice only) → ನೀವು ಬರ್ತೀರಾ? · ಕಾಫಿ ಬೇಕು? → ಕಾಫಿ ಬೇಕಾ?
   - [curriculum convention] Penn lesson 3: *niivu yelli hoogtiiri?* (no -ಆ with a question word) against *niivu maneg-bartiiraa?*; the greeting ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಾ? is a fixed exception (accepted both ways).
   - Design consequence: tiles in `or` items; order alone does not count for the standing rule.

7. **Counting people**
   - Wrong → right: ಎರಡು ಮಕ್ಕಳು → ಇಬ್ಬರು ಮಕ್ಕಳು · ಒಂದು ಹುಡುಗ → ಒಬ್ಬ ಹುಡುಗ · ಇಬ್ಬರು ಪುಸ್ತಕ → ಎರಡು ಪುಸ್ತಕ · from three up, speech often uses the number + ಜನ (ಮೂರು ಜನ, "three people") beside ಮೂವರು [inference, §11].
   - Design consequence: `family`, `numbers`; strand (a) counts it ("human").

8. **Respect slips**
   - Wrong → right: ನೀನು to a shopkeeper or an elder → ನೀವು · ಬಾ to a guest → ಬನ್ನಿ · ಅವನು about a teacher → ಅವರು · ನನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು ಸಾರಾ ಅವರು → ನನ್ನ ಹೆಸರು ಸಾರಾ · ಬನ್ನಿ said, then ಕೊಡು → ಕೊಡಿ.
   - Design consequence: §2.3–2.6; `greetings`, `imperative`.

9. **Sounds English does not separate**
   - Wrong (said) → right: ತ / ಟ, ದ / ಡ, ನ / ಣ, ಲ / ಳ merged (ಅನ್ನ "cooked rice" / ಅಣ್ಣ "older brother"; ಹುಲಿ "tiger" / ಹುಳಿ "sour") · a doubled consonant said single (ಕತೆ "story" / ಕತ್ತೆ "donkey"; ಬೆಣೆ "peg" / ಬೆಣ್ಣೆ "butter") · ಎ / ಏ and ಒ / ಓ merged (ಕೊಡು "give" / ಕೋಡು "horn") · an English puff on every k, t, p · ಫ read f in a Kannada word.
   - [research-backed] Kochetov (2015, preliminary acoustic study, 9 Mysore speakers): geminate laterals 2.2–2.8 times as long as singletons; minimal pairs *kat̪e* 'story' / *kat̪ːe* 'donkey', *beɳe* 'peg' / *beɳːe* 'butter', *maɡa* 'son' / *maɡːa* 'handloom'. Kochetov et al. (2023, MRI, *JIPA*): Kannada contrasts dental and retroflex "across all four manners" (stop, nasal, sibilant, lateral). [inference] English speakers' difficulty with the dental / retroflex contrast is documented for Hindi (en-hi §4); Kannada has the same contrast plus ಲ / ಳ.
   - Design consequence: the script lessons; the sound strand (§7); minimal pairs in the decks; request 3 (§10.8) so that typed romanisation does not credit the other word.

10. **Reading the script**
    - Wrong → right (read): the ottakshara read before the letter it hangs under (ಅಮ್ಮ, ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ) · the arkāvottu read after the consonant it follows on paper (ಕರ್ನಾಟಕ is *karnāṭaka*, the r comes first) · the length stroke ೕ missed (ಕೆ / ಕೇ, ಕೊ / ಕೋ) · ಂ read as m everywhere (ಬೆಂಗಳೂರು is *bengaḷūru*) · ಬ / ವ and ಪ / ಷ confused (similar shapes) [inference].
    - Design consequence: `vowel-signs`, `conjuncts`; the romanisation stays on until the learner turns it off.

11. **Spoken and written forms mixed**
    - Wrong → right: ನಾನು ಮಾಡುತ್ತೀನಿ (half written, half spoken) → ನಾನು ಮಾಡ್ತೀನಿ · ಮಾಡುವುದಿಲ್ಲ in a chat with a friend → ಮಾಡಲ್ಲ · ಮಾಡ್ತೀನಿ in a formal letter → ಮಾಡುತ್ತೇನೆ (out of A1 scope; noted).
    - Design consequence: §2.2; recognition tables in `present`, `continuous`, `past`; written forms accepted in typing, never offered as tiles.

12. **Sandhi in everyday speech**
    - Wrong → right: insisting on ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿ ಇದ್ದೀನಿ → ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೀನಿ (both fine; the fused form is normal) · ಎಲ್ಲಿ ಇದೆ and ಎಲ್ಲಿದೆ both fine · ಇಪ್ಪತ್ತು ಐದು → ಇಪ್ಪತ್ತೈದು (numbers fuse).
    - [inference] Vowels meet and merge across word boundaries in speech, and writing often writes the merged form. The course stores the merged forms that are the everyday word (ಚೆನ್ನಾಗಿದ್ದೀನಿ, ಹೇಗಿದ್ದೀರಾ?, ಇಪ್ಪತ್ತೈದು) and accepts the separate forms where both are normal (§2.2).

13. **False friends and calques**
    - Wrong → right: "you're welcome" as ಸ್ವಾಗತ (that is "welcome!" to a guest) → ಪರವಾಗಿಲ್ಲ, or nothing · "I am 25 years" ನಾನು ಇಪ್ಪತ್ತೈದು ವರ್ಷ → ನನಗೆ ಇಪ್ಪತ್ತೈದು ವರ್ಷ · "brother" is ಅಣ್ಣ (older) or ತಮ್ಮ (younger), "sister" ಅಕ್ಕ or ತಂಗಿ · ತಮ್ಮ also means "their own" (ಅವರ ತಮ್ಮ ಮನೆ): context decides · ನಿನ್ನೆ (yesterday) and ನಾಳೆ (tomorrow) are different words, unlike Hindi कल · ಆಯ್ತು means "OK, done", not only "it became".
    - [inference] Teaching lore and the author's knowledge; native check (§11). Design consequence: the Traps deck; `family`.

14. **Numbers and time**
    - Wrong → right: digits typed for numbers (the course uses words) · 7:30 built from English → ಏಳೂವರೆ · "at seven" ಏಳು ಗಂಟೆ → ಏಳು ಗಂಟೆಗೆ · twenty-one as two words ಇಪ್ಪತ್ತು ಒಂದು → ಇಪ್ಪತ್ತೊಂದು.
    - Design consequence: `numbers`, `time`; the Numbers and money deck. Unlike Telugu, ಒಂದು stays ಒಂದು before a noun (ಒಂದು ಕಾಫಿ ಕೊಡಿ, roadmap) [inference].

## 6. Pronunciation aid (`pron`)

**Evidence.**
- [research-backed] Kochetov (2015) and Kochetov et al. (2023), above (§4.9): length contrasts in consonants, dental / retroflex in stops, nasals, sibilants and laterals; vowels are longer before retroflexes.
- [curriculum convention] The *Kannada script* article: Kannada is "a phonemic abugida"; "Consonantal letters imply an inherent vowel"; before another consonant, nasals are "usually written as an anusvara ಂ rather than a consonant conjunct"; the ra conjunct "is written second even if it is pronounced first in the sequence" (ರ್ನಾ in ಕರ್ನಾಟಕ); ಋ is read *ru*; ೞ, ಱ and ೠ are obsolete ([Kiwix mirror of Wikipedia, 2023-10](https://kaweah.freedombox.rocks/kiwix/wikipedia_en_all_maxi_2023-10/%E0%B2%8A)).
- [curriculum convention] Unicode canonical decompositions, checked in node: ೀ = ಿ + ೕ, ೇ = ೆ + ೕ, ೈ = ೆ + ೖ, ೊ = ೆ + ೂ, ೋ = ೊ + ೕ (so NFC turns a typed sign + length mark into the single sign).
- [curriculum convention] Penn's spoken lessons write an ASCII transliteration with doubled vowels for length and capitals for retroflex (*iddaaLe*, *iSTa*, *beeDa*). This course does not copy it: capitals and doubled vowels would clash with the en-hi / en-pa / en-te key, which the courses share.

**Decision.** `pron: "required"`. The pron is **the en-hi / en-te learner romanisation**, and **generated by `tools/target/kn/respell.js`**, never typed. `node tools/target/kn/respell.js "<text>"` prints `<text> -> <pron>`; the module exports `respell`, `pattern`, `warnings`, `FSTEM`, `EXC`, `VER`.

**Why a generator is reliable here.** Kannada spelling is close to phonemic and, unlike Hindi, it writes every vowel it says: there is **no silent a** (ಮನೆ *mane*, ಕನ್ನಡ *kannaḍa*), so the hard part of the Hindi generator does not exist. What remains is a letter table, the virama, the anusvara rule and English loans said against their letters (ಫ = f, listed by stem so that ಕಾಫಿ and ಫೋನಿನಲ್ಲಿ follow). Kannada has no ts / dz split for ಚ / ಜ (that is Telugu), so ch and j are always ch and j.

**Dropped vowels in spoken forms.** The course's spoken spellings (§2.2) write a dropped vowel as a virama, so the generator needs no rule for them: ಮಾಡ್ತೀನಿ *māḍtīni*, ಮಾಡಿದ್ಲು *māḍidlu*, ಆಯ್ತು *āytu*. Where speech drops a vowel the spelling keeps (ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ, often said *manēli*), the pron follows the spelling and the spoken spelling is an accepted variant with its own pron (ಮನೇಲಿ *manēli*).

**English loans.** Written as modern Kannada writes them (§2.2), read letter by letter: ಬ್ಯಾಂಕ್ *byānk*, ಆಫೀಸ್ *āfīs* (ಫ = f by `FSTEM`), ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್ *ḍākṭar*, ಬಸ್ಸಿನಲ್ಲಿ *bassinalli*. Not shown: the English æ written ್ಯಾ (*byānk* ≈ bænk), and the -u many speakers add after a final consonant (ಬಸ್ said *bassu*) [inference].

**Measured** (self-measured; no native check, §11):
- **Test set 1** (74 words and phrases of course vocabulary, with the expected output written down before the first run; scratch `set1.tsv`): **0 mismatches**; 3 warnings, all intended (ೃ in ಕೃಷ್ಣ, ಃ in ದುಃಖ, ಜ್ಞ in ಜ್ಞಾನ). The expectations were written by the same author as the rules, so the test shows the code does what the key says, not that the key is right.
- **Throws** as designed on Latin letters, Western and Kannada digits, Telugu letters, ೞ, ಱ, the candrabindu ಁ, a lone vowel sign and a lone virama.
- **Silent-error classes that remain** (no warning): (1) **English loans** said against the letters (æ, final -u, English o); (2) **Sanskrit words with aspirates and ಶ / ಷ**, said more or less carefully by different speakers (ಮುಖ often *muka*); the pron keeps the letters; (3) **ಎ- / ಒ- at the start of a word** said ye- / wo- (ಎಲ್ಲಿ ≈ *yelli*, ಒಂದು ≈ *wondu*); (4) **anusvara in a Sanskrit word** where some speakers say a nasal vowel; (5) spoken vowel drops the spelling does not write.
- **Adding words:** writers never edit a pron. When the generator throws or warns, the writer reports the word; the lead adds an English-loan stem to `FSTEM` (its ಫ is f, endings included), a whole word to `EXC` (wrong output) or to `VER` (right, checked) and runs `node tools/target/repron.js kn`.

**Decisions inside the key.**
- **ch, not c,** for ಚ (chh for ಛ, cch for ಚ್ಚ): the en-hi / en-pa / en-te key; `romaLoose` folds ch and chh to c anyway, so a learner who types *c* is "almost".
- **ē and ō** with a macron, like ā ī ū: Kannada has short ಎ ಒ and long ಏ ಓ (ಕೊಡು "give" / ಕೋಡು "horn"), and English speakers hear both as the vowel of "say" / "go".
- **Anusvara** is not written ṁ: it is resolved by rule to n, ṇ or m (ಬೆಂಗಳೂರು *bengaḷūru*, ಗಂಟೆ *gaṇṭe*, ತುಂಬಾ *tumbā*, ಸಂಸಾರ *samsāra*), so the pron says what to do. Before a palatal it is n (ಪಂಚೆ *panche*), before a velar n (not ṅ), as in en-hi.
- **ಶ and ಷ both sh**, as in en-hi; ಕ್ಷ is ksh (ಕ್ಷಮಿಸಿ *kshamisi*); ಋ and ೃ are ru (ಕೃಷ್ಣ *krushṇa*, warns); visarga ಃ is h (warns; rare); ಜ್ಞ is jñ with a warning (Kannada speakers usually say *gny*; it is rare at A1).
- **The arkāvottu** needs no rule: Unicode stores ರ್ before the consonant in reading order (ರ + ್ + ನ), so ಕರ್ನಾಟಕ gives *karnāṭaka*.
- **Archaic letters throw:** ೞ (write ಳ), ಱ (write ರ), ಌ ೡ ೠ ೄ ೢ ೣ, ಽ, ಁ, ೱ ೲ ೳ, U+0CDD, and bare length marks ೕ ೖ.

Built by `respell.js`'s `pattern()`, identical to en-te's (the same letters):

```
^(?!.*(?:aa|ii|uu|ee|oo|c(?!c?h)))[abcdefghijklmnoprstuvyzāīūēōṭḍṇḷṅñ]+(?:-[abcdefghijklmnoprstuvyzāīūēōṭḍṇḷṅñ]+)*[.,?!:;]?(?: [abcdefghijklmnoprstuvyzāīūēōṭḍṇḷṅñ]+(?:-[abcdefghijklmnoprstuvyzāīūēōṭḍṇḷṅñ]+)*[.,?!:;]?)*$
```

### 6.4 Card front, and hiding the romanisation

- **Card front:** the Kannada script, large. Under it the pron line, **shown by default**: after five script lessons the learner reads slowly, and length, doubling and retroflexion are easy to misread.
- **Hideable:** the app's "Blur romanization in Browse" setting exists (`S.hideRom`, `src/app.html`) but covers Browse only; request 7 (§10.8) extends it to cards and example rows. Suggested prompt after `dative`. Heritage learners who already read Kannada may switch it off from day one.
- **Practice items carry no pron** (PRACTICE-SPEC: "No romanization in practice items"). Exception, as in en-hi, en-pa and en-te: in the five script lessons, English prompts may name a sound with its romanisation ("Build kāge (crow)"); answers are always Kannada script.

### 6.5 What the script lessons must teach, so the aid can be read and later dropped

1. `vowels`: a macron = hold the vowel long (ā ī ū ē ō); e and o are short, ē and ō long, and Kannada tells them apart; ai and au.
2. `vowel-signs`: every consonant carries a unless a sign changes it, and the a is always said; the headstroke and how a sign replaces it; the length stroke ೕ on ೀ ೇ ೋ.
3. `consonants-1`: h after a letter = a puff (kh, chh), mostly in Sanskrit words; the dot below = retroflex.
4. `consonants-2`: t d n l plain = dental; ph is not f (except English loans); ḷ; r is a tap.
5. `conjuncts`: a doubled letter is held (*amma*); the ottakshara is read after the letter above it, the arkāvottu before the letter it follows; the anusvara becomes n / ṇ / m by the next letter; virama = no vowel.
6. Every lesson body: "The romanisation is a crutch: read the Kannada first and glance down only to check."

## 7. Standing practice rule

**Rule:** every lesson's practice set keeps re-testing two things, as in en-hi, en-pa and en-te.
- **(a) Agreement, and the person endings.** The verb ends in the person, number, gender and respect of its subject: -ಈನಿ (ನಾನು), -ಈಯ (ನೀನು), -ಈರಿ (ನೀವು), -ಆನೆ (ಅವನು: **masculine**), -ಆಳೆ (ಅವಳು: **feminine**), -ಉತ್ತೆ / ಇದೆ (ಅದು, and every animal and thing: **neuter**), -ಈವಿ (ನಾವು), -ಆರೆ (ಅವರು: people in the plural, and **any one adult you respect**); past -ಎ, -ರಿ, -ಅ, -ಲು, -ತು, -ವಿ, -ರು (§2.2). Counting people takes the **human** form (ಒಬ್ಬರು, ಇಬ್ಬರು).
- **(b) Case endings after the noun.** Kannada marks "to, in, from, with, of, the object" with an ending after the noun (ಮನೆಗೆ, ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ, ಮನೆಯಿಂದ, ಅಮ್ಮನ ಜೊತೆ, ಅವನನ್ನು), often with a **linking** sound (ಬೆಂಗಳೂರಿಗೆ, ಮರಕ್ಕೆ, ಊರಿನಲ್ಲಿ) and with a changed pronoun (ನಾನು → ನನಗೆ, ನನ್ನ); and the one who wants, likes, knows or has takes the **dative**: **the ನನಗೆ pattern** (ನನಗೆ ಕಾಫಿ ಬೇಕು, ನನಗೆ ಗೊತ್ತು, ನನಗೆ ಇಷ್ಟ).

**Why these two.** They occur in nearly every sentence from `no-is` on, have no English counterpart, and can be checked mechanically with a twin form as the distractor (§4.1, §4.3, §4.4). They are Kannada's own: Kannada's agreement is masculine / feminine / neuter for people and things plus a respect plural (not Telugu's masculine / non-masculine), and the case endings take Kannada's own linking sounds.
- **Not chosen:** verb-final order (every `or` item practises it anyway); "no is" and the three nos (§4.2, §4.5: their own lessons `no-is`, `ide`, `past-neg`; a why line about them names no fixed term); the spoken / written split (a register point, taught in recognition tables).

**The script stage.** The five script lessons have no grammar. In those sets only, the hinges are **sound strand** items: dental / retroflex (ತ / ಟ, ದ / ಡ, ನ / ಣ, ಲ / ಳ), aspirated / unaspirated, long / short vowel (ಿ / ೀ, ೆ / ೇ, ೊ / ೋ, plain / ಾ), the vowel sign itself, the doubled consonant and the ottakshara (ಕತೆ / ಕತ್ತೆ), the anusvara, the virama. One sound `mc` item per later set is recommended, not counted.

**Count** (`minOrder: 2`, `minType: 2`): in every set, at least **2 `or` items** and **2 `ty` items** hinge on the rule, and their `why` names it with a keyword.

**What counts as hinging** (mechanical): `or`, strand (a): `a` contains an agreeing form and `x` its twin from another person, gender or number (ಬರ್ತಾಳೆ / ಬರ್ತಾನೆ; ಇದ್ದೀರಿ / ಇದ್ದೀಯ; ಬಂತು / ಬಂದ; ಇಬ್ಬರು / ಎರಡು). `or`, strand (b): `a` contains a noun or pronoun with its case ending, and `x` the bare or wrong form (ಮನೆಗೆ / ಮನೆ; ಬೆಂಗಳೂರಿಗೆ / ಬೆಂಗಳೂರುಗೆ; ನನಗೆ / ನಾನು; ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ / ಮನೆಗೆ). `ty`: `a[0]` contains the same, `x` the twin, and **the twin must not be accepted**. Script stage: `or` tiles are the syllables of one word and `x` holds the sound twin; a one-word `ty` is written syllable-spaced with the unspaced word as the second answer (`ಕಾ ಗೆ|ಕಾಗೆ`) and two or more distractors; the `why` spells the word unspaced.

## 8. Lessons (final order)

**Changes from the roadmap** (all [inference] built on §4–§7; the brief's bar: keep the order unless there is a strong teaching reason):
- **Kept: all 27 items, no lessons added.** Ids lose the `kn-` prefix (`kn-like-want` becomes `like-want`); each roadmap id appears exactly once in a `roadmap` field. Additions considered and folded in instead: a pronoun lesson (`no-is` carries the pronouns), a counting-people lesson (`family` and `numbers` carry ಒಬ್ಬರು, ಇಬ್ಬರು), a negation lesson (negation is taught where its form is: `no-is` ಅಲ್ಲ, `ide` ಇಲ್ಲ, `like-want` ಬೇಡ, `present` ಮಾಡಲ್ಲ, `continuous` ಮಾಡ್ತಾ ಇಲ್ಲ, `past-neg` ಮಾಡಲಿಲ್ಲ), an ability lesson (IGNOU's "ability" goes into `must-can` as ಮಾಡೋಕೆ ಆಗುತ್ತೆ).
- **Present before past:** the roadmap already teaches the present-future (`present`, stage 3) before the past (`past`, stage 4); nothing to change there.
- **Move 1: `vowel-signs` goes from 4th to 2nd**, right after `vowels`. Reason: with the roadmap's order the two consonant lessons would have only the inherent a to build words with, so their practice would be letter naming; with the signs known first (taught on ಕ alone: ಕೈ, ಏಕೆ, ಆಕೆ, ಈಕೆ), `consonants-1` can build ಊಟ, ಆಟ, ಕಾಡು, ಗಾಡಿ, ಕೋಟೆ, ಕಾಗೆ, ಗಿಡ and `consonants-2` ಹುಳಿ, ಹುಲಿ, ಕಾರು, ಕರು, ತಲೆ, ಮನೆ, each from letters already taught. en-pa and en-te made the same move for the same reason. The consonant lessons keep the chart order of rows.
- **Move 2: `locative` goes before `present`** (it was after it in stage 3). Reason: the first things a learner says in the present-future are where they live and work (ನಾನು ಬೆಂಗಳೂರಿನಲ್ಲಿ ಇರ್ತೀನಿ, ನಾನು ಆಫೀಸಿನಲ್ಲಿ ಕೆಲಸ ಮಾಡ್ತೀನಿ), which need -ಅಲ್ಲಿ. With `locative` first, `present` can practise routines with places instead of bare verbs. Cost: `locative` meets ಇದೆ / ಇದ್ದಾನೆ (known from `ide`) rather than action verbs, which is enough.
- **Move 3: `continuous` goes from stage 4 to right after `present`.** Reason: the spoken continuous ಮಾಡ್ತಾ ಇದ್ದೀನಿ is the -ತಾ form of the same stem as ಮಾಡ್ತೀನಿ plus the ಇದ್ದೀನಿ the learner knows from `ide`, so present then continuous teaches one stem twice and contrasts "usually / will" with "right now" while both are fresh; and `time` (a daily routine) then has both forms. IGNOU teaches the continuous last [as the roadmap reports]; no teaching reason to keep it after the past was found: it does not build on the past.
- **Stages** (renamed to match the moves): "Script, sounds and greetings" 6, "First sentences" 9, "Daily life: the present" 8, "The past, and comparing" 4.
- **Renamed:** titles name the point or give a gloss (table); `dative` "To whom and to where: -ಗೆ / -ಕ್ಕೆ"; `continuous` "Right now: ಮಾಡ್ತಾ ಇದ್ದೀನಿ"; `must-can` adds "can" (ಮಾಡೋಕೆ ಆಗುತ್ತೆ).
- **Widened:** `greetings` also teaches ಸರಿ / ಆಯ್ತು and ಹೌದು / ಇಲ್ಲ; `no-is` the negative ಅಲ್ಲ; `ide` the location verb for people (ಇದ್ದಾನೆ / ಇದ್ದಾಳೆ / ಇದ್ದಾರೆ) and ಇಲ್ಲ; `family` the human counting forms and the respect plural; `like-want` ಬೇಡ and ಗೊತ್ತಿಲ್ಲ; `present` the recognition table of written forms.

**The script decks are ordinary decks** (no letter-drill machine needed, §10.1). Rules:
- **Every target is something the voice can read:** a vowel letter, a consonant letter (said with its a), a consonant with a vowel sign, or a word; never a bare vowel sign, anusvara, virama or ottakshara alone (the generator throws on a bare sign).
- **Lone consonant letters:** the pron is the syllable (ಕ *ka*); the meaning names the sound ("ka (k, no puff of air)"). Whether Azure reads a lone letter as the syllable is unverified (request 9); if not, record clips.
- **Meanings must differ** within a deck (the validator checks).
- Letter-card notes give a word built only from letters taught by then.
- `Vowels` (16): the twelve everyday vowel letters (ಅ ಆ ಇ ಈ ಉ ಊ ಎ ಏ ಐ ಒ ಓ ಔ), plus ಆ / ಈ as words (that / this), ಏ (hey) and ಕ. ಋ in the lesson only.

## 10. Typing and script

### 10.1 The script stage (works with ordinary lessons and decks)

Stage 1 is five script lessons and five script decks, all ordinary: nothing depends on the Korean letter-drill machine.

| Lesson | Deck | Letters and signs | What the learner can do after it |
|---|---|---|---|
| `vowels` | Vowels | the 12 everyday vowel letters; ಋ (recognition); ಕ with its inherent a | read a vowel letter; hear short vs long; knows the a is always said |
| `vowel-signs` | Vowel signs | the signs on ಕ; the headstroke; the length stroke | read any consonant + vowel sign once the consonant is known |
| `consonants-1` | Consonants 1: k, ch and ṭ rows | ಕ–ಙ, ಚ–ಞ, ಟ–ಣ | read the first three rows; hear plain vs aspirated; tell retroflex |
| `consonants-2` | Consonants 2: t and p rows, and the rest | ತ–ನ, ಪ–ಮ, ಯ ರ ಲ ವ ಶ ಷ ಸ ಹ ಳ (ೞ ಱ shown as old) | read every modern letter; tell dental from retroflex, ಲ from ಳ |
| `conjuncts` | Conjuncts and anusvara | doubled letters, ottakshara, the arkāvottu, ಕ್ಷ, ಂ, ್, ಃ (recognition) | read whole words, including English loans |

- **How many script lessons:** five, as the roadmap plans (vowel signs moved to second place, §8). Six were considered (splitting the ottakshara from the anusvara), but the anusvara rule is short and every later lesson reuses both.
- **Which conjuncts a beginner must read** (taught in `conjuncts`; every later lesson may use them): the doubled letters (ಕ್ಕ ಗ್ಗ ಚ್ಚ ಜ್ಜ ಟ್ಟ ಡ್ಡ ಣ್ಣ ತ್ತ ದ್ದ ನ್ನ ಪ್ಪ ಬ್ಬ ಮ್ಮ ಯ್ಯ ಲ್ಲ ಳ್ಳ ವ್ವ ಸ್ಸ); the common pairs ್ರ (ಪ್ರೀತಿ, ಶ್ರೀ), ್ಯ (ಧನ್ಯವಾದಗಳು, ಬ್ಯಾಂಕ್, ಆಯ್ತು), ್ವ (ಸ್ವಲ್ಪ), ್ತ (ಮಾಡ್ತೀನಿ, ಹೋಗ್ತೀನಿ: the spoken endings, so these come early), ಸ್ಕ (ನಮಸ್ಕಾರ), ಷ್ಟ (ಇಷ್ಟ, ಎಷ್ಟು), ಕ್ಷ (ಕ್ಷಮಿಸಿ), ಕ್ಟ (ಡಾಕ್ಟರ್), and the arkāvottu ರ್ + consonant (ಕರ್ನಾಟಕ, ಬರ್ತೀನಿ, ಸರ್ಕಾರ). Rare three-letter clusters (ಸ್ತ್ರ) only in recognition; ಜ್ಞ only in recognition (ಜ್ಞಾನ). A conjunct that only Sanskrit words use is glossed in the card note the first time it appears.
- **The rare letters to recognise only:** ಙ ಞ (chart only), ಋ ೃ (ಕೃಷ್ಣ), ಃ (ದುಃಖ), ೞ and ಱ (old texts and inscriptions). The generator throws on ೞ, ಱ, ಁ, ಌ ೡ ೠ ೄ ೢ ೣ, ಽ, ೱ ೲ ೳ, U+0CDD, so they cannot enter stored text by accident.
- The consonants follow the chart order (the rows make aspiration and retroflex / dental visible as a grid) [curriculum convention: the roadmap's grouping; the *Kannada script* article's letter table].
- **If the letter-drill machine is generalised** (request 10), the five decks gain "hear a syllable, tap its letter" drills (ತ / ಟ, ಲ / ಳ, ಎ / ಏ) and "build the word from letters". Nothing in the lessons changes.

### 10.2 What a typed answer is

**Default: word tiles** (`tilesDefault: true`). Kannada writes spaces between words, so tiles are words as written: a noun with its case ending is one tile (ಮನೆಗೆ, ಮನೆಯಲ್ಲಿ, ನನಗೆ), ಜೊತೆ / ಹತ್ತಿರ / ಮೇಲೆ are separate tiles, a verb is one tile (ಹೋಗ್ತೀನಿ, ಮಾಡಲಿಲ್ಲ), the continuous is two (ಮಾಡ್ತಾ ಇದ್ದೀನಿ).

**Typing is optional**, through the existing switch:
- **Kannada script from a phone keyboard.** Apple lists Kannada (Alphabetic), (InScript) and (Transliteration) QuickType keyboards ([Apple, iOS feature availability](https://www.apple.com/ios/feature-availability/)); Gboard has Kannada [inference: not opened, §11]. ### 10.3 Unicode normalisation

- **Stored text:** NFC, Kannada letters only, spaces between words.
- **Two-part vowel signs.** ೀ (U+0CC0) = ಿ U+0CBF + ೕ U+0CD5; ೇ (U+0CC7) = ೆ U+0CC6 + U+0CD5; ೈ (U+0CC8) = U+0CC6 + ೖ U+0CD6; ೊ (U+0CCA) = U+0CC6 + ೂ U+0CC2; ೋ (U+0CCB) = U+0CCA + U+0CD5 (checked in node). NFC recomposes every one; `pracNorm` and the generator both normalise to NFC, so a sign typed in parts matches exactly (measured, §10.5). A bare U+0CD5 or U+0CD6 throws in the generator.
- **ZWNJ / ZWJ.** Kannada web text uses ZWNJ to show a visible virama before a suffix on English loans (the common ಬಸ್ + ZWNJ + ನಲ್ಲಿ) and ZWJ after ರ್ to force the half form instead of the arkāvottu [inference]. The validator rejects U+200B–200F in stored text and `pracNorm` strips ZWNJ / ZWJ from input. **Rule:** stored text has no ZWNJ / ZWJ; loans take the linking vowel before an ending (ಬಸ್ಸಿನಲ್ಲಿ, ಆಫೀಸಿಗೆ, §2.2). A learner who types the ZWJ form of ಕರ್ನಾಟಕ matches exactly (measured).
- **Nukta** (U+0CBC: ಜ಼ for z, ಫ಼ for f) is used by some writers for English and Urdu loans. The course does not use it (ಫೋನ್ is read *fōn* through `FSTEM`); request 1 strips it in comparison so ಫ಼ೋನ್ matches ಫೋನ್ **exactly**. Today it is marked wrong (measured).
- **Anusvara vs a nasal conjunct** (ಬೆಂಗಳೂರು / ಬೆಙ್ಗಳೂರು, ಗಂಟೆ / ಗಣ್ಟೆ): the article notes nasals before a consonant are "usually written as an anusvara"; the conjunct spelling is a slip: "almost" (request 1). It must not touch real doubled nasals (ಅಮ್ಮ, ಕನ್ನಡ, ಅಣ್ಣ): the fold maps both sides the same way, so these still match only themselves (measured).
- **Archaic letters:** ೞ ಱ never in stored text (the generator throws); a learner who types ೞ for ಳ or ಱ for ರ gets "almost" (request 1); the candrabindu ಁ is folded to ಂ.
- **Digits:** neither Western nor Kannada digits (೦–೯, U+0CE6–0CEF) appear in Kannada strings: numbers are words (§2.8). Real-life prices are in Western digits; lessons say so in English and show ₹50 only in English text.
- **No Telugu counterpart needed:** Telugu's rewrite of U+0C33 U+0C4D U+0C32 to U+0C33 U+0C4D U+0C33 (the two spellings of doubled ḷ) has no Kannada equivalent (Kannada writes the doubled retroflex l only as ಳ್ಳ) [inference].
- **Never "almost" in Kannada script:** vowel length (ಿ / ೀ, ೆ / ೇ, ೊ / ೋ, plain / ಾ: ಕೊಡು / ಕೋಡು), dental / retroflex (ತ / ಟ, ನ / ಣ, ಲ / ಳ: ಅನ್ನ / ಅಣ್ಣ, ಹುಲಿ / ಹುಳಿ), single / doubled (ಕತೆ / ಕತ್ತೆ), endings (-ಈನಿ / -ಈರಿ, -ಆನೆ / -ಆಳೆ).

### 10.4 The accent bar

`keys`: `ಂ ್` (anusvara and virama): the two signs learners most often cannot find; a Kannada keyboard has the rest. The bar inserts them after the letter at the caret; NFC handles order. `accents: true` turns missing macrons and dots in romanised answers into "almost".

**Kannada input has no "almost" path today:** in `pracMatch` the `romaAlmost` branch tests Devanagari, Arabic, Gurmukhi and Telugu input; Kannada input falls to the romanised comparison, which only looks at answers outside `[ऀ-෿…]`, a range that contains Kannada (U+0C80–0CFF), so it never matches. The deck quiz (`normT` path) behaves the same. Request 1.

**Which Telugu functions Kannada needs** (each checked in the harness):
- `teNorm` (NFC, strip ZWNJ / ZWJ / danda, the doubled-ḷ rewrite): **needed, changed**: Kannada needs the same stripping plus **removing the nukta U+0CBC**; the doubled-ḷ rewrite has no Kannada counterpart.
- `teSlip` (nasal + virama before a consonant → anusvara; the old Telugu r U+0C31 → U+0C30; U+0C01 dropped): **needed, re-pointed** to Kannada code points: nasals ಙ U+0C99, ಞ U+0C9E, ಣ U+0CA3, ನ U+0CA8, ಮ U+0CAE + virama U+0CCD before U+0C95–0CB9 → ಂ U+0C82; ೞ U+0CDE → ಳ U+0CB3; ಱ U+0CB1 → ರ U+0CB0; ಁ U+0C81 → ಂ U+0C82. Not +0x80 by habit: U+0CDE has no Telugu twin in `teSlip`.
- `teTwin`: **not useful**: its ending pattern is Telugu's person endings after a doubled consonant; on every Kannada case above it returns false (measured), so aliasing kn to te would not catch ಅಣ್ಣ / ಅನ್ನ or ಮಾಡ್ತೀಯಾ / ಮಾಡ್ತೀಯ.
- The Telugu `romaLoose` wrapper (th → t, dh → d, sh → s, doubled consonant → single): **Kannada needs the first three and must not have the fourth.**

**The distractor guard (request 3)** is the generic version of what Telugu solved with a pattern: when typed Latin text reaches an answer only through `romaLoose`, then for each typed word that also folds to a word of a distractor (`x` for `ty`, `o` for `fi`, respelled like the answers), look only at the places where the answer word and the distractor word differ (a vowel's length, a consonant's dot) and require the typed word to agree with the answer there (ā or aa = long; a dot must be typed if the answer has it and the distractor does not). Harness code: `proposed()` in `match.js`. Consequence for learners: on an item that offers a dot-only twin, an English keyboard cannot type the answer (ṇ), so only Kannada script or tiles work there, which is the honest result: *anna* **is** the other word.

`'!strict'` (the fallback until request 3) leaves only the exact comparison, so a romanised answer must carry the macrons and the dots: on a strict item, romanised typing is in practice always wrong, and only Kannada script or tiles work.

### 10.7 `targetScript` and punctuation

- `targetScript`: the character class U+0C80–U+0CE3 (written `[\u0C80-\u0CE3]` in `profile.json`): Kannada letters and signs, **excluding** the Kannada digits (U+0CE6–0CEF) and the Vedic signs U+0CF1–0CF3. `pronAnswersScript` is the whole block U+0C80–U+0CFF, which only decides which answers get a romanised twin.
- **Punctuation:** ASCII . ? ! , as in English; **no danda** in course text. `pracNorm` strips . ? ! anyway, so answers and tiles may carry or omit them.

The Android claim is Google's TalkBack list, not a test. iOS lists a Kannada VoiceOver voice; an AppleVis user reports misreading; Safari's Web Speech access to it was not tested.
- **Keyboards and fonts:** Apple's keyboard list was read; Gboard's Kannada keyboard and the platform font names in §10.6 are the author's knowledge.
- **Learners:** no US count of Kannada speakers was opened; the Census 2015 release does not name Kannada; no survey of motives was found.
- **Unicode:** the Unicode chart PDF redirected and was not read; the decompositions were checked in node, the archaic-letter list comes from the generator's own design and the *Kannada script* article.

**The roadmap and the brief**
- **Present before past:** already true in the roadmap (`present` in stage 3, `past` in stage 4); not changed.
- **Three moves** (§8): `vowel-signs` 4th → 2nd; `locative` before `present`; `continuous` from stage 4 to right after `present`. Stage names changed to match.
- `profile.json` carries fields beyond the brief's shape, as en-pa, en-ur and en-te do: `tilesDefault`, `pronAnswers`, `pronAnswersKeepSpaces`, `pronAnswersScript`, `romaAlmost`, `voiceMatch`, `pronAnswersWarn`.
- The brief asks for "Western digits in prices in English text": done; Kannada strings write numbers as words, because the generator, the voice and the matcher then agree (§2.8, §10.3).
- **The first version of this brief described Telugu** (its language-specific section named Telangana speech and Telugu examples); the lead corrected it within minutes, and this profile follows the corrected Kannada section only.

**Not verified at all**
- How the Azure voice reads lone letters, spoken spellings, length and dot pairs and English loans (request 9).
- That learners read ē / ō and the dots easily; no study compares Kannada romanisations for learners.
- Which romanisation habits Kannada typists really use (*th* for dental t, *ee* for ī): request 2 should be checked against a sample of real typing before it is built.

**Final sweep** (render script, last run): every Kannada run in this file was passed to `respell`; the only throws are, by design, bare signs, letter lists with commas and fragments that the key and the rules name on purpose (vowel signs alone, ಂ, ್, ೕ, ೞ, ಱ, ಁ, ್ಯಾ); the warnings are the intended ones (ಋ, ೃ, ಃ, ಜ್ಞ, ಙ, ಞ). No Telugu characters, no ZWNJ / ZWJ.
