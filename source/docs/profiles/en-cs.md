# English speakers learning Czech: course profile

Course `en-cs`: Czech for English speakers. Every explanation, prompt, `why`, `hint`, meaning and card note is in English. Answers, examples and card targets are in Czech. The machine-readable twin is `tools/target/cs/profile.json`. The `pron` line is generated by `tools/target/cs/respell.js` (`node tools/target/cs/respell.js "<Czech>"` prints `<Czech> -> <pron>`; it exports `respell` and `pronPattern`). Writers never type a pron.

**Evidence tags.**
- **[research-backed]**: a named study or a primary description from a phonetics institute, opened for this profile.
- **[curriculum convention]**: what the syllabi named in the roadmap actually teach.
- **[practitioner opinion]**: a teacher's page with no count of learners behind it.
- **[inference]**: this profile's own reasoning. Anything a writer will copy that is tagged this way is also listed in §11.

Untagged rules in §6 to §10 are design decisions.

## 1. Summary

- **Variant and voice:** Standard Czech as used in the Czech Republic, voice `cs-CZ`. Learners produce the formal **vy** forms. They only recognize the informal **ty** forms. Spoken Czech is named once in §2.3 and is not drilled.
- **Cases:** three are drilled. The subject form, the object form, and the place form. The genitive, dative, instrumental and vocative are named once and are not paradigms.
- **Biggest traps:** vowel length that changes the word (`byt` / `být`); treating the long vowel as the beat; ř; `c` and `ch`; a voiced consonant at the end of a word; soft `d t n` before `i`; gender on `ten / ta / to` and on adjectives; the verb ending; vy with a stranger; the counting rule `1 koruna`, `2 koruny`, `5 korun`.
- **Standing rule:** every practice set re-tests gender (masculine, feminine or neuter form) and, once verbs exist, the verb ending. Not all three cases, and not vowel length. Keywords were tested on 25 why-lines (§7).
- **Pronunciation aid:** an English-letter respelling on every card and example. Long and short vowels look different (`biht` / `beet`). ř is `rzh` or `rsh`, never plain `r`. The beat is CAPITALS on the first syllable. The tool throws when `di / ti / ni` is not on its list.
- **Script:** Latin, with the accent bar `á č ď é ě í ň ó ř š ť ú ů ý ž`. `accents` is false. A missing length mark is wrong, not "almost" (§10).
- **Lessons:** 25. All 24 roadmap items, in roadmap order, plus `traps` at the end. `likes` points at Compliments, so that deck does not need a second new lesson.
- **Decks:** 17, in 5 groups, including Traps for English speakers and Compliments. Prices are in koruna, written as words.

## 2. Variant, register and voice

### 2.1 Variant

The roadmap's variant is Standard Czech (Czech Republic). That is the written standard, `spisovná čeština`, voice `cs-CZ`. [curriculum convention]

The pronunciation facts below are the standard described by the Institute of Phonetics at Charles University and by Karel Tahal's *Grammar of Czech as a Foreign Language* (2010). Where Tahal gives two standard pronunciations, the aid uses the one he calls the usual one. `sh` in `na shledanou` is `skh` (`NAH skhleh-dah-nohw`). Tahal says a voiced `zh` for that cluster is also standard. [research-backed]

### 2.2 You: what learners produce, and what they only recognize

**House rule.** Learners **produce vy** for "you", including "you" to one stranger. They **only recognize ty**.

Produce: `jste`, `máte`, the endings `-áte / -íte / -ujete`, `vás`, `vám`, `váš / vaše`, `Dobrý den`, `Na shledanou`, `Jak se jmenujete`, `Prosím`. A typed answer to "you" is the vy form.

Recognize: `jsi`, `máš`, the endings `-áš / -íš / -uješ`, `tě`, `ti`, `tvůj / tvoje`, `ahoj`, `Jak se jmenuješ`. These appear in multiple choice ("which one is for a friend?") and in a greeting pair. They are never the typed answer to "How are you?" or "What is your name?".

Plural "you" is the same vy. With vy for one person, the verb is the plural form and a participle or adjective stays singular and shows that person's gender: `Jste unavená` (a woman), `Byl jste` (a man). Prompts cue "(a woman)" or "(a man)". [research-backed]

Tahal: ty is for family, classmates, close friends and children (tykání); vy is for more than one person, or for one person as the formal address (vykání), usually with the family name. The past-tense table gives `jste byl` for one man and `jste byla` for one woman. [research-backed]

Charles University's 2026 beginners' plan shows both question pairs in week 1 (`jmenuješ / jmenujete`, `máš / máte`, `jsi / jste`). Recognition of ty is already in that syllabus. Production in this course stays on vy, because a US adult's first Czech is to teachers, offices, a landlord and shop staff. [curriculum convention] [inference]

`vy` in this course is lowercase. The capital `Vy` of a letter is not taught. The offer `Můžeme si tykat?` is one recognition line in the greetings lesson, not a drill.

### 2.3 Spoken Czech, once

**Obecná čeština** is the everyday speech of much of Bohemia, including Prague. It is not the written standard. Learners will hear it. They do not produce it, and a typed answer in those forms is wrong. [research-backed]

Marie Krčmová's encyclopedia entry, the page opened for this profile, describes the usual shifts: `é` toward `ý / í` in some endings and stems, `ý` toward `ej` in the masculine adjective (`dobrej`), a `v` added at the start of some words (`von`), and a shortened `í` in some endings. One note in the greetings lesson says this, with no exercises. Do not put `mlíko`, `dobrej` or `von` on a card as an accepted answer.

### 2.4 Spelling doublets the course allows

One house rule, then the alternates learners will see.

- **Produce `-uju`. Accept `-uji` as the same word.** Tahal lists both for `studovat` (`studuju` and `studuji`) and says the `-u` ending is the usual one. This is a standard doublet, not the spoken Czech of §2.3. Model sentences use `-uju`. A typed answer may be either. [research-backed]
- **Produce `moje` for feminine and neuter.** Tahal gives `moje (= má)` and `moje (= mé)`, and says the short forms can feel like a higher style. `má` and `mé` are recognized, not produced. `tvůj` is recognized only (§2.2). Produce `můj / moje` and `váš / vaše`. [research-backed]
- **`ú` and `ů` are the same sound** (`ooh`). `ú` is written at the start of a morpheme and in loans; `ů` is written in the middle and at the end (`dům / domu`, `stůl`). The aid cannot show the spelling. The vowel lesson teaches it, and a typed answer uses the letter the word is spelled with. [research-backed]
- **`i` and `y` are the same vowel.** `být` and `bít` are both `beet`. `byli` and `byly` are both `BIH-lih`. The letters still matter: `y` does not soften `d t n`, and the spelling distinguishes words the ear does not. [research-backed]

If `diskuse` comes up, spell it that way. Tahal gives it as the example of a loan with hard `di`. [research-backed]

### 2.5 Voice of the English text

US spelling (`color`, `theater`). Short sentences. No articles in the Czech, and the English does not tell the learner to insert one. A prompt that means "you" says who is addressed only by the situation (a stranger, a shop, a teacher). It does not ask the learner to produce ty.

## 4. Error zones of English speakers, ranked

Pronunciation items 1 to 6 are the nine-point teacher list on the Elon Czech page that was opened, checked against Tahal and the Institute of Phonetics. That page is a teacher's list, not a counted study. [practitioner opinion] No experimental study of English-speaking learners of Czech was opened (§11).

Grammar items are what the two syllabi teach first, plus Tahal's description of the forms. They are not a published error corpus.

1. **Vowel length, and the long vowel treated as the beat.** Wrong: `byt` for "to be". Right: `být` (`beet`). Wrong: the beat on the `ý` of `Dobrý den`. Right: `DOH-bree dehn`. Length is independent of the beat. [practitioner opinion] [research-backed]
2. **ř said as r or as zh.** Wrong: `reka` or "zheka". Right: `řeka` (`RZHEH-kah`). After a voiceless consonant, and at the end of a word, it is the voiceless one: `tři` (`trshih`), `tvář` (`tvaahrsh`). [practitioner opinion] [research-backed]
3. **`c` as k or s, and `ch` as English "church".** Wrong: "kena" or "sena". Right: `cena` (`TSEH-nah`). Wrong: "chleb" with English ch. Right: `chléb` (`khlehhp`). [practitioner opinion] [research-backed]
4. **A voiced consonant left voiced at the end, and clusters not matched.** Wrong: `led` with a d. Right: `leht`, the same sound as `let`. Wrong: `kdo` with a k. Right: `gdoh`. `v` devoices itself (`včera` `FCHEH-rah`) and does not voice the consonant before it (`dvě` `dvyeh`). [practitioner opinion] [research-backed]
5. **Soft and hard `d t n`.** Wrong: `děti` with a hard d and t. Right: `DYEH-tyih`. Wrong: `ty` (informal "you") with a soft t. Right: `tih`, because `y` does not soften. Loans stay hard: `politika` is `POH-lih-tih-kah`, not `tyih`. [practitioner opinion] [research-backed]
6. **An inserted vowel in a consonant cluster, and a schwa in an unstressed ending.** Wrong: "vuhlk". Right: `vlk` (`vllk`). Endings keep their vowel: `mluvím` is `MLOO-veem`, with the vowel of the ending still there. [practitioner opinion] [research-backed] Tahal describes `p`, `t` and `k` as unaspirated. The aid cannot show that. Lesson 3 says it in a sentence. [research-backed]
7. **Gender.** Wrong: `ta čaj`, `dobrý káva`. Right: `ten čaj`, `dobrá káva`. Wrong: `Jste unavený` said of a woman. Right: `Jste unavená`. [curriculum convention]
8. **One verb form for every person.** Wrong: `mluvím` for "you speak" to a stranger. Right: `mluvíte`. Wrong: `Já student`. Right: `Jsem student`. The person is in the ending, and `jsem` is required. [curriculum convention]
9. **The object form and the place form.** Wrong: `Dám si káva`. Right: `Dám si kávu`. Wrong: `Mám bratr`. Right: `Mám bratra`. Wrong: `v Praha`. Right: `v Praze` (`FPRAH-zeh`). [curriculum convention]
10. **vy and ty.** Wrong: `Ahoj` or `Jak se jmenuješ` as the line the learner says to a stranger. Right: `Dobrý den`, `Jak se jmenujete`. [research-backed]
11. **The counting rule.** Wrong: `pět koruny`, `dvě koruna`. Right: `jedna koruna`, `dvě koruny`, `pět korun`. [curriculum convention] [research-backed]
12. **False friends that were checked in the Academy dictionary.** `host` is the guest, the person who comes, not the person who invites. `kolej` is a student dorm, and also a rail. `aktuální` is current, of the present moment, not the English word "actual". [research-backed]
13. **Calques.** No article. `To je můj bratr`, not a Czech "the". `líbí se mi` and `chutná mi` put the person in `mi`, not in a subject "I like". `rád / ráda` matches the speaker. [curriculum convention]
14. **The little word in second place.** Wrong: `Jsem byl včera` at the start of the answer when `včera` is the first word. Right: `Včera jsem byl`. Tahal: `jsem` and `jste` take the second slot. The past lesson teaches this. There is no separate clitics lesson. [research-backed]

Aspect (`dělat` / `udělat`) is real and is not in this course. The future lesson says, once, that pairs like that come later. [research-backed]

## 5. Learners

Who, from the pages opened. International students on Czech-taught degrees, including the ČVUT course whose syllabus is a basis of the roadmap, and adults in a beginners' class of the Charles University summer school. The national study-in-Czech page says public universities charge no tuition for programmes taught in Czech, that many people arrive with no Czech, and that English is enough in a big city and among younger people while basic Czech still helps with housing, offices and daily life. [curriculum convention]

What they need first. The sounds that change a word (length, ř, `c`, `ch`), then vy, `být` and `mít`, then gender, then food and the object form. Both syllabi put `být` and `mít` first. ČVUT puts the object form before the place form. The Charles plan drills the object form and does not name the place form as its own point. [curriculum convention]

They do not need all seven cases, the vocative of their own name, or spoken Czech, in this course.

## 6. Pronunciation aid

`pron` is **required** on every card and every example row. It is a generated English-letter respelling. IPA is not used. A card with no pron is not allowed.

The spelling misleads English speakers in the ways §4.1 to §4.6 list. A respelling is justified from the first lesson, because `ř`, `c`, `ch`, `ě`, length, and a voiced letter at the end of a word are all on day one.

The aid is reliable for the words it accepts. It is not a silent guesser. A word with `di`, `dí`, `ti`, `tí`, `ni` or `ní` that is not on the native list or the loan list **throws**. `dě / tě / ně / mě / bě / pě / vě / fě` are spelled, so they do not need the list. Writers add a word to `NATIVE` or `LOAN` in `respell.js` rather than hand-writing a pron.

### 6.1 Key

Read a vowel spelling before the consonants around it. `PRAH` is `p + r + ah`. The `h` of `ah` is not the consonant. The consonant `h` is the start of the next syllable in `Praha` → `PRAH-hah`.

| Aid | Czech | As in |
|---|---|---|
| `ah` / `aah` | `a` / `á` | `pas` `pahs`, `pás` `paahs` |
| `eh` / `ehh` | `e` / `é` | `peče` `PEH-cheh`, `péče` `PEHH-cheh` |
| `ih` / `ee` | `i` or `y` / `í` or `ý` | `byt` `biht`, `být` `beet`. Short `i` is the vowel of "sit"; long `í` is the vowel of "seat", held, with no English y-glide. [research-backed] |
| `oh` / `ohh` | `o` / `ó` | `oh` in `DOH`. `ó` is rare and almost only in loans; the aid writes `ohh` when the spelling has it |
| `oo` / `ooh` | `u` / `ú` or `ů` | `domu` `DOH-moo`, `domů` `DOH-mooh`, `dům` `doohm` |
| `ohw`, `ow`, `ehw` | `ou`, `au`, `eu` | `dlouhý` `DLOHW-hee`, `auto` `OW-toh`, `euro` `EHW-roh` |
| `rzh` / `rsh` | ř, voiced / voiceless | `řeka` `RZHEH-kah`, `tři` `trshih`. Never plain `r` |
| `rr` / `ll` | syllabic r / l | `krk` `krrk`, `vlk` `vllk`. No vowel is inserted |
| `ts` | `c` | `cena` `TSEH-nah` |
| `ch` | `č` | `čaj` `chahy`. This is English "ch", the opposite of the Czech letter `ch` |
| `sh` / `zh` | `š` / `ž` | `šest` `shehst`, `žena` `ZHEH-nah` |
| `kh` | `ch`, and `h` when it devoices | `chléb` `khlehhp`, `bůh` `boohkh` |
| `h` | voiced `h` | the second syllable of `Praha` |
| `y` | Czech `j` | `ahoj` `AH-hohy`, `já` `yaah` |
| `j` | `dž` | rare in this course |
| `dy` `ty` `ny` | soft `ď ť ň` | `děkuji` `DYEH-koo-yih`, the clitic `ti` `tyih` |
| CAPITALS | the beat | `dobrý` `DOH-bree` |

**The beat is marked.** It is the first syllable of a word of two or more syllables, in CAPITALS. A one-syllable word is all lowercase, because the beat has nowhere else to go. The capital is the conventional beat, not extra loudness and not extra length. The Institute of Phonetics notes that the syllable after the beat is often the louder one. Lesson 2 says that in one sentence, so learners do not shout the capital. [research-backed]

**Words that do not keep the beat on their own first syllable.**

- A one-syllable preposition with a vowel takes the beat, and the next word loses it: `do Prahy` → `DOH prah-hih`, `na shledanou` → `NAH skhleh-dah-nohw`. The list is `ve, ke, ku, ze, do, na, po, pro, za, od, pod, nad, před, přes, bez, při, o, u, ob, skrz`. [research-backed]
- `v`, `k`, `s` and `z` have no vowel. They join the next word: `v Praze` → `FPRAH-zeh`, `z Prahy` → `SPRAH-hih`, `s mlékem` → `SMLEHH-kehm`. The `é` of `mlékem` is `ehh`. [research-backed]
- Enclitics are unstressed. The one-syllable ones (`se`, `si`, `mi`, `jsem`, `jsi`, `jste`) are lowercase. `byla jsem` is `BIH-lah ysehm`. [research-backed]
- `ten`, `ta`, `to` and `pan` before a noun do not take the beat. They are one syllable, so they are already lowercase, and the noun keeps its capital: `pan Novák` → `pahn NOH-vaahk`. [research-backed]
- A preposition of two syllables does not take the noun's beat. Tahal's list: `během, kromě, vedle, kolem, okolo, blízko, podle, místo, proti, naproti, kvůli, mezi`. Each word keeps its own first syllable. [research-backed]
- The negative `ne-` is not an exception. It is the first syllable: `nejsem` → `NEHY-sehm`. [research-backed]

A capital later than the first syllable does not match `pronPattern`. `doh-BREE` is rejected.

`ty` (informal "you") is `tih`. The clitic `ti` is `tyih`. Hard `t` plus the vowel `ih` is `tih`. Soft `ť` plus `ih` is `tyih`.

### 6.2 `pronPattern`

The pattern is the string `pronPattern()` prints. A syllable is consonants, one vowel spelling from the key, consonants. A word is either one lowercase syllable, or a first syllable in CAPITALS and the rest lowercase, or (after a preposition that took the beat) a word of two or more syllables all lowercase. Words are separated by a space. The pattern is stored in `profile.json` and is not retyped by hand.

### 6.4 The generator

`tools/target/cs/respell.js`. From `source/`:

```
node tools/target/cs/respell.js "Dobrý den"
```

prints `Dobrý den -> DOH-bree dehn`. `node tools/target/cs/respell.js --pattern` prints the regex. `node tools/target/cs/respell.js --test` runs the locked examples.

What the generator applies, in order: rewrite `sedm` / `osm` (and `sedmnáct`, `sedmdesát`, `osmnáct`, `osmdesát`) with the short `u` Tahal gives; rewrite word-initial `dc` (`dcera` → `TSEH-rah`); palatalize native `d t n` before `i / í`, leave listed loans hard, throw otherwise; `ě` rules; diphthongs `ou au eu`; a `y` sound between `i` and a following vowel (`Anglie` → `AHN-glih-yeh`); regressive voicing, with `v` not voicing the consonant before it; `h` after a voiceless consonant becomes `kh` (`shoda` → `SKHOH-dah`); final devoicing; ř voiced or voiceless from its neighbor; syllabic `r` and `l` when they are not next to a vowel. Identical doubled consonants inside a word are said once (`měkký` → `MNYEH-kee`).

### 6.5 Where it is wrong, or where it refuses

- **It throws** on `tenis`, `aktivní`, `kredit`, `univerzitní`, and on any other word with `di / dí / ti / tí / ni / ní` that is not listed. `aktivní` mixes a hard loan `ti` with a Czech soft `-ní`. Do not guess. Add the word, or rephrase.
- **`se` does not take the beat.** The same spelling is the reflexive. `Jak se máte` is `yahk seh MAAH-teh`. The preposition "with" is usually `s` (`s mlékem`) or, before a cluster, the form the tool will not special-case. Instrumental `se` is not a drilled pattern.
- **`ode`, `pode`, `nade`, `beze`, `přede`** are two syllables and do not pull the beat. Tahal's two-syllable prepositions do not either (§6.1).
- **A long noun still loses its beat to a one-syllable preposition.** `k doktorovi` is `GDOHK-toh-roh-vih`. The phonology page says that with a word of four or more syllables, speakers sometimes keep the beat on the noun. The aid always puts it on the preposition.
- **The glottal stop is not written.** Before a vowel, `z` and `v` devoice as Tahal describes for `z Anglie` and `v Evropě`: `z Anglie` → `SAHN-glih-yeh`. There is no mark for the stop.
- **`i` and `y` are not distinguished.** That is correct for the sound and useless for the spelling. The vowel lesson does the spelling.
- **`n` before `k` or `g` is written `n`.** `banka` is `BAHN-kah`, `angličtina` is `AHN-glihch-tyih-nah`. It is the n of English "bank". The dictionary's line-break for `angličtina` is `ang-lič-ti-na`. The hyphen in the aid is the beat, not that line-break.
- **Loan length follows the spelling.** The Academy's loan page says many loans have two accepted lengths (`procedura` / `procedúra`). The aid says the length that is written.
- **One-syllable words show no beat.** `byl jsem` is `bihl ysehm`. `byla jsem` is `BIH-lah ysehm`, and there the beat is visible.
- **A doubled consonant at a prefix boundary is shortened.** `měkký` should be one `k`, and it is. `oddat` or `nejjasnější` would be wrong. Do not use them.
- **`p t k` aspiration and secondary stress are not shown.**
- **`Kč` throws** (`no vowel`). Write `korun`.
- A hyphen in the spelling becomes a space. Write `email` as one word if it is one word.
- `v`, `k`, `s` or `z` alone throws. Put the next word, or use `ve / ke / ze`.

### 6.6 What lesson 1 must teach so the aid can be read

The letters `č š ž ř ě ď ť ň`, and the six aid pieces `ch sh zh rzh kh dy/ty/ny`. That `c` is `ts` and Czech `ch` is `kh`. That `y` in the aid is the Czech `j`, and `j` in the aid is the rare `dž`. That CAPITALS are the beat and not the long vowel. That `rr` and `ll` have no vowel. Lesson 2 then teaches `ah` against `aah`, `ih` against `ee`, and the rest of the length pairs, with `byt` / `být` and `dobrý`.

## 7. Standing practice rule

**Two things, and only these two: gender, and the verb ending.**

- **Gender.** `ten / ta / to`, the adjective (`dobrý / dobrá / dobré`), and the past participle (`byl / byla / bylo`) match the noun or the person. A masculine person in the object form (`bratr / bratra`) counts as gender too.
- **The verb ending.** `jsem / jste`, `dělám / děláte`, `mluvím / mluvíte`, `studuju / studujete`, and the same for `mít`, `moct`, `muset`, `chtít`.

**Why these two.** They are in almost every sentence once the early lessons have introduced them. The three cases are not: the object form starts at lesson 13, the place form at lesson 20, and a sentence like `Jsem student` has no object form to test. Seven cases cannot be the rule. Vowel length is the clearest sound error (§4.1), and it is enforced by `accents: false`, by the three sound lessons, and by `!strict` on a fill whose options differ only by a mark. It is not the standing rule. Putting it there would turn about half of every order block and every type block into a length pair, including in the restaurant lesson and the case lessons.

This is the decision this profile is least sure of. A reviewer can make vowel length the standing rule instead, and take gender or the verb ending out of the keyword count. The reason to keep length out is in the paragraph above.

**Count.** `minOrder` 2, `minType` 2. In every lesson, at least two order items and two type items have a why-line the keywords match. A 14-item set has three order and three type, so two of each is the bar.

**Before `byt`.** The alphabet, vowel and ř lessons have no verb paradigm yet. All four keyword items are gender: `ten / ta / to` and one adjective (`dlouhý / dlouhá`). Those lessons introduce `ten / ta / to` as "this", and `gender` explains them. From `byt` on, at least one of the order items and one of the type items is a verb ending; the others may be gender. The validator only counts keyword hits. It cannot check the split. The split is a writer rule.

**What counts as hinging.** The why matches the keywords, and the item is actually about that point. A distractor is the other gender (`ten / ta / to`, `dobrý / dobrá / dobré`, `-ý / -á / -é`) or another person of the same verb (`jsem / jste`, `dělám / děláte`). Do not put the keywords on a why whose point is length, ř, or a case name.

**Keywords** (flags `i`). ASCII, so `\b` is an ASCII word boundary.

```
\b(?:masculine|feminine|neuter) (?:form|ending|agreement)s?\b|\bagrees? with\b|\bverb ending\b|\b(?:I|you|he|she|we|they|vy|ty|on|ona|ono|my|oni)-form\b
```

Write "masculine form", "feminine ending", "neuter agreement", "agrees with", "verb ending", "I-form", "vy-form", "she-form", "we-form". Bare "masculine", bare "agreement", "long vowel", and "the accusative case" do not match. For "we", write "we-form". "my-form" also matches and collides with English "my"; do not use it.

1. **alphabet.** `č š ž ř ě ď ť ň`, plus `c` = `ts` and `ch` = `kh`. The trap is reading `č` as the Czech `ch`, and `c` as k. `ten / ta / to` appear here only so the standing rule has a gender to test. The gender lesson explains them.
2. **vowels.** `byt / být`, `pas / pás`, `dům / domu`, and the beat on the first syllable of `dobrý`. The trap is ignoring the length mark, or putting the beat on the long vowel. `i` and `y` are the same sound and different spellings (§2.4).
3. **r-ch.** Voiced and voiceless ř, `ch` as `kh`, final devoicing (`led` = `leht`), syllabic `l` and `r` (`vlk`, `čtvrtek`). The trap is an inserted "uh", and a d at the end of `led`. `p t k` are unaspirated; say so in a sentence, since the aid cannot.
4. **greetings.** Produce `Dobrý den`, `Na shledanou`, `Děkuji`, `Prosím`. Recognize `ahoj`. The four time-of-day lines are in §10. One sentence on spoken Czech (§2.3), with no exercise. The trap is `ahoj` to a stranger.
5. **byt.** `jsem, jste, je, jsme, jsou`. `jsi` is on the recognition list. `To je…`, `Kdo je to?`, `Co je to?`. The trap is leaving out `jsem`, or producing `jsi`.
6. **intro.** `Jmenuji se…`, `Jsem z Prahy`, jobs and languages (`student / studentka`, `mluvím česky`). `Jsem z` and `do` take the form `Prahy`, `školy`. Teach those as chunks. Do not build a genitive table. The trap is `Já student`, and `jmenuješ` as the typed line.
7. **gender.** `ten, ta, to` with a person and a thing. Nouns in `-a` are often feminine, `-o` often neuter, a consonant often masculine, with the usual exceptions named as exceptions (`muž` is masculine, `náměstí` is neuter). The trap is an English "the" with no gender.
8. **verbs.** `dělat` (`-ám`), `mluvit` (`-ím`), `studovat` (`-uju`, and `-uji` accepted). Produce the vy ending, recognize the ty ending. The trap is one ending for every person.
9. **mit.** `mám / máte` for "have". `mám rád / mám ráda` for "I like", and the `rád / ráda` matches the speaker. The trap is `rád` said by a woman, and `mám` for "you have".
10. **negation.** `ne-` on the verb, stressed, because it is the first syllable: `nejsem`, `nemám`. Yes/no questions are the same words with a question mark. `ano` and `ne`. The trap is a separate "do" or "not" word, and the beat moved off `ne-`.
11. **numbers.** 0–100, phone numbers as digits the learner reads one by one in Czech words. The counting rule: 1 takes `koruna`, 2–4 take `koruny`, 5 and above take `korun`. Say once that `korun` is the genitive plural, and do not drill the case. The trap is `pět koruny`.
12. **adjectives.** `dobrý, dobrá, dobré`, and the same three endings on `hezký`, `český`, `nový`. `Jaký je?` The trap is a single form `dobrý` for every noun. `dobří` (masculine persons, plural) is one recognition note, not the drill.
13. **accusative.** Object form. Feminine `-a` → `-u` (`káva / kávu`). A masculine person changes (`bratr / bratra`). A masculine thing often does not (`čaj / čaj`). Neuter does not (`pivo / pivo`). `Dám si kávu`, `Mám bratra`. The trap is the subject form in the object slot.
14. **restaurant.** `Kolik to stojí?`, `Jedna káva, prosím`, the three koruna forms again, `pivo`, `polévka`, `účet`. The trap is a dollar sign, or the wrong koruna form.
15. **family.** `matka, otec, bratr, sestra, dítě`, jobs, and `můj / moje`, `váš / vaše`. `tvůj` is recognition. `jeho` does not change; `její` follows the adjective. The trap is `tvůj` as the learner's own word for a stranger's relative.
16. **likes.** `líbí se mi`, `chutná mi`, `To je mi líto`, `Všechno nejlepší`. `mi` and `vám` are the dative, named once, not a paradigm. This lesson's deck is Compliments. The trap is "I like" with `já` as the subject of `líbí`.
17. **modals.** `můžu / můžete`, `musím / musíte`, `chci / chcete`. The trap is an infinitive with no modal ending, and the ty form as the typed answer.
18. **time.** `Kolik je hodin?`, days, `v pondělí` (the `v` devoices and joins: `FPOHN-dyeh-lee`). Hours use the place form only as the chunk `v … hodin` if it appears; the place-form lesson is later. The trap is an English "o'clock" calque and a hard `t` in `pondělí`.
19. **motion.** `jít` (`jdu`, `půjdu`) on foot, `jet` (`jedu`, `pojedu`) by vehicle. `do Prahy`, `na univerzitu` as chunks for "to". The trap is one verb for both, and the beat on the second syllable of `studovat`-like loans. `půjdu` and `pojedu` are introduced here and used again in `future`.
20. **locative.** Place form after `v` and `na` for "where": `v Praze`, `na poště`, `v divadle`, `ve škole`. `ve` and `na` take the beat. The trap is the subject form after `v` (`v Praha`).
21. **directions.** `Kde je nádraží?`, `Jak se dostanu na nádraží?`, `rovně`, `doleva`, `doprava`. "Where" is the place form; "which way" uses `do` and `na` plus the chunk from `motion`. The trap is one preposition for both.
22. **shopping.** `Co chcete?`, clothes (`tričko`, `černý`), `účtenka`, sizes as words. The trap is ty (`Co chceš?`) as the line to the shop assistant.
23. **past.** `byl jsem / byla jsem`, `byl jste / byla jste`, `dělal / dělala`. The cue is "(a man)" or "(a woman)". `jsem` is in second place (`Včera jsem byl`). The trap is a single `byl` for a woman, and `jsi` as the typed auxiliary.
24. **future.** `budu` + infinitive (`Budu studovat`), plus `půjdu` and `pojedu`. One sentence that aspect pairs come later. The trap is the present used for a clear future plan, and `půjdu` used for a trip by metro.
25. **traps.** False friends and calques from §4.12 and §4.13, plus one review of `pět korun` and of vy. No new grammar. Empty roadmap field.

## 10. Typing and script

Czech is Latin, left to right. The letters beyond `a–z` are `á č ď é ě í ň ó ř š ť ú ů ý ž`.

**Accent bar** (`keys`): `á č ď é ě í ň ó ř š ť ú ů ý ž`. Precomposed letters, separated by spaces. No capitals: the matcher lowercases before it compares. No combining acute and no combining háček.

**`accents` is false.** The app's `foldAcc` strips every combining mark after NFD. It protects `ñ` only. It does not protect a Czech length mark or a háček. With `accents: true`, `accLoose` is on for this target, and `pracMatch` then accepts a folded answer as "almost". That would score `byt` for `být`, `dum` for `dům`, `zena` for `žena`, `rada` for `řada`, and `domu` for `domů`. A missing length mark would be "almost". So `accents` is false. With that flag, `accLoose` is false and the matcher stops before the fold. A missing length mark is wrong. A missing háček is wrong. `domů` typed as `domú` is wrong: `ú` and `ů` fold to the same `u`, and the flag is what stops that being "almost".

The check was a copy of `foldAcc` from `src/app.html`, run in node. `byt` and `být` fold to `byt`. `být` and `bít` do not fold together (`byt` against `bit`): `i` and `y` are different letters, not marks. `domů`, `domu` and `domú` all fold to `domu`. `rada` and `řada` fold to `rada`. `žena`, `čaj`, `děkuji`, `město` and `náměstí` fold to the unmarked letters.

**`!strict` is still required on a fill** whose distractor folds to the same string as the answer. `tools/target/validate.js` demands that mark even when `accents` is false. Pairs that need it: `byt / být`, `pas / pás`, `dal / dál`, `peče / péče`, `vila / víla`, `dům / dum`, `domů / domu`, `domů / domú`, `rada / řada`, and any other pair that differs only by `á é í ó ú ů ý` or by `č ď ě ň ř š ť ž`. `být / bít` does not fold together. It is a real wrong answer without `!strict`.

**A typed answer must include** every length mark and every háček, and the right choice of `i / y` and of `ú / ů`. **It need not include** capitals or sentence punctuation. The matcher lowercases and strips `. , ! ?`. An apostrophe is not Czech spelling. A hyphen still scores as "almost" if the answer's only difference is the hyphen (`pracMatch` does that before it looks at `accents`). Do not build an item whose point is a hyphen.

**`targetScript`:** `[A-Za-zÁČĎÉĚÍŇÓŘŠŤÚŮÝŽáčďéěíňóřšťúůýž]`. A Czech target matches it. The pron is checked against `pronPattern`, not against this class.

No app change. The flag `accents: false` is the switch the app already has. Nothing in `src/app.html` was edited.
