# English speakers learning Swedish: course profile

Course `en-sv`: Swedish for English speakers. Every explanation, prompt, `why`, `hint`, meaning and card note is in English. Answers, examples and card targets are in Swedish. Machine-readable twin: `tools/target/sv/profile.json`, from the same decisions as §6, §8 and §9, so the two files agree. The pronunciation aid is generated by `tools/target/sv/respell.js` (§6). Writers never hand-write a `pron`.

**Evidence tags.**
- **[research-backed]**: an empirical study that was opened. The study is named. **(not English-L1)** means the learners did not have English as a first language, so the finding is a pointer, not proof, for this course.
- **[curriculum convention]**: what a textbook or a published course outline does.
- **[practitioner opinion]**: a teacher's site, with no study behind it.
- **[inference]**: this profile's own reasoning. Claims of that kind are listed for a native check in §11.
- **[tertiary]**: a Wikipedia or Wiktionary page that was opened. Useful for forms and standard descriptions, not for how English speakers err.

Untagged rules in §6 to §10 are design decisions, not findings.

## 1. Summary

- **Course:** `en-sv`, Standard Swedish of Sweden for adult beginners, explanations in US English. 26 lessons in 3 stages, 18 decks in 5 groups. Voice `sv-SE`.
- **Register:** `du` to one person, including staff. `ni` is for more than one person, or for a company. `ni` to one person is not taught as the polite form (§2.3). No capital *Du*.
- **Biggest traps:** long and short vowels, especially `å ä ö u y` and the letter `o`; the sj-sound versus the tj-sound; `en` / `ett` and the definite suffix; verb second and where `inte` goes (§4).
- **Standing rule (§7):** every practice set re-tests (a) `en` / `ett`, the definite suffix and the adjective `-t`, and (b) verb second and where `inte` goes. At least 2 `order` and 2 `type` items hinge on it. Swedish verbs do not change for person, so verb endings are not the rule.
- **Pron (§6):** required. Generated English-letter respelling. Stress in CAPITALS. Pitch accent is not marked. `sj` and `tj` stay different. Reliable on everyday words; the weak spots are listed in §6.5.
- **Lessons (§8):** all 24 roadmap items. `en-ett` moves up, before numbers. Two lessons added: `compliments` and `traps`.
- **Typing (§10):** `accents: true`, keys `å ä ö`. A missing ring or umlaut is "almost" only if the app change in §10 is not built: today `å` and `ä` both fold to `a`, so `här`, `hår` and `har` match each other. That is the app change to build.
- **Evidence:** no opened study of English-speaking adults' Swedish grammar errors (§11). No native speaker has checked this profile.

## 2. Variant, register and voice

### 2.1 Variant: Standard Swedish of Sweden

The roadmap (`docs/roadmaps/sv.json`) sets "Standard Swedish (Sweden)" and the voice `sv-SE`. Decisions that follow:

- **Pronunciation** is Central Swedish, the kind a `sv-SE` voice produces. Nine vowels, each long and short. The sj-sound and the tj-sound are separate. `r` plus `t d n l s` in the same word is one tongue-tip (retroflex) sound. Pitch accent is real, and it is not taught (§6).
- **Say once, do not drill:** Finland Swedish does not use the same pitch accent or the same retroflexes. Southern Sweden often has a uvular `r` and then no retroflex. [tertiary: Swedish phonology]
- **Colloquial forms** `dom`, `nån`, `mej`, `dej`, `sej`, `sen` are for recognition in a note, never the accepted typed answer. The aid may show the spoken shape (`de` and `dem` are both `dawm`). The spelling stays `de`, `dem`, `mig`, `dig`, `sig`.
- **Dates** day before month. **Time:** timetables in the 24-hour clock. `halv tre` is 2:30, not 3:30, the same trap as German *halb drei*. **Money:** `en krona`, `tio kronor`.

### 2.2 What the syllabus actually covers

[curriculum convention] The roadmap basis is Rivstart A1+A2, units 1–6, as a 30-week Level 1. Its own note says the outline lists themes and grammar, not order. The lesson order, the two sounds lessons, possessives, directions and the titles are the roadmap author's judgment.

The BHASVIC outline that was opened is that 30-week course. Its file describes a wider slice of the same book than the roadmap's "units 1–6" line. King's College London's Level 1 outline (Rivstart A1+A2, 2014) says a complete beginners' level covers about chapters 1–9, in three parts. Part 1 teaches the alphabet, articles, present, imperative, pronouns, questions, word order and singular nouns. Part 2 adds the auxiliary plus infinitive, plurals, `tycker om`, object pronouns, possessives and the past. Part 3, which this course does **not** teach, adds `någon` / `ingen`, the definite plural as a lesson of its own, comparison, the supine, perfect versus past, ordinals, and `gå` versus `åka`.

This course stays inside the roadmap's grammar list. Supine, perfect and comparison are not added lessons.

### 2.3 `du` and `ni`

[tertiary] The Swedish Language Council's Frågelådan (question 25179) says `du`, written in lowercase, is the normal way to address one person, including in customer service. `ni`, also lowercase, is for several people and for a company or other organisation. Opinions differ: some hear `ni` to one person as polite, others as distant.

House rule, [inference] from that page:

- Teach `du` for one person: a friend, a classmate, a waiter, a shop assistant, a boss, a doctor.
- Teach `ni` for several people (`Hur mår ni?`) and for addressing a company (`Kan ni hjälpa mig?`).
- Do not teach `ni` as the formal singular, the way German teaches *Sie*. `du` to one person is not rude.
- Never capitalise `du` or `ni`.
- A prompt's English "you" needs a cue only when `ni` versus `du` is the point (`you all`, `the café`). Otherwise the answer is `du`.
- `hen` (in SAOL since April 2015; the Language Council recommends `hen`, not `henom`) is recognised once in `pronouns`. It is not drilled. [tertiary: Swedish grammar]

### 2.4 Spelling house rule

One spelling in typed answers. Alternates go in a note, not in the answer list.

| Write | Alternate the learner will see | Why this one |
|---|---|---|
| `de`, `dem` | `dom` in speech and in informal writing | [tertiary] Språkrådet, 23 March 2023: `de` and `dem` remain the norm for standard Swedish. `dom` is colloquial. |
| `mig`, `dig`, `sig` | `mej`, `dej`, `sej` | The same policy: the aid shows the spoken form (`mey`, `dey`, `sey`); the spelling stays the standard one. |
| `idag`, `igår`, `imorgon` | `i dag`, `i går`, `i morgon`, especially in newspapers | The roadmap's own example is `Idag jobbar jag`. [tertiary] Wiktionary on `idag`: `i dag` is common in newspaper writing; `idag` is the more common form otherwise. SAOL itself was not opened (§11). |
| `kafé` | `café` | Avoids an acute the learner rarely needs. `idé` and `armé` keep `é` if they appear. |
| `ska` | `skall` | `ska` is the everyday form. Whether current SAOL still lists `skall` as a main form was not checked (§11). |
| Compounds as one word | a hyphen in a learner's guess | `kanelbulle`, `smörgås`, `tjugoett`. A missing hyphen scores "almost" in the app (§10). |
| No noun capitals | English-style capitals | Days and months are lowercase: `måndag`, `januari`. |
| Genitive `-s`, no apostrophe | `Anna's` | `Annas`. The optional apostrophe on an s-final word (`Lars'`) is not asked for. |

### 2.5 Voice of the English text

US English in prompts and notes (`apartment` is fine; say `flat` only in a note). Short sentences. Every Swedish word in an English sentence is in italics in prose, and plain in a card target. Do not call `en` / `ett` "masculine and feminine". The terms are in §3.

## 3. Glossary

Use these English terms, each in this sense, and no synonym for them.

| Term | Use it for | Do not call it |
|---|---|---|
| common gender, en-word | a noun that takes `en` and the suffix `-en` (`en bil`, `bilen`) | masculine, feminine, "the en gender" |
| neuter, ett-word | a noun that takes `ett` and the suffix `-et` (`ett hus`, `huset`) | "the ett gender" |
| indefinite article | `en` or `ett` | "a-word" |
| definite suffix | `-en` / `-n` or `-et` / `-t` stuck on the noun | "the ending -en" as a vague label; say definite suffix |
| front article | `den`, `det`, `de` in front of an adjective plus a definite noun (`den stora bilen`) | the definite suffix. Recognition only in this course |
| plural ending | `-or`, `-ar`, `-er` / `-r`, `-n`, or no ending | a "case" |
| verb second | the finite verb is the second element of a main clause | "inverted word order" as the name of the rule |
| sentence adverb | `inte`, and later `alltid`, `ofta`, `aldrig`: after the verb in a main clause | "not-word" |
| present `-r` | `jag bor`, `han talar`, `vi pratar`. The `-r` is the same for every person | a person ending |
| modal | `vill`, `kan`, `måste`, `ska` plus an infinitive with no `att` | a helping verb, in this course |
| `att`-infinitive | `att` plus the verb (`att läsa`) after `tycker om`, `gillar`, and similar | the modal pattern. Modals take no `att` |
| adjective ending | `-` / `-t` / `-a`: `en stor bil`, `ett stort hus`, `stora hus` | agreement, as the name on the card. The why-line says adjective ending |
| possessive | `min` / `mitt` / `mina`. It agrees with the thing owned, not with the owner | "my" as a grammar term |
| `sin` | `sin` / `sitt` / `sina`: the subject's own | a reflexive pronoun in the why-line. Say `sin` |
| quantity | long vowel plus short consonant, or short vowel plus long consonant, in a stressed syllable | "long and short" without saying which consonant |
| sj-sound | the sound in `sju`, `skjorta`, `station`. Written `sj` in the aid | "the sh sound" as if it were the same as `tj` |
| tj-sound | the sound in `tjugo`, `köpa`, `kyrka`. Written `tj` in the aid | sj |
| retroflex | `rt`, `rd`, `rn`, `rl`, `rs` as one tongue-tip sound (`bord`, `barn`, `först`) | two English sounds, in the note that teaches it |
| pitch accent | the two word tones. Named once, not marked in the aid, not drilled | tone, as if it were Mandarin |

`en` the article and `en` the number are the same spelling. The aid has one pronunciation (§6.5).

## 4. Error zones of English speakers, ranked

No opened study counts errors by English-speaking adults learning Swedish. The pairs below are the places the grammar, the teaching sites and the false-friend lists agree. Rank is [inference] from how early and how often they hit, not from a frequency table.

### 4.1 Quantity, and the vowel letters

[tertiary] Swedish has nine vowels, each long and short. A stressed syllable is long vowel plus short consonant (`mat`, `glas`, `bok`) or short vowel plus long consonant (`matt`, `glass`, `bock`). The spelling usually shows this by a double consonant after a short stressed vowel. It does not double a final `m`, and it does not double `n` in a short list (`den`, `han`, `hon`, `kan`, `man`, `men`, `vem`, and others). [tertiary: Swedish orthography]

[research-backed] Thorén's poster (Umeå) treats stress and quantity as the priorities for being understood. Duration is the cue to both. The tonal word accent matters less, varies by region, and disappears in singing. Front rounded vowels (`y`, `u`, `ö`) can be imperfect and still be understood. An English `sh` or a retroflex `sh` for the sj-sound stays clear; older teaching over-weighted the exact tongue shape. Bannert 1987 is the stress result the poster cites. American English listeners in Thorén's thesis work are described as hearing Swedish quantity through the English tense/lax habit. [research-backed, the thesis text that was retrieved]

Wrong → right, for the vowels lesson:

| Wrong | Right | Why it fails |
|---|---|---|
| `här`, `hår` and `har` said alike | `här` `haer`, `hår` `hohr`, `har` `hahr` | Three words. The app currently folds `å` and `ä` together (§10). |
| `mat` and `matt` said alike | `maht` versus `mahtt` | Long `a` plus short `t`, versus short `a` plus long `t`. |
| `bok` with the vowel of English "book" | `boohk` | Long `o` here is the vowel of "food". The aid writes `ooh` so it is not the English word. |
| `god morgon` with the vowel of "good" | `goohd MAWR-gawn` | Same trap. `gå` is `goh`, a different vowel. |

### 4.2 The sj-sound, the tj-sound, and soft k / g

[tertiary: Swedish phonology; Wiktionary pronunciation appendix] The sj-sound (`sju`, `skjorta`, `stjärna`, `station`) and the tj-sound (`tjugo`, `köpa`, `kyrka`, `kjol`) are different phonemes. English speakers collapse both to "sh". `sju` (7) and the start of `tjugo` (20) then become the same word.

Soft `k` and `g` at the start of a word, immediately before `e i y ä ö`, are the tj-sound and `j`. Exceptions the aid must not "correct": `kille` ("guy") has a hard `k` [tertiary: Wiktionary `/²kɪlːɛ/`]; `kex` is hard `/kɛks/` in Svealand and Norrland, soft in Götaland. The house rule is the Central Swedish hard `k`. `sk` before `r` stays `sk` (`skriva`). `fiske` keeps a hard `k`.

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| `sju` and `tjugo` both starting with "shoo" | `sjueh` versus `TJUEH-gaw` |
| `köpa` with a `k` | `TJEUH-pah` |
| `kille` with the tj-sound | `KIHL-eh` |
| `skola` with an sj-sound | `SKOOH-lah` (the vowel is `o`, not a front vowel) |

### 4.3 `en` / `ett`, and the definite suffix

[tertiary: Swedish grammar] Two genders. Common gender is about three quarters of nouns, neuter about a quarter. The definite noun adds a suffix: `bilen`, `huset`. There is no separate word for "the" in the simple definite. When an adjective is in front of a definite noun, Swedish also puts `den` / `det` / `de` in front (`den stora bilen`). That double definiteness is recognition only here. The roadmap's adjective lesson is the indefinite pattern `en stor bil`, `ett stort hus`, `stora hus`.

[practitioner opinion] Elon.io's error overview, a teaching site and not a study, lists `en hus` for `ett hus`, and `den stora bil` with the suffix missing.

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| `en hus` | `ett hus` |
| `den stora bil` | `den stora bilen` (recognition) |
| `ett bil` | `en bil`, `bilen` |

English "the" does not tell you `-en` or `-et`. The gender has to be learned with the noun. Every noun card carries `en` or `ett`.

### 4.4 Verb second, and where `inte` goes

[tertiary: Swedish grammar] In a main clause the finite verb is the second element. If something other than the subject is first, the subject follows the verb: `Idag jobbar jag`, not `Idag jag jobbar`. In a yes/no question the verb is first: `Vill du ha kaffe?` `inte` stands after the finite verb in a main clause: `Jag förstår inte`. In a subordinate clause it stands before the verb: `att jag inte förstår`. That last pattern is one example in `negation`, not a whole extra lesson.

[practitioner opinion] Elon lists `Imorgon jag ska jobba` → `Imorgon ska jag jobba`, `Jag inte förstår` → `Jag förstår inte`, and `Gör du vill ha kaffe?` → `Vill du ha kaffe?` (a modal does not take a `gör`-question).

[research-backed, not English-L1] Håkansson's studies of preschool children learning Swedish as a second language find verb-third after a fronted adverb (`Sen han åker` for `Sen åker han`). Those learners were not English-speaking adults. The shape of the error matches what English speakers do. It is not evidence about English speakers. §11.

Swedish verbs do **not** agree with the subject. `jag bor`, `han bor`, `vi bor`. The `-r` is not a person ending. Do not build a standing-rule item on "the han-form".

| Wrong | Right |
|---|---|
| `Idag jag jobbar` | `Idag jobbar jag` |
| `Jag inte förstår` | `Jag förstår inte` |
| `Gör du vill ha kaffe?` | `Vill du ha kaffe?` |

### 4.5 `sin` versus `hans` / `hennes`

[practitioner opinion] Elon: `Erik ringde hans mamma` → `Erik ringde sin mamma`. `sin` / `sitt` / `sina` means the subject's own. `hans` and `hennes` mean someone else's. The possessive agrees with the thing owned: `sin mamma`, `sitt hus`, `sina barn`.

The roadmap item is `min`, `mitt`, `mina`. `sin` is taught in that same lesson, because it is the English trap, not as a separate lesson. [inference]

## 5. Learners: who, why, what first

[research-backed] The MLA enrollment report (Lusin, Peterson, Sulewski and Zafer, 2023) counts Swedish at 487 enrollments in US higher education in fall 2021, down 25.8% from 656 in 2016. The report's table line is 486 plus 1, totaling 487. These are enrollments, not people. The sequence in that report runs 758 (2009), 732 (2013), 656 (2016), 487 (2021).

[research-backed] The ACS 2023 1-year table, via the Maryland planning page that was opened, gives Swedish ancestry as 3,254,150 people, about 1.0% of the US population in that table.

No opened survey says why US adults start Swedish. [inference] The likely reasons are heritage, a partner, travel, and study or work in Sweden. A 2016 Duolingo note that Swedish was popular inside Sweden because of immigration is not evidence about US learners, and that page was not used here.

What they need in the first hours, [inference] from §4 and from KCL part 1 putting articles with the first nouns:

1. The vowel letters and long versus short, including `här` / `hår` / `har` and `ooh` versus the English words "book" and "good".
2. `sj` versus `tj`, with `sju` and `tjugo` as the pair.
3. `en` and `ett` on the first nouns, not after a month of `en` as the default.
4. `du`, `hej`, `tack`, `Jag heter…`, `Jag kommer från…`.
5. Verb second as soon as sentences are longer than `Jag heter Anna`.

Pitch accent is not in that list. Thorén's ranking (§4.1) puts it below stress and quantity.

## 6. Pronunciation aid (`pron`)

**Decision: `pron` is required.** A generated English-letter respelling is reliable enough for A1. IPA is a worse card aid for this audience. `pron: none` would leave `sj` / `tj`, `å` / `ä` / `o`, and long versus short unmarked, which is where English readers are misled by the spelling.

The tool is `tools/target/sv/respell.js`. Command: `node tools/target/sv/respell.js "Jag heter Anna."` prints `Jag heter Anna. -> jah HAY-tehr AHNN-ah`. It exports `respell` and `pronPattern`. Every `pron` in a lesson or a deck is the tool's stdout, never a hand edit. When a word is wrong, add it to `LEX` in that file.

**How reliable.** Everyday native words with stress on the first syllable are right often enough to teach from: quantity, the vowel tokens, `sj` versus `tj`, soft `k` / `g` at the start of a word, and verb-second sentences made of those words. It does not mark pitch accent, on purpose. It misses some loan stresses and some `r` plus dental across a prefix. §6.5 is the list.

### 6.1 The key

One syllable, lowercase, when the word has one syllable. Otherwise the stressed syllable is in CAPITALS and the syllables are joined with hyphens. Exactly one syllable is capitalised.

**Vowels.** The letter `a` uses `ah` for both lengths. A short stressed `a` doubles a real geminate or a known single consonant (`matt` `mahtt`, `han` `hahnn`, `vatten` `VAHTT-ehn`). `mat` is `maht`. Do not double a consonant just because a cluster was split.

| Letter | Long | Short | Read it as |
|---|---|---|---|
| a | `ah` | `ah` plus a doubled coda when the spelling has a double consonant, or the word is on the short list | father. Length is in the consonant. |
| e | `ay` | `eh` | `ay` is pure, no glide into "ee". `eh` as in bed. |
| i | `ee` | `ih` | see / sit |
| o when it is the `u`-sound `/uː/` | `ooh` | — | food. Never the vowel of "book" or "good". `bok` is `boohk`, `god` is `goohd`, `skola` is `SKOOH-lah`. |
| o when it is short, and short `å` | — | `aw` | law. `kom` `kawm`, `gott` `gawt`, `måste` `MAWS-teh`. |
| å long | `oh` | — | a pure "oh", no English glide. `gå` `goh`, `på` `poh`. |
| u | `ueh` | `uh` | Say "ee" and round the lips. The `h` is a marker so a following consonant stays legal. `du` `dueh`, `hus` `huehs`. |
| y | `ew` | `yw` | German `ü`, French `u`. `fyra` `FEW-rah`. `ny` is written `new`; it is not the English word "new". |
| ä | `ae` | `eh` | `ae` as in "bad", held longer when long. Short `ä` shares `eh` with short `e`. |
| ö | `euh` | `ur` | Long: "ay" with rounded lips. The `h` is a marker. Short: `ur` as in the German aid. **The `r` in `ur` is not an `r`.** `öppna` is `URP-nah`. A real `r` after short `ö` is not written again: `för` `fur`, `dörr` `durr`. |

**Diphthongs:** `ej` `ey` (`hej` `hey`), `aj` `ai` (`maja` `MAI-ah`), `oj` `oy`.

**Consonants.**

- `j` is the `y` of "yes", never the `j` of "jam". The aid writes `j`, not `y`.
- `sj` is the sj-sound: `sju`, `skjorta`, `stjärna`, `chans`, and `-tion` (`station` `stah-SJOOHN`). One token. The real sound varies by region. [research-backed] Thorén: an English `sh` still comes across. Do not drill one town's tongue shape.
- `tj` is the tj-sound: `tjugo`, `köpa`, `kyrka`, `kjol`. It is not `sj`. Losing the difference merges 7 and 20.
- Soft `k` → `tj`, soft `g` → `j`, only at the start of the word and only when `e i y ä ö` is the next letter. `kille` and `kex` stay hard (Central Swedish). `sk` becomes `sj` only when the front vowel is the next letter (`ske` `sjay`). `skriva` stays `SKREE-vah`. `skola` stays `sk` because `o` is not a front vowel.
- `ng` is the `ng` of "sing". `gn` after a vowel is `ngn`: `regn` `rehngn`, `regnar` `REHNG-nahr`.
- `r` plus `t d n l s` inside the word is `rt` `rd` `rn` `rl` `rsh`: `bord` `boohrd`, `barn` `bahrn`, `första` `FURSH-tah`. Across the `för-` prefix the aid often keeps `r` and the next letter (`förstå` `fur-STOH`). §6.5.
- Swedish does not devoice a final `b d g` the way German does. `dag` keeps `d`. `st` and `sp` stay `st` and `sp`, not German `sht` / `shp`.
- A double consonant shortens the vowel. It is not German-style final devoicing.

**Stress.** The capital syllable is the stressed one. Compounds stress the first part (`smörgås` `SMEUHR-gohs`), except a few names in the tool's list (`Göteborg` stresses `borg`). `-tion` stresses the `ooh`: `station` `stah-SJOOHN`. `-era` stresses the `ay`: `studera` `stuh-DAY-rah`. Words in `-ell` stress the last syllable: `hotell` `haw-TEHL`.

**Spoken forms the aid shows, while the spelling stays standard.** `och` `aw`, `jag` `jah`, `ja` `jah`, `det` `deh`, `vad` `vah`, `mig` `mey`, `dig` `dey`, `sig` `sey`, `de` `dawm`, `dem` `dawm`. A stressed demonstrative `det` can be closer to `dayt`. The aid uses the usual unstressed shape.

**Letter names** (a single letter, or an all-caps abbreviation of 2–5 letters) use the Swedish names: `a` `ah`, `o` `ooh`, `å` `oh`, `ä` `ae`, `ö` `euh`, `u` `ueh`, `y` `ew`. `USA` is `ueh-ehs-AH`.

### 6.2 `pronPattern`

Printed by `pronPattern()`. The same string is `pronPattern` in `profile.json`. Letters `c q x z` are banned. `a` only before `h y e w i`. `e` (unless it follows `e` or `a`) only before `h e w u y`. `o` (unless it follows `o`) only before `h e o w y`. `u` only before `h e r`. `i` only before `h` or `e`, unless it is the `aj` diphthong (`i` after `a`). `y` only before `w`, unless it is `ay`, `ey` or `oy`. A word is one lowercase syllable, or hyphenated syllables with exactly one CAPITAL syllable.


### 6.4 What the first lesson must teach

Before any card hides the spelling and shows only the aid, `vowels` teaches this key:

- CAPITALS mean the stressed syllable. A one-syllable word is all lowercase.
- `ah` is the `a` of `mat`. A doubled consonant after `ah` means the vowel is short: `matt` `mahtt`.
- `ooh` is "food", even when the word looks like "book" or "good".
- `oh` is `å` and `gå`. `aw` is short `å` and short `o`.
- `ueh` / `uh` is the Swedish `u`. `ew` / `yw` is `y`. `euh` / `ur` is `ö`, and the `r` in `ur` is not an `r`.
- `sj` and `tj` are two sounds. `j` is "yes".
- The aid does not show pitch accent. Do not add tone marks by hand.

## 7. Standing practice rule

**Rule: gender and verb place.** Every lesson's practice set keeps re-testing two strands:

- **(a) `en` / `ett`, the definite suffix, and the adjective `-t`.** Which article, which suffix (`bilen` / `huset`), and `en stor` / `ett stort` / `stora`.
- **(b) Verb second, and where `inte` goes.** The finite verb is the second element. It is first in a yes/no question. `inte` follows the verb in a main clause and precedes it after `att`.

**Why these two.**

- They are the roadmap's own "hardest" list, after the sounds: `en` or `ett` and the definite ending; the verb in second place and where `inte` goes.
- [curriculum convention] KCL part 1 teaches articles with the first nouns. An English speaker who meets twenty nouns with only `en` will keep saying `en hus`.
- [inference] Swedish has no person endings, so the German-style "verb ending" strand would be fake. The `-r` is the same for `jag` and `han`.
- [inference] Both strands are available from lesson 1 if the sounds decks put `en` or `ett` on every noun, which they do. The JSON has no exemption field, so lesson 1 still needs two `order` and two `type` hits.
- **Not the standing rule:** `sin`, false friends, `halv tre`, `sj` versus `tj`, pitch accent. Those belong to one lesson. They come back as mixed items, and their `why` lines do not use the keywords below.

**Count** (`minOrder: 2`, `minType: 2`): in every practice set, at least 2 `order` items and 2 `type` items hinge on the rule, and the `why` names it with a keyword. A 14-item set has 3 `order` and 3 `type` items, so two of each three. A set may use only strand (a) or only strand (b). From `word-order` on, at least one of the four hinges is strand (b), so verb place does not disappear.

**What counts as hinging:**

- **`order`** hinges if the `why` matches and either the distractor is the other article or the other suffix or the other adjective ending (`en` / `ett`, `-en` / `-et`, `stor` / `stort` / `stora`), or the point is order: a fronted time or place word (`Idag jobbar jag`), a verb-first question, or `inte` on the wrong side of the verb. A distractor never repeats an answer tile.
- **`type`** hinges if the `why` matches and the fallback words include the other gender, the other suffix, or the other adjective ending, or the answer is a fronted sentence, a verb-first question, or a sentence whose `inte` is the point.

**Lessons where the four hinges are hardest:**

- `vowels` and `sounds`, before `en-ett` is the lesson title. The deck still marks every noun.
  - order: `en bil` (distractor `ett`); `ett hus` (distractor `en`)
  - type: "a car" → `en bil`; "a house" → `ett hus`
  - why: "bil is an en-word: en bil." / "hus is an ett-word: ett hus."
- `word-order`:
  - order: `Idag jobbar jag` (the tiles can also spell the English order)
  - why: "Verb second: idag first, then jobbar, then jag."
- `negation`:
  - order: `Jag förstår inte`
  - type: "that I don't understand" → `att jag inte förstår`
  - why: "inte goes after the verb." / "inte comes before the verb after att."
- `adjectives`:
  - type: "a big house" → `ett stort hus`
  - why: "The adjective ending is -t on an ett-word."
- `definite`:
  - type: "the car" → `bilen`; "the house" → `huset`
  - why: "The definite suffix is -en." / "The definite ending on hus is -et."

**Validator keywords** (`standingRule.keywords`, flags `i`):

```
\ben-word\b|\bett-word\b|\bcommon gender\b|\bneuter\b|\bgender\b|\bdefinite (?:suffix|ending|form)\b|\bindefinite article\b|\badjective ending\b|\bverb second\b|\bsecond element\b|\bsecond place\b|\bverb (?:comes )?first\b|\bsentence adverb\b|\binte goes\b|\binte comes\b
```

These are deliberately not keywords: bare `en`, `ett`, `plural`, `ending`, `subject`, `inte` alone, `sin`, `du`. "The subject comes after the verb" does **not** match. Write "verb second" or "the second element". Do not put a keyword in a `why` that does not hinge.

## 8. Lessons (final order)

**Changes from the roadmap**

- **Kept: all 24 items.** Lesson ids drop the `sv-` prefix. Each roadmap id appears once in a `roadmap` field.
- **Moved:** `en-ett` leaves the middle of stage 2 and sits in stage 1, after `intro` and before `numbers`. [curriculum convention] KCL part 1 teaches articles with the first nouns. [inference] If numbers and café phrases come first, every noun the learner has met takes `en`, and `ett hus` is a surprise. `en kaffe` and `ett glas` need the gender before the price lesson.
- **Not merged, not renamed away.** The two sounds lessons stay two: vowels and quantity first, then `sj` / `tj` / soft `k` and `g`. Pitch accent is a paragraph in `sounds`, not a third lesson.
- **Added (2 of the 3 allowed):** `compliments`, `traps`. The third slot is unused. Supine, perfect, comparison and `gå` versus `åka` stay out (§2.2).
- **Inside a lesson, not a new id:** object pronouns (`mig`, `dig`, `sig`) in `pronouns`; `sin` in `possessives`; double definiteness as recognition in `definite` and `adjectives`.

Stage names stay the roadmap's: Sounds and first words; First sentences; Everyday life.

### Stage 1 — Sounds and first words

1. **`vowels`** — Vowels: long, short, and å ä ö u y. Roadmap `sv-vowels`. Deck: Sounds and spelling.
   Long versus short, and the letters English does not have. The trap is `här` / `hår` / `har`, `mat` / `matt`, and `bok` read as English "book". Teach the aid key (§6.4) here. Every noun on the cards has `en` or `ett`, so the standing rule has something to bite on.

2. **`sounds`** — The sj-sound, the tj-sound, and soft k and g. Roadmap `sv-sounds`. Deck: Sounds and spelling.
   `sju` versus `tjugo`, `skjorta`, `stjärna`, `köpa`, `kyrka`, and the hard exceptions `kille` and `kex`. Pitch accent: one sentence that it exists, that the aid skips it, and that stress and quantity matter more (Thorén). Do not drill accent 1 versus accent 2.

3. **`greetings`** — Greetings: hej, tack, hej då. Roadmap `sv-greetings`. Deck: Greetings and courtesy.
   `Hej` all day, `God morgon`, `God kväll`, `God natt`, `Tack`, `Varsågod`, `Förlåt`, `Hej då`. `du` in `Hur mår du?`. `ni` appears as the plural, in one example, not as a formal singular.

4. **`intro`** — Introducing yourself. Roadmap `sv-intro`. Deck: About me.
   `Jag heter…`, `Jag kommer från…`, `Jag bor i…`, `Jag är lärare`, `Jag pratar engelska`. Jobs have no article: `Jag är lärare`, not `Jag är en lärare`.

5. **`en-ett`** — en and ett. Roadmap `sv-en-ett`. Deck: People and family.
   Moved up. Common gender and neuter, with family nouns and the first objects (`en bil`, `ett hus`, `en mamma`, `ett barn`). The trap is guessing `en` for everything. Plurals and the definite suffix wait.

6. **`numbers`** — Numbers and prices. Roadmap `sv-numbers`. Deck: Numbers, time and prices.
   0–100 and the hundreds, `tjugoett` not ones-before-tens, `Vad kostar det?`, `tio kronor`. Prices use the gender from lesson 5: `en kaffe`, `ett glas`.

### Stage 2 — First sentences

7. **`pronouns`** — Pronouns, and how are you. Roadmap `sv-pronouns`. Deck: Feelings and states.
   `jag`, `du`, `han`, `hon`, `den`, `det`, `vi`, `ni`, `de`. Object forms `mig`, `dig`, `sig` in the same lesson (KCL teaches them in part 2; they are the same words as the spoken `mey` / `dey` / `sey`). `Hur mår du?` `hen` once, for recognition. The deck is feelings so the phrases have somewhere to live: `trött`, `hungrig`, `Jag mår bra`.

8. **`present`** — Present tense in -r. Roadmap `sv-present`. Deck: Work and study.
   `jag bor`, `han talar`, `vi pratar`. The trap is adding an English-style person ending, or waiting for one. `är` and the modals do not take `-r`; say so, and leave the modal lesson for later. Deck nouns are jobs and school.

9. **`word-order`** — Verb in second place. Roadmap `sv-word-order`. Deck: Days, dates and weather.
   `Idag jobbar jag`. Days of the week, `idag` / `igår` / `imorgon`, and a little weather (`Det regnar`) so the fronted element is real. The trap is `Idag jag jobbar`.

10. **`questions`** — Questions. Roadmap `sv-questions`. Deck: Questions and connectors.
    `vad`, `var`, `vem`, `när`, `hur`, `varför`, and yes/no questions with the verb first. `och`, `men`, `eller` live here so the connectors deck is not empty of grammar. The trap is `Gör du vill…` and a question that keeps English order.

11. **`negation`** — Where inte goes. Roadmap `sv-negation`. Deck: Home and routine.
    `Jag förstår inte`. One subordinate example: `att jag inte förstår`. Home verbs (`bor`, `äter`, `sover`) so `inte` has a sentence. The trap is `Jag inte förstår`.

12. **`definite`** — The definite ending. Roadmap `sv-definite`. Deck: People and family.
    `bilen`, `huset`, `en bil` versus `bilen`. The neuter `-t` is written and is often quiet in speech (§6.5). Double definiteness (`den stora bilen`) is shown once, as recognition, and produced in `adjectives` only in the indefinite pattern.

13. **`plural`** — Plurals. Roadmap `sv-plural`. Deck: People and family.
    The five patterns, as recognition plus the common ones for production: `-or` (`flickor`), `-ar` (`bilar`), `-er`, `-n` (`äpplen`), no ending (`hus`, `barn`). Not a case system. The definite plural (`bilarna`) is one recognition row, not the lesson's goal.

### Stage 3 — Everyday life

14. **`time`** — Telling the time. Roadmap `sv-time`. Deck: Numbers, time and prices.
    `klockan är åtta`, `halv tre` = 2:30, `kvart över`, `kvart i`. The trap is reading `halv tre` as half past three.

15. **`modals`** — Modal verbs. Roadmap `sv-modals`. Deck: Travel and transport.
    `vill`, `kan`, `måste`, `ska` plus a bare infinitive. No `att`. `måste inte` means "don't have to". The travel deck is the context: `Jag ska åka tåg`, `Jag kan köra`, `Jag måste gå`.

16. **`cafe`** — At the café, and fika. Roadmap `sv-cafe`. Deck: Food and fika.
    `Kan jag få en kaffe?`, `en kanelbulle`, `fika`, `smör`, `Vad gott`. Gender from lesson 5 is the trap (`en kaffe`, `ett glas vatten`).

17. **`imperative`** — The imperative. Roadmap `sv-imperative`. Deck: Health and emergencies.
    `Kom!`, `Vänta!`, `Ta!`, `Hjälp!`. Short forms, often the stem. Health phrases: `Jag är sjuk`, `Jag har ont`. The trap is adding `-r` (`Kommer!` for `Kom!`).

18. **`adjectives`** — Adjective endings. Roadmap `sv-adjectives`. Deck: Shopping and clothes.
    `en stor bil`, `ett stort hus`, `stora hus`, and the predicate `Huset är stort`. Clothes and shopping. The trap is an invariable adjective copied from English. `den stora bilen` is recognised, not the production target.

19. **`possessives`** — min, mitt, mina, and sin. Roadmap `sv-possessives`. Deck: People and family.
    Agrees with the thing owned: `min bil`, `mitt hus`, `mina barn`. `sin` is the added trap (§4.5): `Erik ringde sin mamma`, not `hans mamma`, when the mother is Erik's.

20. **`routine`** — Daily routine. Roadmap `sv-routine`. Deck: Home and routine.
    `ofta`, `alltid`, `aldrig`, with verb second and `inte`: `Jag äter aldrig frukost`, `Jag förstår inte`. Family words recur. The trap is putting `ofta` where English puts "often", before the verb.

21. **`tycker-om`** — Likes: tycker om and gillar. Roadmap `sv-tycker-om`. Deck: Likes and free time.
    `Jag tycker om kaffe`, `Jag gillar kaffe`. `tycker om` is two words; `inte` splits them (`tycker inte om`). `gillar` does not split.

22. **`att`** — att plus the infinitive. Roadmap `sv-att`. Deck: Likes and free time.
    `Jag tycker om att läsa`. The trap is adding `att` after a modal (`Jag kan att köra`) or dropping `att` after `tycker om`.

23. **`directions`** — Directions. Roadmap `sv-directions`. Deck: Around town and directions.
    `till höger`, `till vänster`, `rakt fram`, `Var är stationen?`. `biljett` if it appears uses the tool. The trap is English prepositions (`vänta för`).

24. **`past`** — The past: pratade, var, hade. Roadmap `sv-past`. Deck: Days, dates and weather.
    Three high-frequency pasts, not the four conjugations and not the supine. `Jag pratade`, `Jag var trött`, `Jag hade`. `igår` is the fronted element, so verb second still applies: `Igår pratade jag med Anna`.

25. **`compliments`** — Compliments, and how to answer. Added. Roadmap id empty. Deck: Compliments.
    Kind lines about food, work, a skill and a person, plus the answer `Tack` or `Tack, det var snällt`. Not a deflection and not a compliment in return unless the speaker means it. Phrases are [inference] until a native checks them (§11).

26. **`traps`** — Traps for English speakers. Added. Roadmap id empty. Deck: Traps for English speakers.
    The false friends in §4.6, `måste inte` versus `får inte`, `vill` as "want", and one calque (`väntar på`, not `väntar för`). Gender and verb second still supply the standing-rule items. Do not make the taboo reading of `fart` the joke of a card.

## 10. Typing and script

Swedish is Latin, left to right, with `å ä ö`. `é` occurs in a few loans (`idé`, `armé`, `kafé`). The house spelling prefers `kafé` so the acute is rare.

- **`keys`:** `å ä ö`. Those three are the accent-bar buttons. `é` is not on the bar. A missing acute folds away with the other marks, so `ide` for `idé` is "almost" while `accents` is true. Do not make `é` the point of an item.
- **`accents`:** `true`. The intent, matching German, is that a missing mark is "almost" and a wrong letter is wrong.
- **`targetScript`:** `[A-Za-zÅÄÖåäöÉé]`. The validator only checks that a target contains one of these characters.
- **Apostrophes:** the genitive has none (`Annas`). The matcher treats a dropped inner apostrophe or hyphen as "almost". Writers do not put an apostrophe in an answer.
- **Hyphens:** compounds are one word. A hyphen typed as a space, or omitted, is "almost".
- **Capitals:** ignored by the matcher. Still teach lowercase days, months and `du`. Do not require a capital on a name as the only difference between two answers.
- **Punctuation:** `. , ! ?` are stripped. `Hej` and `Hej!` match.
- **A typed answer must include** the words, the gender article when the card shows it, and `å ä ö` when they are the point of a strict item. It need not include punctuation, capitals, or the quiet `-t` of `huset` unless the item is strict about that letter.

**What the app does today.** Checked in `src/app.html` and `tools/target/validate.js` without redesigning them.

`foldAcc` protects `ñ`, then strips every combining mark (U+0300–U+036F), then maps `ß` to `ss`, `œ` to `oe`, `æ` to `ae`, and `đ` to `d`. The ring on `å` and the diaeresis on `ä` and `ö` are combining marks. So `å` and `ä` both become `a`, and `ö` becomes `o`. `accLoose` is on whenever `accents` is true and the target is not Korean or English. `pracMatch` then treats that fold as "almost" unless the item is strict. `här`, `hår` and `har` all match each other. The validator's fold strips the same marks and does not protect `å ä ö` either.

`FLOURISH` has entries for Spanish and German, among others, and none for `sv`. `FL()` uses `FLOURISH[target]` or, if that is missing, the Korean set. `PRAISE` returns the Korean line when there is no flourish entry. There is no new script and no new font to add: the Latin courses already use DM Sans, which includes `å ä ö`.

**App changes, in priority order**

1. **A Swedish letter slip so `å` and `ä` do not fold to the same letter.** A missing mark (`a` for `å` or for `ä`, `o` for `ö`) is "almost". The wrong mark (`å` typed for `ä`, or `ä` for `å`) is wrong. Today's fold cannot do that.
   If it is **not** built: `accents` stays `true`. Writers set `strict` on any item where `å` / `ä` / `a` or `ö` / `o` is a different word (`här` / `hår` / `har`, `bär` / `bår`, `kär` / `kår`, `säl` / `sål`, `kör` / `kor`). The course does not pretend the engine can tell `å` from `ä`. The aid still can: `haer`, `hohr`, `hahr`.

2. **An `sv` entry in `FLOURISH`**, with Swedish praise (`Bra!`, a level-up line, a streak line, `Bra jobbat!`, `Hej då!`) and glyphs that include `å ä ö`. Same font as the German entry.
   If it is **not** built: level-up praise and the glyph rain fall back to Korean.

No other app change. No new keyboard layout beyond the three keys. No new font file.

## 11. Gaps and unverified claims

A native speaker has not checked this profile, the aid, or the compliment lines.

- No opened study of English-speaking adults' grammar errors in Swedish. §4's ranking is inferred. Elon.io is a teacher's list, not data. Håkansson's verb-third finding is preschool learners who were not English speakers.
- SAOL was not opened. The house spellings `idag` / `igår` / `imorgon` and `ska` follow the roadmap and the Wiktionary usage note on `idag`, not a line in SAOL. `i dag` is the form to expect in newspapers.
- `biljett` had no IPA on the Wiktionary page that was opened. Stress on the second syllable is unverified against a transcription.
- `kanel` had audio and no IPA on the Swedish entry. The aid's stress follows the Danish entry.
- `Minneapolis` in the tool is an unverified Swedish-flavoured pronunciation.
- Compliment wording (`Bra jobbat`, `Vad gott`, `Du är snäll`, `Tack, det var snällt`, `Vilken fin tröja`) is ordinary language, not a cited dialogue study.
- Name frequency is not from SCB. The names are common choices, not a ranking.
- Café prices, Swish, and "Sweden is cashless" were not opened. Do not put them on cards.
- The BHASVIC file's unit range is wider than the roadmap's "units 1–6" line. This course follows the roadmap's grammar list. If a later pass opens SAOL or a current Rivstart contents page, the spelling house rule and the "stop before the supine" line should be checked against it.
- The tool's `LEX` list (§6.5) is only as good as the pages behind it. `Göteborg`, `söndag`, `smör`, `varsågod`, `idag`, `middag`, `lördag`, `kom`, `hon`, `barn`, `rolig` were checked on Wiktionary this pass. Other `LEX` rows are the working list for A1 and should be on a native's first look.
- Thorén's poster and thesis support the decision to skip pitch accent and to keep stress, quantity, and the `sj` / `tj` split. They are not an error corpus of English speakers' grammar.
