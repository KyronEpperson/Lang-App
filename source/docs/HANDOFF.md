## How to get a working build home in a new chat

The build home of the old chat (`/home/claude`) does not carry over. Everything it needs is mirrored in the repository `KyronEpperson/Lang-App` under `source/` on every release.

1. Clone the repository to `lang-app/` beside the build home.
2. Copy `lang-app/source/{src,tools,docs,packs,build.sh,harness.js}` into the build home; the static site files (`index.html`, `sw.js`, `fonts/`, images) are at the repository root and go in `korean-site/` (see `source/README.md`).
3. `./build.sh`, then `node harness.js` must end with no errors before anything else.
4. Release with `bash -x tools/curriculum/ship.sh "<version>" "<subject>" "<body>"` (it runs every test, writes the release report and audio coverage, copies the mirror, commits and pushes `main`; Vercel deploys from `main`).

Standing rules: Supabase publishable key only; never open, print or commit secrets; never force-push `main`; ElevenLabs / Azure keys only from the owner's own terminal; no posting under the owner's name. Strict CSP in `src/app.html`: no `//` comments in one-line code, no inline handlers, edits by assertion-guarded replacement.

## Owner's standing decisions

- Build and release without waiting for native-speaker review; user bug reports will correct later. Do not ask about reviewers.
- Courses for Cantonese speakers are in written Cantonese. Hmong = White Hmong (RPA), words only from the sourced glossary (`tools/hmong/`).
- Dialect and variety choices: ask once when it decides every card, otherwise decide and say so.
- Every lesson in every language: a teaching section, then that section's flashcards (five shown, then quizzed, to the end of the deck), then the next section, and a review of all the lesson's decks at the end (2026-11-24; see "Lesson sections").
- Fixes and improvements (2026-11-25): plan and make them without asking first; tell the owner the problem and the fix; apply it to ONE course, release, then ask whether to apply it to all courses.
- Every course opens with a foundation that teaches its writing system the way Korean lesson 1 does, fitted to the script (2026-11-25; see "Character foundations").
- Replies: brief, bullets, direct. Visuals as PNG.

- **What a card saves.** `st` memory length in days (3 figures) · `df` difficulty, 1 to 10 · `h` one number: the last four days the card was answered (bit set = no miss that day), how many days it was answered (up to 4), and whether the last answer was wrong · `mb` the box the judge wrote. `box`, `due`, `seen`, `lapses` and `last` are still written so that older releases keep working: box 1 after a miss, else 2 to 6 by memory length (under 3, 7, 14, 30 days, then 30 or more). Per course: `S.mem = {q, g, n}`, the two personal numbers and how many answers have moved them, and `S.memNote` (the one-time note was hidden).
- **Reading saved progress.** `mjRead(card)`. No `st`: the memory length comes from the box (box 1 is a third of a day with an open miss; boxes 2 to 6 are 1, 3, 7, 14 and 30 days; past lapses raise the difficulty). `mb` differs from `box`: an older release answered the card since, and `mjSync` brings it into line (their miss cuts the memory as a miss here would, their right answer lifts it to at least that box's days). Reading never writes. The plan said "convert on first load"; this gives the learner the same result and rewrites nothing until a card is answered.
- **Rules that are easy to get wrong.** Time is days since the card's last answer; "same day" is the local calendar day. A miss stays due today and is cleared only by a right answer on a later day. One lapse a day at most; a first answer that is wrong counts as a missed day; a card is parked as a leech after misses on 4 different days. On a card's first day its strongest right answer counts, so "Got it" and then a right pick starts where a first right pick does. A right answer at most quadruples a memory; a miss cuts it to 4 days at most. A repeat one minute later adds under 1% to a memory of a day or more and a few percent to a memory of hours (1.5% at a third of a day), which never clears a miss. The personal numbers move only on a card the judge itself graded last, and only after half a day or more; one answer moves the first by at most 16%. With the starting numbers Memorized comes on the third right answer (day 4 picked, day 8 typed); a learner whose personal numbers have risen well above 1 can get there on the second.
- **Answer log** (`S.rlog`, last 500 rows): `[time, box before, right, ms to answer, hour, question type (0 picked, 1 typed, 2 "Got it"), days since the last answer (-1 on a first answer), forecast (-1 on a first answer), judgement before (0 new, 1 learning, 2 memorized, 3 needs practice)]`. Rows from older releases have 5 fields; the Progress chart places them by box. These rows are what release C needs to tune the starting numbers.
- **Who chooses what (release B).** Two notions carry all of it. *Risk* (`mjRisk`): the chance that a card is forgotten by this time tomorrow if it is left alone; a card just due with a 1-day memory is at 0.175, with a 30-day memory at 0.103. *Waiting for practice* (`memWaiting(decks)`): judged needs practice, not answered right today, not suspended, not buried for today. A card missed today and then answered right still shows as needs practice until a right answer on a later day (release A's rule), but it no longer waits. One order serves every chooser (`memSort`): waiting cards, then learning cards not yet answered today, then memorized ones, each group with the most at risk first; last, the cards already answered right today, the one answered longest ago first.
  - *Quiz after each new five* (`loopEarlier`, `loopQuiz`): the five asked three ways, plus the first 15 (`MEM_EARLIER`) of that order among the earlier cards of the picked decks, memorized ones left out. 30 questions at most.
  - *"Practice these first"* (`practiceFirst`; `#firstStart` on the Practice screen, shown by `quizChips`; the result screen of a round's quiz in `learnLoop`, which has no pass mark since 2.104.0; 2.108.0's "Review these 5 again", "Retry quiz" and "Review missed" come under it): the main button once 10 (`MEM_HOLD`) cards of the picked decks wait. It runs the Weak spots quiz (three passes) on the 20 most at risk; its result screen applies the same rule again. "Next 5 cards" / "Learn 5 at a time" stay on offer: new cards are never blocked in Practice (owner's decision in the plan).
  - *Review* (`orderReviews`, also used by sessions for their due cards): the two due cards least at risk first as a warm-up, then the cards that need practice, then the rest, the most at risk first; inside a group no two cards of one deck in a row where possible. Due lists are no longer shuffled as a whole: with "Decks start shuffled" on, cards within about 0.01 of risk of each other change places (cards learned together would otherwise come in deck order every time); only a whole deck opened as "All" is shuffled.
  - *New cards in Review* (`rvNewOK`): no new batch after too many fresh misses, as before (`rvLoad` now counts the cards whose last answer was wrong, read from the judge), and none while 10 cards wait for practice in any deck. Due cards come before new ones anyway, so the second rule mostly brings parked leeches and needs-practice cards that are not due in front of new cards, as extra practice (`rvExtras`: waiting cards first, then by risk, each right answer in the sitting halving the risk). The last-resort lines in `rvRefill` still keep the page from running dry.
  - *Weak spots* (`weakAll`, `weakPool`): every card judged needs practice in any deck, minus suspended and buried-for-today ones; overconfident cards first, then the order above; one run takes 20; the button shows the total.
  - Left alone: the quiz picks and the new-card rule of sessions, `learnFive`, blitz, exam, "Practice these" on Progress, "learned" at 60% for opening the next deck, and the flashcards inside a lesson (`lsRun`, 2.107.0: each quiz brings back up to five earlier cards of the section's deck, picked at random). "Review missed" (2.108.0) asks its cards through `loopQuiz` with no earlier cards.
